# Yoda

Evidence file for Yoda, **Star Wars Legends** continuity. Each entry is a short description of what he does or what is said about him, followed by the full quote in a code block with its source. Anti-feats and limitations are in their own section at the end, each backed by quotes.

**How to read this file**

- Quotes are copied word for word from the sources, with whitespace and curly quotes normalized. `[…]` marks an omission inside a quote.

- The source line is the last line inside each code block, so one copy gets the quote and its citation. Novels are cited with the chapter where we could find it.

- **†** after a source means the quote was transcribed from a published source that was not re-checked against our own copy of the text (film dialogue, DVD commentary, guides, comics). Everything else was matched against the text on file.

## Limitations

*These are anti-feats, limits and caveats, each followed by the evidence behind it.*

### 30. Alone against Sidious, Yoda realizes he does not "have it": the Sith had adapted over a thousand years while the Jedi trained to re-fight the last war, and he thinks he cannot destroy the new Sith with a lightsaber.

```
It came when Yoda found himself alone against the dark.

In that lightning-speared tornado of feet and fists and blades and bashing machines, his vision finally pierced the darkness that had clouded the Force.

Finally, he saw the truth.

This truth: that he, the avatar of light, Supreme Master of the Jedi Order, the fiercest, most implacable, most devastatingly powerful foe the darkness had ever known …

just—

didn’t—

have it.

He’d never had it. He had lost before he started.

He had lost before he was born.

The Sith had changed. The Sith had grown, had adapted, had invested a thousand years’ intensive study into every aspect of not only the Force but Jedi lore itself, in preparation for exactly this day. The Sith had remade themselves.

They had become new.

While the Jedi—

The Jedi had spent that same millennium training to re-fight the last war.

The new Sith could not be destroyed with a lightsaber; they could not be burned away by any torch of the Force. The brighter his light, the darker their shadow. How could one win a war against the dark, when war itself had become the dark’s own weapon?

He knew, at that instant, that this insight held the hope of the galaxy. But if he fell here, that hope would die with him.

Hmmm, Yoda thought. A problem this is …

— Revenge of the Sith novelization (Matthew Stover, 2005), ch. 20
```
