# Qorl

Evidence file for Qorl, the TIE fighter pilot stranded on Yavin 4 after the Battle of Yavin, **Star Wars Legends** continuity. Each entry is a short description of what he does or what is said about him, then the full quote with its source on the last line inside the same block. Anti-feats and limitations have their own section at the end, each backed by quotes.

**How to read this file**

- Quotes are word for word from the source texts, with whitespace and curly quotes normalized. Quotes are extended to the full sentence or paragraph.
- The source is the last line inside each code block, so one copy grabs both. Novels carry a chapter number.
- Sources read for this file: Young Jedi Knights 1: Heirs of the Force (Kevin J. Anderson and Rebecca Moesta, 1995), read in full on 2026-11-19 CST. Young Jedi Knights 2: Shadow Academy (same authors, 1995), read in full on 2026-11-20 CST (notes: `notes/materials/174-yjk-02-shadow-academy/`). OCR slips in the e-book text were corrected against the 1995 print scan. Notes: `notes/materials/173-yjk-01-heirs-of-the-force/`.
- Later Young Jedi Knights books (The Lost Ones onward) are not read yet; his later appearances will be added when they are.
- Entries about the same fight are split: what he does well is under the feat sections, what goes wrong is under Limitations.
- Canon material is not covered.

## Background and Battle of Yavin

### 1. Qorl gives his service number and then remembers his name.

```
He rattled off the numbers as if he had memorized them.

Service rank and operating number only.

“Not your number,” Jaina persisted. “Your name. I’m Jaina. This is my brother Jacen.”

“CE3K-1977,” the TIE pilot said again, without emotion.

“Your name?” Jaina asked a third time.

Finally her question seemed to perplex him. He looked at the ground, looked at his tattered uniform. His mouth opened and closed several times, but no sound came out, until finally he said in a croaking voice, “Qorl … Qorl. My name was Qorl.”

— Heirs of the Force (Kevin J. Anderson and Rebecca Moesta, 1995), ch. 14
```

### 2. In Heirs of the Force, Qorl recalls being aboard the Death Star when it came to destroy the Rebel base.

```
“Death Star,” Qorl said. “I was on the Death Star. We came here to destroy the Rebel base after Grand Moff Tarkin blew up Alderaan.

This was our next target.”

— Heirs of the Force (Kevin J. Anderson and Rebecca Moesta, 1995), ch. 14
```

### 3. Qorl describes the Battle of Yavin: every TIE squadron launched, and all his companions were destroyed by X-wings.

```
“The Rebels knew we were coming.

They fought. They sent their defenses against the battle station.

All TIE squadrons were launched.

“I flew with my squadron. All my companions were destroyed by X-wing defensive fire.

I was damaged in the cross fire … one solar panel out of commission. I spun away from the Death Star, out of control.

— Heirs of the Force (Kevin J. Anderson and Rebecca Moesta, 1995), ch. 14
```

### 4. Qorl was told to make a good landing if he could and to wait. He crash-landed in the jungle and was thrown from his craft.

```
“I needed to get back to effect repairs. All comm channels were jammed, filled with dozens of requests for assistance. My orbit was decaying, and I spun toward the fourth moon of Yavin. I kept trying to hail someone on the comm channels. When I finally got through, I was told I would have to wait for rescue. They instructed me to make a good landing if I could—and to wait.”

“So you crashed,” Jaina said.

“The jungle cushioned my fall. I was thrown out of my craft into the dense brush … when one of the solar panels caught and lodged in the trees above. I limped over to my TIE fighter. Stayed as close as I dared, afraid that it might explode.

— Heirs of the Force (Kevin J. Anderson and Rebecca Moesta, 1995), ch. 14
```

### 5. From the jungle floor, Qorl watched the Death Star explode.

```
“I looked up into the sky just in time to see the Death Star blow up. It was like another sun in the sky. Flaming chunks of debris fell through the air. It must have started dozens of forest fires. For weeks, meteor showers were like fireworks as the wreckage rained down onto the moon.

— Heirs of the Force (Kevin J. Anderson and Rebecca Moesta, 1995), ch. 14
```

## Survival and Endurance

### 7. For twenty years Qorl hid his wrecked fighter from the Rebels, surviving in rags patched with furs, his left arm crippled in the crash.

```
The pilot’s protective uniform hung in rags from his gaunt frame, worn to tatters and patched with furs from an incredible number of years living alone in the jungle. His left arm, injured during the crash, was drawn up like a twisted claw against his chest. He stepped forward, cracking twigs under his old boots as he made his way to the crash site that was no longer secret. He had camouflaged the wrecked Imperial craft many years ago, hiding it from Rebel eyes. But now, despite all his work, it had been discovered.

— Heirs of the Force (Kevin J. Anderson and Rebecca Moesta, 1995), ch. 9
```

```
He had carved out a large shelter from the bole of a half-burned Massassi tree, in front of which he had lashed a lean-to of split branches. Altogether, it provided him with a simple but comfortable room, like a living cave.

— Heirs of the Force (Kevin J. Anderson and Rebecca Moesta, 1995), ch. 14
```

## Piloting

### 14. Qorl lifts off in the restored TIE after twenty years on the ground and climbs out of the canopy with surprisingly skillful maneuvering.

```
Qorl’s TIE fighter, which had lain hidden and crippled on the surface of Yavin 4 for more than twenty years, finally rose into the air. Its twin ion engines made the characteristic moaning sound that had struck fear into the hearts of so many Rebel fighters.

With surprisingly skillful maneuvering and a burst of speed, Qorl’s fighter climbed up through the forest canopy and soared away toward the Jedi academy.

— Heirs of the Force (Kevin J. Anderson and Rebecca Moesta, 1995), ch. 18
```

### 15. Qorl had been one of the best pilots in his squadron. With a badly healed arm he still flies well, and he once landed his ship under heavy fire despite grievous engine damage.

```
He had been an excellent pilot—one of the best in his squadron—soaring through mock battles and enforcing the Emperor’s unbending will.

Air currents buffeted him, and the pilot reveled in the sensation of flight. He had not forgotten, not even after so many years. The vibrating power that pulsed through the fighter’s engines, along with a sense of freedom and liberation after so long an exile, buoyed him.

Qorl watched the knotted green crowns of Massassi trees flowing beneath him in the storm of his ship’s passage. With his thickly gloved, badly healed arm, he found it difficult to control the Imperial craft—but he was a fighter pilot. He was a great pilot. He had managed to land his ship, despite grievous engine damage, under heavy enemy fire.

— Heirs of the Force (Kevin J. Anderson and Rebecca Moesta, 1995), ch. 20
```

## Combat and Tactics

### 22. Qorl takes a practiced firing stance against the oncoming air speeder.

```
But the armored Imperial soldier stood his ground at the center of the clearing, spreading his legs for balance and assuming a practiced firing stance. He pointed his blaster at the oncoming air speeder, unflinching.
```

### 23. Qorl plans his strike: trap any fighters in their hangar, collapse the Great Temple, then deal with the freighter and the power station.

```
It was probable, Qorl realized, that the Rebels kept a large force of more powerful fighters in an underground hangar bay. If so, his first task was to prevent those craft from launching—even if only by damaging the doors enough to keep the ships trapped inside.

He decided his best strategy would be to continue his straight-line course and fire with full-power laser cannons on the main structure of the Great Temple. He would blow the entire building to rubble—perhaps causing it to collapse internally, thus eliminating the Rebels and destroying all their equipment inside.

Then he could swoop around and take care of the single light freighter, even if it managed to get up off the ground. His third target would be the power-generating station.

With the Rebels completely paralyzed by his lightning attack, he would swing back for the last time. He would charge up his laser cannons again and go for the kill, mopping up anything he had missed the first time.

From start to finish, it would take only a few minutes to bring the Rebels to their knees.

— Heirs of the Force (Kevin J. Anderson and Rebecca Moesta, 1995), ch. 20
```

### 24. When the crystal snake strikes, its fangs cannot get through Qorl's gauntlet. He rips it free one-handed and jettisons it.

```
The crystal snake dug its spearlike fangs into the thick leather of Qorl’s gauntlet, but was unable to penetrate all the way to his skin.

As he flung his hand back and forth, Qorl could feel the heavy weight of the crystal snake writhing, snapping, though he could see almost nothing at all.

He let the TIE fighter fly itself as he reached with his good hand to grab the long body of the serpent just behind its head. He ripped the fangs free and stuffed the thrashing creature into the cockpit jettison chute.

— Heirs of the Force (Kevin J. Anderson and Rebecca Moesta, 1995), ch. 20
```

### 35. Qorl's laser cannons fail on his attack run because Jaina sabotaged them, and his diagnostics still read normal.

```
He squeezed the button again and again—and nothing happened! The weapons systems did not respond.

Qorl flicked on the backups as he spun the TIE fighter in the air, barreling down again on his target. Over and over he tried to fire, but the laser cannons were completely dead.

His eyes swept the diagnostic panels, but all the readings seemed normal.
```
