# *Willow*, *Last Crusade*, *Young Indiana Jones*, *Radioland Murders* (Smith pp. 162–219)

## *Willow* (1988) (pp. 162–176)

Lucas’s story, Ron Howard directs, Bob Dolman writes. A child with a ‘great prophesied destiny’ is, Smith notes, also ‘central to the Republic trilogy’. One character’s line that ‘magic is the bloodstream of the universe’ ‘recalls Obi-Wan Kenobi’s initial explanation of the force’ in *Star Wars*. That parallel is Smith’s, not a Lucas quotation about the Force.

Howard on Lucas as a ‘creative producer’; Warwick Davis on the shoot. **LUCAS ON WILLOW:** ‘I’ve always gotten about 50/50 reviews, at best . . . the initial *Willow* reviews were not . . . favourable. Most American reviews are . . . very glib, tell you what the movie’s about, then some sort of very sticky little statement. That’s about all they amount to. I don’t really give them much concern.’ (*Starburst* #126, February 1989.)

## *Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade* (1989) (pp. 177–186)

Story by Lucas; Jeff Boam screenplay; Spielberg directs; Sean Connery as Henry Jones, Sr.; River Phoenix as young Indy (the seed of the TV series). Reviews and the father/son structure are covered. No Vader/Force material.

## *The Young Indiana Jones Chronicles* (1992–1996) (pp. 187–214)

The series ‘grew out of’ the *Last Crusade* opening. Lucas: ‘A bunch of ideas popped out … I turned them into screenplays [and] I came up with seventeen hours. It was like a fountain!’ He writes a *TV Guide* launch piece on combining education and adventure. ABC burns off the first run too quickly for Smith’s taste. Episode-by-episode notes occupy many pages (Carrier and Flannery years; later TV-movie re-edits). **LUCAS ON ‘PHANTOM TRAIN OF DOOM’:** he wanted ‘to make a real old Indiana Jones adventure’; researches lead him to a unit nicknamed ‘The Old and the Bold’ — ‘old men on an adventure with this young teenager’; later ‘one of my favourite episodes’.

## *Radioland Murders* (1994) (pp. 215–219)

Mel Smith directs; Lucas executive-produces a 1930s radio-station comedy that becomes a test bed for digital set extensions. **LUCAS ON RADIOLAND MURDERS** (*American Cinematographer*): ‘Soon *Radioland*’s fix-it shots and digital set extensions and enhancements will be so commonplace we will not regard them as special effects.’ Smith adds that within a decade this was ‘absolutely right.’
