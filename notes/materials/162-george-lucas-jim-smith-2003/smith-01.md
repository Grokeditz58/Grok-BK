## Introduction (pp. 1–5)

Smith’s pitch: Lucas is ‘the most successful independent moviemaker who has ever lived’, a ‘one-man brand’, easy to underrate because he has ‘only directed five theatrical feature films’ (a 2003 count: *THX*, *Graffiti*, *Star Wars*, *The Phantom Menace*, *Attack of the Clones*). The book therefore covers sequels and TV work Lucas ‘created and wrote the story outlines for’ even when he is not the credited director, using Ron Howard’s phrase ‘a creative producer’.

Format of later chapters: credits, PRINCIPAL CAST, TAGLINE, SUMMARY, TRAILER, SCREENPLAY, CASTING, PRODUCTION, ALTERNATIVE VERSIONS, CUT SCENES, QUOTES, MUSICAL NOTES, RECURRING CONCERNS, VISUAL INTERESTS, FORWARDS/BACKWARDS, EXTERNAL REFERENCES, RELEASE, CRITICISM, AWARDS, AFTERLIFE, TRIVIA, TITLE TATTLE, EXPERT WITNESS, and **LUCAS ON [TITLE]**.

**p. 4, RECURRING CONCERNS — primary-watch Lucas statement (scan-verified; Index of Quotations points to Crawley, *Starburst* #125, January 1989):**

> As the man himself once said, ‘The underlying issues, the psychological motives are the same . . . in all my movies: personal responsibility and friendship, the importance of living a compassionate life as opposed to a . . . selfish life.’

Typography locked as printed (British single quotes; spaced ellipses). The same page also reports Harrison Ford as ‘repeatedly quoted’ telling Lucas, ‘You can write this shit, but you can’t say it’.

**Notes on nomenclature (pp. 4–5):** Smith will call Episodes IV–VI the ‘Imperial trilogy’ and Episodes I–III the ‘Republic trilogy’. ‘These aren’t terms used by Lucas or Lucasfilm.’ The 1977 film is usually called *Star Wars*, not *A New Hope*.

## “Before USC” (pp. 6–7)

‘This is not a biography.’ Brief personal sketch anyway. **Slip:** ‘George Walton Lucas Junior was born in Modesto, California in May **1943**.’ The standard date (and Shields 2002, notes 160) is 14 May **1944**.

Crash: the *Modesto Bee* caption ‘George W Lucas Jr survived this crash’; three days ‘his life was in the balance’; three months in and out of hospital. Smith says Lucas has made clear in ‘countless interviews’ that the crash caused him to reassess his life, become ‘more contemplative’, and enrol at Modesto Junior College (social sciences, anthropology, comparative religion). Film became, ‘by his own admission, “an addiction”.’ Haskell Wexler encouraged the USC application. Industry legend: Lucas told his father he would be ‘a millionaire at 30’. *American Graffiti* later netted him $7 million at 29.

## Student films (pp. 8–18)

- **Look at Life (1965), p. 8–9.** One-minute Life-magazine photomontage (Nixon, LBJ, MLK, a child in a Klan uniform). Caption/VO includes ‘Hate not the oppressor for hate comes before strife and love covers all sins.’ Smith already applies Lucas’s later phrase ‘a cubist film’ (used of *THX-1138*) to the juxtaposition method.
- **Herbie (1965), p. 9.** Light on a stationary car, Herbie Hancock on the soundtrack. Early ‘circles and curves’.
- **Freiheit (1966), p. 9–10.** A boy (Randall Kleiser) is shot crossing a border; title = German ‘freedom’.
- **1:42.08 to Qualify (1966), p. 10–11.** Undergraduate colour racing short. **Lucas:** ‘As a boy [I] never thought about making movies — never in a million years. I wanted to be a racing driver.’ (Index: *Starburst* #126, February 1989.)
- **anyone lived in a pretty (how) town (1967), pp. 11–12.** e e cummings poem; first Techniscope. Draft/diabetes: Lucas ‘found that he had diabetes — something he himself was unaware of at the time — and [was] rated 4F’. (No Air Force / speeding-tickets subplot of the kind in the 2015 Rise of an Empire comic, notes 161.)
- **The Emperor (1967), pp. 12–13.** Mock-imperial tour of DJ Bob Hudson. Smith flags a ‘middle-aged man proclaiming himself “the Emperor”’ as integral to both *Star Wars* trilogies. Marcia Griffin assists the edit; they had met ‘whilst co-editing a documentary (see Other Projects)’.
- **THX 1138 4EB (1967), pp. 14–15.** Fifteen-minute postgraduate film, Godard’s *Alphaville* as a model (epub scanno ‘Alpbaville’). VO: ‘This is authority. You will stop where you are.’ First prize, Third International Student Film Festival, 1968. After the feature it was subtitled *Electronic Labyrinth*. A boxed sidebar, ‘Lucas and Coppola’, sketches the Zoetrope friendship, a later public spat over *Tucker*, and continuing closeness (1999 BBC *Omnibus* at Skywalker Ranch).
- **6.18.67 (1967), pp. 16–17.** Lucas’s student ‘making of’ *Mackenna’s Gold* (titled only as the last day of shooting). No interviews; landscape over the production. Honourable mention, Third National Student Film Festival. A 1971 LA PBS documentary, ‘George Lucas: Maker of Films’, included this short, *THX 1138 4EB*, and a ‘lengthy interview’.
- **filmmaker (1968), pp. 17–18.** 64-minute documentary on Coppola’s *The Rain People*, funded with $12,000 of that feature’s publicity budget. Coppola VO: ‘The system will fall by its own weight. It can’t fail to!’

## Feature *THX-1138* (1971) (pp. 19–27)

Credits include Lucas and Walter Murch on the screenplay, Coppola as executive producer, Lucas as editor (the IA epub occasionally OCRs ‘Film’ as ‘Rim’). Taglines: ‘Visit the Future Where Love is the Ultimate Crime!’ / ‘The Future is Here.’ / ‘Visit the Future Where Escape is the Ultimate Crime!’

Plot (p. 19): THX comes off sedation, is punished, escapes; police are called off because the pursuit ‘is judged by management to be too costly’; he emerges into a sunset. The opening Buck Rogers trailer is recut so the voice-over says ‘the twentieth century’ rather than ‘the twenty-fifth’, suggesting ‘a portrait of the present’ (p. 19).

Screenplay: expansion of the short, first urged by Carl Foreman; an unidentified playwright’s draft is rejected; Lucas writes with Murch. Casting notes on Donald Pleasence (print spelling; Smith also uses ‘Pleasance’) drawing on a German POW camp, and extras hired from Synanon / Delancey Street. Production in BART tunnels, Marin Civic Center, Lawrence Livermore. Warners later cut four minutes; Lucas ‘has since repeatedly described the process as being “like watching someone cutting the fingers off of his child”’ (p. 22). Studio advice to ‘put the freaks up front’ becomes a New Hollywood catchphrase.

**Recurring concerns, p. 23 — Smith’s analysis, not a Lucas quote:** ‘LUH’s initial maternal response to THX’s obvious distress (vomiting, unconsciousness, distraction) transfers into erotic attachment; the same process occurs across *The Phantom Menace* and *Attack of the Clones* with the relationship between Anakin Skywalker and Padme Amidala.’ Cars as ‘a symbol/method of escape’.

**Lucas on form, pp. 24–25:** Asked if the film was science fiction, ‘Lucas responded that it was a “documentary fantasy”.’ ‘THX was designed for the camera,’ he later said, ‘some of the dramatic was sacrificed in favour of the camera.’ Described by its writer/director as ‘a cubist film’.

Release: poor US box office (Smith cites ‘around $5,000’ as a contemporary *Variety* disappointment figure — treat as reported, not independently checked). Cannes Directors’ Fortnight; Lucas and Marcia backpack and sneak into their own premiere. Novelisation by Ben Bova.

**LUCAS ON THX-1138 (p. 27; Index also lists this under p. 28, quoting Slater / *Show*, April 1970):** describing the film as a ‘cubist’ portrait of Los Angeles: ‘[the movie’s protagonist] comes off drugs and discovers he’s . . . living in a cage . . . with the door open ... we are all living in cages and the doors are wide open and all we have to do is walk out.’
