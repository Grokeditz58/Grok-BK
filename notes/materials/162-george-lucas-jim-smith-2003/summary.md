# 162 summary — Jim Smith, *George Lucas* (Virgin Film, 2003)

Secondary film-study of Lucas’s career, written and published in 2003, after *Attack of the Clones* and before *Revenge of the Sith*. Smith is often a careful quoter (the book ends with an Index of Quotations), but he is also an advocate: *The Phantom Menace* is defended at length, and the *Episode III* chapter is a pre-release reconstruction plus **explicit speculation**. Treat draft-history and “balance of the Force” material as Smith’s account unless a Lucas line is given.

## Priority hits (Vader / Anakin / the Force / Sith / Chosen One / power / canon)

- **What the films are “about,” p. 4 (scan-verified; Index of Quotations: Starburst #125, January 1989):** Lucas: ‘The underlying issues, the psychological motives are the same . . . in all my movies: personal responsibility and friendship, the importance of living a compassionate life as opposed to a . . . selfish life.’ Reprised in the Postscript (p. 275, scan-verified) as ‘the importance of living a compassionate life, as opposed to a selfish one’ (the reprise drops the mid-sentence ellipses). The same theme is restated in the *Jedi* chapter (p. 137) as Smith’s paraphrase of Lucas: the series is about living ‘a compassionate life’ — selflessness over selfishness.
- **The Force in the 1977 drafts, p. 55 (scan-verified):** A ‘Kiber Crystal’ (‘a physical embodiment of the force’) ‘was written in and then written out.’ Early Vader is ‘sometimes a rogue Jedi, sometimes a member of a rival clan called “the Knights of Sith”’. Smith reports that Lucas ‘claims to have written an outline which comprised in essence the events of *Star Wars* and its two sequels’; Smith adds that the claim is ‘often dismissed’, ‘never got nearly as far as full script form’ if it existed, and was set aside for a shorter, more saleable film. Brian De Palma’s sarcasm about ‘the farts of others’ is given as the reason ‘the force of others’ lost the second half of its name (p. 55, continuing onto p. 56).
- **‘Documentary fantasy’ / ‘used future’ / fairy tale:** On the 1977 film Lucas wanted a ‘cohesive reality’ and ‘the seeming contradiction of . . . fantasy combined with the feel of a documentary’ (*American Cinematographer*, July 1977; Index pp. 52–53). Smith repeatedly applies Lucas’s THX phrase ‘documentary fantasy’ to *Star Wars*. Novelisation notes, p. 55: Lucas calls it ‘a movie for the kid in all of us’ that would drive away ‘the grimness of everyday life’ and stop it ‘following us into the movie theater’. He would spend the rest of his life describing the film as ‘a fairy tale’ (p. 55; again in the Postscript, p. 276).
- **Campbell, p. 53 (Index):** ‘The book began to focus what I had already been doing intuitively.’
- **Father reveal, pp. 102–103:** Smith’s account, not a Lucas quote: no version of the *Empire* script, including the shooting draft, contains ‘I am your father’. The shooting draft had ‘Obi-Wan is your father’. Kershner and Lucas told Hamill the real line in secret. Smith argues the fatherhood motif was ‘certainly’ set by the time *Empire* began filming in 1979, even if it may not have been ‘set in stone’ during *Star Wars*. Kasdan, on when Lucas knew Luke and Leia were siblings: ‘I don’t know when he knew that ... I don’t know if that was [always] part of the scheme’ (p. 101).
- **Dark side as metaphor, p. 110 (Smith, not Lucas):** In plot terms the Force used selfishly ‘somehow physically and mentally corrupts’; ‘in realistic terms this is simply a metaphor through which Lucas tries to make the basic point that to do the wrong thing, even for the right reasons, is morally harmful to the individual.’ Luke’s choice on the gantry is ‘Death or dishonour.’
- **Jedi ending, p. 131:** Lucas’s handwritten first draft ‘ends, as the film does, with Vader dying saving his son from the Emperor’. Kasdan suggested Luke turning, Luke dying, or Luke using the Death Star on the Imperial capital; ‘all of these were rejected by Lucas who felt very strongly that the ending as planned was the core of the film’. Smith’s own title reading (p. 140 area): the film is about ‘the return of one particular Jedi, Anakin Skywalker.’
- **Midichlorians / Chosen One, p. 221:** These appear in Smith’s plot summary of *The Phantom Menace* (Qui-Gon believes Anakin is ‘the chosen one’ who will ‘bring balance to the force’; midichlorians ‘communicate the will of the force’; Anakin’s count is unusually high). This is Smith restating the film, not a new Lucas interview.
- **Demystifying the Force, p. 240 (Smith’s analysis):** In *The Phantom Menace* Lucas ‘strips the force of its pseudo-religious status whilst making Darth Vader overtly messianic.’ He ‘subtly raises the idea that Ben and Yoda are manipulating Luke in the Imperial trilogy’, and apparent contradictions ‘dovetail with fearful precision’ once the 1977 film is watched afresh. Flag as Smith, not Lucas.
- **Chosen One / ‘balance,’ pp. 262–263 — pre-2005 speculation, labelled as such:** Smith suggests the prophecy’s ‘balance’ might be ‘the extermination of the Jedi’; that thousands of Jedi and only two Sith means the Force is out of balance; that Luke in black is himself ‘balance’; that Vader’s death leaves ‘only one Jedi left alive, his son’. The next sentence is: **‘This is all pure speculation.’** Do not promote it as Lucas, as later canon, or as the finished *Episode III*.
- **Dark side as possession / second names, p. 263 (also speculation):** Smith wonders whether the dark side ‘is in some sense conscious’ and like ‘possession’ or becoming ‘schizoid’, citing Sith double names and Vader’s ‘That name no longer has any meaning for me.’ Again, Smith thinking out loud before the 2005 film.
- **Postscript, pp. 275–277 (scan-verified on 275–276):** Lucas has called *Star Wars* ‘my destiny’ and his ‘narrative symphony’. Values: compassionate life (see above). Later elaboration of the fairy-tale brief, p. 276: ‘someone has to tell young people what we think is a good person. “This is what a good person is; this is who we aspire to be.” You need that in a society. It’s the basic job of mythology.’ **Not a religion, p. 277:** Lucas ‘demystifying and redefining the force and the Jedi within the fiction itself’; he dismissed Coppola’s suggestion of a Jedi church with ‘forget it’, ‘refused to make the force his own dianetics’, and is described as a storyteller ‘without “any interest in power”.’
- **No 7 / 8 / 9, p. 264:** LUCAS ON EPISODE III: ‘I will not do a 7, 8 and 9. This is it. This is all there is.’
- **No canon-hierarchy statement** (no G-canon, no EU ranking). ‘Canon’ appears only in ordinary senses (Jules Verne’s canon; film-history ‘canonisation’ of Tim Robbins). No power-level maths.

## Other Lucas-in-his-own-words worth keeping

- Film as ‘an addiction’ (p. 6); ‘As a boy [I] never thought about making movies — never in a million years. I wanted to be a racing driver’ (p. 11).
- THX is a ‘documentary fantasy’ and ‘a cubist film’; ‘THX was designed for the camera’; LUCAS ON THX-1138: ‘[the movie’s protagonist] comes off drugs and discovers he’s . . . living in a cage . . . with the door open ... we are all living in cages and the doors are wide open and all we have to do is walk out.’
- ‘I’m not a good writer. It’s very, very hard for me. I don’t .. . have a natural talent for it’ (Index: p. 57).
- Special Edition, Greedo (Index: p. 71): ‘In terms of Han’s character I didn’t like the fact that the first thing he does is gun someone down in cold blood.’
- LUCAS ON STAR WARS: ‘. . . life sends us along funny paths. We think we know where we are going but we don’t. I was really determined to be a documentary filmmaker and do avant garde things on the side.’
- LUCAS ON EMPIRE: ‘It’s the middle act of a three-act play ... it is a darker film [but] you don’t have an exuberant happy second act . . . that’s drama.’
- ‘How much does a film cost? How much does a film make? It’s very depressing to dwell on that. No film is about what it cost’ (p. 81 area).

## Factual slips and dating limits

- **Birthdate, p. 6:** ‘May 1943’. Wrong. Standard (and Shields 2002, notes 160) is 14 May **1944**.
- *Episode III* and *Indiana Jones IV* chapters are 2003 advance sketches. Smith’s *Episode III* outline (pregnant wife hidden; Padmé takes Leia to Alderaan; Ben appeals to Vader then duels) does not match the 2005 film and should not be used as a plot source for it. *Indy IV* is dated 2005 here, with Kate Capshaw ‘confirmed’ and a Lucas line that ‘Indiana will have a son’; the film released in 2008 was a different script.
- Intro slips / typographical oddities (some may be typesetting rather than Smith): ‘Paul Schraeder’, ‘Wilfred Huyuck’, ‘Lucas Rim’ / ‘Rim Editor’, ‘nor merely accessible’, ‘Check Dederich’ (usually Chuck Dederich). ‘Qui-Gon Ginn’ throughout the *Phantom Menace* chapter (for Jinn).
- The IA epub is OCR and is full of scannos (`Alpbaville`, `TFIX-1138`, `Flollywood`, `Episode ffl`). Those are not automatically the print book’s errors; quotes below follow the Virgin scan when the page was opened.

Smith’s own Anakin/Padmé–LUH ‘maternal response … transfers into erotic attachment’ parallel (p. 23) is **analysis, not a Lucas quote**.
