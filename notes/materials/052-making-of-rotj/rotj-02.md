# Making of RotJ: notes part 2, ch. 2 (end) to the story-conference sidebar (pp. 43–70)

## Ch. 2 (end), pp. 43–53
- Jabba spec: "a sac-like stomach that wiggled and sloshed when Jabba laughed ... And a runny nose." Watts: "This film is the monster movie." (p. 43).
- Lucas on directing: "Directing is emotional frustration and tremendously hard work—7 days a week, 16 to 18 hours a day." Freeborn was loaned out to *The Dark Crystal*: "probably three or four weeks" (Kazanjian, p. 43).
- **Ewok design.** Johnston: "We did 300 drawings of little furry guys ... ‘That’s it! We'll kill ’em with cuteness! Try them all cute.’" (p. 44). Lucas: "The idea was just a short Wookiee ... In the early versions of the script, those primitives were Wookiees." (p. 44). Barron says the teddy-bear turn felt like "maybe too obvious a marketing idea", which McQuarrie disliked. Rodis-Jamero pitched Ewoks who stay out of the war: "I was completely wrong." (pp. 44–45). Lucas's sayings: "‘Paper is cheaper than film’" and "‘No one ever said it would be easy.’" (p. 45).
- Pan Am deal: about $300,000 in travel in exchange for 90 seconds in a promo and a credit "acknowledgment" (p. 45).
- **Marquand.** Background: Emmy-winning *Search for the Nile*; *The Legacy* ("It was a crucifying experience"); *Birth of the Beatles*; *Eye of the Needle* during the *Heaven's Gate* turmoil; his father Hilary was a Labour minister of health (pp. 45–46). His son James: "He said to me that the Ewoks are like the Vietcong." (p. 46). Marquand: "a horse dragging this thing along ... with George holding the reins." (p. 46).
- Ford lobbies to kill Han: "He’s got no mama, he’s got no papa, he’s got no future ... so let’s allow him to commit self-sacrifice." (p. 46). Kasdan: "I had no intention of writing for anyone ever again" (p. 46).
- **Revised rough draft sidebar (June 12, 1981; 95 pp.)**, pp. 47–53:
  - Wedge flies a TIE fighter in Leia's convoy. Jerjerrod to Vader: "It would appear that you still have some feelings for your troublesome offspring." / Vader: "The only feeling I have for him is hatred." / "Get off my bridge!" (p. 47).
  - The Jawas Atetu and Tweety. The "SARLACC NEST" replaces the sloth pit. Luke to Han: "She loves you, Han. My feelings for her are different than yours." (p. 48). The Emperor kidnaps Luke on Tatooine.
  - Yoda in the cell: "Ben can no longer help you. His power to stay in the netherworld has been spent." Ben returns "in the flesh": "I could no longer stay in the netherworld" (pp. 49–50).
  - Ackbar first appears, as "a pale blue non-human creature", and Mon Mothma gives a speech (p. 50). Vader to the Emperor: "It is for me to train my son"; he is choked: "The boy is mine to train. Your place is with the fleet." (p. 51). Yoda: "Evil one, this will be the last day of your existence." (p. 51).
  - Vader's sword hand lands in the lava. Luke: "I cannot. I will not turn to the dark side." The last five pages are mostly blank (pp. 52–53).
- pp. 54–55: a McQuarrie double-spread painting of a shuttle and two Death Stars under construction. McQuarrie: "the painting I like most is only a simple flyby in outer space" (p. 53 caption).

## Ch. 3: A City Too Far (June–Aug 1981), pp. 56–70
- Tippett: "The monsters came from really practical and pragmatic considerations—but they were inspired by terror." His creatures are "annoying little gremlins ... more likely to knock over your cup of coffee or untie your shoelaces" (p. 56). Kenny Baker and Jack Purvis were body-cast for Ewok molds (p. 56).
- Fox's Levy worries that Fox loses money if Jedi earns ≤$70M. Kasdan signs on to write, with a WGA clause that "Lucasfilm ... shall be treated as a wholly impersonal corporate name" (p. 57). *Dragonslayer* earns only $14M (p. 57).
- July 4, 1981: the Skywalker Ranch groundbreaking, with a time capsule holding a "QUESTION AUTHORITY" button and the microfilmed fan-club list (p. 58).
- **Locations chosen:** the Miller-Rellim redwoods near Crescent City, and Buttercup Valley near Yuma. Marquand: "Yuma was a joke, an absolute joke ... the Bible story about somebody building their house on sand." Lucas: "Tunisia didn’t have very many sand dunes ... In Yuma, they’re close to hotels." (p. 58).
- Sy Snootles: "‘Oh, she’s got to have some Mick Jagger lips’" (Marquand quoting Lucas, p. 59). **Ackbar:** Lucas let Marquand choose, and he picked the "Calamari man with a red face". Marquand: "good people aren’t necessarily good-looking people and that bad people aren’t necessarily ugly people." (p. 59).
- The story conference at Park Way House: five 10-hour days, Lucas, Kasdan, Marquand and Kazanjian. Lucas had just adopted Amanda: "On Jedi, I was trying to find time to spend with my daughter." Marquand: "Larry said, with a smile, ‘Well, let’s kill Yoda.’" (p. 59). Kasdan: "George can be goofy." (p. 60). The transcript runs to 268 pages.
- Had Abbadon is dropped. Lucas: "I could put the Emperor on board; then you could kill the Emperor and destroy the Death Star and the Empire might not build any more." / "a 12-year-old can understand." (p. 61). Rinzler's view: Han and Leia "suffered a kind of collateral damage" (p. 61).
- **Story conference sidebar (pp. 62–70)**, all from the transcript:
  - Yoda's death. Lucas: "‘You’re on your own, kid. You are the last of the Jedi now.’" Marquand: "‘Watch out for your father. Keep your underpants clean.’" Lucas: "death is not a terrible, horrible thing" (pp. 62–63).
  - The lava pit. Lucas: "You know that the only reason that you have created that lava pit is to throw somebody into it. It telegraphs the movie." (p. 63).
  - **The famous exchange** (p. 64). Lucas floats Luke putting on the mask: "‘Now I am Vader.’ Surprise! The ultimate twist." / Kasdan: "That’s what I think should happen." / Lucas: "No, no, no. Come on, this is for kids." / Kasdan: "I think you should kill Luke and have Leia take over." Lucas: "You’re a product of the 1980s." Kasdan: "the movie has more emotional weight if someone you love is lost along the way". Lucas: "This is a fairytale. You want everybody to live happily ever after".
  - Lucas's thesis: "There are three parts to the movie: Jabba, the Ewoks, and Luke and the Emperor ... Luke redeems his father, which is the classic fairytale ... feel absolutely good about life." (p. 65).
  - Blind Han (Kazanjian's idea). Lucas: "Maybe we could make him drunk, too. The drunk blind man." Han whacks Boba into the pit "like a homerun" (p. 65).
  - On Ben: "he says that Vader betrayed and murdered his father, which doesn’t necessarily mean that he killed him". The Force: "when you die, your energy drains into the Force, which is made up of all living beings." Guinness "doesn’t want to talk about the Force." (p. 65).
  - Leia disguised as the bounty hunter delivering Chewie (Marquand's idea). Kasdan: "a great Shakespearean court scene: girl dressed up as a boy." (pp. 66–67). Jabba: "‘So you like to kiss? I like to kiss, too.’" Leia strangles Jabba with the chain: "It’s The Godfather" (pp. 67–68).
  - R2 hides the lightsaber (Kasdan's idea). On the new saber: "you get a letter in Starlog magazine. Big deal." Lucas: "Not only did Luke lose his weapon and was castrated ... He has built his own lazer sword; he is his own man, he is not a son anymore. He is an equal." (p. 68).
  - **The story of Anakin** (pp. 69–70). Lucas: "Anakin Skywalker starting hanging out with the Emperor, who at this point nobody knew was that bad, because he was an elected official." / Kasdan: "Was he a Jedi?" / Lucas: "No, he was a politician. Richard M. Nixon was his name." The Force is "Like yoga ... Also like karate." Yoda is "a teacher, not a real Jedi." Kasdan: "I accept it, but I don’t like it." Luke's mother "confides in Ben ... She is pregnant ... Ben has to fight him and he throws him down into a volcano".

## Story conference sidebar (cont.), pp. 70–77
- **The backstory.** Vader "falls into the pit, his other arm goes and his leg". Ben takes Luke to "a couple out there on Tatooine". Leia and her mother "go to Alderaan and are taken in by the king there, who is a friend of Ben’s. She dies shortly thereafter". Leia remembers her mother dying "when ‘I was two years old.’" (p. 70).
- **The title problem.** Lucas: "There are already people sending me letters saying Jedi don’t take revenge" (p. 70). On Ben's "He is our last hope": "He discounts women because he is a male chauvinist pig." (p. 70). On Ben's guilt: "‘It was my own pride in thinking that I could be as good a teacher as Yoda. I wish that I could stop the pestilence that I’ve unleashed on the galaxy.’" (p. 70).
- **Lando.** Kasdan: "What about killing him now". Marquand: "Lando should fly straight into the Death Star." Lucas: "Put him with the fleet and have him lead the rebel attack." (pp. 70–71).
- **Vader and the Emperor.** "They both want him converted to the dark side: the Emperor to replace Vader, and Vader to replace the Emperor. They are perfect bad guys." Ben "can cloud the mind of the Emperor", which is how he becomes "even more powerful". On Yoda and the Emperor: "Yoda isn’t a Jedi, the Emperor isn’t a Jedi ... he’s like the grand priest ... Jedi are the chiefs, they are the warriors" (p. 71).
- **The throne-room finale takes shape.** Lucas: "The Emperor starts preaching to him Jim Jones-style." Luke "turns off his lazer sword, throws it at the Emperor. ‘I have controlled my hate; I don’t hate my father. If you are so much on the dark side, you kill me—I dare you.’" Then: "‘You little twerp!’ ... lightning bolts ... and then Vader kills the Emperor." (pp. 72–73). Vader's unspoken plan: "then we are a team, then we are father and son." (p. 73).
- **Had Abbadon goes back and forth.** Kasdan: "I think Had Abbadon is worth saving ... Forget the Death Star." Lucas finally: "I go back to getting rid of Had Abbadon. Forget it." The idea of the trap is born ("The Death Star is supposed to be inactive, it is not"), even though it "makes the Emperor seem kind of stupid." (pp. 73–76). Kazanjian on the Emperor: "He’s almost using himself as a lure." Kasdan: "Maybe get rid of the Ewoks." He also pitches Luke usurping the Emperor's power. Lucas: "He does not want to be mayor, he does not want to be president; he has sworn to be a police officer". Marquand: "you spend a lot of time throwing scorn on each other’s ideas." (pp. 75–76).
- **The unmasking.** Lucas: "Taking his mask off is a very dramatic thing". Vader: "‘I want to see you without the aid of this machine. I want to reject the machine.’" Kasdan: "If when you take off the mask and he’s not a bad looking guy, it’s a real cheat." Lucas: "It has to be a real father ... The whole machine thing becomes a partial metaphor for the dark side of the Force, which is: Machines have no feelings." On *Empire*'s glimpse of Vader's head: "It wasn’t supposed to be that grotesque". Kazanjian: "Does he shave?" (pp. 76–77).
- The Falcon: "We just 86’d the Falcon." Kasdan: "He is the one who blows up the Death Star with the Falcon." On the celebration: "the little fuzzy wuzzies having a party ... Why can’t you cut around the whole universe and see every planet celebrating?" (p. 77). [Reader note, not from the book: the galaxy-wide celebration was finally added in the 1997 Special Edition.]
- p. 76: a sketch by an unknown hand of "the death of Boba Fett, as discussed in the story conference".

## Ch. 4: A Poet's Emperor (July–Sept 1981), pp. 78–92
- Storyboards: Johnston, Rodis-Jamero, George Jenson and Brook Temple. Johnston: "he comes back with a big red pen and scratches them all out [laughs]" (p. 78). There were about 200 speeder-bike drawings. Johnston's kitbashed mockup used a Formula One nose, the NASA shuttle front and German anti-aircraft gun plates. Lucas: "‘That’s it. We're done.’" (pp. 78–79).
- **Costumes.** Marquand pushed for Rodis-Jamero over Oscar-winner John Mollo. Lucas: "‘You’ve got two weeks to prove it.’" ... "‘Oh, yeah. You got the job.’" (p. 79). Aggie Rodgers joins and brings her baby to meetings, which "used to drive Howard crazy" (p. 80).
- **Leia's slave outfit.** Rodgers: "George didn’t tell me ‘Frank Frazetta,’ but I had a bunch of Frazetta books". Sculptor Richard Miller: "‘George loves it. When he doesn’t say anything, that’s how we know he loves it.’" (p. 80).
- **McQuarrie leaves.** He finished "over 300 sketches and about 18 paintings for Jedi before calling it quits". McQuarrie: "I feel sort of burned out and tired ... I just lost momentum." Barron: some called *The Art of The Empire Strikes Back* "‘The Art of Ralph McQuarrie,’ and Ralph was hurt by that." At a thank-you: "‘I was one of the first people that George hired’ ... George got up and said, ‘No, you were the first one.’" (pp. 81–84). The Roddenberry anecdote: "‘I don’t like the script.’" (p. 81).
- Editor Sean Barton: "He’s very shy and I’m quite shy, so initially we hardly said a word to each other" (p. 85). *Empire*'s first run ended July 23, 1981, at $181,379,640 domestic (p. 85).
- **The actors.** Fisher: "They think I’m some kind of space bitch." / "I asked George for some sort of a drinking problem ... And I book myself into a convent." (pp. 85–86). Hamill wanted Luke to go dark and come back. Ford: "I desperately wanted to die ... George has a predisposition to happy endings." Marquand couldn't tell Ford that Leia is Luke's sister. Ford, later: "There was no future in dead-Han toys" (p. 86).
- The ranch. Greber: "‘Every dollar we put into this ranch turns into 15 cents.’" Lucas: "‘this is my dream, not your dream.’" (p. 87).
- **Blue Harvest.** The code name was used for the Miller-Rellim lease ($5,000 fee donated to the Rowdy Creek Fish Hatcheries), with the tagline "Horror beyond imagination" and a fake Halloween 1983 release (p. 87). Jabba's palace "optically enhanced" creatures were planned: Lava Man, Amoeba Man, Stop-Motion Man, Fog Man (pp. 87–88). Deep Roy did Ewok fittings (p. 88).
- Fan rumors: Boba Fett was "a beautiful woman, Luke’s father, or, perhaps, Luke’s mother". Rinzler admits he was among those who thought the Emperor was a clone of Ben ("the author included"). Hamill's favorite rumor: "Solo and Vader are somehow fused". Peter Mayhew's fan letter: "‘Don’t you think it’s disgusting that Wookiees don’t wear clothes?’" (p. 89).
- Marquand on the Elstree lava field: "‘Forget it! We’re not doing that.’" The "Jedi ‘One Line’" is dated Aug 5, 1981. Marquand: "I knew that I wanted him to eat a frog." (p. 89). Start date Jan 11: "this paper will self-destruct!" (Kazanjian, p. 89). Muren: Lucas "re-did the whole thing correctly" for the bike chase (p. 90).
- Finance: Lucasfilm's net income to July 1981 was $12,191,000; the Bank of Boston underwrites Jedi (p. 90). Merchandising split: Fox gets 10% after principal photography (p. 90). Marvin Davis buys Fox. Rinzler: "Erroneous rumors would say that Jabba the Hutt was modeled on Davis" (p. 90). The deal is closed with a $2.4 million Empire betterment for Fox, and network broadcast of Star Wars is delayed until January 1984 (p. 91).
- **Kasdan's draft** (Sept 14; 106 pp. typed): Lucas: "I had to assume going into it that half the people didn’t believe Vader was Luke’s father". The Emperor's line: "Everything that has transpired has done so according to my design." (pp. 91–92). Rinzler: Vader "appears to have lost his mojo ... becomes more of a plot-puppet". Kasdan: "It’s very hard to give weight to a story where everything is going to come out okay" (p. 92).
- **Second draft sidebar (Sept 21, 1981)**, from p. 93: the moon is now "JUS-ENDOR"; Han is blind after carbonite; Jabba: "‘I have seen you like to kiss ... And I like to kiss, also!’" / "LEIA Death? / THREEPIO Worse." (p. 93). Luke to Jabba: "If you have even the brains of a bantha, you’ll begin to bargain with me now." (p. 94).

### Visuals, pp. 68–94 (checked on the scans)
- p. 69: a McQuarrie production painting of the droids walking the road to Jabba's palace. pp. 72–73: Johnston's Endor landing platform with a giant dish/gun among the tree stumps, and Reynolds's shuttle-in-docking-bay concept.
- **pp. 74–75: two dark McQuarrie paintings of Vader leading Luke through the Imperial City on Had Abbadon.**
- pp. 80–83: Johnston's early bike-chase storyboards ("LEIA'S POV ... BIKER FIRES AT LEIA"). p. 85: Rodis-Jamero's concept for Leia's slave bikini. p. 88: the "Blue Harvest / Horror Beyond Imagination" logos. pp. 92–93: the pig-guard costume in progress and Tippett sculpting Ackbar's head.

## Second draft sidebar (cont.), pp. 94–99
- Kasdan's Dagobah scene has Yoda confirm Vader's real name, "Anakin". Ben: "What I told you was true... from a certain point of view." / "a great many of the truths we cling to depend greatly on our point of view." / "My mistake was thinking I could be as good a teacher as Yoda." (p. 95).
- Draft-only beats: Ben "took you to stay with my brother Owen on Tatooine"; Leia was "adopted by my friends—the Governor of Alderaan and his wife". On Yoda's death: "It’s very nice actually. And quite a relief . . . from a certain point of view ... All his former students are waiting." Luke ignites his saber and "hurls the lightsaber into the woods": "I don’t like this. Any of it!" / "Will you help me?" / "I can’t. But I will be with you always." / "(unimpressed) Thanks a lot." (pp. 96–97).
- Han: "We've all chosen to die young." "Fly casual." Emperor: "I wonder if your feelings on this matter are clear, Lord Vader." Rinzler calls this Kasdan having "brilliantly condensed" the scene (p. 97). C-3PO tells the Ewoks the saga (p. 97). Luke to Vader: "Throw off the chains of the Emperor." (p. 97).
- **Roy Carnon's UK duel storyboards** (p. 98, fall 1981) tried ideas that were cut: "Luke using karate, Vader with two lightsabers, Luke hiding beneath the floor" (p. 99). In the draft, the Emperor is hurled "through the wide window into the void of space", and unmasked Vader is "an elderly man with a white beard" (p. 99).

## Ch. 5: Setup on Space Street (Sept 1981–Jan 1982), pp. 100–121
- Prowse: "There is no negotiation." Rinzler: he "had inadvertently leaked story points early on while making Empire and was therefore an ongoing security risk." (p. 100). The *Raiders* Japanese poster left out Ford (Kazanjian memo, p. 100).
- Marquand on casting: Bib Fortuna (Mike Carter) should have "pink eyes". The fat dancer: "In my mind she is Jabba’s daughter." (p. 100).
- **Salacious Crumb.** Tippett: "‘Wait a minute guys while I tie my soolacious,’ instead of shoelaces, because I’d had a couple of pints". The name was refined "in homage to ... Robert Crumb". Lucas credits the idea to Ben Burtt. Lucas: "One day I came in and here was Salacious and I fell in love with Salacious." (pp. 101–102). Carson's note to Kirk Thatcher: "‘Kirk, get back to work!’" (p. 101).
- Budget: $28,341,730 total; the UK cost of living was up 50%. Kazanjian: "Our first shipment of lumber, alone, was $101,000." Moving the Falcon would have cost $40,000, so Lucas said "‘Let ILM paint it in.’" (pp. 101–102). A wardrobe memo budgets "Extra costing for ‘Triumph of Will’ scene", about $13,000 for the Emperor's-arrival troops (p. 102).
- Penny Marshall at the cold-wax bra fitting "was shooting daggers from her eyes" (p. 103).
- Ewok casting: the call ran in the *Sun* "on page three under the picture of a nude". Warwick Davis was "two foot eleven, 11-year-old, ‘bright as a button’"; his grandmother heard the radio call (p. 103).
- **Title.** The Nov 1, 1981 revised second draft is titled *Return of the Jedi* (p. 103). Lucas "literally cut and stapled parts of Kasdan’s script onto lined pages" and changed about 50% of it (p. 103). Then on Nov 13: "‘The name of the second sequel ... is now officially Revenge of the Jedi.’" (p. 109). Lucas: "Larry took my script and rewrote it and then I took his script and rewrote that" (p. 103). Emperor/Vader as partners: "‘Lord Vader, you weren’t on your game, were you?’ ‘No, Master. I’m sorry,’" (p. 104).
- **The bike-chase videomatic.** Muren and Johnston shot it with "Barbie-scale" toys, a tiny Hitachi camera and cardboard-tube trees "left over from E.T.", working out "100 shots in a week". Lucas: "I made a leap of faith and left it to Dennis" (p. 104).
- **Revised second draft sidebar** (pp. 105–108). Endor's name is said to come from 1 Samuel 28. Oola is dropped into the rancor pit. Boushh: "‘This bounty hunter is my kind of scum, fearless and inventive.’" Yoda: "Twilight is upon me and soon night must fall." Lando: "I commanded the Nabis fleet at the battle of Tamab". New lines: "Your overconfidence is your weakness." / "Your faith in your friends is your weakness." / "I am a Jedi as my father was before me" (pp. 105–108).
- **Locations.** Yuma's dunes had moved. Marquand: "These dunes are moving." Lucas: "The Sarlacc pit was a mess from the very beginning ... It should have been shot in England." Greber argued about "a ship out in the desert". In Crescent City: "200 inches of rain", a "quagmire" (Reynolds) (p. 109). Lucas shrinks sets: "‘We don’t need this corridor’—and save us $70,000." (p. 110).
- Marquand's rebel briefing room "sort of like Britain’s last stand in 1940". Lucas: "‘They win because they’ve got the best equipment.’" (p. 110). Jabba took "four tons of clay" (Freeborn; sculpted by John Coppinger) (p. 110).
- The Ewok test in the snow at Black Park (Nov 25, 1981): "Their eyes misted up instantly." Kenny Baker: "George said they don’t really come to life until they blink". Tebrooke decorated costumes with "bits of dead birds, including a bird skull" (p. 111). Watts: "We never did get the articulated Ewoks" (p. 112).
- **Shooting script** (Dec 1). Lucas: "one of the disadvantages of doing the final chapter is that you have to tie up all the loose ends". The distributed third draft held **fake scenes**, "such as Luke killing the Emperor" (p. 112). The third-draft sidebar (pp. 113–114) quotes the decoy: Luke "hurls the Emperor's body into the bottomless shaft". Unmasked, Vader is "a horrible mutant ... Luke is repulsed". Han: "‘You know I love you’", in a gunfighter-style shootout. "Gammorian Guards" (p. 113).
- The Fox deal (Dec 4, 224 pp.): a $10M loan, with Fox's share at 30% of the first $35M rising to 40% beyond $105M. Marvin Davis: "‘Georgie boy, you’re going to make me rich with movies and I’m going to make you rich with oil.’" A key-man clause ties Fox's sequel rights to Davis owning the studio (p. 115).
- James Earl Jones's agent mentions "the voice of Varda [sic] ... and the six others being planned." (p. 116). Lucas: a lightsaber "became heavy and unwieldy once activated", so it must be held two-handed (p. 116).
- **Casting the Emperor.** McDiarmid read at 10:45 ("good profile", "reddish hair"), and Ben Kingsley also read. **Alan Webb, 75, was cast** ("lots of energy for 90" [sic]). Lindsay Anderson was possibly considered. Sebastian Shaw auditioned "for ‘dad’" three times; Alan Rickman read for Jerjerrod (pp. 116–117). Production-meeting notes: "We should have some blood in this film. Not in Peckinpah proportions" (p. 117).
- The Jan 3, 1982 San Anselmo flood: Burtt chose to film it ("Do I try to rescue the equipment or do I make a documentary film? I chose the latter."). It forced the move to Sprockets on Kerner (pp. 117–118). The Computer Division's Genesis effect. Hamill: "‘You traitors!’" / Lucas: "‘It’s a business, kid.’" (p. 118).
- Lucas's handwritten Yoda page: "Once you start down the dark path, forever will it dominate your destiny." (p. 118 caption).
- Arrival in England: Ford arrived "in a wheelchair due to lingering back pain". The actors told Marquand: "‘We are a club ... And you’re now an honorary member.’" (p. 119). Rodgers on Luke's black: "‘just copy the white one from Star Wars in black.’" Fisher on the bikini: "to frighten me into exercise, I think". Marquand: "I couldn’t believe the way Princess Leia was dressed in Cloud City. It was a disgrace." (p. 119). p. 120 has the joke "Notorious San Rafael Gang of Four" wanted poster: Lucas, "holed up in Northern Californian Redwoods with elite bodyguard of small furry creatures".

## Ch. 5 (end), pp. 120–121
- The insurance policy is about $4 million. Lucas arrives Jan 7 with the shooting script, Williams's temp Jabba music and the Betamax videomatic. **Only Lucas, Kazanjian and Marquand had the secret "blue" pages** (p. 120). Hamill: "I felt like Boris Badenov in Rocky and Bullwinkle". Ford: "‘Well, I understand, you know, you love Luke, that’s okay.’" Lucas: "Harrison used to say, ‘Give me as few lines as possible’" (p. 120).
- Marquand: "I’d almost found Threepio a drag in Empire". Daniels: "I visualized Threepio as someone who might lead an army, but only because he was trailing at the back" (pp. 120–121). The Ewok set went over budget by more than $100,000. A hole was cut in Jabba's throne for Salacious's puppeteer. Lucas: "I made him Jabba’s sidekick" (p. 121). Hamill: Lucas said "this film is a culmination of everything that’s gone before" (p. 121).

## Ch. 6: The Friction of Multiple Lenses (Jan–Feb 1982), pp. 122–155 (part 1, to p. 144)
- **Day One** (Jan 11, 1982): the sandstorm, chosen as a "non-decision" (Watts). Kazanjian: "when we started shooting, we were already about $1.5 million over budget." Spielberg's telex: "It’s about time somebody made Blue Harvest. Break a leg." (p. 122). Before the shoot, Fisher escaped toxic heater fumes and Hamill's car was stolen (p. 122).
- First take: "R2-D2 veers off course and careens into a rock" (Peecher). Kazanjian: the third R2 unit also failed, so they "Drilled a hole in the front and put a wire on it so we could just pull it." Daniels walked into a rock. The crew "wrote our names on our backs" (pp. 123–124). Hamill was "the only actor to perform the first day of all three pictures". Plan: 92 UK shooting days (p. 124). Ziff's telex: "Please ask Cindy ... if my cat still alive. If not, put it in the freezer." The full-sized Falcon was then burned on the back lot (p. 124).
- The VistaVision camera, "the dinosaur", is the same Technirama unit Kubrick used on *Spartacus* (p. 124).
- **The Ewok village set:** about 25 feet off the floor; 39 Ewoks; salt tablets for the actors. Marquand: "This set was a damnable thing, ridiculous, really, absurd". Fisher: "they would just fall over, fainting from the heat". Ford, with a bad back: "‘No, I’m hanging here, I’ve got to look uncomfortable.’" (p. 125).
- Oola: Marquand wanted Femi Taylor's skin with "a purplish haze". Lucas: "‘No, no. She’s green!’" (p. 128). Lucas: "The difficulty is that Jabba can’t move" (p. 128). Watts calls the film "The Monster Movie" (p. 128).
- **Lucas on set every day.** Marquand: "‘you wrote this goddamn thing, so let’s get it right.’" Roffman: Marquand was "nowhere near the league of Irvin Kershner". Kazanjian: "there was never a time when George was not on the set." Lucas: "Empire had gone 10 weeks over schedule ... I couldn’t afford to go through that again." (pp. 129–130). Roger Christian joins as uncredited second-unit director (p. 129).
- **McDiarmid recast.** Alan Webb withdrew (snowbound, with flu) and died June 22, 1982. McDiarmid: "George paid me a compliment about my nose and I thought, I think I’ve got it." Lucas: "Star value is only an insurance policy for those who don’t trust themselves making films." (pp. 130–131).
- C-3PO's campfire tale. Davis: "The look of surprise and then resignation on Harrison’s face wasn’t acted." Hamill: Davis was "like sunshine in shoes ... I thought the script was a letdown after the first two" (p. 131).
- **The sibling scene (blue pages).** Hamill: "It was like being in the Nixon administration"; his son was declared "a security risk". Lucas: "that really gets hard to swallow". Fisher: "‘Carrie, your dad isn’t Eddie Fisher. Hitler is.’" Hamill on Fett as "my mother": "George said, ‘So are fairy tales.’" Fisher: "I felt like a Barbie doll." (pp. 132–133).
- **Jabba:** 18 ft long, operated by Philpott, Barclay and Edmonds, all from Henson's *Dark Crystal* team; the set was "a fire hazard". Marquand: "Jabba is a pig and I found myself yelling at this ghastly slug, ‘Come on, blink your eyes!’" The frog: "a dangling leg ... it flailed around in distress. I heard, ‘Cut!!’" (pp. 135–136). Marquand: "It was hell." (p. 136). Reynolds: "It was like Piccadilly Circus." (p. 136).
- **Tippett's creature notes (sidebar, p. 137).** Gargan has "two extra pairs of jiggling breasts". Bib took "eight hours of makeup every single day".
- **Leia and Jabba.** Barclay to Fisher: "‘That’s not my shoulder.’" Fisher: "Strip me—and I’m silent!" / "This was no bikini. It was metal. It didn’t go where you went ... Dignity was out of the question." Marquand: "Carrie loved Jabba". Philpott stuck Jabba's tongue "right in Carrie’s ear" (pp. 138, 141). Fisher decided to become a writer during these scenes (p. 141).
- The song Sy Snootles sang on set was "a little bit disco"; John Williams's son composed and sang the guide track (p. 139 caption). Ziff: "Salacious stealing show." (p. 140). Christian on the Ewok dancing: "George loved them. Nobody else did." (p. 138).
- Security: there were two Vaders (Prowse and Bob Anderson); neither got full scripts. The *Yuma Daily Sun* leak compares the name to "Purple Haze" (*More American Graffiti*'s code name) (p. 139). Lucas: "We used to call it my director’s cough" (p. 140).
- Kazanjian: "the iron fist in the velvet glove". Tippett: "‘She’s the Bank of Boston!’" (p. 141). Fisher's struggles: Barton says "a lot of drink and drugs and partying"; Fisher: "I’m, you know, serviceable." (pp. 142–143).
- **Prowse and Shaw.** Kazanjian on Marquand's red lights on the Star Destroyer bridge: "George ... said, ‘Get it out of there. Stop it.’" (p. 143). Lucas on the unmasked face: "‘when that helmet comes off and you see the face of Laurence Olivier or John Gielgud, people are not going to be able to take it seriously.’" Shaw: "Luke couldn’t have a black daddy" (pp. 143–144). Marquand: "the worst place of all and the hottest set was the interior of Jabba’s sail barge" (p. 144).
- Lucas on color design: "The bad guys exist mostly in a black and white world; the good guys live in an organic world of browns and greens." (p. 146). Daniels "physically gave up" on the barge set. Fisher: "‘I really, really want to kill him myself.’" (p. 146).
- Christian's A-wing-into-the-bridge shot nearly wrecked the camera boxes. Watts: "‘You earned your entire salary today.’" (p. 146). Jeremy Bulloch finished as Fett after 12 days (p. 146). Kenner's General Madine figure already had a beard, so Dermot Crowley had to wear one (p. 147). Daniels' claustrophobia under Salacious: "‘Get me out, get me out’" (p. 147).
- Pigeon droppings on the briefing-room set. Daniels: "on one of the consoles right in the middle of it, there’s a great pile of pigeon shit." (p. 147).
- **Lucas and Marquand.** Christian: Marquand "didn’t have the right tone that George needed, which was the Star Wars tone." Marquand: "It’s never fun ... it’s not a bundle of laughs." Hume: the producer "was very much ‘part director.’" Lucas: "my participation is maybe one-tenth of what it would be if I were directing." Reynolds: "He was intimidated by George". Fisher: Marquand said "stand like an English sentry", then Lucas said "more swashbuckling." Watts: "George came and he never left". Fisher: Marquand "would be sort of grand and very British". Bloom contrasts the "overwhelmed" Marquand with Kershner the "old pro". Lucas: "I imposed my will a little bit". Barton on the multi-camera "obsession of George’s". Marquand: directing it was "rather like being a famous conductor ... You didn’t compose the music. Some other guy did and he’s in the next room." (pp. 150–153). p. 153 has a photo of the three trilogy directors together.

## Ch. 7: An Ending of Elstree (Feb–Apr 1982), from p. 156
- Crowley shot a bluescreen sequence directed by Lucas, with "pages of extra dialogue" and Madine saying "‘May the Force be with you.’", all cut (p. 156).
- **McDiarmid's Emperor voice.** Marquand gave him the Clive Revill tape from *Empire*. McDiarmid: "‘Clive Revill is great, but he didn’t really know the character he was playing.’" / "it looks like Somerset Maugham, who’s turned into a toad. So the voice became a combination of English upper class and trying to sound like a toad." (p. 156). EV-9D9: Marquand played its voice on set. Lucas: "‘He’ll sit.’" (p. 157).
- The Death Star hangar cost about $750,000, with 240 ft of set; its shiny black floor "was the killer" (pp. 158). Prowse: "I begin to feel more evil with every plate they put on." Hamill on Prowse's accent: "it’s very hard when you have these classically trained Imperial officers in the same scene with David." Prowse: "I was virtually ostracized from the picture"; he learned from a *Daily Mail* reporter about Shaw being on the call sheets. Kazanjian: "I would not have lied to David." Prowse: "I just hope that when they come to unmask me, I’m not too old." (pp. 158–160).
- **The Emperor's makeup:** four hours a day. Lucas joking: "‘go to bed as late as possible, so you can get up looking your worst.’" Tippett: "ancient, not old". McDiarmid on his first day in the hangar: "‘I thought it was going to be a quiet party just for the three of us.’" (pp. 160–162).
- **Shaw's one day** (Feb 24, 1982). Asked by McDiarmid why he was there: "‘I don’t know, dear boy ... I think it’s something to do with science fiction.’" Tippett: the makeup was meant "to justify why the man had to seek refuge in a mechanical body." Shaw: "The scene was so beautifully written that we never altered the dialogue at all." / "Mark nearly took my ears with it" (pp. 162–163).
- **Ghost Vader.** Kazanjian suggested it, then doubted it ("he’s like Hitler"). Lucas: "‘Isn’t that what your religion is all about?’" Lucas: "When Vader joins the Force he is able to retain his original identity because of Obi-Wan and the Jedi Order, because they’ve learned how to do it." Shaw: "George just said, ‘Look happy, smile.’" (p. 169).
- **The throne room.** The decisive revision adds Vader's line: "Sister? Your feelings have now betrayed her, too . Twins! Obi-wan was wise to hide her, but now his failure is complete. If you will not turn to the dark side, then perhaps she will." (p. 164). Lucas's long analysis: "the Emperor is the master and Vader is the apprentice ... Vader doesn’t quite understand that he’s on the chopping block ... He does it out of the super energy of wanting to protect his son, out of compassion. Vader finally has compassion for his son and realizes that his life has been a sham." (pp. 164–166). McDiarmid made "friend" "a hate word" (p. 166). The throne chair was too slow, so he moved it with his feet (p. 166).
- **The duel.** Lucas: "‘It’s just a couple of guys banging sticks against each other’ ... Luke goes through three changes". Hamill: "George retreats from raw emotion." Peter Diamond: "Mark has now got a Flynn-quality about him." Hamill: "This thing with dismemberment I’ve been dead set against from the very beginning ... I’m a Jedi. I could have just grazed him." Lucas on the red elevator light: "a move toward hell" (p. 170).
- ILM in 1982: tension between Edlund and Muren; Rose Duignan's three-unit system. Duignan's 1976 interview: Lucas asked "‘Do you take shorthand?’ ... ‘Can you start Monday?’" Farrar on the Ewok dailies: "‘My God, what are we involved with?’" (pp. 167–169). McQuarrie designed the *E.T.* mothership "(based on a dream he’d had long before)" (p. 167).
- The Crescent City recce. Muren's 8-fps Bolex walk-through "would give him an idea..." (p. 171). The Yoda puppets were finished on the morning of the shoot and came out "a little too green" (Kazanjian). Marquand: "because Yoda is a very old man and on his death bed ... his eyes can go slightly off center." (p. 171).
