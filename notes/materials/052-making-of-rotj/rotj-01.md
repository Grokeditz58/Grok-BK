# The Making of Return of the Jedi (J. W. Rinzler, Del Rey 2013): notes part 1, front matter and ch. 1 (pp. 2–15)

- Source: the owner's MEGA cbz (Aa10UbKb), 382 page scans: cover, front matter, pp. 2–368. File "Page NNN" matches the printed page number, which is used for citations ("MRotJ p. N"). Every page image was viewed, and the text was read from an OCR transcript of each scan made for this reading. OCR slips are silently fixed only where the scan is clear.
- Official Lucasfilm/Del Rey "definitive story". It is the third of Rinzler's making-of books. Nothing fan-made.

## Front matter
- Dedication: "To Genevieve, Sarah, and Judith / In memory of Ralph McQuarrie, a fantastic visionary artist and gentleman" and "hats off to Stuart Freeborn". Copyright © 2013 Lucasfilm. "Additional research by Brandon Alinger".
- Cover: Luke "caught between his father, Sith Lord Darth Vader (David Prowse), and the Emperor (Ian McDiarmid)"; Elstree, March 1982, photo by Albert Clarke. Frontispiece: Fisher and Hamill on the barge in Buttercup Valley, April 1982.
- Acknowledgments: Kazanjian opened his archives. Muren and Pablo Hidalgo read the manuscript. Craig Barron reviewed the matte sections. Rinzler: "I’ve promised him not to write any more of these books".
- Contents: 12 chapters (ch. 1 "The Revenge of a Slavering Hulk", Feb 1979–Feb 1981, through ch. 12 "Joy of the Jedi", May 1983–Sept 1987), script-draft summary sidebars (undated outlines; rough draft Feb 24, 1981; revised rough June 12, 1981; story conference July 13–17, 1981; second draft Sept 21, 1981; third draft Dec 1, 1981), an epilogue, complete cast and crew credits, a bibliography and an index.
- **Brad Bird foreword** (pp. vi–vii): the Portland opening night of *Star Wars*, where "the opening shot of a movie got an ovation" and Vader was booed on sight. His beefs with *Jedi*: a second Death Star "even more powerful" ("The first one could blow up a planet—how much “more powerful” could it get?") and the "cocky dude who shows up in Jabba’s palace". He closes on Lucas, freed after the Disney sale, as "Luke Skywalker’s other father in the last shot of Return of the Jedi: finally freed of all the machinery . . . / . . . and smiling."
- **Introduction** (pp. viii–ix): "Episode IV was a scrappy battle ... Episode V represented a bid for independence." It relies on John Philip Peecher's 1983 interviews with Marquand and Kazanjian ("I lucked upon them in a random box in the Lucasfilm Archives"), on Maureen Garrett's fan-club tapes, and on over 30 new interviews. **Tense rule:** "says" means a quote from 1982–83; "would say" means a later one (mostly 2002–2012).

## Ch. 1: The Revenge of a Slavering Hulk (pp. 2–15)
- September 1980, Tunisia, on the *Raiders* shoot: Lucas and Kazanjian first discuss SW III. Kazanjian: "If Empire had gone down the drain, we could have stopped" (p. 2).
- Deal terms with Fox: Fox would loan Chapter III $25 million as a pickup fee; Fox takes the first $50 million of gross; Fox's merchandising interest drops from 20% to 10% after *Jedi* (pp. 2–3). Weber's letter, with tempers fraying: "the true cost of the intervening delay must be measured in terms of a disintegrating relationship rather than in dollars and cents" (p. 6).
- **Gary Kurtz's resignation**, Dec 11, 1979: "I hereby tender my resignation as Director of your Company" (p. 3). Kazanjian: "One day Gary Kurtz comes in and says, ‘What are you doing here?’ I had to tell him that he was not on the picture" (p. 4). Kazanjian produces; Robert Watts and Jim Bloom are co-producers.
- ILM made permanent: Tom Smith hired as ILM director of production; Bloom's "‘The six Ps.—Proper-Planning-Prevents-Piss-Poor-Performance.’" (p. 5). Lucas: "what a stupid, nineteenth-century idea film is" (p. 5). Ed Catmull and Alvy Ray Smith form the Computer Development Division (p. 5).
- **Title.** "Revenge of the Jedi" is announced on May 14, 1980 (p. 7). Kazanjian: "George came to me and he said, ‘The title of Episode VI is Return of the Jedi,’ ... ‘I think it’s a weak title.’ He came back one or two days later and said, ‘We're calling it Revenge of the Jedi.’" Hamill's joke title: "‘The Other Shoe to Drop.’" (p. 7).
- Lucas's early scene list, no. 15, has "Han + Leia confront Luke..." with the note "Sister!", "perhaps the moment when Lucas finally decided that Princess Leia was in fact Luke Skywalker’s sister" (p. 7 caption).
- **Ford's return.** Ford had signed for only two films. Lucas: "‘Well, basically, we’ll defrost him.’" (p. 9). Hamill: "George would’ve only needed him for one day to be unfrozen at the end" (p. 8).
- Kershner declines: "I didn’t want to become just simply another Lucas employee" (p. 9). Guinness, privately: "It’s a rotten, dull little bit, but it would have been mean of me to refuse." (p. 9).
- Kazanjian's bio: DGA trainee, *Camelot*, *Finian's Rainbow* with Lucas, *The Wild Bunch*, Hitchcock's *Family Plot*, *More American Graffiti*, *Raiders* (pp. 9–11).
- **Undated outlines** (pp. 11–12). The Imperial capital is "Had Abbadon", from Revelation 9:11's Abaddon. Early notes: "mother image," "girlfriend for Luke," and **"the Emperor is the evil one—he kills Luke’s father. Vader begs Luke to kill him—he does."** Other notes: "Ben or Yoda tells Luke: ‘Your father is more machine than man.’"; "Ewaks" and "Yussem"; a "V-ray gun"; "Battle between Yoda and Emperor?" ... "Vader wavers."; and "Vader kills the Emperor instead". Yoda can't return "into physical form while the Emperor is alive" (p. 12 sidebar).
- **Ewoks and Vietnam.** Lucas: "the main theme of the film was that the Imperial Empire would be overrun by humanity in the form of these cute little teddy bears." Jedi "in the original script, was really only the last 20 pages [sic]" (p. 11).
- **The cut 1976 Jabba scene.** Lucas asked Fox for about $80,000 "and Fox said: ‘We'll give you 40.’" (p. 11).
- Box office: the *Empire* negative cost was $35,829,258 (p. 15). Ford: "at least $11 worth of entertainment in Empire. So if you paid four bucks and didn’t get an ending, you are still seven bucks ahead" (p. 15).
- Art department: Johnston, Rodis-Jamero and McQuarrie. Lucas's briefing: "George never let go of the door handle. He said, ‘There are seven highlights to this story ...’ Then he walked away." (p. 16). The red dot means approval. McQuarrie quits the Ewok designs: "They were starting to look teddy bear—like and I wasn’t for that." (p. 16). Johnston on the bikes: "they need to look like they’re going 200 miles-an-hour." (p. 15).
- Marquand, October 1980: "‘No, it’s impossible. I wouldn’t think of it.’" (p. 16).

## Ch. 1 (cont.), pp. 16–33: writing; the Lucasfilm South shutdown; the rough-draft sidebar
- Lucas writes alone: "It’s very, very painful to write" (Kazanjian, p. 18). Lucas: "trying to tell somebody what the story is, like I did on Empire [with screenwriter Leigh Brackett], just doesn’t work" (p. 18).
- **The guild fights.** The DGA fine over the *Empire* credits went from $250,000 to $25,000. Lucas: "I consider it extortion." He resigns from the WGA. Kershner could not be rehired: "Basically it was the Director’s Guild that kept him from working on the next movie" (p. 18). *Screen International* claimed Spielberg was "very interested" (p. 19).
- Lucasfilm South is closed and Weber is let go (Dec 15, 1980). Greber becomes president: "‘No, I’d like to be the new president.’" (p. 19). Roffman: "Charlie and George were just on different planets" (p. 19).
- Desert scouting by Watts, Bloom and Reynolds ("like the Three Stooges") ends at Yuma (p. 19). **Rough draft finished Feb 24, 1981, 97 pages**, given only to Kazanjian and Bloom (p. 19).
- **Rough draft summary sidebar (pp. 20–33).** Rinzler: the draft "has two Death Stars, which are always better than one" and Vader is "actively trying to recruit and preserve Luke in order to defeat the Emperor".
  - Had Abbadon and its moon Tarasand. The Emperor sits on an island in a "fuming lake of red-hot lava. It is a vision of hell." He Force-chokes Vader: "You are weak, more machine than man." Jerjerrod is told: "Lord Vader is still strong enough to cloud his thoughts."
  - Tatooine: an "Ollony" and the "Del Andues". Luke's gift is a "Durellian spice extractor". Jabba: "I have killed many Jedi Knights in my time . . ." The "sloth pit" plays the Sarlacc's role.
  - **Twin reveal:** "Your twin; strong with the Force is she, but untrained." / "She was taken to Alderaan by your Mother." / "Dead he thinks she is." (p. 24).
  - Sicemon with "giant flying whales". Luke builds a "lazer sword" and manacles Han with the Force. Ewaks: Wicket, Teebo, Chief Chirpa, Logray. Ben: "My corporeal self is old and Vader is far too strong."
  - **Vader kills Jerjerrod:** "No one will be going with you, bureaucrat." / "your importance has been greatly exaggerated." (p. 25). Ben to Vader: "I do not wish to save him. His fate is already decided. It is you I would save." Yoda to the Emperor: "I survived, as did many others." (p. 28).
  - The Emperor: "I have foreseen my destruction at the hands of a Skywalker. But it is not to be you, perhaps there is another". Vader hits him and "knocking them both into the fiery lake of lava". The ending: "He is not a shimmering image, but real flesh and blood"; Yoda: "A reason for celebration this is..." (pp. 32–33).
  - McQuarrie's ether-gravity theory of barge propulsion: "the same as the 19th century concept of ether" (p. 25 caption).

## Ch. 2: Directors Cut (Feb–June 1981), pp. 34–43
- Fox offers a joint venture with $10 million instead of $25 million. Greber: "‘all negotiations are off. There is no offer. Goodbye. We start from zero.’ George loved that, but it created a huge problem" (pp. 34–35).
- Lucas: "By this time these movies had gotten to be like television shows" (p. 35). The candidate directors included Alan Parker, Peter Weir, Bruce Beresford, Peter Yates, William Fraker, **David Lynch**, Richard Donner, Terry Gilliam, John Carpenter, David Cronenberg, Joe Dante, Tony Scott and others (pp. 35–36).
- Marquand's *Eye of the Needle*. Lucas: "a very tight, very clean, strong movie" (p. 36).
- Creature planning: about 34 Ewoks, with "midgets or dwarfs in costumes". The ILM Monster Shop is under Phil Tippett: "I was a stop-motion animator—what did I know about running a creature shop?" Yussems are dropped because a 20-foot Yussem and a 3-foot Ewok can't share a frame (p. 36).
- **Jabba:** Lucas: "Sydney Greenstreet ... Marlon Brando in the Godfather". Tippett: "All that I needed to do was put a fez on Jabba, and he would’ve been Sydney Greenstreet." Rodis-Jamero: "‘He’s using a model and I only have paper. A model always wins.’" (p. 37). The Ralston "critter" on a leash later became the Klingon "dog" in *Star Trek III* (p. 37 caption).
- ILM's "ugly adolescence" (Bill George, p. 38). The Sprocket Systems C building is a prototype mixing facility: "if Revenge is not a successful movie. I'll still have this" (p. 39).
- Redwoods: the Miller-Rellim Redwood Company near Crescent City, found by Miki Herman with local contact Lenny Fike (p. 39). Lucas: "I felt that I’d run out of environments by the time I got to Jedi ... giant Sequoias." The 1981 re-release was numbered Episode IV (p. 39 caption).
- **Lynch.** Lynch: "he took me upstairs and showed me these things called [Ewoks] and now this headache is getting stronger" ... a Ferrari ride, "a restaurant that only served salad", "a migraine headache" (p. 41). Kazanjian: "George called me up Saturday and said, ‘I want to go with David,’ ... within three days he declined ... Richard doesn’t even know this story." (p. 41). Lucas: "I may have gone a bridge too far on that one." (p. 42).
- Marquand gets the job and celebrates: "fell into the pool, then went out and bought another bottle of champagne" (p. 42).
- Rancor: Lucas's brief was "‘I want this big monster in a pit’". Tippett pleads against a man in a suit: "But George said, ‘Try it.’" (p. 43). Empire's foreign rentals were $46,482,000 against the first film's $73,425,000 (p. 42).
