# The Making of Star Wars: Return of the Jedi, part 3: Ch. 7 (end) and Ch. 8 (pp. 171–205)

Source: J. W. Rinzler, *The Making of Star Wars: Return of the Jedi: The Definitive Story* (Del Rey, 2013). Hand-read from the scanned book; page numbers are the printed folios. Quotes are verbatim from the book (they are interview quotes the book collects, credited to the speaker as the book does).

## Ch. 7 (cont.): Yoda, Ben, the throne room, the rancor and the Elstree wrap, pp. 171–185
- **Yoda's death scene** was filmed crouched under a desk in seven or eight blocks. Marquand: after "‘Cut!’ ... you’d see Yoda just disappear through his bed, ZOOOP!" Hamill: "Yoda was real for me, absolutely real." Oz: "I know that character inside out." Lucas skipped the scene to shoot X-wing pilots on second unit (p. 175). The Feb 15 revision gives Yoda "Once you start down the dark path, forever will it dominate your destiny . . . there is... another... Sky..." (p. 171).
- **Guinness's last day.** Lucas spent a Saturday at his house reworking the lines, adding "The other he spoke of is your twin sister. She will find it no easier than you to destroy Darth Vader." (p. 175). Guinness had flu and didn't know his lines, so Marquand shot Hamill first. Marquand: "of not doing anything ... of being not there ... it works beautifully for Ben Kenobi." Guinness: "I’m gone in a cough and a spit. Suppose I have a fan somewhere who has paid money to see me?" To a friend: "It was a dreary boring job, but I liked the director and had nothing to do with any other actors—thank God—except Mark Hamill, who is pleasant." (p. 175).
- The Jedi Yoda's upper lip "should more closely match Einstein’s moustache"; his cross-eyes were possibly a side effect of the closing-eyelid mechanism (p. 176 caption).
- **Throne room.** Hamill and McDiarmid both thought of Tommy Cooper's botched magic gestures during the lightning (p. 176). Kazanjian: "‘George, we don’t see Darth Vader making the decision. There are no close-ups of him.’" Lucas then shot Vader turning his head (p. 176). McDiarmid: "from the technical point of view, it was an uneasy one." Hamill: "We gave George at least three alternatives in the way it ends" (p. 177). Prowse on the Emperor throw: Bob Anderson "is nearly 60 and couldn’t lift the Emperor"; Prowse, on crutches, "did it in one take." McDiarmid: "Once, he let me go and I went spinning around his head." (p. 177). McDiarmid on the set: "‘Oh, you mean the Oval Office?’ ... But it was Nixon time, wasn’t it?" (p. 178 caption).
- Hamill: "it was six weeks more than I had" (the time Ford and Fisher got off) (p. 177).
- **The rancor:** Marquand: "The rancor behaved like some spoiled Hollywood brat—he never turned up for work." Wood cutouts were used for eyelines. Lucas: "even the worst, most horrible monster you can imagine was loved by his keeper. And the rancor probably loved his keeper." The giant hand cost "$150,000 [sic]". Hamill: "one of my biggest acting dreams came true: I actually got to be held in a giant rubber hand." (p. 178). The *Hollywood Reporter* leaked that Vader would be killed off (p. 178).
- Nien Nunb in the Falcon cockpit (Mike Quinn): "‘Lando, you gotta install sick bags on that thing!’" Marquand: "Nien Nunb has a brother called Ten Nunb. I haven’t met him, but I know he’s out there somewhere." Billy Dee Williams: "He’s like Picasso, in a way." (pp. 178–180). The script expanded the Death Star run, with Jerjerrod's "discharge gates" (cut) (p. 180).
- Marquand: "Artoo is the only one who really gets on my nerves as an actor ... He is a pig ... He shouldn’t be allowed on any set." (p. 181). Marquand's favorite scene was the landing platform where Luke is delivered to Vader (p. 181).
- The bunker line "‘That is correct, rebel scum!’" was cut down to "‘You rebel scum.’" An extended bunker shootout was cut (pp. 181–182). The rebel hangar was a painted backing costing about $50,000, against the "three-quarter of a million dollars Death Star set" (p. 182). *Raiders* won four Oscars (Reynolds: "I was walking on air") (p. 182).
- **Elstree wrap**, April 1, 1982. Lucas to Marquand: "‘We’ve been shooting for 60 days and we’ve got 20 more ... We’re going to America and start all over again.’" Daniels: a construction worker "was in tears ... because George had never thanked him", and "if he gave it to everybody he would be a puddle on the floor of digested matter ... these films, which are, if you like, gifts. Giant gifts." Tomblin: "It’s a pity he no longer directs pictures." (pp. 182–183).

## Ch. 8: "The 4:12 to Yuma" (April to May 1982), pp. 186–213
- **Jabba's barge in Buttercup Valley:** 130 × 150 × 65 ft on 130 posts, "Possibly the largest location set built in movie history", with a 400 × 400 ft barbed-wire fence against bikers' threats to "torch" it. The crew: "Building what . . . where?!" About $2.5 million for "a two-and-a-half-minute sequence" (pp. 186–187). Bloom's telex quotes the Statue of Liberty poem when Immigration approves the UK crew (p. 186).
- Lucas killed the $50,000 Sarlacc tentacle. Kit West quotes him: "‘Get a bit of cloth and wrap it around the man’s leg and put the camera in reverse and pull it off him.’" (p. 188). Fisher and her stand-in: "‘You know, I’ve had a crush on you since Star Wars.’ / ‘Yes, well, don’t get fresh,’" (p. 188). Hamill: "just like a Bob Hope/Bing Crosby On the Road movie" (p. 189).
- **Steadicam and Garrett Brown.** Muren's Bolex test led to Brown shooting at 3/4 fps, first in ILM's corridors on Apr 12, then in Samuel Taylor Park. Brown: "honor and greed dictated a cautiously optimistic response." (p. 189). Ralston's first effects shot, the Vader-ship flyby "MA", "would become his nemesis" (p. 189).
- **Lucas and Marquand on location.** Their shared command was "‘Give me cuts.’", with 6–7 camera setups per shot (p. 191). Roffman: Lucas's "dismissive attitude was subtle, while distinct." Marquand: "It’s a craft, not really an art." Fisher: "Richard kowtowed to him [Ford]" (p. 191).
- **The Sarlacc injuries.** Fisher: "They jumped into the sand vagina ... ‘Pauline Kael ... is going to have a blast with that.’" Weston's cast was signed "Don’t lose your sense of Yuma!" Tippett: "I cracked on that one. I cried then, it was so terrible." (pp. 191–192). An effects technician, John Chapot, was badly burned by a steam machine (p. 192). Billy Dee Williams was burned by a squib: "I’m screaming to Han, ‘Stop, stop!’ but he’s too busy acting." (p. 192). Chewie's double caught fire (p. 200).
- McQuarrie: Reynolds "re-enlarged the sails ... almost to the size I had initially planned" (p. 192 caption).
- Fans at the fence, rumors and the press. Hamill: "‘What is “dying” in the Star Wars galaxy?’" The *Yuma Sun*: "The predominant atmosphere ... is one of boredom." (p. 198). Fisher pretended to be English to fans hunting for Carrie Fisher; Kenny Baker played "Danny Boy" on harmonica at the Stardust bar (p. 199).
- **The "Blue Harvest" horror film** dreamed up during a sandstorm: "‘Horror Beyond Imagination.’" Lucas: "‘We could make it about a killer who hides in trailers and call it Blue Harvest.’" Lucas on the wind: "‘It’s good luck: I always have a day like this on my pictures.’" Kazanjian: "It’s been blowing for thousands of years, but today—it’s my fault!" Hamill to Ford: "‘You’re supposed to look at me in awe!’" (p. 200). Bloom: the effects crew "were dying to blow it up" (the barge) (p. 200).
- **Crescent City.** Bloom housed the little people in Brookings, fearing "an Under the Rainbow syndrome". Fisher: "every single one of them goes, ‘Hi, Carrie.’" (p. 201). **Lucas, on day 72: "I’m not having fun ... I’m the only one who knows where we’re going and where we’ve been ... I’m only doing this because I started it and now I have to finish it ... The next trilogy will be all someone else’s vision."** (p. 201).
- **Wicket and Paploo.** Kenny Baker got food poisoning from a chili dog, so Warwick Davis played the Leia scene. Because the custom suits couldn't be swapped, Davis's character was renamed Wicket and Baker's became "Paploo" (pp. 203–204). Fisher on Davis: "‘Am I in frame? Is this a two-shot?’" Lucas: "He was 100 percent into the character." Fisher: "I’ve been a testy space-soldier ... And now I’m so nice and feminine, it’s almost confusing." (p. 204).
- Ford: "There is no difference between doing this kind of film and playing King Lear ... Dress up and pretend." (p. 204). Kazanjian sent Reynolds home over the fully built Norman's Log (p. 204). Marquand to Fisher: "‘Darling, you’re fucking up my shot,’ and I burst into tears." (p. 204). Lucasfilm's complaint made Paramount retitle *The Vengeance of Khan* as *The Wrath of Khan* (p. 205).
- The rebel Endor helmets were Paul Huston's design, "like a World War II bomber pilot’s gear" (p. 203). Edwina Pellikka hand-painted the camouflage (p. 203).
- **The Endor battle (scene 115):** about 40 Ewoks and 130 Imperials, five cameras, 325 crew fed. Tomblin: "‘Just the same, only better.’" Marquand: the stormtroopers "were actually out of work lumberjacks". Lucas: "They’re designed to be cute and fuzzy, virtual teddy bears; but they’re also the ones who save the day ... I knew the older kids would have trouble buying into this idea, but we decided to do it anyway." Hamill: "I did tell George I thought the Ewoks were too cute, that they should be more feral and rat-like." (p. 206). Kodak 5293 stock "literally saved our lives" (Hume) (p. 206). Ford asked for a pistol that really fired blanks, which is why brass casings eject in the battle (caption, p. 206).
- **The Ewoks' "Revenge of the Ewoks" prank** on wrangler Ian Bryce: a fake note, "‘Ian, we went to the airport. We’ve had it.’", then T-shirts. Ewoks water-bombed the canteen. Mayhew: "‘I got Bigfoot!’" (pp. 206–209). Daniels overheard the blue-page line "‘He’s my brother.’" (p. 210). Fisher: "I’m a pretty intense broad and after a certain amount of time, I was done. But you can’t be done." (p. 209).
- At ILM, Hamill signed "Follow the Force" and Ford signed "Force Yourself!" (p. 210). Prowse mentioned Ewoks at a convention, and Lucasfilm considered "breach of contract and elimination of participation" (p. 210). Tomblin's unfinished short *Return of the Ewok* (p. 211).
- **Speeder-bike bluescreen.** Marquand recorded "this long spiel" for Lucas to play back; Spielberg was watching. Fisher read Colette on the bike: "‘boring’—I’ve heard it described as ‘unenthusiastic hostility.’" (pp. 211–212). Principal photography officially wrapped May 14, 1982, after which Fox released the last $7M of its $10M loan. **88 shooting days, against 84 for Star Wars and 144 for Empire** (p. 212). Watts and Marquand play the AT-ST officers Chewie pulls out (pp. 212–213).
- Lucas, watching Ewoks unharnessed: "‘The easy part is over.’" with about 500 effects shots and 2,000 elements to come (p. 213). Hamill: "nine schools in twelve years ... Part of me never wanted it to end." Baker: "‘Never again, never again,’" (p. 213).

## Ch. 9: "Harnessing the Elements" (June to November 1982), pp. 216–257
- ILM was burned out. Muren: "I remember not being terribly interested in doing it. I was just so burned out." Smith: "We were the best in the world when George brought us Jedi." The work was split three ways: Edlund (skiff, tunnels, reactor), Ralston (space battle), Muren (rancor, walkers, bikes) (p. 216). Smith's estimate: about $22K per shot and a projected ILM cost of about $9,475,230; Empire's 407–422 shots had cost $7,601,357. The Monster Shop's bill was $1,188,969.63 (pp. 217–218). Johnston: "Jedi has to outdo Empire. It keeps getting harder every time." (p. 218).
- Lucas to Marquand on the first cut: "‘I don’t want to see it. I want to see it when it’s all put together.’" (p. 218). **Burtt**: "I’m working on what I call the all-alien version ... Every character speaks another language" / "We have some real problems with the Ewoks in terms of being convincing" (p. 219).
- **Steadicam bike plates** (Humboldt Redwoods, Jun 14): one frame every 1.3 s while Brown walked about 7 ft between frames, giving "more than 120 mph" on screen. Muren: "It was like laying out a freeway". Lucas: "I have always been interested in speed ... television and my love for cars and fast-pace rock-and-roll" (p. 220). Lucas went to his 20-year Downey High reunion on June 26 (p. 223).
- Johnston: "George is really the designer, with a capital ‘D.’ ... I don’t think there are too many directors or producers ... who have their egos under such control." (p. 223). McQuarrie returned on contract for the last portfolio paintings (p. 225). Barbara Gallucci was the model shop's first woman: "every time I pick up anything, the room stops." (p. 225).
- **The director's cut screening** (Aug 1982) had no effects shots. Roffman: Marquand said "‘We’ve worked really hard and this is as good as this movie’s going to be.’ They watched it and basically said, ‘Thank you, Richard.’ George took it over at that point". Lucas: "The first cut on a film is never wonderful ... It is always horrible". What that cut contained: Jabba speaking English "a little like Truman Capote", Lando hugging goodbye, Vader Force-strangling Jerjerrod, Luke and Leia's sibling scene ending in "a goodbye kiss", WWII dogfight footage, and the Emperor ordering Endor destroyed ("You will destroy it!") (pp. 226–227). Dunham: Lucas told him "‘Always save a person whose opinion you trust for the very end.’" (Marcia) (p. 227). Barton: "much more like Raiders in space." (p. 227).
- **The rancor "potato".** Tippett: "a cross between a bear and a potato." The man-in-suit was dropped on Aug 31 for a Bunraku-style rod puppet (p. 229). ILM dailies: "There is one empty seat, second row center. It is reserved for George Lucas." (Peecher, p. 229). Edlund: "we all hang our swords and departmental rivalries outside" (p. 230).
- Chicon IV (Sept 1982): Ganis: "With this film we intend to deliver all that you expect" (p. 230). Brown on the second Steadicam trip: "it looked like we were running down a hyperactive puppet grandmother." (pp. 230–232).
- **Secrecy at ILM.** Duignan walked in on the sibling scene. Lucas: "‘I will kill you. I will kill you if you tell.’" Smith: a page leaked Yoda's "deathbed" (pp. 232–233).
- **The divorce.** Ralston: "We were wondering what happened to George". **Lucas: "I was destroyed, because I had no idea; it just came out of the blue ... It was an act of great energy just to get up in the morning and go to work. I was so, so depressed."** Rinzler: "the triumph of finishing his space fantasy trilogy would take place in the shadow of personal anguish." (p. 233).
- Lucas on Han's carbonite: "a continuation of the motif of death and rebirth ... in relationship to Darth Vader, who comes out of his evil hibernation and is reborn as Anakin Skywalker." Tippett: "the most horribly difficult thing that I was involved with." Muren: "It makes absolutely no sense ... But it’s been copied many, many times since then." (p. 234).
- Storyboards (pp. 235–237) include Johnston's "Mad Maxx" death-dive pilot, a nod to George Miller's *Mad Max* (caption, p. 235). Lucas is "a five-to-niner" (p. 235).
- **Editing.** Duignan's tape-recorder plan for Lucas's KEM notes was "completely hopeless, a fiasco." (Blau). Dunham's two-page notes for one shot ("Move this TIE fighter two fields north") were posted at ILM "for years". Lucas to Marcia, defending Barton: "‘You can make it different, but you’re not going to make it better.’" Dunham: "George has this gift that I’ve never seen in anybody". Lucas on R2's sound: "‘You’ve got to sync those sounds right on those movements.’" (p. 238).
- Bloom left to produce *The Grid* (p. 238). The model shop's October tally was 103 models plus 52 "blowup" ones. The Death Star tunnels used "five or six miles of cardboard tubes from carpet rolls" (pp. 238–239). OSHA rated the matte department the most dangerous at ILM because of its acrylic paint (p. 239).
- **The matte "Brat" department.** Its lineage ran Percy Day → Peter Ellenshaw → Alan Maley. Chris Evans tracked down Lucasfilm through a limousine company's Rolodex. Pangrazio "demanded to be paid in gold". **David Fincher, age 18**, told his interviewer "the only one I can think of that I really respect is Akira Kurosawa." (pp. 239–240). Evans hid a tiny Transamerica pyramid in the "waistband" matte painting (p. 250). Marquand: "‘Why did we build this big huge set if you guys are painting it out in every shot?’" (p. 250). Craig Barron wore the Vader suit for the ramp pickup and "slid down the ramp in a pile of Darth Vader" (p. 252).
- **Lucasfilm Games is born.** Greber recalls Atari asking: "‘Will you do games for us?’" The book: "Lucasfilm Games (later, LucasArts) was born." The Computer Division's Death Star briefing graphics were six shots for $50,000 (Reeves and Duff) (p. 247).
- Ganis: "We went for broke ... We just blasted away." Ralston on the trailers: "frankly, they’re kind of lame" (p. 247). Williams spotted the music on Nov 2, with new themes for the Emperor and Yoda's death. Lucas: "a grease that each movie slides along on, as well as a glue that holds it together". At Burtt's suggestion the bike chase has no music (pp. 247–249).
- Three-year-old French Kodak stock left over from *Raiders* caused the blue-layer problems: "Regards, from a four-foot hole in the ground, Howard." (p. 249). A hand-animated shadow was drawn over the Emperor's close-ups (caption, p. 249). The effects-shot count rose from 473 to 635 (p. 249).
- **The sandstorm cut** (Nov 18): Kazanjian: "you’ve got to bring the audience down, so let’s pull it out." The glove pickup carries the hand information instead (p. 254). Title research ("Revenge of the Hero" vs. "Return of the Hero"): "The implications of the term ‘Revenge’ do not fit the good guy/Jedi image" (p. 254).

## Ch. 10: "Butterfly Effects" (November 1982 to February 1983), pp. 258–
- **The Fine Cut** ran 2:07:15. It added scene 4A, Luke building his lightsaber in a cave. Kazanjian: fans asked at every convention "‘How does Luke get his lazer sword back?’" and "Richard didn’t want to shoot the sequence." (p. 258). The Death Star-targets-Endor idea was dropped. Lucas: "by putting the Emperor on board, that we already had a time lock" (p. 258).
- **The funeral pyre** was added because "the audience was going to say, ‘Naw, Darth Vader didn’t die.’" (Kazanjian). Dunham asked "‘what happened to Vader?’" and Lucas "got pretty heated ... The next thing I knew, the funeral pyre scene was in the film." (pp. 258–259). For security, the storyboard for the ghost shot substitutes "Bob Brown" for "Anakin Skywalker", after Lucas added Anakin to the shot at Elstree (caption, p. 260).
- **"Black Friday"** (Nov 22): up to 100 effects shots were changed. Ralston: "a horrible, horrible drastic cut ... about 250 ships we’d shot" were thrown away. Dunham to Kazanjian: "‘Well, maybe we can use it in the next movie.’" Lucas: "it’s like a baby: When they cry, you can actually tell why ... It’s a partnership" (pp. 259–260). Lucas: "You can’t be sick; you can’t have normal emotions." (pp. 260–261). Lucas: "You look at the Jabba the Hutt scene and say, ‘Oh, that’s what he wanted the cantina to be.’ ... ‘That’s what the end battle was supposed to be in the first one.’" (p. 261). About 2,200 storyboards in total (p. 260).
- Marcia walked into Duignan's candlelit pizza party: "‘I have too much work to do to bother with this’" (p. 261). Lucas: "If she was breaking into tears, it probably had nothing to do with the movie" (p. 261). Lucas's handwritten 4-A page has C-3PO saying "Why couldn't that bounty hunter have taken Captain Solo to a more pleasant environment?" (caption, p. 261).
- "CBB": Bruce Nicholson redid a shot Lucas had already finaled. Lucas: "‘I’m not using it,’ and walks out ... what a good message—because we never wasted another minute." (p. 263). Ralston: "We frequently became a fix-it shop, to repair the stuff they blew over in England" (p. 262).
- *Year of the Jedi* newsletter: a 1.4M first printing of Kahn's novelization (Kahn was recommended by Spielberg), 100,000+ fan-club members, and 11-year-old Rusty Miller's *Jedi Master’s Quiz Book* (p. 264). **James Earl Jones** turned down credit on SW and ESB because of the *Exorcist*/Mercedes McCambridge controversy ("‘No, it was David Prowse’s performance.’"): "The only reason I took a credit on Jedi was that it was Darth Vader's swan song" (p. 264).
- **The picture was locked Dec 4** at "2 hours, 13 minutes, and 13 frames" (Dunham's running 13-frame joke) (p. 264). Dunham: reel four had "an edit every eleventh frame". Lucas, "disappointed with who Boba Fett had become": "‘Just throw him in the pit.’" Lucas: "I had this nagging feeling that we could’ve done better." (p. 264).
- Hamill opened as Mozart in *Amadeus* on Dec 7. Lucas told him: "‘Do what you want—life is too short.’" Hamill: "You find yourself giving an impassioned speech to a big lobster in a flight suit." (p. 267). About 85% of the dialogue was looped. Vader's "So you have a twin sister . . ." was added in ADR (pp. 267–270).
- **Ewokese** came from an 80-year-old Kalmyk speaker, "Grandma Vodka", plus Tibetan and Nepalese words. Daniels: "‘Y manu machoo Vader con yum num’ ... I wanted to say, ‘Goo boo Vader,’ but George wouldn’t let me do that. He thought it sounded rude." (pp. 270–271). Bauersfeld voiced both Bib Fortuna and Ackbar ("I do not speak Huttese"). Fosselius (*Hardware Wars*) voiced the rancor keepers. Larry Ward voiced Jabba, with "my wife’s macaroni and cheese being fondled in a bowl" for the slurp. Barron is the AT-ST driver's scream. Mark Dodson's laugh became Salacious Crumb. Howie Hammerman did the Sarlacc burp (p. 272).
- The pyre was shot at Skywalker Ranch on Dec 20 in torrential rain (Deschanel, Marquand) (p. 273).
- **Ben Burtt's cameo.** Lucas: "‘Ben, you go do it.’" Burtt plays the Imperial knocked over the bunker railing and dubbed his own fall: "It’s actually me imitating the Wilhelm". A genuine Wilhelm scream is in the Sarlacc scene (p. 273).
- **Sound sources:** Jabba's door combines Fort Cronkite bulkheads, the *Raiders* boulder rumble and an acacia being felled. A Howard Johnson's air conditioner gives the spacecraft rumble. **The Emperor's lightning** uses Ken Strickfaden's original 1931 *Frankenstein* lab props (p. 274). The "Optical Dogs" took their name from a leftover *Poltergeist* dog element that kept turning up in Jedi dailies as a joke (p. 275). Repola: "George personally approves every single element ... every ship, every planet, every lazerburst." (p. 275).
- Lucas added Han's line "Thanks for coming back for me, kid. I owe you one," on Dec 20 (p. 276). The Computer Division's force-field graphic: Catmull said "nothing particularly outstanding" (p. 276). Exploding the barge on the ILM roof rained pieces on Bill George's lunch. A pyro TIE knocked out photographer Roberto McGrath, who needed stitches. A model "tree" flew out of a Star Destroyer fireball (pp. 276–277).
- **The return of "Return"** (around Dec 17). Marquand: "Jedi don’t seek revenge." Ganis: "‘But, George, Revenge of the Jedi is such a better title.’ And he said, ‘Jedi don’t take revenge.’" Kenner destroyed about $250,000 of packaging (p. 277). Evans's Ewok-celebration matte was done "backward", with plates rear-projected into scraped-out areas of the painting (p. 280).
- Lucas threatened to insert a black slug for any effects shot not delivered by April 1 (p. 280). Sound editors cut effects for C-stands, thinking they were ships (p. 281). *E.T.* passed Star Wars ($194M in rentals), and Lucas ran a Variety ad: "May the Force always be with you." signed "Your Pal" (pp. 281–282).
- **Williams at Abbey Road** (from Jan 19): "It felt like one piece, one continual experience." / "Performance" (pp. 281–282). Stolen ADR reels (p. 282). McDiarmid kept his own voice. Lucas at the scoring: "‘God, you know, it reminds me of a silent movie.’ And he said, ‘Well, in many ways it is.’" (McDiarmid). Burtt: the duel was scored, "a terrific cue for a choir" (p. 282). Annie Arbogast's Huttese temp vocal stayed in the film. Toto was considered for the source music (p. 282). "The Lyrics" sidebar prints the Huttese "Fancy Man" ("My body heat is risin’") and the Ewokese "Celebration" ("YUB-NUB EEE CHOP YUB-NUB / (Freedom . . . We got Freedom)") (p. 283).
- The second Death Star model: "‘It’ll be about the same amount as the Dino Ferrari you bought.’" (Peterson). The model was lit from the construction side and the film flopped (p. 286). **The rancor rod puppet** was shot at 72–96 fps with Muren operating "as though he were shooting some sort of unleashed animal". Tippett worked with a smashed hand. "‘Lets move it like the Cyclops,’" (Harryhausen). Lucas laughed so hard at the guard's arm that "we ended up making the rancor slurp the arm like a piece of spaghetti." The roar came from Max, a neighbor's dachshund (pp. 287–291).
- The reactor chamber model was about 5 ft × 22 ft and used about 500 GE light sticks; the plasma effect was a laser in nitrogen and Freon (pp. 291–294).
- The crawl edit (Feb 16) later changed "freedom fighters" to "rebels" and "democracy" to "freedom" (p. 295). Smith: Lucas told them "‘If you can keep going, so can I.’" **Lucas: "Jedi almost killed everybody".** Starkey: "‘I’ve realized that for nine years, every single waking day of my life has been about Star Wars.’" Lucas: "This one was grim for me, just as bad as directing ... since April 17, 1973" (p. 295). Johnston: "We were the galley slaves." Edlund: "We all hated the Ewoks". Ralston: "To produce the dreams, you have to go through the nightmares." (p. 295).

## Ch. 11: "Post-Traumatic Film" (February to May 1983), pp. 296–
- **Lucas on pace: "Jedi is almost incomprehensible in certain areas. It’s designed more for kids ... It’s a form of minimalism."** Farrar tested whether cuts under 14 frames would register (p. 296). The mix began in the middle, at reel 5 (the Emperor's arrival), "really loud" (p. 296). Terry Eckton slipped Tarzan's yell into Chewie's vine swing: "‘Oh, it’s great. Leave it,’" (p. 297). Burtt on the bike chase: "orchestrating the sound effects like you might orchestrate music." (p. 297). The "whoop-whoops" are S-3 Vikings recorded at NAS El Centro (caption, p. 298).
- Ralston hid a human skull in the "MA" shuttle shots, and gum and a tennis shoe in a fleet shot (pp. 291, 298, 300). Lucas wanted a walker in a non-VistaVision handheld plate: "‘I know. I want a walker right there.’" (p. 298). Lucas: "The way I describe Steven is as a director: Steven shoots the movie and then you just cut it together. I shoot around the movie, and then I have to figure out a way of cutting it into a movie." Starkey: "Editing is really writing and George didn’t stop writing when he was editing." (pp. 298–300). **SB-19, the Falcon among the TIE swarm**, is "the single most complex shot in the history of ILM", about 70 elements (Vargo) (p. 300). The scaled-down "killer shot" SB-92 (caption, p. 302).
- The AT-ST heads were electroplated nickel at $500 each, and "I started using paper cups and trash cans to make my models." (Huston). Little men with lead weights sat inside, never seen. Johnston: "George likes to see stuff like the two-legged AT-STs, cos he’s really a kid himself" (pp. 304–305).
- **THX / TAP:** THX was named for Tom Holman ("TH ... X for crossover") and *THX 1138*. The Theater Alignment Program started Mar 1, 1983 with three mailers, the last including a Lucas letter: "Ultimately, it is your responsibility to see that the film’s presentation is technically correct" (pp. 306–307). Kessler: "Back in the 1930s, studios controlled all facets of a film and we are determined to get back to that" (p. 307). ILM's stage work ended Feb 28 (p. 310).
- The PG rating cost $8,000, given Mar 17. *Cinefantastique* leaked the plot. Ganis: "It was Leak City". Lucas: "if it were an Agatha Christie, it would be in the toilet right now ... It used to be that you just didn’t give away the end of a movie before it opened. Now it’s become the thing." (pp. 310–311).
- **Vader's skull** was Burtt's idea: "‘Why don’t we see the outline of a skull on his face? ...’ ... ‘It’s taken care of. We'll see it.’" Burtt used the raspier breathing recordings for the dying Vader (p. 311). Williams "literally, conduct[ed] me mixing the music" (Thom) (p. 311). Lucas's mix notes include "dialogue louder on ‘I love you,’ from Han; stop Vader's breathing when mask comes off" (p. 311). Burtt: "Jedi had the smoothest sound of the three films" (p. 311).
- **The log-tripped AT-ST.** Lucas "said very quietly, ‘I don’t like that.’ He wanted it to be funny." (Tippett). Jacks were used to make the logs bounce: "17 major changes of position in about three seconds—an animator’s nightmare!" (pp. 312–313). The celebration fireworks were shot at Golden Gate Fields and run upside down. Ralston used inch-wide X-wings from a kit (p. 313). The satellite-dish explosion was shot in-camera in one take; Peterson's buy-back architectural trees "were all toast" (p. 315). Ralston tried a yogurt container in SB-19 but Lucas thought it "odd" (caption, p. 315).
- **The Final Cut** ran 2:16:09 (13 reels), approved Apr 5 (p. 315). **Northpoint sneak preview, Apr 9**, billed as *Twice Upon a Time*. Roffman on the Emperor's fall: "the audience went crazy. They were cheering like mad." Cards: 626 of 720 rated it "excellent", none "poor". The Ewoks were the top new characters, Luke the favorite, C-3PO the least liked. Comments: "Didn’t have enough women . . . Awesome, totally awesome." (p. 316). **The lightsaber-building scene with Vader's telepathy ("‘Luke, join me...’") was cut after the preview.** Burtt: "It was filmed, scored, done, and mixed". Hamill: "such a better entrance" (pp. 316–317).
- **Lucas: the effects "contribute maybe 15 or 20 percent ... it will not work as a popular movie running on 25 percent."** Lucas: "I was never quite happy with the music at the end of the movie." (p. 317). E.T. Oscars for Muren, Burtt and Williams (Apr 11) (p. 317). Lucas then left for Sri Lanka and *Temple of Doom*: "The story in Temple of Doom was indicative of the way I was feeling at that point" (p. 322). Break-even was estimated at $115M (p. 322). Ewoks, Jabba and Boushh toys were held back until after release (p. 322).
- Marvel's comic adaptation went on sale early in Chicago (p. 322). Lucas's hands are on the lightsaber poster: "‘Everybody’s holding the lightsaber wrong.’" (caption, p. 324). **Lucas to *Time*: "What if we have finally got to the end of this shaggy dog story and everybody says, ‘That’s it?’ ... The sacrifice I’ve made for Star Wars may be greater than I wanted ... I was burned out a couple of years ago".** To *Rolling Stone*: "That dumb screenplay I first wrote 10 years ago is at least finished ... If it’s not good enough, then tough luck." (p. 324). The Academy press screening was "at best termed mixed" (p. 324). Each print carried a hidden ID code (p. 324). A dying six-year-old saw Jedi as his last wish and "died less than two days later" (p. 325). Marquand's thank-you letter: "Working with George to make JEDI is an experience I shall treasure." (p. 325).

## Ch. 12: "Joy of the Jedi" (from May 25, 1983), pp. 326–
- **Opening-day lines.** "‘Is Christ coming?’" (El Paso). The House Ways and Means Committee reserved a balcony. A banker told his boss "I’ve got a very contagious flu." A Boeing graphic artist: "It’s not so much an escape, but an affirmation of a positive attitude toward life" (pp. 326–327). At the Egyptian, the audience counted down from 60. Kazanjian: "the minute the scroll started, the audience went dead quiet. It was truly a highlight of my life." (pp. 328–329). Warwick Davis's handwritten letter to "Mr. Lucas" asking for Ewok toys (caption, p. 328). On Long Island the film skipped from reel three to reel five (p. 329). Lucas: "for the audience to be spiritually uplifted and to feel absolutely good about life" (p. 329).
- **Reviews.** Knight (THR): "the stuff of comic books"; Canby: "Even the moviemakers seem tired of Han, Leia, and Luke"; Ebert: "The camera in Jedi slides casually past forms of life that would provide the centerpiece for lesser movies." Arnold (WaPo): Lucas "helped close some of the psychological wounds left by the war in Vietnam". Siskel: "a well-made toy for the eyes ... almost flawless." Sarris: his godson thought it the best of the three, and the Emperor recalled Disney's *Snow White* witch. A priest: "the association of the transcendent with personal power". *The New Yorker*: Luke "might be ordering a veggie burger"; "What a director like Richard Marquand does is take the fantasy out of fantasy." Newsweek: "the most disappointing". Marquand defended the cast: "Mark Hamill gives the performance of his career." Lucas: "Having a lot of people be happy ... or having a handful of critics fawn over you." Variety: "good news, bad news, and no news" (pp. 329–331).
- **Box office:** a record opening day of $6,219,629 on 1,002 screens; about $46M in the first week; $70M in 12 days. The audience was about 60/40 male/female (pp. 331–332). Lucas on the Ewok plush, for his daughter Amanda: "I want my daughter to have one. That’s what I care about." (p. 332).
- **Cast verdicts.** Hamill: "like finding your old high-school yearbook up in the attic". **Ford: "I don’t think it had a very successful ending, with that teddy bear picnic."** **Fisher: "I thought it was the weakest one."** Muren: "the Ewoks, they seemed to come out of E.T., kind of as E.T. came out of Yoda." McDiarmid: "‘I’m Darth Vader's boss,’" (to 4-year-olds). Daniels: "I got to play God for a day". Kasdan: "There’s an enormous amount of poetry in the trilogy and that all comes from George ... The satisfaction of the Vader story is enormous." McQuarrie: "God has lost some of his power, so I think for a whole generation Star Wars is big." Ralston: "So what if Darth Vader, who is Hitler incarnate, is forgiven for everything he has done in two seconds?" Hamill: "three movies that tell you how James Bond got his license to kill" (pp. 333–335).
- Prowse's *Daily Mail* interview ran the day before the UK release. Prowse: "the reporter put the words in my mouth. I didn’t learn the truth until I actually saw the finished film". Ganis: "he was difficult to work with, but ... bad advice from his management." (p. 335). TASS compared Reagan to Vader. Reagan asked for a screening; Kazanjian went, and the White House followed it with *War Games* ("did not go over well") (p. 336). **The final cost was $43,348,502.** Lucas received $1M plus $25,000 for writing. Finance had lost about $3M speculating on the pound (p. 336).
- The "$3 million": finance executives had lost it speculating on the pound before production, then added it to Jedi's cost. "That’s when George blew up. The next thing we knew, they’d lost their jobs." (Kazanjian) (p. 337). **In Kenya, Nien Nunb's lines turned out to be real Kikuyu**: "‘What are you doing over there? All of you, please come here.’" with 1,300 people in Nairobi "rolling in the aisles" (p. 337). There were print thefts, including one at gunpoint, and VHS bootlegs at $150–200 by early May (p. 337).
- **Lucas on retiring** (Aug 1 on the calendar): "Suddenly, my life is going to be mine. It’s not going to be owned by Luke Skywalker and his friends." The divorce was announced to executives in Greber's office. **Lucas: "Nothing is for free ... the price, unfortunately, isn’t in money. It’s in your soul ... I hope I can get back some of the joy I managed to put out there."** (pp. 337–338). Caption: "I enjoy making movies where I can actually create a whole world that I can control ... I don’t know whether you’d call it utopian, but I guess, yeah, probably it is." (p. 338).
- Huston found a Star Wars poster in Rome: "Oh, no..." (p. 338). There was a Special Achievement Oscar for visual effects ("Thanks, George!"). Edlund lobbied to add Tippett as a fourth recipient; they petitioned to include Nicholson but were refused (pp. 340–342). Domestic gross was over $250M, making it #3 all-time until *Home Alone* (p. 341). Hamill: "Star Wars is like reading the Scout Handbook, it’s so sincere" (p. 341).
- **Future trilogies.** Hamill: "The fourth trilogy, which he had and then dropped, was really surreal, more like the last 10 minutes of 2001". Lucas: "My family will be first and the movies second ... This time I’ve kicked it down for good, I think." Bloom: the next films would show Vader and Ben young, and Yoda "about 850 years old". Starkey: Lucas told the early episodes "like it was part of an oral history". Lucas: "Steve Spielberg has expressed an interest" (pp. 342–343). Greber: "‘So when are you gonna start writing the story for the next one?’ ... He said, ‘I’m not.’" (p. 344). Lucas: "The company was created to serve me, but it’s turned out the opposite ... I have a company of 313 people ... The kid who started Apple’s made 10 times the money that I have." (p. 344). Greber: Lucas "went into a black period" (p. 344). The next film was projected at about $80M, so Lucas waited for the technology (p. 344). Lucas: "if you have tiny figures walking around in giant and beautiful sets, it will be boring" (p. 344). Kellogg's "C-3PO’s" cereal ad boards (caption, p. 343).
- After Marvin Davis sold Fox in 1984, Lucasfilm told Fox its right of first refusal had lapsed. Fox was left with a 30-day non-binding "‘first kiss.’" (Roffman) (p. 345).
- **Marquand's death.** After *Until September*, *Jagged Edge* and the *Hearts of Fire* debacle, he had a stroke driving back from Gatwick, "but he’d managed to change gears and manipulate the pedals". He died Sept 4, 1987, aged 49. Lucas attended the funeral. Marquand, after Jedi: "I’ve ... really drunk this life right to the bottom of the jug. Whatever happens afterward would be good, too." (pp. 345–347). Fox chairman Hirschfield's thank-you ad (caption, p. 345).

## Epilogue, pp. 348–351
- Lucas: "Being creative is like a drug ... In a creative addiction, you have all the pain and suffering before." (p. 348). The ILM diaspora: Edlund's Boss Film (Lucas: "‘Let him hire people at double the salaries. He’s going to put himself out of business.’"), Tippett Studio (1984), Pixar sold to Jobs (1986), Sonic Solutions, Fincher's anti-smoking PSA, Johnston directing, Ralston at Sony (pp. 348–351). Kazanjian's gag gift was Ralston's sneaker (p. 348).
- **Guinness's diary, Jan 16, 1997: "Stars Wars people ask me for an interview—I continue to refuse ... They are ghastly bores."** McQuarrie: "I was an artist who did something that was widely seen and enjoyed. As a goal, that’s it for me." Burtt: "I didn’t want to hear another lazer gun for the rest of my life" (p. 349). Fisher: "We went through a war together—three of them." Williams: players tell him "I was eight years old when I first saw Star Wars" (p. 350). Lucas on the Special Edition: "I’d expected Star Wars would go away ... The popularity of the special edition was a revelation." Lucas: "I basically get the joy back with kids." (p. 351). Storyboard artist David Russell, whose father was a Tuskegee Airman, worked on *Red Tails* (p. 351). The book ends with the Oct 30, 2012 Disney sale; Hamill was "gobsmacked" by Episode VII, with Arndt and Abrams attached (p. 351).
- ILM team photo with a numbered who's-who (132 names; Lucas was comped in) (p. 349).
