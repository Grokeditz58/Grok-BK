# Kaminski, *The Secret History of Star Wars* (e-book v3.0, 2008): notes — Chapter X: Returning Home (pp. 309–335)

Fully read. Citations: Kaminski p. N; Kaminski Notes n. Y (Ch. X starts at n.698). Kaminski glosses flagged. Staff/crew/cast labeled. November 1, 1994 writing start; first draft *The Beginning* (Jan 13, 1995) is still an Obi-Wan picture; draft two invents midichlorians, the Prophecy, and a central Qui-Gon; three contradictory age timelines locked; Special Edition mania; cameras roll June 26, 1997.

## November 1, 1994, and first draft *The Beginning* (pp. 309–316)

1983 “goodbye” (n.698): “you fall in love with the characters, and you fall in love with the environment. It’s like a home… I have a home there. And so there’s always going to be a desire on my part to go home again, or to be with my friends again.” Also (n.699): “As attractive as the Star Wars world is, sooner or later you have to leave home and go on to some other place.” Over a decade later he writes in “the same binder in which he had written the original Star Wars.”

**November 1st, 1994**, after taking the children to school, covered by a documentary crew for a twelve-part starwars.com series (n.700), typography locked: “This is the first of November, 1994. Today is my first day of writing the new Star Wars series. Took my kids to school this morning. My oldest daughter was sick all night. I got no sleep whatsoever.” Tour of the office: “beautiful, pristine yellow tablets,” “A nice fresh box of pencils,” slumps into the chair: **“Now all I need is an idea.”** “Infamous Star Wars binder,” red two-inch, tabs: “character,” “plot,” “outline,” “jedi,” “empire.” Undated Anakin notes “which appear[] to be a recent creation,” “Ages 9-20”: “A boy who builds droids and races powerpods / Ernest and hardworking / who dreams of becoming a starpilot and a jedi / good at heart / Blue eyes / Whenever he gets near a machine he gets an intuition and knows what makes it work / Is he a mutation? Who was his father? Why was his mother outcast” (“Ernest” as printed). Lucas (n.701): “It’s great to be able to sit by myself… I don’t feel a lot of pressure, it’s kind of fun.” Bookshelves: mythology and anthropology, plus Burroughs, Brackett, E.E. Smith. (n.702): scenes “come in a mosaic… then there’ll be some missing ones and then I have to sort of sit down and really work hard to get from point A to point B.”

Later (n.703): “The story for the three new films was meant to be the backstory of the other films… sketched out in a rudimentary fashion when I wrote the first trilogy… I knew, for example, that there was a character named Anakin Skywalker who grew up on a small planet, had special skills, and was found by the Jedi. I knew where everybody came from, who they were, and how they got to be what they were. A lot of the story points were there. But the actual scenes and many of the characters were not.” *The Making of Episode I* (n.704): “Quietly, secretly, Lucas devoted five days each week to writing, while Rick McCallum travelled the world in search of locations.”

**First-draft scroll** (n.705): “It is a time of decay in the Republic. The taxation of trade routes to the tiny planet of Utapau is in dispute… the greedy Federation of Galactic Traders… the Supreme Chancellor has secretly dispatched a young Jedi Knight to settle the conflict....” Plot, typography locked where dialog is quoted: Obi-Wan (full knight, in his 30s) uncovers the invasion, meets **Jar Jar** (who “speaks normal English”), saves Queen Amidala, finds Anakin in Mos Espa; **Qui-Gon does not appear until Coruscant.** Obi-Wan’s lines and mannerisms are essentially later Qui-Gon’s. Before the podrace, Anakin on Sebulba: “He doesn’t know he’s bad… He is full of pain, and I think he is afraid of me.” Obi-Wan: fear “is the root of all evil and the path to the darkside.” “The greatest challenge a Jedi must face is to resist the temptation of fear. The fear of letting go of things: possessions, friends, yourself… accepting change.” Padme: “You look like you’re trying to solve the problems of the universe.” Obi-Wan: “Only our own, but maybe they will become the problems of the universe. I don’t know...” Duel with Maul: “levitating objects, moving extremely fast, and vibrating to the point of becoming almost invisible,” leaping “over one another in an incredible display of acrobatics.” On Coruscant, Obi-Wan introduces **“Quigon Jinn”** as “my mentor and good friend.” Council is three Elders only: Mace Windu, Yoda, Ki-Adi-Mundi. Yoda: the boy’s existence “was foreseen,” but he is too old and his future is “blank” and dangerous. Mace: “He is your ward now, Kenobi… there is little chance he will be accepted.” **Valorum is not removed**; the Senate is merely useless; she returns to fight. Anakin, eyes closed in the co-pilot’s chair, brings the ship out of lightspeed “right in front of the planet.” Gungans 10,000 strong; Jar Jar “courageously becomes a war hero”; **Anakin and Padme together pilot a two-man starship and destroy the droid control-ship.** Quigon struck down; Obi-Wan slams a door on droids; they hurl objects. Face to face: “Your style of fighting is old, but I understand it now.” “You learn fast.” “You don't bother to learn.” “I don't have to.” Kenobi cuts him in half: **“Learn not...live not, my master always says.”** Palpatine “reveals he has become Supreme Chancellor.” After the funeral, Yoda calls Anakin forward; he kneels; each Jedi touches his forehead. Victory parade on Utapau.

Completed **January 13th, 1995**, titled *The Beginning* (n.706). Lucas (n.707): “Writing the script was much more enjoyable this time around… I wasn’t constrained by anything… With CG at my disposal, I knew I could do whatever I wanted.”

**Johnny Vaughan**, *The Big Breakfast*, 1999 (n.708), typography locked: Vaughan imagines “this great big leather book, covered in dust, it's the Chronicles of Space and you've written the whole thing already.” Lucas: “No, that's wrong.” “You don't have the complete story, mapped out from the start, all those years ago?” “No.” “Okay, nice one, so you're winging it.” Lucas: “No, I have a little story treatment, a little outline… in the first one I had all the scripts, but I had to rewrite the scripts so they went along because since they became three movies they had to have different structures… the ones I'm working on now was the backstory which I'd written out… this is what the Clone Wars were about; it's just a little outline that goes right through the plot of the movie… it's only about seven or eight pages.” Vaughan: “all those people that think you've schemed the whole thing up, but in fact, George Lucas, he makes it up as he goes along.”

Kaminski: “only ten to twenty percent of Episode I was taken from his original material” (n.709). Episodes II–III “largely pre-existent” (Clone Wars, love, volcano, Vader); Episode I “uncharted,” only pre-meditated points being Obi-Wan discovers Anakin (“which did not even survive in the final film”) and Palpatine becomes Chancellor (“occupying one scene”). Plot borrowed again from *The Hidden Fortress*, in some points “more closely than the final version of Star Wars”: farmers in a town, barter for transportation, buy a slave who joins them. Trade routes, handmaidens, master-padawan protecting royalty, invasion also in the 1974 rough draft. Podrace rooted in the 1981 radio drama’s T-16 Beggar’s Canyon race (Hamill as Luke).

**Dinotopia.** Theed “a nearly identical copy” of James Gurney’s 1992 *Dinotopia: A Land Apart From Time* waterfall city; parade finale “a direct port.” Columbia optioned a film, producers came to ILM after *Jurassic Park*; “Supposedly, Lucas showed the book to his children, and it became his son’s favourite book.” Ian McCaig had storyboarded the unmade feature, then joined Episode I; Peter Walpole later set-decorated the 2002 miniseries. **Gurney** (n.710): “I nearly dropped my popcorn.” Website statement (n.711): a child asked “Why did George Lucas put Dinotopia into his Star Wars movie?” “The strangest thing was that George Lucas himself called me the day after the movie came out. He said that he was concerned that I might be concerned.” Gurney’s only concern was the reverse attribution when *Dinotopia* was filmed. “Even if Mr. Lucas is not comfortable with publicly acknowledging any inspiration from Dinotopia, I am happy to admit my admiration for Star Wars.”

**Doug Chiang** heads the new art department, January 1995. Writing overran; release pushed at least to 1998, principal photography 1997. **Rick McCallum**, 1995 (n.712): no definite timeline until scripts are complete; hoped second-unit summer next year, principal 1997. Still planning to shoot all three back to back (n.713). That would have required I, II, and III written, designed, and cast by 1997. Plan still to pass first drafts to another writer and hire a director. McCallum, summer 1995 (n.714): Darabont “is definitely being considered… as has Jonathan Hales… We’re not, however, going to make any final decisions until George completes the storylines.” Second Star Wars Summit, Skywalker Ranch, April 20th, 1995 (n.715): “There’s a possibility that I might direct one of them… I’m writing them, at least writing the first drafts myself, then I’ll probably bring other writers in to do second and third drafts, but there’s a possibility that I’ll direct the first one.” By end of April he “hadn’t yet started the second draft”; storylines still being reworked “as late as summer of 1995” (n.716)—re-developing Qui-Gon, which “would lead to the creation of the Prophecy as well as the midichlorians.”

## Draft two: Qui-Gon, midichlorians, three age timelines (pp. 317–331)

Also considering Special Edition work on the sequels (n.717): “they don’t have the same restriction that the first film had with scenes having to be taken out because we were rushed.” August 1995–January 1996 THX video of the “rougher” originals as “last chance”; nine full-sized X-wings; Spain a 150-foot Falcon and a twenty-story billboard (n.718); nine million units in the first week (n.719).

Summer 1995 is “the last time the back to back shooting plan was spoken of.” Subsequent drafts written by Lucas himself. **Frank Darabont** (n.720): “he just kept going. He didn't stop one day and say, ‘Okay, bring in the writer.’ He was the writer. He brought himself in. People were wondering why the movie was delayed two years from its original announcement. Well, the reason was that George—and very appropriately so—chose to fashion the script himself… That's his world; he invented it.”

**Qui-Gon promoted to central Jedi; Obi-Wan demoted to padawan, dropped in age, supporting role.** “Plot-wise, a new element.” Advantages: master-padawan relationship (to contrast later Anakin tension); someone to talk to in act one. Kaminski: “There is no clever answer to be given—the character wasn’t a storytelling necessity, since Obi Wan filled the same purpose in draft one, and it could even be said that the character introduced some inconsistencies in the original trilogy.” Possible motives: “always interested in portraying a true master-padawan duo (reaching all the way back to the 1973’s Journal of the Whills)”; Qui-Gon’s death later reveals immortality; “Jinn” is Arabic for “spirit,” “indicating that Lucas might have had this device in mind.” “One of the only story points in Episode I from the original outline”—Obi-Wan discovers Anakin—“was done away with.”

**Midichlorians and the Prophecy** invented “to support his character” and give the Council “more incentive to suddenly begin training a ten year old slave-boy.” Midichlorians allow an “objective ‘rating.’” Original Jedi: anyone could be trained on a master’s opinion; now, as “an organized religion, with dogmatic codes and councils and with initiates indoctrinated from birth, there needed to be a very important and objective reason.” Microscopic lifeforms “loosely based off real-life mitochondria” (n.721). Roots: third-draft Obi-Wan “old and has little of the Force left in him”; Kiber crystal; “Lucas also makes reference to them behind the scenes in 1977” (n.722). Anakin “the highest midichlorian count ever seen—higher than even Yoda… so powerful that it is possible that he was even conceived by the midichlorians themselves—as he is now revealed to be the product of a virgin birth” (n.723). Binder notes still asked “Who was his father?” / mutation. Kaminski: “this also may indicate that Revenge of the Sith’s ‘revelation’ about Anakin’s origin was invented for that film.” Cryptically, in the Episode I rough draft “Shmi’s last name is not Skywalker but Warka” (n.724). Chosen One prophecy so the Council “must now seriously consider his tutelage.” Qui-Gon’s dying wish that Obi-Wan train him solves the end-of-film mentoring problem. “Anakin’s importance was increased yet again.”

Qui-Gon “more or less became” first-draft Obi-Wan. “The ‘arrogant’ and ‘reckless’ Obi Wan described in the original trilogy and present in the first draft was now meek and obedient, with much of his dialog relegated to ‘yes, master.’” Lucas (n.725): “In the beginning, Obi Wan is at odds with Qui-Gon, who rebels against the Jedi rules. But by the end of the film, he has become Qui-Gon by taking on his rebellious personality and his responsibilities.” “The character described in the Dagobah scene in Return of the Jedi no longer existed… no longer took upon training Anakin out of arrogance because he thought he could be ‘as good a teacher as Yoda.’ … no longer would his pride ‘have terrible consequences for the galaxy’… He essentially returned to his original image from Star Wars, a valiant role model that was good to the core.” Reason: later films would make Anakin the reckless padawan; two arrogant leads “would have been less interesting.”

**“The ‘impatient’ and ‘angry’ Anakin of the prequel trilogy largely did not exist prior to the mid-90’s.”** *Empire* “Much anger in him—like his father” must be read with Obi-Wan’s “Was I any different when you taught me?”—frustration and impatience, “not of an angst-ridden delinquent”; “Luke was not an angry young man.” Change came because Anakin was “now being combined with Darth Vader, who was written as an impatient and frustrated young adult, while Father Skywalker had prior been a respectable Jedi master who was well into middle-age… Lucas had to choose Vader’s.” Power-lust likewise from the Vader side of the 1978 merger (third-draft Vader left after brief training, labelled “bad”; Father “good,” Clone Wars veteran). “Vader’s side won.”

Kaminski literary parallels, flag as speculation: Hiroshi Inagaki’s *Samurai* trilogy / *Musashi* (“Japan’s Gone With the Wind”); monk: “you won the fight, but you lost as a samurai… You are too strong.” Structure: initiation / struggle / master—Anakin “would reject the advice.” **Katsushiro** from *Seven Samurai* for the secret courtship (imagery carried into Episode II, n.726); **Kikuchiyo** / Mifune as Jar Jar parallel.

**Three age timelines, typography locked in Kaminski’s charts (pp. 324–330). Flag as Kaminski reconstruction from scripts, *Annotated Screenplays*, and *Guide to the Star Wars Universe*, not a Lucas table.**

1977 film logic (pre-fusion): Luke officially 19 (script said 20; some sources 18); Leia originally 16 in the fourth draft, later raised to match. Drama of Anakin’s death / purge / Empire “could only have happened roughly twenty years prior.” Obi-Wan “in his seventies” when writing *Jedi* (*Annotated Screenplays*, n.727); third draft also “in his 70’s”; *Guide* consistent; Kaminski uses ~75. Father an equal, “grew up together,” close in age, Obi-Wan maybe “a few years older,” “damned fool idealistic crusade” like Biggs and Luke. Clone Wars “not implied to be so recent”—young men enlisting, “akin to… WWI/WWII,” then only “much later” Vader and the Empire. Third draft: Ben a “Bendu Officer,” “commander of the White Legions”; Luke: “I am going to become a startrooper and fight in the wars. My father was a Jedi!” Leia mentions General and student only incidentally; third draft “One of my disciple’s (sic) took the crystal”—plural, “Darth was the last in a long line.” Footnote: 2008 *Clone Wars* series, Anakin trains Ahsoka, “Lucas would follow through.” Another footnote: drafts write “Clone Wars”; Hamill in the film says “Clone War.”

Timeline one (1977), at *Star Wars*: Vader approx. 40; Obi-Wan approx. 75; Father/Anakin approx. 75 (perhaps slightly younger). Backward sketch: (−20) Luke born, Jedi exterminated, Obi-Wan wounds Vader, Vader kills Anakin, Vader leaves; (−21) mentoring; (−40) Vader born; (−55) they become Jedi and fight the war; (−70–75) both born on Tatooine.

Timeline two (1980–*Jedi*): fusion keeps Obi-Wan’s age; Vader “ten years younger than Obi Wan, pegging him at 65” (n.728); unmasked by 77-year-old **Sebastian Shaw**. At *Star Wars* (~three years earlier): Vader approx. 62, Obi-Wan approx. 75—meaning he “fell to the darkside at 42, indicating that Darth Vader began his Jedi training in his forties—and Yoda thought Luke was too old! Likely, Lucas never even considered this aspect.” *Guide to the Star Wars Universe*, first edition, 1994 (n.730): Clone Wars “some thirty-five years prior to the start of Star Wars IV: A New Hope,” “the result of the revised storyline from timeline two,” also allowing Boba Fett, “at the time conceived of as being part of the enemy forces of the Clone Wars” (n.729), to have participated.

Timeline three (1999 / draft two): “the third timeline created.” Anakin must be Obi-Wan’s student, so “Lucas had to go with Vader’s age and not Father Skywalker’s, making Anakin 20-23 in the last two prequel films, instead of 50.” Qui-Gon replacement makes Obi-Wan still a student in Episode I, placed at “the oldest possible age a Jedi padawan realistically could be—25.” “Now, instead of being roughly 75, Obi Wan is a scant 57—not even a legal senior citizen!” Final: Anakin 9 and Obi-Wan 25 in Episode I; at *Star Wars*, officially 32 years later, **Vader is 41, Obi-Wan is 57.** First draft had been “more consistent with the first and second timelines.”

Anakin initially conceived as twelve; slid to nine because “a twelve-year-old leaving his mother… is not nearly as traumatic” (n.731). “Having him pilot a starship was my main worry… I also had Artoo-Detoo in there with him, helping to fly the ship.”

## Directing, casting, Kasdan declines, Special Edition, cameras roll (pp. 331–335)

Second draft so long that release is pushed to 1999; budget from “roughly $60 million” to “roughly $100 million.” Lucas (n.732): “When you’re making a $100 million movie and it’s your own money—pretty much all the money you’ve got—there’s a huge risk. If I didn’t get my money back on Episode I, I wouldn’t have been able to make Episode II.” Announced he would direct, September 25th, 1996 (n.733). (n.734): “we were going to be attempting new things; and, in truth, I didn’t quite know how we were going to do them—nobody did. So I figured I needed to be there at all times.”

Casting director **Robin Gurland**, scouting since 1995; many on three-film contracts, some needing to resemble the original-trilogy cast. **Liam Neeson**, 45, for a man “supposedly in his sixties”; Gurland (n.735): “that strong mythic hero quality.” Neeson prepared with the original trilogy and *Seven Samurai* (n.736). **Natalie Portman**, sixteen, “convincingly portray both a fourteen year old queen in Episode I and a twenty-four year old senator in Episode II all within three years.” **Ian McDiarmid** “just happened to be the right age to play his ‘younger’ self (he was actually only thirty-eight years old when first portraying the character in 1982).” **Samuel L. Jackson** “began a campaign to appear in the films in any capacity” (n.737); granted Mace Windu. **Ewan McGregor**: Gurland compared headshots to young Alec Guinness; uncle **Denis Lawson** (Wedge). McGregor (n.738): “I had to undergo a lot of dialog coaching to get a younger-sounding version of that voice. It was quite tricky.” Anakin: Gurland saw “over three thousand children in person”; **Jake Lloyd**, seven (*Jingle All the Way*, *Unhook the Stars*). Rumours: Kenneth Branagh, Charlton Heston, Lando’s father “Grando” played by Gregory Hines; *Entertainment Weekly* June 13th, 1997: Harry Connick Jr. as a Jedi, Claudia Ramirez as “Lady Kenobi,” Guinness to narrate Episode III.

**Lawrence Kasdan** asked to take a pass, declined. *Baltimore Sun* (n.739): “I saw him a couple of weeks before he left to shoot Phantom Menace… the first remark he made to me was, ‘Hey, do you want to write Phantom Menace?’ I asked, ‘Aren't you starting to shoot it?’ ‘Yeah,’ he said, ‘but it would be great if you took a second pass at it.’” Also (n.740): “He asked me to write that movie and work on this trilogy, and I said 'no' for various reasons. Basically I thought he should do it. Because what happened on Empire and Jedi was that between me and Irvin Kershner… and Richard Marquand… George's relationship to the movies had gotten one step back. I thought he should take responsibility and make exactly the movie he wanted to make, and that's exactly what he did.” Kaminski: “diplomatic way of saying he simply wasn’t interested”; he had written *Jedi* only to repay Lucas and had not written for anyone else since. Footnote: the ask was spontaneous, “much like a college student asking his roommate to proof-read his term paper on the night it is due (unlike Darabont, who was actively sought out before scripting began).”

Special Edition far beyond 1993’s “few enhancements.” Trailer November 1996; *Time* February 10th, “The Return of Star Wars”; *The Magic and Mystery* with Scott, Cameron, Howard and the original principal cast; Pepsi, Vader vs. an elderly usher. *Entertainment Weekly* January 10th: Fox spent “over $20 million”; “one of the biggest gambles of the year.” Opened **January 31st, 1997**, number one, **$35 million, “the biggest January opening ever, even to this day”**; stayed number one four weeks “before being dethroned by Empire Strikes Back.” *Empire* February 21st, *Jedi* March 14th (slipped from March 7th). SE of *Star Wars* over $138 million; combined $460 million, “once again as the most successful film of all time… inching past E.T.” Lucas on taking his son (n.741): “I’ve never really seen it through a four year old’s eyes before and it’s pretty amazing.” Early February (n.742): “This was supposed to be a nice little twentieth anniversary for the fans and it’s turned into the same event [as 1977] all over again… It’s like being in the twilight zone.” Confirmed the prequels “were not going to be minor releases.” **June 26th, 1997, cameras rolled—“and for the first time in over twenty years, Lucas himself was officially behind the camera.”**
