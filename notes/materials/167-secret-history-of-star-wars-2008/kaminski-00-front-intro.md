The book “was born out of discussion, debate and shared research with other Star Wars fans,” mostly TheForce.Net (which Kaminski calls “infamous as of late due to its strong political ties to Lucasfilm”) and Originaltrilogy.com. Named: Noah Henson, Geoffrey McKinney, Chris Olivo, “Toshe_Station”; second-edition feedback from Greg Kirkman, Duane Aubin, David Furr. The Starkiller Jedi Bendu Script Site; Jan Helander and Bjorn and Brendon Wahlberg as “convenient reference tools.” **Key methodological statement, typography locked:** “much of this manuscript is comprised of quotations from individuals gleaned from secondary sources. This, in fact, is one of the purposes of this book, to demonstrate that the fractured history of Star Wars has remained buried in time over the years and need only to be stitched together… many of these are from as early a time period as could be found, as the history has shifted in its telling as time has transpired.” Sources consistently cited: *Starlog* and *Rolling Stone*; Kerry O’Quinn’s July–September 1981 *Starlog* interviews “exceptionally illuminating into Lucas’ early writing efforts.” Recommends Mississippi Press, *George Lucas Interviews*; Pollock, *Skywalking*, “the golden standard for an objective analysis”; Bouzereau, *The Annotated Screenplays*; Rinzler’s making-of of the 1977 film, “invaluable supplemental information for the second edition,” “mandatory reading,” and containing material not included here. Second edition added “approximately sixty pages of new content.” Third edition “significantly re-edited,” with additions and corrections; Kaminski hopes it is “my final statement on the matter of the story of Star Wars’ story (barring any earth-shattering discoveries).”
- Pp. 7–8, “Foreword to the e-book,” dated Michael Kaminski, March, 2007 (the first-edition date; retained in v3.0). Opens on May 25, 2005, watching *Revenge of the Sith*. Today the saga is “unofficially known as The Tragedy of Darth Vader.” Kaminski’s thesis in the foreword, typography locked: he first became aware “sometime in 2003” “that Darth Vader, the iconic figure of evil, and Anakin Skywalker, the flawed Jedi who turns to evil and becomes Darth Vader, were originally conceived as separate people. Not separate constructs, as they now might be said to exist in the saga ‘from a certain point of view’—but entirely different characters, totally independent of one another, each existing in some imagined history within the same narrative time and space.” “a cursory evaluation of Lucas’ own early notes and script material, which have been published and available for many years now, reveals that Darth Vader and the father of Luke Skywalker were characters that existed together, onscreen as separate entities.” He argues Lucas “has even distorted the truth in certain instances.” The book could not be commercially published “due to the gigantic amount of copyright issues”; he keeps the direct quotations because “it would… rob the book of its impact.” Offered free online as “a fan creation.” “nearly two years after I began it,” “just in time to celebrate the thirtieth anniversary.”

## Introduction (pp. 9–16) — KEY thesis and two conflicting Lucas accounts

Scenic opening, April 17, 1973, Mill Valley, a twenty-nine-year-old Lucas writes the title *The Star Wars* on a yellow pad. **n.1** (p. 421) immediately qualifies this as “somewhat inaccurate” and used “more for dramatic effect”: the Medway Avenue house is the one they bought just before *Graffiti*; the house where he actually first wrote *Star Wars* (1968–mid-1973, after Portola) is still unknown to Kaminski. *Journal of the Whills* already existed and was discarded. The May 25, 1973 footer on a leaked treatment is treated as probably a secretary’s addendum; Rinzler, *The Making of Star Wars* p. 11, has Jeff Berg giving UA’s David Chasman a typed fourteen-page treatment by May 7, 1973, “expelling this May 25th date as myth.”

Pp. 10–11, typography locked. Kaminski first restates “the accepted story” (Campbell, serials, story too big, divided into three, six-chapter backstory, Episode IV first). Then: “Lucas even tells it in his own words,” **n.3 = *Star Wars* Definitive Edition Laserdisc interview, 1993:**

“The Star Wars series started out as a movie that ended up being so big that I took each act and cut it into its own movie…The original concept really related to a father and a son, and twins—a son and a daughter. It was that relationship that was the core of the story. And it went through a lot of machinations before I even got to the first draft screenplay. And various characters changed shapes and sizes. And it isn’t really until it evolved into what is close to what Star Wars now is that I began to go back and deal with the stories that evolved to get us to that point…When I first did Star Wars I did it as a big piece. It was like a big script. It was way too big to make into a movie. So I took the first third of it, which is basically the first act, and I turned that into what was the original Star Wars…after Star Wars was successful and I said ‘Well gee, I can finish this entire script, and I can do the other two parts.’ ”

Then, after the prequels, “so too in turn changed Lucas’ account,” **n.4 = BBC interview by Anwar Brett, May 18th, 2005:**

“You have to remember that originally Star Wars was intended to be one movie, Episode IV of a Saturday matinee serial. You never saw what came before or what came after. It was designed to be the tragedy of Darth Vader. It starts with this monster coming through the door, throwing everybody around, then halfway through the movie you realise that the villain of the piece is actually a man and the hero is his son. And so the villain turns into the hero inspired by the son. It was meant to be one movie, but I broke it up because I didn't have the money to do it like that - it would have been five hours long.”

(Note typography: “realise”; hyphen-spaced “like that - it would” in this quotation.)

Kaminski’s questions, typography locked: “What if Darth Vader was never written as the father of anyone? What if initially there was no prequel trilogy? What if the original trilogy itself was invented on the fly all through its making?” His own conclusion (not a source quote): the “real story” is that Lucas “stumbled through a story,” then “gradually added on… arriving at ideas through serendipity, accident and necessity.”

Footnote *, p. 12, **staff/source:** J.W. Rinzler, writing *The Making of Star Wars*, based the book on “thousands of pages of interview transcripts from before 1978.” Rinzler: “We decided that that would be the basis for the book, because everybody’s memories are so unreliable.” (libraryjournal.com, CA6434292.)

P. 13, typography locked. Lucas to Gary Arnold, 1979, **n.5 = Arnold, *Once Upon a Galaxy,* 1980, p. 223:** “The Star Wars saga is essentially about Luke’s background and his destiny.” Kaminski: “You will not find this statement spoken by the man today, where he instead refers to the saga as ‘The Tragedy of Darth Vader.’” He dates “seismic shifts” in the telling from 1978. A “confounding problem”: viewers read the later “Father ‘Anakin’ Vader” storyline back into earlier material “which simply aren’t there when viewing the material on its own.” Main feature of the book: “the notion of Anakin Skywalker and his fall to the darkside and subsequent redemption by his son Luke, were totally absent from the earliest versions of 1977’s Star Wars—even the finished film itself.” By 2005 the saga “had absolutely no relation to the story contained in the initial 1977 film but still used its content and plot.” These are Kaminski’s conclusions.

Pp. 14–16, 1977-as-released reminder (Kaminski’s reconstruction, not a 1977 interview): no “Episode IV,” no “A New Hope”; farm boy, wizard mentor, droids, pirate, gentle giant; “The Force”; Vader survives; medals. Then, typography locked, his 1977-perception claims: “The Emperor was not a wicked sorcerer but a crooked politician, modelled after Richard Nixon. Yoda did not train Ben Kenobi, because we didn’t know Yoda existed (and neither did Lucas, as we will discover). Darth Vader was not Luke’s father and Princess Leia was not Luke’s sister.” “Anakin Skywalker does not exist, so far as the audience is concerned. Darth Vader is the name of a man, a seemingly robotic henchman… who was once a student of Obi Wan’s but betrayed him long ago and murdered Luke’s father.” Publicity and novels: “Dark Lord of the Sith”—“without any further elaboration of what a ‘Sith’ was”; “Sith” could have been a race or an Imperial rank; “he is stated as a Sith Lord, presumably one of many, who also serve the Emperor, and it was not clear that Darth was even human.” Jedi “portrayed as a specialized mystical police force rather than a religious organization; anyone who is eager and willing can sign up and be trained—only those pure in heart and deed will succeed.” “You, the audience member, can use the Force if only you will believe!” “At its core, the film was a metaphor for believing in yourself.” “Yes, it was a very different galaxy.” These last characterizations are Kaminski’s reading of the 1977 film, not attributed quotations.
