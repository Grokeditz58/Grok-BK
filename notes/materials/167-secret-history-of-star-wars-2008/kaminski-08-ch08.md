# Kaminski, *The Secret History of Star Wars* (e-book v3.0, 2008): notes — Chapter VIII: Endings (pp. 270–287)

Fully read. Citations: Kaminski p. N; Kaminski Notes n. Y (Ch. VIII starts at n.589). Kaminski glosses flagged. Staff/crew/cast labeled by speaker. After *Jedi*: Lucas will not make more *Star Wars*; the marriage ends weeks after release; ranch-as-Xanadu collapses; merchandising and TV spinoffs keep the company alive; the prequel outline sits on the shelf while the franchise hibernates.

## Burnout and the decision to stop (pp. 270–272)

**Dale Pollock**, early 1983 (n.589): “The most logical thing for George Lucas to do is direct more Star Wars movies, which is precisely what he won’t do. He only completed the first trilogy, he says, because ‘I had a slight compulsion to finish the story.’ Jedi may be the last film George Lucas really cares about. He has little emotional investment in future Indiana Jones movies, or even in the next Star Wars trilogy, should it ever be made.” Pollock again, on Elstree during *Jedi* (n.590): Lucas “fantasizes selling Star Wars—the concepts, the characters, and the plots for all six films.” Would a lousy inheritor hurt? “Not at all, Lucas says. ‘I’ve always thought I did a bad job. This might make me feel better.’…Later he admits that the fantasy of dumping Star Wars is an escape valve… ‘Emotionally, it would be very hard to do.’”

On seeing his daughter (n.591): “I see [my daughter] a couple hours a night and maybe on Sundays if I’m lucky… It’s been very hard on Marcia, living with someone who constantly is in agony; uptight and worried, off in never-never land.” To *Time*, May 1983 (n.592), typography locked: “The sacrifice I've made for Star Wars may have been greater than I wanted… After Graffiti, Star Wars could have gone in the toilet and it wouldn't have mattered financially… I'm burned out. In fact, I was burned out a couple of years ago, and I've been going on momentum ever since. Star Wars has grabbed my life and taken it over against my will. Now I've got to get my life back again—before it's too late!”

1981 on vacations (n.593): film school 24/7; first vacation 1973 after *THX*, next 1977 Hawaii after *Star Wars*; promised Marcia two vacations a year after *Star Wars*, “That lasted for one year”; “1983 is my goal; I intend to take off for a year, if I can, and just not do anything. And that is why I’m not planning any other projects.” Eve of *Jedi* release (n.594): three-year cycles, “ten to twelve hours a day, six days a week,” shooting “sixteen to eighteen hours a day,” “five hours of sleep a night”; “I’m not having much fun. It’s all work. It’s very anxiety-ridden, very hard, very frustrating and relentless… It has disrupted my family life… Amanda is two years old now, and she’s magic… The last thing in the world I want is to turn around and have her be eighteen and say, ‘Hi, dad, where have you been all my life?’ ”

## Marcia, the divorce, and her credited influence (pp. 272–278)

Pollock, early 1983, before the split: Marcia on wealth (n.595): “I think some of the striving has been taken out of my life… I was a great achiever, a self-made girl who started from nothing and worked hard and got rewarded. In a way, I regret having all those obstacles removed.” Most telling (n.596): **“Getting here was a lot more fun than being here.”** Amanda adopted 1981; “adoption was a necessity due to the fact that George himself was diagnosed with infertility.” Marcia had wanted a family since 1969 (n.597). To **Peter Biskind**, 1997 (n.598): “For me, the bottom line was just that he was all work and no play.”

Divorce “only weeks after the final Star Wars film opened.” Lucas, *Empire of Dreams*, 2004 (n.599): “I finished Return of the Jedi; I figured that was the end of it for me… fate has a way of stepping in. I ended up getting divorced right as the film, Jedi, was finished, and I was left to raise my daughter.” Early June 1983: staff called in; George and Marcia held hands and announced the divorce after fifteen years (n.600).

**Richard Walter** at a party at **Randal Kleiser**’s just before the divorce (n.601): Marcia “blurted it out that it was extremely isolating; it was like Fortress Lucas.” People who worked with him: “He’s brilliant, but it’s so cold. I feel like I’m suffocating.” Marcia: she “‘just couldn’t stand the darkness any longer.’” While George was shooting *Jedi*, she supervised the ranch; the library’s stained-glass dome was designed by artist **Tom Rodriguez**. “Marcia began seeing him.” (n.602)

Marcia to Biskind (n.603), typography locked: “I felt that we had paid our dues… I wanted to stop and smell the flowers. I wanted joy in my life. And George just didn’t. He was very emotionally blocked, incapable of sharing feelings… the empire builder, the dynamo… I felt we were partners, partners in the ranch, partners in our home, and we did these films together. I wasn’t a fifty percent partner, but I felt I had something to bring to the table. I was the more emotional person who came from the heart, and George was the more intellectual and visual… But George would never acknowledge that to me… In his mind, I always stayed the stupid Valley girl. He never felt I had any talent, he never felt I was very smart and he never gave me much credit. When we were finishing Jedi, George told me he thought I was a pretty good editor. In the sixteen years of our being together I think that was the only time he complimented me.”

Kaminski: “secret weapon.” Edited *American Graffiti* (Oscar nomination) and *Star Wars* (Oscar—the only Oscar the Lucases ever earned); also Scorsese’s *Taxi Driver*, *Alice Doesn’t Live Here Anymore*, *New York, New York*. Met on **Verna Fields**’s overflow crew; Fields paired professional editor Marcia with just-graduated Lucas. **John Milius** (n.604): “She was a knock-out… We all wondered how little George got this great looking girl. And smart too, obsessed with films. And she was a better editor than he was.” **John Plummer** similarly puzzled. Marcia on George (n.605): “He’s got a very childish, silly, fun side, but he doesn’t even like me to talk about that because he is so intensely private.” Lucas (n.606): “Marcia and I got along real well. We were both feisty and neither one of us would take any shit from the other… I didn’t like someone who could be run over.” Married February 22nd, 1969; work via **Michael Ritchie** to Scorsese.

Pollock (n.607): “Marcia’s faith never waivered—she was at once George’s most severe critic and most ardent supporter… ‘I’m real hard,’ Marcia says, ‘but I only tell him what he already knows.’ … Marcia’s suggestions were among the few Lucas took seriously.” Pollock / Spielberg (n.608): “Marcia is indispensable to Lucas because she compensates for his deficiencies. Where George is not unduly concerned with character and lacks faith in an audience’s patience, Marcia figures out how a movie can be made warmer, how the characters can be given depth and resonance.” Mother **Dorothy** (n.609): breakfast in bed after late nights. Marcia (n.610): “I have an innate ability to take good material and make it better, and to take fair material and make it good… I think I’m even an editor in real life.” *Raiders* ending (n.611): Marion missing from Washington delivery; Marcia “levelled the boom… no emotional resolution… because the girl disappears.” **Duwayne Dunham**: “Everyone was feeling really good until she said that.” Spielberg reshot Marion on the government-building steps “in downtown San Francisco.”

**Mark Hamill** (n.612), typography locked: “You can see a huge difference in the films that he does now and the films that he did when he was married. I know for a fact that Marcia Lucas was responsible for convincing him to keep that little ‘kiss for luck’ before Carrie [Fisher] and I swing across the chasm in the first film: ‘Oh, I don't like it, people laugh in the previews,’ and she said, ‘George, they're laughing because it's so sweet and unexpected’— and her influence was such that if she wanted to keep it, it was in.” Also the mouse droid / Chewbacca roar: “George wanted to cut that and Marcia insisted that he keep it. She was really the warmth and the heart of those films.” *Jedi* her last editor credit. Footnote, **Denise Worrell**: Lucas says she is “great with emotions and characters, the dying and crying scenes. She cut Yoda’s and Vader’s deaths in Jedi. But she also cuts the space battles. George listens to her very carefully.”

Friends unsurprised: **Ronda Gomez** (n.613): “He just didn’t want to have fun… Marcia wanted to go to Europe and see things. George wanted to stay in the hotel room and have his TV dinners.” After *Star Wars* they tried to conceive, could not because of his infertility, then delayed adoption because “he had to choose between Star Wars and family, and he chose Star Wars” (n.614). 1983, just before the divorce (n.615): “I was desperate to have a family. Yet I knew when I had a family I couldn’t devote ninety-eight percent of my time to the company. Since my daughter, the focus has changed.” Also 1983 (n.616): “I love Star Wars… I just don’t want to sacrifice my entire life for it.” Marcia on a brief “normal” window (n.617): everyone leaves at six; dinner at the table; they bathe Amanda together; “George sometimes feeds her a bottle in the TV room.” She suggested a counsellor; he refused (Biskind: likely from his small-town father, n.618); suggested a trial separation; he “begged her to wait until the last Star Wars film was done” (n.619). Marcia (n.620): “I know he wanted me to stay, but it was just too little too late.” **Walter Murch** (n.621): “I think what Marcia saw was that his success was winding him tighter and tighter into a workaholic control-driven person, and she thought that this was destructive, which it probably is in the long run.” She “could care less about Skywalker Ranch,” was happy as a union editor; *THX* had been edited in their attic. Lucas to Pollock on a *Jedi* shoot day (n.622): “Marcia complains that I either live in the past or in the future, but never in the present… I think when I finish this film I’ll be able to live in the present—maybe.”

## *Jedi* reception, settlement, ranch collapse, merchandising years (pp. 278–284)

*Jedi* broke opening records but met “harsh criticism”; “for the first time, one of Lucas’ films was regarded as a disappointment.” Siskel: lacked “the humanity and richly drawn characters that brighten Star Wars”; *Newsweek*: “downright repetitive!”; *New York Times*: “a certain evident boredom.” Ewoks as “obvious merchandizing ploy.” **Roger Ebert**: “fun, magnificent fun… a complete entertainment.” Domestic gross over $260 million.

Lucasfilm president **Bob Greber** to Pollock, 1983 (n.623): “People sometimes forget that Marcia Lucas owns half of this company and is a very important part of it.” Settlement: “as much as $50 million according to Peter Biskind’s reports” (n.624); footnote: “More conservative estimates place it closer to $35 million. (Baxter, p. 335).” Custody split (n.625) but “it was decided that George would care for her”; Marcia’s visits “quickly tapered off.” Marcia and Rodriguez bought a house in Belvedere; she became pregnant with her first natural daughter, **Amy** (n.626). Falling-out with Coppola earlier in the decade (Zoetrope back to L.A.; Lucas refused a loan, money “tied up with Empire”). Coppola (n.627): “When he got divorced, George came to me and wanted to be my friend. He apologized.” New Wave cohort collapsing in parallel: Coppola bankrupt, Scorsese disasters, Spielberg marriage heading toward “an infamous $100 million settlement,” Bogdanovich, Schrader, Friedkin.

Lucas to **Lesley Stahl**, *60 Minutes*, sixteen years later (n.628): “The divorce kind of destroyed me. It did take me a couple of years to sort of unwind myself and come out of it.” He didn’t see it coming. Stahl: “there was another man.” Lucas: “Ten years younger than I was. It was one of those classic divorce situations.” **Spielberg**, during the *Temple of Doom* period (n.629): “[The divorce] pulverized him. George and Marcia, for me, were the reason you got married… And when it didn’t work… I lost my faith in marriage for a long time.” Marcia took her half in cash. Ranch-as-communal-Xanadu “essentially being forgotten and deserted”; facilities usable only by the blockbusters it was “built to dethrone”; the stained-glass library “went virtually unused.” “In the end, Skywalker Ranch turned into the $20 million Lucasfilm office, and from it ruled George Lucas, alone and without purpose.”

ILM as asset (*Poltergeist*, *Star Trek II*, *E.T.*). Merchandising rights, taken in exchange for a pay cut, “the main source of funding for his empire.” LucasArts for mid-80s games. TV: *The Ewok Adventure* (1984), “a ‘gift’ for his daughter”; first use of the name **Mace**, from early *Star Wars* notes, later the prequels; 1986 *Ewoks: The Battle for Endor*, first film retitled *Ewoks: The Caravan of Courage*; 1985 Saturday-morning *Ewoks* and *Droids*. 1986 Disney *Captain EO* (Michael Jackson, directed by Coppola); 1987 *Star Tours*.

Pollock 1983 on experimental films (n.630): “Lucas wants to rediscover the virtues of being an amateur… films that are abstract and emotional, without plot or characters… ‘If they don’t work, I’m certainly not going to show them to anybody. If they do work, they’ll probably get a very limited release.’” Kaminski: same “security blanket” pattern as “that’s the way it always was”—vowed after *Graffiti*/1974, after the trilogy/1978, in 1983, after the prequels/2005, and in 2008 after the then-planned TV series (~2010). “As of 2008 he has not yet.” Coppola (n.631): “Star Wars robbed America of one of its most challenging filmmakers”—Kaminski: “it was a choice Lucas made on his own.” **Marcia** (n.632): “By the time George could afford to have a film facility he no longer wanted to direct… ‘For someone who wants to be an experimental filmmaker, why are you spending this fortune on a facility to make Hollywood movies? We edited THX in our attic, we edited American Graffiti over Francis’ garage, I just don’t get it, George.’ The Lucasfilm empire… seemed to me to be this inverted triangle sitting on a pea, which was the Star Wars trilogy. But he wasn’t going to make any more Star Wars, and the pea was going to dry up…” **Milius** (n.633): “One of the great losses is that Marcia never became a filmmaker and continued as an editor… the other great loss is that George stopped making movies… Because he was a really dynamic filmmaker.”

Relationship with **Linda Ronstadt** starting 1984, “apparently went as serious as an engagement ring” (n.634), then fizzled. Adopted **Katie**, then **Jett**; never remarried. To Stahl (n.635): “she was somebody who just didn’t want to get married. And eventually I wanted to get married… I guess I’m wearier and wiser and older. And I have a big life. And I have three kids.” Editdroid → AVID; ILM first fully integrated CGI in *Young Sherlock Holmes*; 1986 **sold Pixar to Steve Jobs for $10 million**. Marcia (n.636) on being frozen out: Easter parties at Francis and Ellie Coppola’s; then stopped being invited; Ellie later: “when Francis and George were working on Tucker, George asked him not to invite you… It’s not enough that I’m erased from his life, he wants to blackball me too… It’s like I never existed.”

*Temple of Doom* “written by him in the bitter shadow of his divorce trial, lending the film a dark and violent tone that led to the creation of the PG-13 rating.” Other credits: exec-produced Murch’s *Return to Oz* (“which Lucas bailed out of serious debt at the hands of producer Gary Kurtz”), Huyck’s *Howard the Duck* (“Lucas’ creative involvement is often exaggerated”), Coppola’s *Tucker*, Henson’s *Labyrinth*, Spielberg’s *The Land Before Time*; conceived/produced *Willow* (1988, Ron Howard); produced *Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade* (1989 hit). Lucas later (n.637): “I had the 20-year gap because I finished (1983's) ‘Return of the Jedi’ and I had a daughter… I was raising my daughter and felt that was the most important thing… Then I adopted other children and spent 15 years raising them… Because of the divorce, (Lucasfilm) was in difficult financial straits… I basically ended up getting a job where I could go to work at 10 or 11 (a.m.) and come home at 4 or 5 (p.m.).”

## Prequel outline on the shelf; franchise hibernation (pp. 284–287)

May 1983 (n.638): “I am afraid that if I did another Star Wars movie now, I’d be straying from my path… To me that would be like being seduced by the dark side, but more than anything else, I think I’d be unhappy.” Sequel trilogy “had all but evaporated”: *Jedi* had made Leia the “Other,” eliminating the planned apprentice / New Republic thread; he “publicly speculated” a later post-*Jedi* trilogy with the aged original cast “but this did not seem very likely… although Lucas would not admit this for over a decade.”

Prequels “remained in Lucas’ mind as a possible storyline… he genuinely liked the story and had sketched out a fairly elaborate plot,” but no desire to make them then. *Time*, 1983 (n.639): they “will be altogether different in look and tone… more melodramatic, showing the political intrigue and machiavellian plotting that led to the downfall of the once noble Republic. They will have only enough outward action to keep the plot moving. Obi-Wan Kenobi… and Darth Vader will be seen as younger men, while Luke Skywalker will make a brief appearance as a baby in Episode III.” Outline “vague, giving only the major plot revelations, and was under a dozen pages long, according to Lucas.” Kaminski’s summary of that 1977-onward outline (flag as Kaminski’s reconstruction of what Lucas then described, not a locked 1977 document): Obi-Wan recruits and trains prodigy Anakin; Palpatine, “a devious politician and secret Dark Lord,” becomes Emperor and lures Skywalker; trap wipes out the Jedi; volcano duel, Obi-Wan burns him; Emperor salvages him as bionic Vader; wife escapes with secret children; Luke to Tatooine with “Obi Wan’s brother,” Leia to Bail Organa; Vader hunts remaining Jedi; Clone Wars as the period when Kenobi and Anakin “become galactic heroes”; C-3PO and R2-D2 “the only common elements throughout the entire saga.”

*Rolling Stone*, 1987 (n.640): “I’ll do more [Star Wars films] eventually; I just don’t know when… I can make more Star Wars and make zillions of dollars, but I don’t need to do that, and I really don’t have the interest right now. There is a story there I would like to tell. It’s just that it isn’t beating in my head hard enough to say, ‘I have to get this out of here.’” Also (n.641): “Star Wars is sitting on the shelf—and I’m not under any pressure to do anything with it.” 1989 Disney-MGM opening (n.642): “It will be unbelievably expensive… And that's one of the things holding it up.” After the divorce he could not self-finance a blockbuster until the 1990s and “wasn’t interested in placing the franchise in the hands of a studio.” ILM CGI leaps (*The Abyss*, *T2*) approaching what the prequels would need.

Renaissance only beginning: no new toys since mid-80s; only a “very limited and seldom-remembered 1985 triple-bill”; first video cassette to make a million dollars in rentals; *Spaceballs* (1987) already felt “out of date”; toys in garage sales versus Transformers and My Little Pony; West End Games RPG for a small fanbase. Winter 1987: *Bantha Tracks* and the Official Star Wars Fan Club closed, “with a written promise from George Lucas in one of the final issues that the club would be reactivated once more Star Wars films were made.” Original film into the National Film Registry, 1989. Close, typography locked: “Star Wars was thought to be dead, but in reality it was only in hibernation.”
