# Kaminski, *The Secret History of Star Wars* (e-book v3.0, 2008): notes — Chapter XIII: The Circle is Complete (pp. 377–415)

Fully read. Citations: Kaminski p. N; Kaminski Notes n. Y (Ch. XIII starts at n.834). Kaminski glosses flagged, especially reconstructions of unfilmed structures and the “three published storylines” thesis. Staff labeled. The chapter’s core: Episode III is restructured around Anakin; Dooku’s death moves to the opening and invents Grievous; a 55-page rough draft still has Palpatine claiming to have arranged Anakin’s conception; post-production pick-ups totally rewrite the turn from power-lust/Jedi-coup to a Padme-saving Faust pact; 2004 DVDs then retrofit the original trilogy into “the Tragedy of Darth Vader.”

## Art department first, no script; Mustafar; August 2002 rethink (pp. 377–386)

McCallum (n.834): “we knew way back in 1990 that Episode I and II were going to be very tough… [Episode III] was the film that everybody who was older wanted Episode I to be… I think we finally delivered some peace and safety to everybody.” Lucas, *Empire*, 1999 (n.835): “The third film is very, very, very dark. It’s not a happy movie by any stretch of the imagination. It’s a tragedy… Ultimately the final story is between Yoda, Obi-Wan, Anakin and the Queen. It’s really their story. Those four characters.” **James Earl Jones**, 2001 (n.836): asked Lucas in Idaho, “Do I work again?” “Well, at some point in the third episode, Darth will become bionic and he should sound like you then… there might be no more than five minutes at the end.” Told him this “as far back as the 1990’s” (n.837).

First art meeting April 25th, 2002, before *Clones* even opened. **J.W. Rinzler** (n.838): “embryonic and epic”: ~three years later; Clone Wars wrapping, “seven battles raging on seven new planets”; Kenobi, Windu, Yoda on different planets; Boba Fett “as a fourteen- or fifteen-year old”; Alderaan; maybe Kashyyyk; beat-up vehicles, individualized clone armor “similar to the way American troops did in Vietnam.” “For most of preproduction, the concept art department will be working blind—without a script, no treatment, no outline.” McCallum: “We never truly know what George is doing… no different than Episode II.” June (n.839): “The first ten minutes are going to be a huge spectacle… You’re not going to know what is going on, other than it’s the Clone Wars.”

Volcanic world first “Mufasta,” later Mustafar. Recurring image: Vader’s lava castle cut from *Empire*; Emperor’s lava throne cut from *Jedi*. **Joe Johnston** (n.840): “When we were doing Star Wars, I remember I had done a series of sketches of Vader’s home, and there was a sea of lava his house looked out on… George said we would save this for somewhere down the line.” Footnote: Johnston is “either speaking in generic terms or is confusing the first two films.” Lucas overlooking art (n.841): “These are old, old, old. Mufasta’s been around a long time… I’ve always had this set piece: the end between Obi-Wan and Anakin… It’s all volcanic land, with lava shooting up, so it’s almost monochromatic in its red and blackness. I’ve had that image with me for a long time.” Vader’s assembly as *Frankenstein* (1931). Lucas, *Starlog*, July 2002 (n.842): “The scene where Anakin does actually become Vader is pretty good… it’s in the vocabulary of a time – of the 1930’s and 1940’s.”

Design process (n.843–844): Lucas describes a scene; artists design; **Alexander Jager**: “it seemed like George was going from what we were drawing, rather than telling us what to do.” August 2nd last meeting before vacation (n.845): “This will be my last time off before three years straight on Episode III.” August 23rd, to **Trisha Biggar** (n.846): “we can talk about the costumes, but it’s hard to discuss the script. I know the story, but...” By November, planets and major sets decided (n.847); first act drastically changed (n.848), still no writing. November 1st, 2002 (n.849): “You said you’ve been writing,” McCallum protests. “I’ve been thinking about it.” Days later: a droid General for the Separatists, “one of the main villains” (n.850)—**Grievous**.

Lucas on the August 2002 outline (n.851), typography locked: “Back in August, I started writing this thing… But the script starts to have its own life. Characters start to tell you what to do—and you end up with problems. By the third film, you have a lot of characters left over from before, and they’re all running around yipping and yelling and saying ‘what about me?’… I got far enough with the outline to realise that the bridge between Episode III and Episode IV still had about fifty feet apart. So I had to disassemble Episode III and rethink it, to make it line up with Episode IV… I have to have a through-line. And I have to stick with it.”

Kaminski reconstruction, flag as such: childhood Episode I left “a three-film story to be mostly carried into two films.” What went by the wayside? Boba Fett (once confirmed, n.852); Nute, Bail, Padme/Rebellion more prominent; Sifo-Dyas? Footnote: 2002 *Clones* DVD commentary, Lucas said the archive-erasure / Kamino mystery “will be answered in Episode III, which obviously did not come to pass.” Revision “primarily a re-structure” (Lucas and Rinzler, n.853). Kaminski’s belief: originally Anakin kills Dooku later as the turn, mirroring *Jedi* (Luke urged to kill Palpatine’s apprentice); because the arc felt abrupt, Dooku’s execution moved to the beginning, inventing Grievous to inherit Dooku’s mid-film role; **Samuel L. Jackson**’s “repeated request for a grandiose demise” (n.854) made Mace the sacrificial victim. Lucas to Jackson that summer (n.855): “You’re pretty much the only important character that I can actually kill off, so it has to be a pretty spectacular death.” “While the plot would hence be re-configured, the essential nature of Anakin’s turn remained as it was originally conceived, and would furthermore remain as such in the scripts and the original photography, until Lucas totally re-wrote it in post-production.”

First recorded beginning-death of Dooku comes after the October opening revision, itself after the August rethink. Original April opening: seven-planet montage. Replaced by Palpatine kidnapped, battle over Coruscant, Grievous introduced, Dooku killed. “Grievous had never been referenced by Lucas prior to this story change”; first record November 8th, “roughly a week after the new opening sequence was first introduced” (n.856). Rough-draft footnote: Anakin kills Dooku out of personal hatred after Palpatine says Dooku arranged the Tusken kidnapping. Final structure: Dooku at the start is “the first act which begins his downward spiral”; “it is as if we see what would happen had Luke made the other choice.” Original turn: dark side “almost like a drug”; Tusken massacre the first taste; Anakin “truly believed the Jedi to be traitors.” Palpatine relationship in *Clones* “only minimal”—“The only actual scene between them… was one added in pick-ups” (footnote). Kidnapping invented so Anakin can kill Dooku solo with Chancellor Palpatine present, too early to reveal Sidious; Obi-Wan conveniently knocked out. Grievous then lets Obi-Wan leave the planet so Palpatine can become Anakin’s manipulator. Sifo-Dyas and archive erasure left unanswered “almost as if Lucas simply forgot,” despite the 2002 promise (n.858). Boba confirmed for III (n.859) but cut; “Revenge of Boba Fett” unseen. Lucas (n.860): “I had a lot of extraneous stories going on that I could have tied up,” but focused on Anakin.

Lucas (n.861): “The issue is that I’ve painted myself into a corner… I thought it was going to be the easiest one, but there are so many things to balance that I soon found it locked me in.” Eighty percent of the story still in III. Long “jazz riff” quote (n.862), typography locked: I and II each “twenty percent”; “the third film was actually more like eighty percent… I constantly had to cut it down… when you really got down to it, it was really Darth Vader's story… Padme starting the rebel alliance. A lot of these other things with Obi-Wan… Yoda and the Jedi counsel, all these other things had to go by the wayside… This one has the entire story in it really, except you don't get the background of that story, which is what Episode I and II is.” Rinzler (n.863): Lucas can sum up the revised story in one word: “Anakin.” “The prequels were now a character study rather than a background piece.” Footnote, mid-90s (*Annotated Screenplays*, p. 168): “usually the characters are archetypes… I’m not dealing with psychological problems. My films are storytelling movies, not character movies.” Lucas in 2005: not until after Episode I was written and shot did he “consciously [become] aware of the new Anakin-biography form” (n.864). November 1997, with **Terry Brooks** (n.865): “George felt there should be more focus on Anakin Skywalker than what he was able to do in the movie” (n.866). Path to the six-part “Tragedy of Darth Vader.”

## Characters recast; Padme’s death; rough and later drafts (pp. 387–398)

Obi-Wan’s 1978 pathos (tragic teacher, pride with “terrible consequences”) “done away with” in Episode I draft two; in III he is “the trilogy’s best example of a traditional hero,” “returning him to the immaculate do-gooder of 1977,” even with “I have failed you.” Early drafts: he volunteers to kill Anakin; final film: cannot kill his “brother.” Initial prequel plan: Obi-Wan waits for Luke to redeem Anakin—III “would not hint at this at all.” Contradicted by *Empire*/*Jedi* (“You must face Darth Vader again”; “I can’t kill my own father”; “Then the Empire has already won”). 1997 comment (n.867), typography locked: “In Jedi the film is really about the redemption of this fallen angel. Ben is the fitting good angel, and Vader is the bad angel who started off good. All these years Ben has been waiting for Luke to come of age so that he can become a Jedi and redeem his father. That’s what Ben has been doing, but you don’t know this in the first film.”

Padme “essentially does nothing… stand around pregnant and barefoot in the kitchen” because “Mother Skywalker had no place in Lucas’ backstory… she was never meant to be a part of it.” Rebellion-founding material cut. “Lucas never talked about the wife of Anakin Skywalker prior to the prequels, and by his own admission never developed her” (n.868). OT plan: she took Leia to Alderaan, died when Leia was a young child; “She was kind, but sad.” Rinzler, October 18th, 2002 (n.869): “For the first time, it is rumoured that Padme might die in this film. She may be seen last on Alderaan.” Polis Massa not created until November 8th. Official Databank, early 2001 (n.870): “Kenobi secretly transported Leia and her mother to… Alderaan”; altered April 2004 (n.871) to remove the mother. Bail’s December 2001 entry (n.872) similarly had Padmé hiding with Organa. Kaminski: onscreen death in III “certainly seems to be a new development”; if heartbreak, it was “a slow, gradual one… not the instantaneous one of Episode III.”

Rough draft: Anakin’s vision is Padme “engulfed in flames, perhaps a premonition of his own fate,” not death in childbirth. October 25th, 2002 (n.873): “a thirty second wrap up”—Yoda to Dagobah, Obi-Wan with Luke to Tatooine, Bail with Leia to Alderaan, “implying indeed that Padme dies.” Same day, new opening described (n.874): “Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom aboard a ship,” ~twenty minutes, Anakin–Dooku duel. December 6th, still no script; McCallum moans “oh, god…” (n.875). By January, Boba eliminated (n.876); Tarkin mentioned, unused Cushing footage contemplated; “a fairly major piece of news that a child version of Han Solo is to be seen” on Kashyyyk (n.877–878). Outline in acts, then a script “for [McCallum’s] eyes only” (n.879–880): “I interpret it for them… everything will change.” **January 31st, 2003**: 55-page rough draft typed, titled *Revenge of the Sith* (n.881).

**Rough-draft locks (Rinzler).** Nightmare: “Padme is consumed by flames” (n.882). After the Council, Yoda (hologram from Kashyyyk) hears **Qui-Gon’s spirit** (voice only): the Force is out of balance. **A ten-year-old Han Solo** tips Yoda to Grievous’s whereabouts. Mace confronts Palpatine alone; Anakin cuts off Mace’s hand; Mace “engulfed by the bolts… falling to the ground dead (and not thrown out the window).” “The tremendous effort needed to generate the sustained lightning has transformed PALPATINE’s face into that of DARTH SIDIOUS” (n.883). Order 66 footnote: 666 / possibly Executive Order 9066. Seduction (n.884), typography locked: Anakin first refuses “until the Chancellor makes a startling confession: ‘I have waited all these years for you to fulfill your destiny […] I arranged for your conception. I used the power of the force to will the midichlorians to start the cell divisions that created you.’ ‘I don’t believe you.’ ‘Ahhh, but you know it’s true… You could almost think of me as your father.’ ‘That’s impossible!’” Promises to save Padme; Anakin gives in. Hologram of youngling slaughter; Sidious: “You are a jedi no more, Anakin Skywalker… Today you begin your new life as Darth Vader, Lord of the Sith and bringer of peace and justice to the galaxy.” Obi-Wan requests to go after Anakin; Yoda does not confront the Emperor, flees with Bail to Polis Massa. Padme’s entourage (Typho, handmaidens) gunned down by clones, Anakin calls them off; Obi-Wan sneaks aboard; Force-choke, thrown against a wall. Duel: Obi-Wan cuts off legs only; Anakin “bursts into flames”; “There is no dialog” (n.885). Ends with Vader on a Star Destroyer bridge (as *Clones* once ended with Palpatine and the cruisers, later switched to the wedding).

Kaminski: rough draft “throws as many different incentives as he could at Anakin”; most later eliminated, “notably the admission that Palpatine is his father… dealt with much more ambiguously in the Darth Plagueis tale added in the next draft.” Palpatine’s withering (Episode II makeup, office half-red/half-black, **Ian McCaig** n.886, **Gavin Bocquet** n.887) “done away with” for III: he appears “younger and stronger than in Episode II.” **McDiarmid** (n.888): “He seemed to have turned into action-man.” Instant reveal when he exerts lightning; later pick-ups make it look like Mace’s deflection scars him, used to frame the Jedi and, in a more drawn-out reshoot, to win Anakin. McDiarmid (n.889): “He told me I should think of my eyes as Palpatine’s contact lenses… my face was actually Sidious’ mask, and then when I put on the [Sidious monster makeup] mask I became him.” Padme’s death: Rinzler’s rough-draft summary does not specify heartbreak; wall-throw “perhaps implying… injuries”; final film cut Anakin flinging her to the floor “likely to emphasis her death-by-heartbreak.” Immolation skimmed, no regret, Obi-Wan even volunteers.

March 2003 “state-of-the-script” (n.890): “I still have elements, like Artoo-Detoo and Threepio, which I haven’t really fit in everywhere… I’m looking at the shooting very much as another draft.” Challenge? “Inertia. [laughter] Procrastination… I actually write five pages a day. I force myself.” **April 10th, 2003**, first draft finished. **Ian McCaig** (n.891): at Lucas’s house, “His pencil was down and he was just sitting there with his head bowed. Then, looking calm and drained, he came down and said ‘I just wrote the end.’” First draft 56 pages longer; **Han Solo cut**; Palpatine discloses Grievous and tells **The Tragedy of Darth Plagueis “The Wise”**; Grievous now four-armed (Clone Wars “Terminator,” later revised to “slimier, moustache-twirling, serial-style villain”). Footnote: Grievous maybe a transformed 1974 General Darth Vader. Palpatine (n.892): “I offer you knowledge, Anakin. Don’t join their rebellion…I am your pathway to power.” Yoda now instructs Obi-Wan to kill Anakin (Obi-Wan objects) and confronts Sidious. Mustafar (n.893): droids attack, Anakin destroys them; “I have brought peace to the galaxy.” Padme: “Obi-Wan was right. You’ve changed.” Anakin: “For the better. I have become more powerful than any Jedi ever dreamed of being […]Together we will rule the galaxy.” Padme dies saying “He’s not evil…”; Palpatine to Vader: “I’m afraid she was killed by a Jedi…she is no longer our concern.”

June 13, 2003, 135-page revised second draft: both take on Dooku; **first time the dream is death in childbirth, “a Macbeth-like circle”**; Jedi distrust; Mace skeptical of the Chosen One; Palpatine suggests Padme has been meeting Obi-Wan; Mace accompanied by Eeth Koth, Kit Fisto, Saesee Tiin. Immolation dialog (n.894): “This is the end for you, my friend. I wish it were otherwise. This is your last chance to join me.” Then “Help me, Master.” “Don’t ask me Anakin…you’re evil… I can’t help you.” “I hate you!” Medical droid now: dying of a broken heart; she lives to see the children; Sidious tells Vader he killed her in his anger; last shot the Lars homestead. Jealousy subplot (two Palpatine scenes; Obi-Wan visit knowing Anakin loves Padme) downplayed in the finished film. Episode I first draft: Padme “gives Obi-Wan a long, adoring look” (n.895).

June 26th, 2003, fourth draft, 129 pages, days before filming. Extra Plagueis lines (n.896): “Plagueis never saw it coming. It’s ironic he could save others from death, but not himself. According to the legend, it was a power his apprentice found no value in. So the ability to save loved ones was lost with Plagueis ‘the wise.’” Those last lines cut, so Palpatine can use the power as bait and later say they must “discover the secret”—“This may also indicate that any implication of Palpatine’s parentage may be a falsehood.” Fourth draft also has “You were the chosen one!” Lucas on scripting (n.897): dialog “you can change on the set or even afterward—you know, I’m not known for my dialog. [laughs] I think of it as a sound effect… The core issue, ultimately, is greed, possessiveness—the inability to let go… Anakin becomes emotionally attached to things, his mother, his wife. That’s why he falls—because he does not have the ability to let go.” Filming June 30th, 2003, entirely in-studio. Acting coach **Chris Neil** (**Natalie Portman**, n.899; Neil, n.900): “George’s approach is to play Anakin as not consumed by the dark side from the get-go, but over the course of the movie.” Lucas (n.901): “a script and a movie are two different things.”

## Post-production rewrite of the turn; 2004 DVDs; reception (pp. 399–415)

Rough cut shown to ILM, then **Spielberg** and **Jeff Nathanson**, June 2004 (n.902), typography locked: “some of the people had strong opinions… Some people were having a hard time with the reason Anakin goes bad. Somebody asked whether somebody could kill Anakin’s best friend, so that he gets really angry. They wanted a real betrayal… They didn’t understand the fact that Anakin is simply greedy. There is no revenge. The revenge of the Sith is Palpatine. It doesn’t have much to do with Darth Vader; he’s a pawn in the whole scheme… I decided that I’m not going to alter the film to make it more commercial or marketable. I have to be true to my vision, which is thirty years old, but I have to be true to it.”

Then he changed his mind. Cut every scene not about Anakin; unexpected juxtapositions “worked much better”; themes “seemed poetic” (n.903). Padme “now became the issue—and would subsequently alter Vader’s motivations in the sequels. He would now turn to the darkside out of a quest to save a loved one.” Second vision added as an edit trick: Obi-Wan’s “hold on Padme” from unused end-of-film material, inserted into a daytime apartment scene that originally was Anakin sensing Obi-Wan had visited. New Anakin–Yoda scene (“premonitions,” plural); new love/baby-plans scene before the first nightmare. **The entire turn sequence re-shot.**

**Original filmed version (pp. 401–402):** Palpatine reveals himself; Anakin is conflicted but “essentially accepting the Sith in this scene.” Mace enters **with Anakin already present**; Palpatine Force-retrieves Anakin’s saber (n.904); Anakin watches, then cuts off Mace’s hand; Anakin “collapses in disbelief that the Jedi were indeed attempting to take over”; Sidious knights him and tells him to kill the rest. Original motivation residual in “I want more, but I know I shouldn’t,” Mustafar “rule the galaxy,” “Obi Wan was right—you’ve changed.” Dark side “like a drug” (*Empire*: “If once you start down the dark path…”).

**Reshot version:** Palpatine first sympathizes that the Jedi undervalue Anakin; ending reshot so the dark side holds the key to saving Padme. Anakin **refuses, stays loyal, turns Palpatine in**. Waits in the Council chamber; Padme and Anakin look across the city; Palpatine’s telepathic “if the Jedi destroy me, any hope of saving her will be lost”; Anakin barges in as Mace appears victorious; “He must live… I need him!”; “What have I done?”; “Just help me save Padme’s life…I can’t live without her.” *Sith* DVD “depth commentary” (n.905): “In the original script, Anakin is in disbelief not over his actions, but that the Jedi would attempt to take over the Republic. In pick ups, the dialog was changed to keep the subject on Anakin saving his wife.” Lucas (n.906): “Originally, this was written where Anakin was actually seduced in this scene and he became the Sith pretty much in this scene… it just didn’t play that well, in terms of how fast he converted.” Also (n.907): having Anakin tell the Jedi “strengthens the scene when Mace comes in… he knows that he’s the bad guy.” (n.908): “The real issue here is trying to save Padme… I can see what the problem is and how to solve it… we needed to be reminded why Anakin was turning; although it was put in there very strongly [already], it wasn’t strong enough.” (n.909): “two sharp ‘right turns’… Anakin turning to the dark side and killing Mace is a very hard right… the things that he’s struggling with are so subtle that it may be hard for people to understand why his obsession to hold on to Padme is so strong.”

Kaminski: first half revised, second half not—hence the jarring jump from regretful knighting to “I will kill all the Jedi.” Lucas’s later commentary bridge: exploration of the dark side to save Padme then corrupts him; “the strongest [inconsistency], Anakin’s slaughter of the Jedi, is still jarringly out of place.” Not enough time before May 2005 “to coherently re-align the entire plot.” To Jackson and McDiarmid during 2004 reshoots (n.910): “The only scene I hadn’t thought through enough is the [turn scene].” To Christensen the next day (n.911): “It’s basically Faust in the end… you make a pact with the devil… You cannot change the inevitable.”

Kaminski’s “three published storylines” thesis (flag as Kaminski): stand-alone fairy-tale *Star Wars*; the Trilogy; the six-episode Saga. “he had released each previous version of the story, in effect publishing the intermediate versions.” 2004 (n.912): “In the [original] three films, it appeared the story is about Luke, but if you see all six films then you realise the story is about Darth Vader.”

**2004 original-trilogy DVDs** (November, Christmas of Episode III’s year), Special Edition only, hoped-for 2007 delayed because of the format. Re-color-timed “slicker and more high-contrast”; Naboo in the *Jedi* celebration; Shaw’s face altered; **Hayden Christensen inserted into the final *Jedi* scene**. July 29th, 2003, during *Sith* photography: new **Ian McDiarmid** shots replace the original *Empire* Emperor (n.913). New dialog locked (pp. 408): “We have a new enemy. The young Rebel who destroyed the Death Star. I have no doubt that this boy is the offspring of Anakin Skywalker.” Vader: “How is that possible?” Emperor: “Search your feelings, Lord Vader. You will know it to be true.” Kaminski: meant to put the revelation onscreen, but “incompatible with the film itself”—Vader is already obsessively searching, “I’m sure Skywalker is with them.” “Is Vader sincere or he is faking it? Certainly there is support for both directions.” Lucas “crusade to try to erase the originals” (n.914): “The other [version], it’s on VHS, if anybody wants it.” (n.915): “The Special Edition, that’s the one I wanted out there.” 1997 “experiment in learning new technology” (n.916) and “a nice little twentieth anniversary” (n.917) absorbed into the post-prequel story. “the ‘prequels’ no longer existed.”

Subsequent viewings: Vader’s Blockade Runner entry as “Anakin’s long-term imprisonment”; Luke leaving training “more ominous.” Lucas (n.918): “If you see them in order it completely twists things about… Now Darth Vader is a tragic character who’s lost everything. He’s basically a bitter old man in a suit. ‘I am your father’ was a real shock. Now it's a real reward… It’s an extremely modern, almost interactive moviemaking. You take blocks and move them around, and you come out with different emotional states.” “I never arrived at a degree of satisfaction where I thought the screenplay was perfect… If I hadn’t been forced to shoot the film, I would doubtless still be rewriting it now” (n.919). **Hamill** (n.920): opening night, 10 o’clock, “Hiya, kid! You famous yet?… We’re doing the monaural mix tonight… I mean, he is gonna fix up Star Wars after it’s already opened!” “Episode IV A New Hope” later added to the crawl; two on-camera Imperial officer deaths Lucas worried about in 1983 *Skywalking* (n.921) corrected in 1997; sound remixed “at least five times.” Kaminski: *Star Wars* as “experiment in altering audience perception, not through the changing of content but by the changing of context.”

Teaser November 2004, first glimpse of full-suited Vader; “the very last Star Wars film ever,” Sequel Trilogy hopes dispelled. *Wired* (n.922): “there was a lot of controversy around here about the fact that I wasn't doing the obvious… People expected Episode III, which is where Anakin turns into Darth Vader, to be Episode I… Most people said, ‘This guy must have been a horrible little brat -- a demon child.’ But the point is, he wasn't born that way -- he became that way and thought he was doing the right thing… The idea is to see how a democracy becomes a dictatorship, and how a good person goes bad -- and still, in the end, thinks he's doing the right thing.” First PG-13 *Star Wars* (n.923): “I would take a nine or a ten year old to it… But I don’t think I’d take a five or a six year old… I could pull it back a little bit, but I don’t really want to.” Advance screenings included Spielberg, Robbins, Barwood, Murch; “happier with this film” (n.924). Opened Cannes. Political readings: “If you’re not with me then you’re my enemy” near Bush post-9/11; burning Jedi temple / homeland security. Cannes (n.925): “I didn't think it was going to get quite this close… I hope this doesn't come true in our country… When I wrote it, Iraq didn't exist… The parallels between what we did in Vietnam and what we're doing in Iraq now are unbelievable.” **Todd McCarthy**, *Variety* (n.926): “the best in the overall series since ‘The Empire Strikes Back.’” *Time* May 9th: “The Last Star Wars…Darker, Scarier, Better” (n.927). *New Yorker* and *Rolling Stone* unfavorable; *Chicago Tribune* “a smashing success”; *Baltimore Sun* “a pop masterpiece”; *Hollywood Reporter* “a qualified yes”; *New York Times*: “It's better than ‘Star Wars.’” Highest-grossing of the year, nearly $400 million domestic, despite record-low attendance (n.928). Lucas, 2005 (n.929): “I expected this to be one movie… It was kind of a whim which turned into my life… I had mixed feelings about being George Star Wars Lucas… It’s not the worst thing in the world.” To **Charlie Rose** (n.930): divorce “set me back quite a ways… six years to get back to point where I was financially even… one of the reasons to go back to Star Wars was that it would hopefully make me financially secure enough to where I wouldn’t have to go to a studio and beg for money. And so I took all the money that I had at that point and I invested it in The Phantom Menace, and then I did the other one [Attack of the Clones] and now I’ve got it all in this one [Revenge of the Sith].” Close: “Everything had finally come full circle.”
