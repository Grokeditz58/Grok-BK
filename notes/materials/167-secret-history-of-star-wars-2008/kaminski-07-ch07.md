# Kaminski, *The Secret History of Star Wars* (e-book v3.0, 2008): notes — Chapter VII: Demons and Angels (pp. 231–269)

Fully read. Citations: Kaminski p. N; Kaminski Notes n. Y (Ch. VII starts at n.505). Kaminski glosses flagged. Staff/crew/cast labeled by speaker. Core of the chapter: *Revenge of the Jedi* drafts humanize and then redeem Vader; Leia is plugged in as the abandoned Sequel Trilogy’s “Other”; the title change and “always Return” claim are rebutted.

## Redemption not pre-planned in the final-film manner (pp. 231–238)

“I am your father” “was so momentous, Lucas had no room to develop the series beyond this plot point,” and *Star Wars III* “would become, inevitably, about Vader’s redemption, and thus forever change the saga.” “All indication is that Vader was not initially supposed to be redeemed, at least not in the manner in which the eventual film played out.” Mid-1980, as he was revising (n.505): “In the next film, everything gets resolved one way or the other. Luke won the first battle… Vader won the second… and in the third film, only one of them walks away. We have to go back to the beginning to find out the real problem.” Kaminski: “going back ‘to the beginning,’ in other words to Vader’s fall from grace,” is a “relatively new metamorphosis.”

**Gary Kurtz** on a thread “totally tossed out the window [for Jedi]” (n.506), typography locked: “Vader trying to convince Luke to join him to overthrow the Emperor… It wasn't him saying I want to take over the world and be the evil leader, it was that transition. It was Vader saying, ‘I'm looking again at what I've done and where my life has gone and who I've served and, very much in the Samurai tradition, and saying if I can join forces with my son, who is just as strong as I am, that maybe we can make some amends.’” Rough-draft “redemption” is “loyalty and honor,” not “compassionate… salvation”; Kaminski traces it to 1974 Prince Valorum and *Hidden Fortress* General Tadokoro.

Title first mentioned to **Alan Arnold** in 1979 (n.507). Officially announced May 1980 as *Revenge of the Jedi* (*Bantha Tracks*, n.508): preproduction January 1981, release spring 1983. When Vader’s redemption became the focus, “the story ceased to be centered on vengeance and instead revolved around deliverance.” To Kerry O’Quinn, July 1981, after the revised rough draft (n.509): “Yeah, but it’s a long way from the plot to the script… what seems like a great idea when it’s described in three sentences suddenly doesn’t hold together when you try to make five or six scenes out of it. So plots change a lot when they start getting into script form.”

*Raiders*: Lucas (n.510): “I probably had more fun on that picture than any other… I had all the confidence in the world in Steve [Spielberg] and I was not at risk financially.” **Spielberg** (n.511): “he said he had three stories in mind—it turned out George did not have three stories in mind, we had to make up all the subsequent stories”; river-raft and mine-cart set-pieces cut from *Raiders* and reused in *Temple of Doom*. **Kurtz** (n.512): “This idea that the roller-coaster ride was all the audience was interested in, and the story doesn’t have to be very adult or interesting, seemed to come up because of what happened with Raiders… and the fact that that seemed to make a lot of money.” Domestic: *Star Wars* $307M, *Empire* $209M (“a full $100 million less”), *Jedi* “a whopping $50 million more successful than its predecessor.” Kid-friendly turn: “while the only puppet in Empire had been a grumpy Buddhist philosopher, Jedi would give kids an entire palace full of zany muppets, including a brief musical number, and a whole planet full of teddy bears.” Footnote: “This shift to more children-friendly fare can also be seen as a reflection of the fact that Lucas now had an infant daughter.”

**Kasdan** (n.513): “[I] thought the movies could hold more character and more complexity… George thought they should be simpler in another way. It was a serious philosophical difference.” **Kurtz** last-minute fleet establishing shot just before 70mm (n.514): one composite “wasn’t particularly good, and George said, ‘Oh well, maybe we should just let it go.’ I said, ‘It’s worth at least one more go through. One bad shot can ruin the whole movie, basically.’” Recut “to be fast and action-oriented… was a complete disaster because it simply wasn’t the way the material was shot” (n.515). Ranch as the real goal: 1980 (n.516) “came out of filmschool”; “five and six years and cost in excess of $20 million… way beyond my personal resources” (n.517); personal net worth “somewhere around $20 million” (n.518), much already in ranch real estate; failure would mean the bank owns the film and he is forced to sell “the $30 million Lucasfilm corporation” (n.519). Set visit 1979 (n.520): “I don’t want to spend the rest of my life making Star Wars pictures, but I do want to get them set up so that they’ll operate properly without my having to get completely involved in all of them. They’ve got to be self-generating to support the facility.” To **Jean Vallely**, 1980 (n.521): profits from *Empire* and *Revenge* invested in outside companies to build and maintain the ranch; if those two don’t do it, “that’s the end. I’m not going to spend the next fifteen years of my life trying to make hit movies to get the ranch.” **Charles Weber** fired; nearly half of Lucasfilm laid off with six months, vocational counsellors, cash settlements. **Dale Pollock**, 1983 (n.522): ranch would be “a motion picture think tank, where movies would be conceptualized, rather than physically made… neither a film studio nor a film campus but something in between.”

## Rough draft (Feb 20, 1981) and Leia as the “Other” (pp. 238–245)

Lucas, 1981 (n.523): “[The series] started out as a simple fairy tale… When [Revenge] comes out, people will say, ‘Oh, my god. How obvious! Why couldn’t they think of something more interesting than that?’ ” The early two-sequel outline (Jedi training, new Rebel base, confront Vader) “had basically been funnelled into Empire,” leaving the third film “stretched thin.” Jabba “was more an afterthought than anything else… because Han Solo had become such a popular character” (n.524). Recycled: 1974 Wookiee subplot as Ewoks; Death Star attack, initially with two Death Stars. **Kurtz** (n.525): “The idea of another attack on another Death Star wasn’t there at all… It was a rehash of Star Wars, with better visual effects. And there were no Ewoks ... it was just entirely different. It was much more adult and straightforward, the story.”

**Denise Worrell** on the “tree-house” writing room (n.526): Marcia-decorated carriage house; desk with Mickey Mouse phone, Wookiee pencil holder, telescope; books: Joseph Campbell’s *Hero With A Thousand Faces*, *Bartlett’s Familiar Quotations*, *Webster’s Dictionary*, *Harper’s Bible Dictionary*, Syd Field’s *The Foundations of Screenwriting*, *Roget’s Thesaurus* open to “imagination”; “five three-ring notebooks containing Lucas’s notes and sketches for the entire Star Wars epic, past, present and future”; picture of Marcia and the baby. “If I spend eight hours ‘writing,’… I probably spend three hours writing and the rest of the time thinking.”

**Rough-draft plot, typography locked in notes where dialog is quoted later.** Action on **Had Abbadon**, “capital of the Galactic Empire—a planet covered in cities and enshrouded in smog. This is the planet that would eventually become Coruscant.” Two Death Stars orbit the Green Moon. Emperor “dwells in the very bowels of the planet, so deep underground that his throne sits atop a lake of lava, a truly satanic image.” Emperor Force-chokes Vader, then privately tells Jerjerrod Luke will be turned, not destroyed. “Jerjerrod and the Emperor compete with Vader for Luke.” Unlike *Empire*, Vader “is now implied to have some feelings for his son.” Tatooine dream: Luke criticizes Ben for lying; Yoda will join Ben in the Netherworld; after Jabba, another dream: Yoda says destroy Vader; **Obi-Wan reveals Luke has a sister—Luke knows it is Leia.** Luke surrenders; Veers (loyal to Vader) delivers him; Vader tempts, then kills Jerjerrod and takes Luke to the Emperor. Ben’s ghost to Vader: turn good, pass through the Netherworld, “Obi Wan will rescue him before he becomes one with the Force so that he will retain his identity.” Vader refuses. Duel “jumping from rock to rock over a river of lava.” Luke cuts off Vader’s arm; Vader “begs Luke to kill him”; Luke refuses. Lightning; Ben and Yoda appear as a shield; Vader charges the Emperor; **both fall into the lake of lava.** Celebration: Luke tells Leia she is his sister; Ben appears “in flesh and blood,” then Yoda, “finally, the elderly Annikin Skywalker.” Kaminski: Vader’s sacrifice still “more for practical means to keep Luke as his own apprentice… not wildly different from the finale of Empire,” “unintentionally it seems, tumbling into the lava.” “First steps towards humanising Vader.”

**The “Other” and Leia as sister.** Sequel Trilogy abandoned, so the “Other” had to be resolved with a pre-existing character. New Jedi would diminish Luke; Han as last hope “did not seem like a very good idea.” Recycles the abandoned Brackett-draft twin-sister subplot (minus her being a Jedi) and ends the love triangle. **Hamill**, *Prevue*, 1983 (n.527): “Luke’s got a mission, and a romance would dilute his forward line through the story.” Only biological relation to “the most powerful Force-using family” makes her “another hope.” Film line: “The Force is strong in my family… My father has it. I have it. And my sister has it. Yes, it’s you, Leia.” **“This is the first introduction in the films of some kind of biological link to the Force.”**

Backstory complications flagged as Kaminski reconstruction, then later-draft facts: Vader must not know of a daughter; hiding the son on the father’s birth planet “made little sense”; Owen/Beru as possible Force-using siblings. Solution from *Jedi* story meetings (n.528; cut from film, in the novelization): after the volcano duel Kenobi brings infant Luke to Tatooine “to live with Kenobi’s brother Owen,” making Owen and Beru “impostors.” Twins required because Annikin turned when Luke was a baby; later *Jedi* drafts: Annikin “never even knew his wife was pregnant at all,” so he must discover Luke off-screen between *Star Wars* and *Empire*. Leia taken to **Bail Organa**, “governor of Alderaan”; adoption invented “to justify… Leia Organa and Leia Skywalker.” “There are no such references to her being adopted before 1981” (n.529, *Annotated Screenplays*). Parallel: Lucas “himself had just adopted a baby girl at this time in 1981.” **Hamill** (n.530): “I tried to get George to admit, I said, come on you made that up on the plane ride over here. He said, no, I had the whole thing written.” Kenobi’s 1977 story can only be excused as a lie; later “certain point of view”; Kenobi turned from “noble Jedi master” to “tragic failure.”

Ewoks from 1974 Wookiees (n.531): “The Wookie planet that I created for Star Wars was eventually turned into the Ewok planet in Jedi. I basically cut the Wookies in half and called them Ewoks! … Chewbacca was sophisticated technologically and I wanted the characters involved in the battle to be primitive.” Recycling (n.532): “Usually, if I like something and I have to drop it, I put it on the shelves… ideas aren’t precious… you have to be willing to throw it away.”

## Revised rough draft (June 12, 1981): Anakin as tragic hero (pp. 245–253)

“It is in this revised rough draft that the true nature of Anakin Skywalker finally emerged” and “the six-part saga of Star Wars would be truly solidified.” Vader “a fallen hero, one who lost his way… serving the Emperor seemingly against his will, a tragic victim himself.” Jerjerrod made Vader’s superior specifically “to make the audience identify and sympathize with Vader.” Opening with Piett: “Or greatly feared,” Vader grumbles of the welcoming fuss; “The disgusting little bureaucrat is attempting to lay a trap for me.” Key dialog, typography from script block (pp. 246–247): Jerjerrod: “the Emperor is disturbed with your failure to deal with young Skywalker… You will supervise the construction of the Battle Stations.” Vader: “But, I have all but turned him to the dark side of the force.” “He can't do this. The boy is mine!” Jerjerrod: “It would appear that you still have some feelings for your troublesome offspring.” Vader: “The only feeling I have for him is hatred.” Then “The Rebellion will not be easily crushed, and my son will not be easily turned.” “Get off my bridge!” Alone: **“Luke, beware, you are the Emperor's prey now.”** Kaminski: “From the opening of the film, this is a very different Darth Vader than the one we were introduced to in 1977.” Much of this exchange later transplanted to the Emperor’s arrival, roles reversed.

Emperor to Jerjerrod in a coded chamber: “Take extra precautions. He is far stronger than his father… Vader is to know nothing of this. Young Skywalker must be brought directly to me… and watch Lord Vader closely.” Kaminski parenthetical: “striking similarity to one between General Grievous and Darth Sidious in Revenge of the Sith.” After Jabba, Luke kidnapped in a sandstorm. Yoda: Ben’s power is drained; Luke cannot kill his father; **Yoda reveals the twin sister is Leia.** Emperor: “I did not turn him to the Dark Side. That is something he did for himself…as you will do for yourself.” Vader kills Jerjerrod (“I no longer wish to be annoyed by your simpering ways… your importance has been greatly exaggerated”) and storms the palace: “a bizarre sort of hero, killing the Emperor’s minions to save his son.” Throne room: Emperor stops Vader’s breathing until he kneels: **“Forgive me, master.”** “Vader’s servitude… is finally revealed as not a partnership of evil… but a rather tragic one of unwilling obedience.”

Tombs: Ben in the flesh; Yoda as ghost; Emperor says “You!” to Yoda. Kaminski: maybe a prior confrontation, “a fact which we would not find out until 2005,” though Lucas later implied the Emperor never knew of Yoda (n.533) and the novelization has him only vaguely remembering; **Frank Oz** (n.534): compiled what Yoda knew, “I asked George Lucas at lunch one day for his thoughts on Yoda, and he said, ‘Just make it wonderful.’” Emperor carries his own lightsaber. Duel; Vader’s arm into lava; Luke: “if he is to be destroyed, you must do it.” Lightning at Ben, Luke jumps in, Yoda shields. Emperor, typography locked (p. 252): “Perhaps there is still another Skywalker… your father!” Vader flies at him; both into the lava. Luke: “He turned back to the good side.” Ben: “Yes, he did.” Yoda, now flesh, has saved Vader’s spirit so he “retain[s] his identity”; Annikin appears; Yoda sighs watching from the side. “The script is perhaps the most important piece in the development of Anakin Skywalker and the prequel trilogy.” Lucas “thought this revised rough draft humanized him too much. The next draft… would downplay Vader and his role as a sympathetic character.”

## Marquand, meetings, Kenobi’s confession, Han almost dies (pp. 253–262)

Kasdan hired after June 12, 1981; ready to retire from writing, “willing to help out the man who gave him his career.” Lucas considered directing (n.535): “I took one look at the amount of work and thought, ‘Oh my god, my life is complicated enough.’” **Kershner** not asked to return; 2004 (n.536): “One, I didn’t want to. Two, I was asked halfway through shooting Empire… I didn’t want to go through that again… I didn’t want to be a Lucas employee. And I’d read the script of Jedi — not the whole script, but a scaled-down version — and I didn’t believe it.” Spielberg blocked by unions (Lucas had quit after *Empire*). **David Lynch** (n.537): “Obviously, Star Wars is totally George’s thing,” went to *Dune*. **Howard Kazanjian** (n.538): “You’re working for George—it’s his story, his baby… you’re representing his wishes.” *American Cinematographer* 1983 / Kazanjian (n.539): “a director that was rather young, that was flexible, that had not established himself as a great independent filmmaker, that would follow the tradition of Star Wars, that would let George be as closely attached as he likes.” **Marquand**: orchestra conductor to composer (n.540); “It is rather like trying to direct King Lear—with Shakespeare in the next room.” (n.541) **Kurtz** (n.542): after failing to “direct vicariously over the telephone” on *Empire*, “on Jedi, he was determined to find a director who was easy to control… the film was sort of one that George might have directed if he had directed it himself.” Marquand (n.543) wanted “that sort of presentation on Jedi rather than the highly sophisticated, sexy way in which Kershner made Empire… an incredible, glossy, glorious sort of machine.” **Hamill** (n.544): “We went back to that smash-and-grab technique, which was also used for Raiders… Just go, go, go!” Lucas on set nearly every day, second unit, even portions of main unit; **Carrie Fisher** story of contradicting direction; **Dale Pollock** saw Lucas check lenses and move cameras (n.545). Alan Hume / Alec Mills “falling out” rumor flagged as unfounded (Hume’s autobiography “does not hint at this at all”). Budget $32 million, fronted by Lucas, no major overruns, “meticulous planning of producer Kazanjian.” Release date set before a script was written.

Story meetings July 13th–17th, 1981, Lucas / Kasdan / Marquand / Kazanjian (n.546). Kasdan (n.547): “Will you do me this one favour? I really need your help.” Proceedings taped; Kasdan left with “a voluminous transcript” (n.548). Leia, not waiting on the Green Moon, infiltrates Jabba’s palace as a bounty hunter. Marquand felt Yoda’s off-screen death a let-down (n.549). Lucas asked Kasdan for “a great scene for Alec Guinness with powerful Shakespearian dialog” (n.550, Bouzereau). Ben: Anakin already a great pilot, Kenobi “arrogant and thought he could train the young man as well as Yoda,” volcano duel, “the entire galaxy is suffering for his arrogance… cost many billions of people their lives,” emphasized more in the third draft (n.551). Name shift **Anakin** (not Annikin): speculated Ken Annakin tribute, Scandinavian Anniken “favour/grace,” or biblical Anakim. New timeline: Vader “never knew his wife was pregnant.” Owen is Ben’s brother; “Owen always resented Ben for imposing Luke on them” (n.552). Footnote: *Empire* second draft, Yoda trained both Obi-Wan and Father; revised second draft changed it so Yoda trained Obi-Wan who trained Father, “because the initial second draft forgot to account for the fact that since Father Skywalker was now also Darth Vader he would also have been Obi Wan’s student.”

Long Ben/Luke excerpt (pp. 257–260), typography locked in substance: “many of the truths we cling to depend greatly on our own point of view.” “What happened to your father was my fault… Anakin was a good friend… I took it upon myself to train him as a Jedi. I thought that I could instruct him just as well as Yoda. I was wrong. My pride has had terrible consequences for the galaxy.” “He is more machine now than man. Twisted and evil.” “When your father left, he didn't know your mother was pregnant… So I took you to live with my brother Owen on Tatooine... and your mother took Leia to live as the daughter of Senator Organa, on Alderaan.” Leia “became a princess by virtue of lineage... no one knew she'd been adopted, of course.” Foster parents “had always told her to contact me on Tatooine, if her troubles became desperate.” “The Force is strong with her, as it is with all of your family.” Kaminski: “‘from a certain point of view’ was the best Lucas could do to excuse the obvious contradictions… somewhat clumsy and unconvincing.”

Leia reveal moved earlier; story meetings: Leia “too hard,” should cry, maybe “run away and hide” (n.553); Han consoles her in the revised second draft. Lucas (n.554): “It’s one thing for Darth Vader to tell Luke that he’s his father, and it’s another thing to have Luke tell Leia that he is her sister and that Darth Vader’s his father. That really gets hard to swallow.” Mother: Leia remembers her as “very beautiful. Kind, but sad.” *Annotated Screenplays* (n.555): sister and mother sent to friends in a distant system; mother died shortly; then adopted by the governor of Alderaan—**a second group of protectors** in draft two, not simply Alderaan from the start. Lucas (n.556): “The part I never really developed was the death of Luke and Leia’s mother. I had a backstory for her in earlier drafts, but it basically didn’t survive. When I got to Jedi, I wanted one of the kids to have some kind of memory of her because she will be a key figure in the new episodes I’m writing. But I really debated on whether or not Leia should remember her.” *Star Wars* second draft: Luke visits her headstone on the farm hill—later used in *Attack of the Clones*. That earlier mother backstory “didn’t survive… because Lucas had completely altered Skywalker family history with his fusion of Father Skywalker and Darth Vader in 1978.”

**Kasdan** (n.557): “I also felt someone had to go. Someone had to die… We should sacrifice somebody.” Suggested Luke die and the sister take over; Lucas “arguing how upset he was as a child when a hero was killed” (n.558). **Harrison Ford** (n.559): “I thought Han Solo should die. I thought he ought to sacrifice himself for the other two characters… I said ‘he’s got no mama, he’s got no papa, he’s got no future, he has no story responsibilities at this point, so let’s allow him to commit self-sacrifice.’” Lucas (n.560): “It would really have put an unfortunate twist on everything if we had killed off one of the main characters. Luke needed to live, and we needed to have Han and Leia together at the end. The fact that the boy gets the girl—or the girl gets the boy—in the end was a key factor and was as important as Luke overcoming his demons.” Kasdan’s other idea: Luke pretends to join the dark side, wears Vader’s mask, is told to destroy the fleet, aims the Death Star at Had Abbadon (n.561)—dropped. Two Death Stars combined into one; **Had Abbadon dropped entirely** (n.562). Lucas (n.563): “In the end it didn’t seem necessary to show the home planet of the Empire.” Also budgetary, script aimed at $25–30 million (n.564).

## Unmasking, pyre, title change (pp. 262–269)

Vader’s age “changed drastically” and “is explored in detail in chapter X”: roughly forty in *Star Wars*, then nearly Obi-Wan’s age as Father, unmasked as **seventy-seven-year-old Sebastian Shaw**. **Kazanjian** (n.565): “George Lucas did a first draft screen play— one he sort of doesn't admit doing— which laid down the plots, story, and characters. It wasn't quite resolved in the last fifth of the movie, but we all knew what was happening.” Second draft shifts redemption to Luke’s perspective (Vader’s Jerjerrod/Emperor subplot cut, Vader more ambiguous/threatening); Luke already believes there is good in him—“for such an about-face in character from Empire, it is rather odd that Luke’s epiphany is left entirely between the films.” Reactor-shaft death so Vader survives for a goodbye. Lucas decides to unmask him. Lucas (n.566): “I didn’t have a very specific idea about what Vader might look like underneath the mask… Ben had sent him into a volcano… he is kind of this three-quarter mechanical man and one-quarter human, and the suit he wears is like a walking iron lung… until then I just knew that he was pretty messed up simply because he could barely breathe or speak.” 1977 (n.567): “His face is all horrible inside. I was going to shoot a close-up of Vader where you could see the inside of his face, but then we said, no, no, it would destroy the mystique of the whole thing.” *Empire* meetings treated him as “a horrible mutant” (n.568). **Kershner** (n.569): capsule-scar glimpse “shot this scene very carefully… I imagined that beneath the mask Vader was hideous; his mouth was cut away, and he had one eye hanging low. I was very surprised to see that he was an ordinary man in the third film.” *Annotated Screenplays* (n.570): helmet-off might seem to kill him; Lucas wanted Vader to “see his son in a human way,” voice “a much weaker version… much older-sounding,” “in his sixties, about ten years younger than Ben,” “sad-looking, not repulsive. Maybe one of his eyes could be completely white with no pupil and the other could be sort of clouded over.” Kasdan suggested “a light grey beard.” Second draft: “an elderly man with a scarred face and a white beard”; third draft “a bit more gruesome… a horrible mutant, hardly recognizable as human.” Final: “a sad old man— bald and scarred… pale… ‘you were right about me’… and then gracefully dies.”

Final script late. **Robert Watts**: “The screenplay is the blueprint for everything… the final script did come very, very late.” Kasdan second draft on time for September 1981, revisions to November, third draft December. Filming began January 1982; Luke’s anger trigger still vague. Lucas (n.572): Marquand “said the script sort of said ‘Vader says something that upsets Luke’ or something vague like that… I had never really come up with a satisfactory answer… in the process of evolving the importance of Leia as the sister—you know, it was sitting right there in front of my face. And it became obvious that turning her to the darkside would be the thing that would set Luke off again.” Funeral pyre added in pick-up (n.573): “It gave more closure in terms of Luke’s relationship to his father and letting go of his father.” Editor **Duwayne Dunham** (n.574): “I remember that we said, ‘what happened to Vader? Did Luke leave him on the Death Star?’ So the scene was shot up at Skywalker Ranch.” Lightning freeze-frame: robotic arms and legs, artificial neck vertebrae, “implying that his neck was broken in his tumble into the volcano”; points to Dr. Curtis Saxton, *Star Wars Technical Commentaries*. *Jedi* novelization, Kenobi on The Duel (n.575): “You should not think of that machine as your father… When your father clawed his way out of that fiery pool, the change had been burned into him forever—he was Darth Vader, without a trace of Anakin Skywalker. Irredeemably dark. Scarred. Kept alive only by machinery and his own black will.” Vader’s dying thoughts (n.576): “Memories of molten lava, crawling up his back ... no. This boy had pulled him from that pit — here, now, with this act. This boy was good.”

Lucas admits being on set every day (n.577): “I hadn’t realized that ultimately it’s probably easier for me to do these things than to farm them out.” Kazanjian (n.578): simultaneous construction/shooting in two California places, ILM, and London. U.S. locations shot as **“Blue Harvest,”** tagline “Horror Beyond Imagination.”

**Title change—conflicting accounts, not collapsed.** Kazanjian (n.580): “George came to me and he said the title of Episode VI is Return of the Jedi. And I said, ‘I think it’s a weak title.’ And he came one or two days later and he says ‘we’re calling it Revenge of the Jedi.’… Just before it got to the theaters, George came back and he said, ‘I want to go back to Return of the Jedi.’ Now, the logic behind that was a Jedi does not take revenge.” Footnote: this implies Kazanjian was already hired and discussing the film “before Empire Strikes Back was even released.” **Jay Cocks**, 1997 *Star Wars: The Magic and Mystery* (n.581): T-shirts said *Revenge*; Lucas later: “Revenge isn’t a Jedi concept.” Lucas’s own version (n.582): “we called the film Revenge of the Jedi to throw people off. The title was always intended to be Return of the Jedi… Fox started promoting the film before we could tell them not to use the title. We were lucky that they didn’t start promoting the film under the title Blue Harvest.” **Kaminski rejects the decoy story:** the film already had the code name *Blue Harvest*; Lucasfilm had promoted *Revenge* since May 1980; Lucas had used *Revenge* privately since 1979 (n.583); “his own script drafts bore this name, as did call-sheets and other internal Lucasfilm documents.” **Jim Bloom** suggested *Blue Harvest* because of equipment-fee overcharges (n.584). *Star Trek II* retitled from *The Vengeance of Khan* to *The Wrath of Khan* to avoid confusion. **Dale Pollock**, 1983 (n.585): “Although Revenge of the Jedi was the first title announced, Lucas always intended for the film to be called Return of the Jedi. He just didn’t want to give away the plot. The switch came no less than six months before Jedi’s release and cost several thousand dollars.” Kaminski’s reconstruction: genuine *Revenge* in 1979 when the film was about fighting back; as the script became about redeeming Vader, “the vengeance angle… was no longer present,” and revenge “would not even be a Jedi trait.” **Steve Sansweet**, *The Vault* (n.586): “an internal debate raging, with producer Howard Kazanjian favoring what he felt was the stronger Revenge, and George Lucas slowly coming to the conclusion that the word just didn’t fit the movie.” Octocon, Ireland, 1982: fans criticized Kazanjian that Jedi don’t take revenge (n.587). November 1982 market-research report (n.588), typography locked: “While younger respondants are somewhat excited by the term revenge, many movie goers over 30 are turned off by its violent connotations. The implications of the term revenge do not fit the good guy/Jedi image… if the saga is to continue with the good versus evil theme it has delivered to date, return, rather than revenge might be more effective in communicating this message.” On time and on budget; release bumped two days to May 25th, 1983, to coincide with the original film. Chapter close: as *Star Wars* bows out, the 1980s event-picture era arrives.
