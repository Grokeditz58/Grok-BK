# Kaminski, *The Secret History of Star Wars* (e-book v3.0, 2008): notes — Chapter III: Enter Luke Starkiller (pp. 84–130)

Fully read. Citations: Kaminski p. N; Kaminski Notes n. Y (Ch. III notes start at n.191). Kaminski glosses flagged. Staff/crew/cast statements labeled by speaker. This is the core chapter for Vader’s 1975–77 form and for the claim that Father Skywalker and Vader were separate in the finished 1977 film.

## Second draft, January 1975: *Adventures of the Starkiller* (episode one) *The Star Wars* (pp. 84–97)

Lucas on rewriting (n.191): “I find rewriting no more or less difficult than writing… I struggled through the first draft and dealt with some of the problems. But now the next step is even more painful, because I have to confront the problems in a more serious way.”

Primary expansion: “the force of others” becomes a literal superpower. Droids reinstated as Hidden Fortress peasants/clowns. Lucas (n.192): “Having two bureaucrats or peasants is really like having two clowns—it goes back to Shakespeare… which is probably where Kurosawa got it.” Jedi-Bendu / Sith recast as *Lensmen*-style superheroes. **Stephen Hart** (n.193) and **Kristen Brennan** (n.194) on *Galactic Patrol*: Kiber crystal // lenses; Ashla / Bogan // Arisians / Boskone; midichlorians later; “coruscant.” *Galactic Patrol* sits on Lucas’s writing-room shelf in the first Episode I web documentary (Kaminski’s report). “Jedi-Bendu” as amalgam of *jidai geki* + *Dune* “Prana-Bindu.”

One clearly mythological beat: an ancient holyman named Skywalker who discovered the Force and had twelve sons (zodiac / disciples motif).

Lucas briefly contemplated splitting the first script into two films (rescue from Alderaan; then Yavin / Death Star) (n.195); instead he simplified. Shift from Kurosawa toward John Ford: Annikin transplanted to a farm and renamed Luke. Footnote, Baxter p. 157: “I used that [name] because I was identifying with the character.” Lucas (n.196): “I was searching for a story for a long time. Each story was a totally different story about totally different characters before I finally landed on the story.” Lucas (n.197): “I wrote from my point of view, as if I were going into that world… It’s nearly impossible to separate an author from his characters.”

**Rinzler** transcribes notes (n.198): “Sith knights look like Linda Blair in Exorcist…Vader—do something evil in prison to Deak…Luke reluctantly accepts the burden (artist, not warrior, fear); establish Luke as good pilot…farm boy: fulfills the legend of the son of the sons; pulls sword from the stone…Leia: tomboy, bright, tough, really soft and afraid; loves Luke but not admitting to it…Make Han in bar like Bogart—freelance tough guy for hire…whole film must be told from robots’ point of view…the empire has a terrible new weapon, a fortress so powerful it can destroy a planet, possibly even a sun.”

**Jan Helander** second-draft summary (n.199) locked in substance: Deak and Clieg, sons of the Starkiller, sent to brother Luke on Utapau for the diamond-like Kiber Crystal (intensifies either side of the force a hundred fold). **Lord Darth Vader, a Black Knight of the Sith,** boards; Clieg killed; Vader thinks Deak is “the last son of the Starkiller,” “the son of the suns.” Droids escape to Utapau. Luke lives with younger brothers Biggs and Windy, Uncle Owen, Aunt Beru, **and their daughter Leia** (a cousin cameo, “conceptually different from Princess Leia”). Luke has never met his legendary father. Owen has taught warrior ways including the laser sword; spiritual ways only the father can teach. Cantina; **Han Solo (who claims to be a starpilot)** and Chewbacca; Han is merely a cabin boy, fakes a reactor failure, tricks Captain Oxus and crewman Jabba the Hutt into evacuating; science officer Montross Holdaack lifts off with them. Ogana Major destroyed. Rescue Deak from Imperial dungeons of Alderaan; garbage chute; Luke heals Deak with the crystal, mental message from father. On Yavin’s fourth moon Luke meets “his wizened old father for the first time.” Han takes the reward and leaves. Luke attacks with Threepio and ranger Bail Antilles as gunners; father uses the crystal to fight the Bogan. Vader realises the Starkiller is alive, joins in his own fighter, “just about to destroy Luke’s ship, when Han reappears, sending the Sith knight to his doom.” Threepio and Antilles hit the weak point. The Starkiller praises the victory “as the start of the revolution.”

Lucas (n.200): “I sort of tacked the air battle on… because it was the original impetus of the whole project.”

**Kaminski, typography locked, p. 89:** “It should be noted that never was the concept of the protagonist’s father succumbing to evil present. In all the drafts, Luke’s father was a revered Jedi warrior and the Sith villains separate characters, with Darth Vader continuing to be an unimportant henchman of the Empire.”

Draft-two Vader “inherited most of the characteristics of Valorum from the first script, now belonging to the Sith cult, dressing in black and being much more intimidating. He also wears a breath mask in one scene, and serves Prince Espaa Valorum, who is mentioned but never seen.” Combined Vader+Valorum as all-purpose villain; Hoedaack would become Tarkin; Emperor no longer featured. Vader’s presence is “surprisingly little”: bold boarding (almost identical to the final film), lightsaber vs Deak, then not seen until he pilots a craft at the end **and is destroyed**. “the Emperor has many Sith Lords in his service… and in fact we briefly meet some of them” (also in the third draft).

The father, known only as “The Starkiller,” “shares more in common with Yoda and Ben Kenobi”—wrinkled, grey-haired, said to be hundreds of years old, strongest Force user, revealed in the last act.

Death Star / Alderaan middle: *Where Eagles Dare* parallels (Kaminski hedges coincidence). Climactic dogfight: *633 Squadron* and *The Dam Busters* (n.201); clips used as placeholders in the rough cut. Lucas (n.203): “Every time there was a war movie on television, like Bridges of Toko-Ri, I would watch it.” Rinzler: notes on *Ben-Hur* and Ford’s *They Were Expendable* (n.204). Lucas on Hidden Fortress POV (footnote, *Annotated Screenplays* p. 10): “The part that was the most interesting in Hidden Fortress was that it was told from the point of view of the farmers, and not from the point of view of the princess.”

Printed Owen/Luke/Beru farm dialog, pp. 92–93: Luke has never been past the planet; mother already dead; Deak in Alderaan dungeons; father needs the Kiber Crystal; Owen has “trained seven of your father’s sons”; Luke not the most gifted, would rather “carve a jud stone, or work on your catalog of the ancients.”

**Lucas on Luke and Han (n.205), typography locked:** “I was dealing with two opposites, and these are the two opposites in myself—a naïve, innocent idealism and a view of the world that is cynical, more pessimistic. My starting point was the idea of an innocent who becomes cynical. Should Luke be a brash young kid or an intellectual? Should he be a she? At one point, I was going to have a girl at the center. Luke Skywalker might never have been; he might have been a heroine… [Han] came out of Luke…as the opposite of Luke. Han Solo evolved from my wanting to have a cynical foil for the innocent Luke. A lot of the characters came out of Luke because Luke had many aspects. So I took certain aspects of the composite Luke and put them into other characters.” In this draft Han “does not doubt the Force… and even feels it himself.”

Lucas on character evolution (n.206): original idea “an old man and a kid… In the original script the old man was the hero. I wanted to have a seventy-five-year-old Clint Eastwood… Then I wrote another script without the old man… Then I had a story about the kid and his brother, where the kid developed—and a pirate character developed out of the brother [second draft]… pretty soon they got to be the dirty half dozen they are today.”

Episode concept first appears: “Episode One” in *The Adventures of The Starkiller*; dropped in draft three; back in the final draft; eliminated from the 1977 film; reinstated after *Empire*, renumbered Episode IV. End-title teaser printed p. 94: a thousand new systems join the rebellion; “the most daring of which was the kidnapping of the Lars family, and the perilous search for: ‘The Princess of Ondos.’” Kaminski: Lucas was planning a never-made sequel quest for a princess.

## Republic history, Darklighter, Nixon/Palpatine (pp. 94–97)

First draft: only a once-benign Empire, no Republic. Second draft: first significant prequel-background step; rise of the Emperor based on Rome, Germany, France, and Nixon. Lucas (n.207): “The political issues have to deal with democracies that give their countries over to a dictator because of a crisis of some kind… soon after Nixon’s presidency… he suggested that they change a constitutional amendment so that he could run for a third term… ‘If the military will back me, I’ll stay in office.’ … An emergency in the Republic leads the Senate to make Palpatine, essentially, ‘dictator for life.’ ”

Long Luke-to-Biggs-and-Windy history scene printed pp. 95–96: in another time, “a holy man called the Skywalker became aware of a powerful energy field”; Force of Others has two halves, “Ashla, the good, and Bogan, the paraforce or evil part”; secret entrusted only to twelve children, the Jedi Bendu of the Ashla; Great Senate corrupted by Power and Transport guilds; Jedi denounced as traitors; race wars, terrorists, police state; “During one of his lessons a young PADAWAN-JEDI, a boy named Darklighter, came to know the evil half of the force… ran away from his instructor and taught the evil ways of the Bogan Force to a clan of Sith pirates,” who became the Emperor’s bodyguards. “Our father is a Jedi. He is called ‘The Starkiller’.”

**Kaminski, p. 97:** “the notion of a Jedi succumbing to the darkside (the Bogan) is introduced. In this case it is part of the distant-past, tracing the beginnings of the Sith… This history would be transferred over to Darth Vader for the next draft… marking one of the final transformations into the character we see in the film.” First draft in which the Force is an actual power one can harness; religious supernaturalism and super powers “are not introduced until this second draft.”

## McQuarrie designs the mask; Lucas’s 1977 “volcano” explanation is retroactive (pp. 97–101)

**Ralph McQuarrie** commissioned around the second draft; first completed colour rendering is Vader vs Deak in the opening. Lucas, summer 1977, “very first public reveal” of the suit explanation (n.208): “It was a whole part of the plot that essentially got cut out…Vader kills Luke’s father, then Ben and Vader have a confrontation, just like they have in Star Wars, and Ben almost kills Vader. As a matter of fact, he falls into a volcanic pit and gets fried and is one destroyed being. That's why he has to wear the suit with a mask, because it's a breathing mask. It's like a walking iron lung.”

**Kaminski rebuttal, typography locked, pp. 97–98:** “By the time August 1977 rolled around, when the above comment was made, Lucas had invented the backstory involving ‘The Duel’… but this story was made to retroactively fit in with Vader’s already-invented appearance. The proof? At the time Vader was designed, breathing mask and all, Obi Wan didn’t exist! And in fact, Vader did not kill Father Skywalker because Father Skywalker—or The Starkiller as he was known in the second draft—was alive and well within the script, and survives to the end (unlike Vader, who perishes). Vader at this point was not a former Jedi, and had no history. The backstory of The Duel apparently did not come about until after the fourth draft was written.” Original reason for the mask: to travel through space between a Star Destroyer and a Rebel ship blown open to vacuum. Deak also has a mask in the McQuarrie painting. Draft-one Darth Vader wore no mask (that draft had no boarding opening); a Sith in draft one did, and “this specific design would be incorporated into the Tusken Raiders.”

**McQuarrie (n.209):** “George asked me to create a guy with a cape that fluttered in the wind, with a wide brim helmet like the headgear of medieval samurai… Early in the script there was a description of Vader crossing between two ships in space so I created this mask so he could breathe in space, with a suggestion of teeth in the mask’s grill work. George loved it.” Sketching began November 1974, while Lucas was still writing (n.210). McQuarrie elaborates (n.211): Lucas brought 1930s SF magazines and books on Japanese medieval armour; helmet “somewhere between that and a German World War II helmet. In probably one day, I made all the drawings that pretty much defined Darth Vader… George didn’t envision Darth Vader with a mask—he said he might have his face covered with black silk. But I got worried for Vader’s health, because he has to transfer to another spacecraft through outer space with stormtroopers who had armored space suits… George said, ‘Well, all right, give him some kind of breath mask,’—which he wore through all three films.”

Second-draft entrance description printed p. 98: “an awesome, seven-foot BLACK KNIGHT OF THE SITH… LORD DARTH VADER, right hand to the MASTER OF THE SITH. His sinister face is partially obscured by his flowing black robes and grotesque breath mask…” Earliest McQuarrie pencils: ordinary man, no helmet, average-length hair, slight build, futuristic gas mask, no electronics, no mechanical limbs. Intermediate Bedouin/epee stage; McQuarrie added armour (n.212). McQuarrie (n.213): “The space suits began as being necessary for [Vader and the stormtroopers’] survival in space, but the suits became part of their character.”

Speculative visual sources, pp. 99–100, flagged as Kaminski’s: Kirby *New Gods* Darkseid (“darkside”), Orion discovers Darkseid is his father, “The Source”; Doctor Doom (scarred, iron suit, cape) — “Supposedly Lucas himself has admitted the influence, though I am ignorant to such a reference”; “The Lightning” from Republic’s 1939 *Fighting Devil Dogs*. “it may be surmised… that these three characters were not necessarily deliberately copied… On the other hand,” Lucas gave McQuarrie comics and 1930s pulp (n.214–215).

**Dale Pollock** error flagged: Pollock says Lucas contacted McQuarrie after the third draft and that a painting is “the lightsaber duel between Ben and Vader”; Lucasfilm later describes it as Luke and Vader (n.216). Kaminski: the painting is “very clearly Vader and Deak,” proven by Rinzler.

## Friends’ notes, Campbell, heroine experiment, third-draft outline (pp. 101–107)

**Willard Huyck** (n.217): “We’d say, ‘George, this character doesn’t work,’ and George would go ‘Uh-huh’ and make a note, and then fly home.” Lucas, 1974, on Coppola collaboration (n.218): “Half the time he says I’m full of shit, and half the time I say he’s full of shit… he calls me a stinky kid.” **Coppola** (n.219): “My recollection was that George had a whole Star Wars script that I thought was fine, and then he chucked it and started again with these two robots lost in the wilderness.” **Hal Barwood** (n.220): “It started off in horrible shape. It was difficult to discern there was a movie in there… it was very hard for us to wrap our heads around the idea of a golden robot and this little beer can… But George never gave up.”

**Gary Kurtz** claims the more simplified, mystical Force from draft three was due to his recommendation (n.221): “Anybody who read those drafts… said, ‘What are you doing here? This is absolutely gobbledegook’… Comparative religion is one of the things I studied in university… I also studied the Buddhist and Hindu sects, and studied Zen and Tibetan Buddhism, and also Native American spirituality; shamanistic methods and so on. I got out a lot of my old books, and we talked about it.”

Joseph Campbell “rediscovered” before draft three is, Kaminski says, “quite exaggerated… even by Lucas himself.” “much of the similarity… is due to coincidental alignment” (n.222). Lucas (n.223): “Yes, I’ve done a fair amount of reading, but a lot of Star Wars just came intuitively… If I ever consciously used anything that I read, it was to make the story more consistent with traditional fairy tales… if there was a part in which Luke had two trials, I would try to make it three… But if adding a third trial jeopardized the story, I wouldn’t do it. I can’t give any specific examples.” Draft two, Kaminski argues, is in some ways closer to Campbell’s pattern (call, refusal, supernatural aid, threshold, underworld, initiation, atonement with the father). More accurate 1970s Lucas (n.224): “I spent about a year reading lots of fairy tales—and that’s when it starts to move away from Kurosawa and towards Joe Campbell… About the time I was doing the third draft I read The Hero with a Thousand Faces, and I started to realize that I was following those rules unconsciously. So I said, I’ll make it fit more into that classic mold.”

Heroine experiment (n.225–226): “The original treatment was about a princess and an old man… the second draft really didn’t have any girls in it at all. I was very disturbed about that… at one point Luke was a girl. I just changed the main character from a guy to a girl.” Much production artwork with this conception; then Deak replaced by the princess and Luke returned to male. Siblings stripped; Owen transformed from gentle father figure to cruel brute; Anchorhead garage scenes, closer to Modesto.

May 1st, 1975 six-page synopsis sent to **Alan Ladd Jr.**, titled “The Adventures of Luke Starkiller. Episode I: The Star Wars” — not “Episode four” (Pollock error; *Annotated Screenplays* and Rinzler corroborate Episode I) (n.227–230). Sample: “An engaging human drama set in a fantasy world that paralyses the imagination…” Princess returns. Separate undated four-page notes, a “missing link” (Rinzler, n.231). Rinzler summary of this third-draft outline (n.232): Owen strikes Luke, Beru protects him; Luke kisses sleeping aunt and leaves; picks up a “poor old man” who talks about Jedi adventures, will train him “for food”; “Old man can do magic, read minds, talk to things like Don Juan” — **this is the Castaneda/Don Juan link flagged in Ch. II.** Cantina; “Bugs molest Luke… Old man cuts them down.” “She’s a tough babe; doesn’t appreciate their help—a trap? Han punches her in the face and Chewbacca carries her out.” Old man gets the Kiber crystal; Sith “become ill,” wound the old man. Vader “let’s them go.” Yavin; “Luke lands on the Death Star with the bomb—‘runs into Vader; sword fight—Han to the rescue… Make Luke a Jedi knight.”

Notes (n.233): “Sith rips rebel’s arm off… only seven Sith—one in each sector…” **Matthew Robbins** and **Hal Barwood** wanted Luke introduced earlier; Lucas (n.234): “Francis had read the script… Steven Spielberg had read the script. All of my friends had read the script… Matt and Hal though the first half hour of the film would be better if Luke was intercut with the robots, so I did that.” Note: “combine Han and Biggs,” never used (or, Kaminski, perhaps in the growing Luke/Han camaraderie).

Old man becomes Ben Kenobi: Father Skywalker’s commanding officer; father told Luke as a boy to go to him if anything happened; next draft would make them equals who grew up together. Force-learning stages and “man of the Force” “suggest an influence of Castaneda’s Don Juan” (n.235). Lucas drops the surface-of-the-Death-Star bomb / Vader swordfight ending (n.236): “in trying to intercut the dogfight with the old-fashioned swordfight, I realised that the film would have stopped dead. It was too risky.”

*Apocalypse Now* temptation: Coppola ready to finance; Lucas had four years in *Apocalypse*, two in *Star Wars*; chooses *Star Wars* (n.237). Graffiti fan mail; *Apocalypse* “very much like THX,” *Star Wars* “very much like American Graffiti”; using Coppola’s sons aged eleven and eight as models.

## Third draft, August 1st, 1975: Ben Kenobi, dead father, Vader as fallen student (pp. 107–116)

Titled “The Star Wars. From the Adventures of Luke Starkiller.” Helander summary (n.238) locked in substance: Vader is “a Sith Lord and right hand of the Emperor”; Leia Organa captured; Luke Starkiller on Utapau; Biggs Darklighter now a friend, not a brother; Luke wishes “his late Jedi father were there”; General Ben Kenobi, “proving his point by angrily cracking open his artificial arm”; “little Force left in him”; Organa Major destroyed; Alderaan is the Imperial city; Ben retrieves a Kiber Crystal, duels Vader, slams a blast door, escapes (not killed); Han leaves; Luke flies with the crystal; Han returns, Vader collides with his wing man and “spins out of control”; Luke hits the exhaust port; gold medallions.

Departures: more humor; Han a cynical foil; Owen crueller, spends Luke’s academy savings on droids; father’s name was Annikin; still Starkillers not Skywalkers. **“Most significantly, Father Skywalker is now dead and gone, and Ben Kenobi is introduced for the very first time.”** Luke knew his Jedi father; Owen raised him after the father was “killed in battle.” Fallen-student plot appears: draft-two Darklighter history attached to Vader; Vader a once-disciple of Kenobi. Ben: one of his disciples took a Kiber crystal at the battle of Condawn and became a Sith Lord — later revealed as Vader. Luke says his father was also killed at Condawn, “but there doesn’t seem to be any connection between Ben’s disciple and Father Skywalker’s death, evidently being a combat fatality.” Many Sith, so a Sith killer “potentially could have been any of them.” Clone Wars referenced “for the very first time”; unclear whether Condawn is part of them. Footnote: Rinzler reports a private December 1975 conversation (Making of p. 107) in which Lucas “neglects any mention of Vader killing Skywalker, only that Skywalker is killed in a big battle.” See Appendix G.

Lucas (n.239): “It isn’t really until it evolved into what is close to what Star Wars now is that I began to go back and deal with the stories that evolved to get us to that point.” Backstory notes “about seven or eight pages” by the final draft (n.240–242): “I never really intended it to be turned into a movie… just a little backstory of what the Jedi were like, and what the Republic was like, and what the relationship of Obi Wan and Darth Vader was…and the Emperor; ‘what is the Empire?’”

Father pushed further into obscurity: major in draft one, cameo in draft two, dead before draft three, “replaced with Ben Kenobi.” “this would become a problem for Lucas when crafting the sequel, when Father Skywalker was re-introduced and the two characters revealed to be essentially one and the same.” Ben fused from General Luke Skywalker, Kane Starkiller, and The Starkiller of draft two; “uber-father… with a slightly more whimsical touch in the vein of wizards Merlin or Gandalf.” Footnote: Lucas would kill Ben in revised draft four for the same “serves no purpose” reason. Clone Wars: Kenobi keeps a “journal of the Clone Wars”; “it seems Lucas himself had no specific idea of what they were other than a famous long-ago war akin to World War II”; Kenobi once “The commander of the White Legions.” Ben/Luke introduction dialog printed pp. 111–112 (“I’m Luke Starkiller, guardian of the Bendu… You’re General Kenobi?!? The Jedi knight! The commander of the White Legions?”).

*The Searchers* unconscious influence: Kenobi // Ethan Edwards; neighbour named Lars; later explicit mimicry in draft four when Luke returns to the ravaged farm. Footnote coincidence: Martin/Ethan search for adopted sister Debbie // Leia later made Luke’s sister in 1981; Scar massacred the family // Vader killed the father and the Larses.

Ben’s Force explanation printed pp. 113–114: “an energy field in oneself, a power that controls ones acts, yet obeys ones commands… All living things generate this Force field”; stored in a Jedi, collected/transmitted through a Kiber crystal; crystal taken at Condawn; “One of my disciple’s [sic] took the crystal and became a Sith Lord”; Sith use the Bogan Force; “The Force is on your right, the Bogan is on your left… The Crystal Darth stole was the last one in the possession of the Jedi.” **“Of note is Kenobi’s above reference to ‘Darth,’ illuminating the forgotten aspect of the film that Darth was Vader’s literal first name and not a title or rank.”** Artificial limbs now on Ben (and previously Montross). Vader still wears the breath mask in the opening; “much crueller and sinister… even seen drinking from a flask—this curious incident indicates that, once again, he only wears his mask for the opening sequence, or at least has removed it for this particular scene.” Faces Kenobi, Kenobi escapes unkilled; Vader survives the Death Star, “his ship limping off into space to fight another day.”

**Kaminski thesis restated, typography locked, p. 114:** “it is abundantly clear that Luke’s father is his own character. Darth Vader is definitely not the father of Luke Skywalker, and nowhere is anything like that even remotely implied… From the very beginning, Vader has remained as a henchman of the Empire, an enforcer, a lackey, albeit a powerful one from the second draft onward… Darth Vader began this way and in the final film of Star Wars he remains as he always did.” “Luke’s father was never meant to be anyone sinister.” Condawn / “dawn” may have later inspired the volcanic setting; first-synopsis General Skywalker thrown off a thousand-foot cliff into a boiling lake (grabs a vine). First hints of a series: Luke to be developed as a Jedi, Vader limps away; contracts fought for shortly after this draft (n.243).

## Fourth draft and revised fourth: “murdered by a young Jedi, Darth Vader”; The Duel; Burtt’s iron lung (pp. 116–130)

Notes for the next draft (n.244): “Change Death Star name… Luke doesn’t know father Jedi; Ben tells Luke about father.” December 29th, 1975 conversation with **Alan Dean Foster** (n.245): “I’m thinking I’m taking the Kiber Crystal out. I thought it really distracted from Luke and Vader; it made them seem too much like supermen… I’m dealing with the Force a little more subtly now… I could write a whole movie just about the Force of Others… Vader runs off in the end, shaking his fist: ‘I’ll get you yet!’ In a one-to-one fight Vader could probably destroy Luke… I’m going to have [Luke’s] father leave him his laser sword… [Luke’s] uncle isn’t a son of a bitch anymore; because he gets killed, I have to make him more sympathetic, so you’ll hate the Empire.”

December 17th, 1975, to **Charles Lippincott**, just days after Fox green-lit the film (n.246): “Each story was a totally different story about totally different characters before I finally landed on the story… Now what I’m doing in this rewrite is I’m slowly shaving down the plot… After that, I’ll go through and do another rewrite, which will develop characters and dialog.”

Fourth draft, January 1st, 1976, titled “The Adventures of Luke Starkiller as taken from the Journal of the Whills. Saga I: Star Wars.” Additional Sith cut; Vader now their “single representative”; Kiber gone; Death Star the all-purpose threat (Rinzler: budget cuts, n. footnote p. 117; Jan 10, 1976 Lucas/Kurtz meeting describes an alternate Death Star escape). **“Luke now discovers that his father was once a Jedi—but there is still no mention of Vader killing him.”** Sixteen-year-old Leia the center of a mild Luke/Han love triangle. Force of Others now only “the Force,” generalised, mystical. Late December 1975 (n.247): “I put this little thing on it: ‘A long time ago in a galaxy far, far away, an incredible adventure took place.’ Basically, it’s a fairy tale now.”

**Gloria Katz** and **Willard Huyck** polish in England; “about thirty percent of the dialog by Lucas’ estimate” (n.248–249): “I felt it could be improved, so I had Bill and Gloria help me come up with some snappy one-liners.” Sassier Leia; Han blows away an alien bounty hunter; Ben now formerly “Obi Wan,” so Luke doesn’t realise Leia’s Obi Wan is old Ben. Extension of the hut scene: not only was the father a Jedi, **“He was betrayed and murdered…by a young Jedi, Darth Vader.”** (n.250)

A month into shooting, “Starkiller” changed to “Skywalker” (n.251). Lucas (n.252): “When Charles Manson was in the news, people who knew about the name Starkiller started asking, ‘Are you making a movie about mass murderers or something?’ I said ‘Okay, I won’t use that name.’ ” Also during production: Vader kills Kenobi; disembodied voice aids Luke. Lucas (n.253): revised fourth draft started in London preproduction, continued in Tunisia, finished back in England.

pp. 118–120: Kenobi as the character who “ties the story together.” Kaminski’s reconstruction of the 1977 backstory (Father lived on Tatooine with Ben; Owen is his brother; Clone Wars; Vader a student who fell, hunted Jedi, **murdered Father Skywalker**; Owen resents Ben, lies that the father was “a boring old navigator on a spice freighter”). Owen/Beru/Kenobi lines used as evidence: “didn’t hold with your father’s ideals, thought he should have stayed here and not gotten involved”; “Luke’s just not a farmer, Owen, he has too much of his father in him” / “That’s what I’m afraid of”; third draft more explicit: “That boy’s going to get himself killed; he’s just like his father.” Port of Lucas leaving Modesto against his fearful father.

**Kaminski, typography locked, p. 120:** “just in case you have not been paying attention, Darth Vader was not the father of Luke. Nor was Princess Leia his sister. This is especially ironic, seeing as nowadays, with the ‘Saga,’ this is what the entire story is about… not only was it not part of the original scripts, it was never even part of the original film itself!” Private notes and conversations through 1975–76 and “well into 1977” still treat the characters as separate. 1977 private conversation (n.254), typography locked: “When the Jedi tried to restore order, Darth Vader was still one of the Jedi. What he would do is catch the Jedi off-guard and, using his knowledge of the Force, he would kill the Jedi without them realizing what was happening. They trusted him and they didn't realize he was the murderer who was decimating their ranks. At the height of the Jedi, there were several hundred thousand. At the time of the Rebellion, most of them were killed… Eventually, only a few, including Ben and Luke's father, were left. Luke's father is named Annikin.”

Retcon discussion, pp. 121–123: Guinness’s pause in the hut “now reads as him pausing to make up a lie,” but “it was never the original intention.” Novelisation (n.255): “although Uncle Owen could lie to Luke, it was something the noble and truthful Ben could not bring himself to do.” “These types of retroactive changes… are known in fan communities as ‘retcon.’” They “make Kenobi into a lying manipulator… while Vader becomes more humanized.” Kenobi calls him “Darth,” not “Vader,” twice in the Death Star duel (“Only a master of evil, Darth”; “You can't win, Darth”). “Back in the days of the film’s release, everyone referred to him as Darth, such as Carrie Fisher in The Making of Star Wars 1977 television special, as it was obviously his first name.” Fuller examination deferred to “the appendix” (Appendix C). Lucas, in a production meeting with cameraman **Gil Taylor**, refers to the scene merely as “the final battle between Ben and the warlord” (n.256). Vader: “When I left you, I was but the learner. Now, I am the master.” vs Kenobi: “I was a Jedi knight, the same as your father” — if they are the same person, “direct contradiction” (young student vs older knight).

pp. 123–125: no hint in the 1977 film itself of a prior Obi-Wan/Vader duel. “We meet again, at last” need not mean a rematch. Kenobi is silent to Luke about any volcano. Draft three: Vader “merely took the crystal and joined the Sith.” Draft two Darklighter “ran away from his instructor.” Draft-three Vader drinks from a flask; mask not yet a permanent fixture. Second-draft note: Sith faces “like Linda Blair from The Exorcist” (n.258). Permanent mask in the finished film “purely coincidental” in the sense of not being prompted by a life-support backstory; possible in-world reading as intimidation, like stormtroopers, later echoed in *Willow*’s General Kael.

**The Duel invented after the fourth draft, told to Mark Hamill on set in 1976,** initially a scarring, not a near-fatal iron-lung event: Obi-Wan and Vader fight, Vader falls into a volcanic pit, emerges intact but hideously scarred, must wear mask and full-body suit “much like Doctor Doom.” Genesis “probably… somewhere between January, when the initial fourth draft was written, and March, when filming commenced.” **Hamill,** 1980 (n.260): “I remember very early on asking who my parents were and being told that my father and Obi-Wan met Vader on the edge of a volcano and they had a duel. My father and Darth Vader fell into the crater and my father was instantly killed. Vader crawled out horribly scarred, and at that point the Emperor landed and Obi-Wan ran into the forest, never to be seen again.” Kaminski: Hamill’s version is “somewhat unique” (father and Vader fall together; Emperor scares Obi-Wan “into the forest”); maybe variations, or foggy memory. Later comments: after the purge, Obi-Wan and Father were the last Jedi, Vader hunting survivors (n.259).

Lucas, *Rolling Stone,* August 25th, 1977 (n.261) — first public reveal, already quoted in shorter form at p. 97 — adds: Ben **Burtt** “did about eighteen different kinds of breathing, through aqualungs and through tubes… whether it would be totally rhythmical and like an iron lung… It may be in one of the sequels… I was going to shoot a close-up of Vader where you could see the inside of his face, but then we said, no, no, it would destroy the mystique of the whole thing.” Kaminski: Lucas initially wanted breathing only over dialog, speech filtered through the mask; Burtt added constant iron-lung rhythm in post (n.262). “When Lucas saw how robotic Vader looked… he decided that perhaps Vader wore the suit as a form of life-support and that he was partially re-assembled as a cyborg… Vader might not have truly become mechanical until the final film, until the sound mix.” Mechanical-parts motif had appeared “in every draft except the final one.” **Ben Burtt** (n.263): “The original concept I had of Darth Vader was a very noise-producing individual… breathing like some queezing windmill, you could hear his heart beating, he moved his head [and] you heard motors turning… we sort of had to cut back on that concept. In the first experimental mixes… he sounded like an operating room.”

First official Lucasfilm mention of The Duel, late 1977, *Star Wars Poster Monthly* (n.264): Vader “almost killed by Ben Kenobi, who was understandably enraged at his disciple's fall from grace… during the fight, Vader stumbled backwards and fell into a volcanic pit… What remained of his human body was dragged out and preserved by encasing it in an outsize black metal suit -- virtually a walking iron lung… Only his heavy, rasping breath reveals the suit's true function.”

Closing: Huycks, collaborators (Coppola, Robbins, “Bill Heiken,” Katz, Kaufman, Scorsese, De Palma). 1977 interview (n.266): four completely different plots; “I chucked it and started a new one… That happened four times”; “the script was constructed this way, scene by scene.” Lucas (n.268–269): “This film has been murder… Graffiti, I wrote in three weeks. This one took me three years… I’ve never fully resolved it… I kept simplifying it, and I kept having people read it… But I’m still not happy with the script. I never have been.” Chapter ends: production beginning; “Star Wars had been born.”
