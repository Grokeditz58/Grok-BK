# Kaminski, *The Secret History of Star Wars* (e-book v3.0, 2008): notes — Appendix H: Script and Writing Sources (pp. 526–533)

Fully read. No appendix footnotes. Cite as Kaminski p. N. This is a dated inventory of known drafts, treatments, conferences, and notes, plus some “surmised documents which are indicated as such.” *Annotated Screenplays* lists additional sequence revisions and annotated copies omitted here; Rinzler adds annotated Episode III copies. Notes are included “only those accounted for with documentation or excerption,” “basically only extensively covers Star Wars.” Unique inventory details not already locked in the chapters are listed below; dates already used in I–XIII are restated only where this appendix adds a title, page count, credit, or caveat.

## 1973–1976: *Star Wars* (pp. 526–528)

- **List of characters**, undated, believed circa January 1973, just prior to the *Journal of the Whills*, with the list of planets. “The first name listed is Emperor Ford Xerxes XII. Others include Thorpe, Lars, Kane, Hayden, Mace, Bail and Leila. Lucas then combined names and assigned them roles.”
- **List of planets**, same period: Yavin, Yoshiro, Anchorhead, Starbuck, Kissel.
- **Journal of the Whills**, undated two-page handwritten plot summary, C.J. Thorpe and Mace Windy, begun circa January 1973.
- **The Star Wars**, treatment May 1973: ten-page handwritten, typed to fourteen, begun in April, completed early May; *Hidden Fortress* remake, General Skywalker protecting “the princess.”
- Undated notes: plot, character, “fascist Galactic Empire and its rebellion.”
- **The Star Wars**, rough draft May 1974: Annikin Starkiller trained by Kane Starkiller and General Luke Skywalker.
- **The Star Wars**, first draft July 1974: “Same as above but with different names.”
- Further undated 1974 notes.
- **Adventures of the Starkiller. Episode I: The Star Wars**, second draft January 28th, 1975: Luke on a farm; father is “The Starkiller”; Kiber crystal; brother Deak.
- Notes, January 30th, 1975 (*Annotated Screenplays*); whether these are the same as Rinzler’s post-third-draft-outline notes “is unknown.”
- **The Adventures of Luke Starkiller (episode one) “The Star Wars”**, second-draft synopsis May 1st, 1975: six pages, typed for Fox executives; princess re-instated.
- **Third draft outline**, circa May 1st, 1975: unique elements “a proto-Kenobi in the form of an ‘Old Man’ on the side of the road who accompanies and instructs Luke,” and “Luke facing Darth Vader in a lightsaber match at the very end.”
- Undated notes, mostly Luke’s arc.
- Kaminski (flag as surmise): around the third draft, background notes that would form the prequel story “became more solidified” (Ben Kenobi; Vader as failed student; possibly Palpatine’s rise); fourth draft adds Vader killing Skywalker; after the fourth, the volcano duel. “The ‘prequel’ treatment is not an actual treatment—rather it is an organized and unorganized collection of the background info… totalling between 7 and 12 pages long, with the shorter estimation being the more likely one.”
- **The Star Wars: From the Adventures of the Starkiller**, third draft August 1st, 1975: Leia reinstated; Ben introduced.
- Undated notes; Lucas “says that, sometime around the third or fourth draft, he also sketched out sequel plot points. My estimation is that it more likely occurred during the writing of the fourth draft.”
- **The Adventures of Luke Starkiller as taken from the Journal of the Whills. Saga I: The Star Wars**, fourth draft January 1st, 1976: other Sith and Kiber crystal eliminated; Vader as father’s killer; Death Star and Alderaan prison combined; Beru and Owen die; “Obi Wan” moniker.
- **Revised fourth draft**, March 15th, 1976: Willard and Gloria Huyck rewrite some dialog; Lucas tweaks during shooting (Starkiller→Skywalker; Ben killed). “In this draft Kenobi says Vader is the killer of Luke’s father.”

## 1977–1982: *Empire* and *Jedi* (pp. 528–530)

- **The Empire Strikes Back**, treatment November 28th, 1977. Page count unknown; a sample in Hearn “reveals it to be quite detailed, and must have spanned two or three dozen pages.”
- Story conferences, Leigh Brackett and Lucas, November 28th–December 2nd, beginning the same day as the treatment, “about a week,” designated “Chapter II,” “tape-recorded and transcribed.”
- Surmise (flag): while Brackett wrote (December–February), Lucas may have brainstormed additional stories; early 1978 twelve-film announcement; Pollock implied concrete writings—“This is not supported by any evidence, however, and must be treated with the appropriate level of conjecture.”
- **Star Wars Sequel**, first draft February 23rd, 1978, Brackett. “Amusingly, Brackett never even titled it, simply scrawling ‘star wars sequel’ on the first page. There doesn’t appear to be any numerical designation.” Ghost of Father Skywalker reveals a sister; Han leaves to search for his father-figure.
- **Episode II: The Empire Strikes Back**, second draft April 1978, Lucas: “The ‘great divide’ of Star Wars where Vader reveals he is Luke’s father.” Han frozen.
- Revised second and third drafts, also April 1978, also Lucas; “No title is ascribed to this draft, according to Annotated Screenplays.”
- Story conferences, Kasdan, Kershner, Kurtz, and Lucas, October 1978, apparently recorded; *Annotated Screenplays* does not account for them. “Pollock places them in November but since Kasdan completed a draft in late October they must have occurred in that month at the very latest.” “Kurtz’ presence seems to be more of a formality”; “according to Kershner he himself concentrated on character, Kasdan on dialog and Lucas on plot.”
- Fourth draft October 1978: *Annotated Screenplays* “strangely does not account for a fourth draft, but states that there are ‘fourth draft revisions dated October 24th, 1978.’ This was the first draft that Lawrence Kasdan wrote.”
- **Star Wars Episode V The Empire Strikes Back**, fifth draft February 20th, 1979, shooting script, “credited to Kasdan and Brackett, although virtually none of Brackett’s script contributions supposedly survive.” Dialog and blocking loose; Kershner improvised. “The version of this screenplay publically available omits many of the original scene configurations in favour for transcriptions of the revised (and, for at least one scene, reshot) versions as per the final edit of the film. A good example is the carbon freezing of Han Solo, which Alan Arnold’s book details was originally scripted very differently.”
- **Star Wars Episode VI: Revenge of the Jedi**, first draft February 20th, 1981, handwritten per *Annotated Screenplays*, not summarised there; “the ‘rough draft’ was completed only days later,” so “merely a rougher version of that.”
- Rough draft February 24th, 1981: “somewhat more straightforward”; Vader “in a more traditional villain role, competing with Jerjerrod and the Emperor for possession of Luke.”
- Revised rough draft June 12th, 1981: Kaminski: think of the “first draft” as a rough, the “rough draft” as its revision, and this as “the real first draft”; “the story is told in parallel from Vader’s perspective as he is turned into a sympathetic character.”
- Story conferences, Kasdan, Marquand, Kazanjian, and Lucas, July 13th–17th, 1981, recorded and transcribed; producer’s inclusion “more as a formality.”
- Second draft September 21st, 1981, Kasdan; credited “story by George Lucas, screenplay by Lawrence Kasdan.” “elimination of Had Abbadon, the return of Vader to a more villainous role and the instatement of Dagobah.”
- **Star Wars Episode VI: Return of the Jedi**, revised second draft November 1st, 1981, screenplay credited to both; *Annotated Screenplays* describes handwritten notes resulting in another “Revised second draft” dated November 11th, “with these drafts being titled Return of the Jedi.”
- Third draft December 1st, 1981, Kasdan and Lucas.
- Notes, December 15th, 1981, “Dubbed the ‘wookie doodle pad,’” miscellaneous handwritten notes by Kasdan, “apparently with regards to Han Solo.”
- *Annotated Screenplays* also lists “Lucas revisions dated January 4th, 1982.” Shooting shortly thereafter.

## 1993–2005: prequels (pp. 530–533)

- Prequel expansion notes and outlines, circa 1993, supplementing notes accumulated by *Jedi*; “By the next year they are collected in a red binder and organized by subject, such as Jedi, Empire and Outline.” Preliminary interest since 1992; screenplay 1994; 1993 “a good estimation” and the year production was announced.
- **The Beginning**, rough draft January 13th, 1995, begun November 1st, 1994. Obi-Wan thrust into the invasion of Utapau, discovers slave-child Anakin, vows to train him. Revised rough and typed first draft also attributed to January 13th, 1995; “Presumably, these three are virtually identical.”
- Second draft 1995?: replaces Obi-Wan’s lead with Qui-Gon, demotes Kenobi, introduces midichlorians. “Quotes from Lucas indicate that by late summer 1995 he still had not completed a second draft, but I estimate that a late 1995 date is appropriate.” Possible early-1996 revision; *Making of Episode I*: screenplay “more or less” complete by 1996, when pre-production went into full swing.
- Third draft May 13th, 1997; revised third June 6th, 1997, the shooting script. “It is unknown when the change occurred to ‘Phantom Menace.’ It may well have been not until 1999, and the 1998 rough cut is still called The Beginning.”
- Additional scenes August 1998 and three more periods in the next seven months, “including one where Palpatine notices young Anakin.”
- **Episode II: Jar Jar’s Great Adventure**, rough draft March 13th, 2000. “The title, of course, is meant to be a joke, since Lucas had not come up with one yet.” Lucas says he had “quickly completed many drafts before this”; **Doug Chiang** remembers a draft in January. “the wedding of Anakin and Padme occurred mid-way through the story and was a larger focal point.”
- Story conferences, Lucas and Jonathan Hales, circa June, “roughly a day and a half, with most of it being Lucas explaining the story and his intentions.”
- First draft? circa June 2000: Hales supplements; Lucas phones with recommendations. Second draft? circa June: Hales revises; Lucas then revises the shooting script himself. Third draft (shooting script) circa June, Lucas and Hales, “completed roughly a week before the June 26th principal photography start.”
- Additional scenes March–April and October–November 2001, “notably introducing the concept that Dooku was Qui Gon’s master.”
- Episode III preliminary outline, August 2002, on vacation in Canada; story “too full,” must reconfigure.
- List of planets, October 25th, 2002, “more for the art department,” thirteen planets with brief descriptions.
- Revised Episode III outline, circa December 2002–January 2003, three acts broken into major sequences; “the structure that would be utilised in the screenplays.”
- **Star Wars Episode III: Revenge of the Sith**, rough draft January 31st, 2003, begun in December, “a highly simplified script or a very detailed outline, being only 55 pages.”
- First draft April 10th, 2003, 110 pages: “removes the child Han Solo as well as Sidious’ claim that he is Anakin’s father, adding the tale of Darth Plagueis and Yoda’s confrontation with the Emperor.”
- Second draft circa June 2003; revised second June 13th, 2003, “Notable for the change in Anakin’s dream that Padme dies in childbirth.”
- Third draft circa June 2003; fourth draft June 26th, 2003, 129 pages, days before filming, “Adds additional dialog bits, including Kenobi’s ‘you were the chosen one!’ monologue.”
- Principal photography June 30th, 2003; onset, Lucas tells Christensen he rewrote the turn “to make it more drawn out”; Plagueis tale moved from Palpatine’s office to an opera house.
- Post-production “massive story changes… rewriting Anakin’s character so that he stays loyal to the Jedi and is driven to the darkside out of his obsession for Padme,” “primarily through additional scenes shot in pick ups in August 2004 and January 2005.”
