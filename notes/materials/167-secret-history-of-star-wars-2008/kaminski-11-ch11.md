# Kaminski, *The Secret History of Star Wars* (e-book v3.0, 2008): notes — Chapter XI: The Madness (pp. 336–353)

Fully read. Citations: Kaminski p. N; Kaminski Notes n. Y (Ch. XI starts at n.743). Kaminski glosses flagged, especially the Rotten Tomatoes critique and the autobiographical Lucas/Anakin/Luke thesis. Staff/crew/cast labeled. *Phantom Menace* production, teaser mania, reception, Jar Jar, and Lucas’s “I have become the very thing that I was trying to avoid.”

## Shoot, teaser, and the turn from hype to sour (pp. 336–340)

Leavesden Studios, then Tunisia (again a sandstorm destroyed sets), then Italy; wrapped after only nine weeks. Heavy bluescreen; senate “nearly all bluescreen”; practical sets often built only to the actors’ heads. McCallum: Liam Neeson’s 6'4" “cost the production many thousands of dollars.” Tight security; grainy leaks; rumours Neeson was playing Anakin’s father; inaccurate “out of focus” reports (focus-puller **Graham Hall**).

November 1998 teaser: “Every generation has a legend… Every journey has a first step…” Earlier that summer *Austin Powers 2* had spoofed the anticipation. Theaters reported “as much as half of movie audiences leaving after the trailer”; downloaded “over a million times, an astounding feat in 1998”; midnight toy-store openings; lines from April; CNN: national cost of skipped work “would be $293 million” (n.743). “A level of hype… that had never been seen before, ever, and has not been repeated since.”

Pre-release audience reviews “optimistic” (n.744). **Richard Corliss**, *Time*, late April (n.745): “intimations of something fresh, handsome, grand”; Jar Jar “a vexing, endearing mix of Kipling's Gunga Din and Tolkien's Gollum”; “there is reason to give Episode 1 the subtitle… A New Hope.” (He reversed himself after seeing the film, “less than a month later.”) **Roger Ebert**: “If it were the first ‘Star Wars’ movie, ‘The Phantom Menace’ would be hailed as a visionary breakthrough… We are standing at the threshold of a new age of epic cinema.” May 16th appraisal of Jar Jar (n.746): “I am delighted.”

“Worst reviews were among the earliest” (n.747). After the May 19th release, older fans “unsurprisingly disappointed”; “ultimately deemed ‘so-so’” (n.748). Lucas labelled “a bloated, soulless technocrat.” Ethnic-minority boycott allegations (Neimodians Asian, Watto Arab, Jar Jar Rastafarian); “even homosexual groups went after Jar Jar” (n.749). Anti-JarJar sites (Jarjarbinksmustdie.com); online “bashers” vs “gushers.” Over $400 million domestically, “the single highest-grossing release in the entire Star Wars series,” but “utterly perplexed by the averageness,” many did not return, “hence leading to Episode II’s box office gross to be massively smaller.”

**Walter Murch**, *Wired*, 2005 (n.750): “For me, those films pummel you into submission… If you told me a 14-year-old had done them on his home computer, I would get very excited, but if you tell me it's George Lucas– with all of the resources available to him– I know it’s amazing, but I don’t feel it’s amazing. I think if George were here and we could wrestle him onto the carpet, he’d say, ‘Yeah, I’ve gotten into that box, and now I want to get out of that box.’” **Ewan McGregor**, 2006 (n.751): “The thing about Star Wars for me… is that I’ll always hold the first three in the 70’s with just huge regard, and the one’s I did—as the one’s I did.”

**Frank Darabont**, 2000 (n.752), typography locked: asked if the script he read is what hit the screen, and whether he told Lucas “I wouldn't change a single damn word”: “It is very much the movie that hit the screen. I still like it very much. I find the backlash perplexing… To hear some people tell it, it's like crapping on the Mona Lisa. But this has more to do with people's expectations… No film could possibly have pleased them… George Lucas is capable of many, many things. But making you feel like that wonder-struck eight-year-old again isn't one of them. It can't be done… In terms of its intelligence and its approach, I'd put Menace up there with Empire Strikes Back… But when you go in expecting the greatest movie ever made, you're in for a letdown, no matter how good the movie is.”

## Jar Jar, midichlorians quietly dropped, reviews vs memory (pp. 340–349)

Kaminski: 1977 also had recoil—women and minority roles, “wooden dialog,” **Harlan Ellison** “venomous diatribes” in *Starlog*, “refuted by… Mark Hamill,” an “ongoing feud.” Those critics “a small minority.” Jar Jar accusations (dreadlocks, “yousa”/“messa,” “Jah,” Black actor **Ahmed Best**). Lucas “an avid fan of silent film”; first-day writing documentary: he watches a silent train-top comedy and says “How?… How do they do that?” Best (n.753): “The major influence for Jar Jar was a lot of Buster Keaton… for me it was Bugs Bunny… there were a couple of Buster Keaton movies that we were inspired by… especially in the army scene.” Kaminski also discusses **Stepin Fetchit** and Columbia’s **Michael Dyson** (n.754); “it’s unlikely that Lucas would deliberately design Jar Jar to emulate a racial stereotype,” but speech patterns (“mesa,” “yousa,” “mui mui,” “Jah,” “bombad”) and “mesa your humble servant” “implements some degree of design.” Flag this as Kaminski’s argument. Other Gungans (Tarpals, Boss Nass) share mannerisms “yet [do not] evoke as strong racial comparisons.” Lucas, BBC *Newsnite*, July 14th, 1999 (n.755–756): “How in the world you could take an orange amphibian and say that he’s a Jamaican? It’s completely absurd.” Accusations “started out as a way of selling newspapers.” “now with the internet, the American press uses the internet as their source… some of the critics would described him as a kind of comic sidekick Steppin Fetchit character [and then] they say ‘Oh, its racist.’… the head Jedi is an African-American. Nobody even mentions that… [Jar Jar] speaks more like Yoda than anything else.”

Aftermath, Kaminski: Jar Jar, who “had more screentime than Obi Wan” in Episode I, “reduced to an ancillary character in Episode II, and was practically absent from the third”; Gungans “never heard from again.” Midichlorians “not to be made mention of again, except in passing in Episode III.” Episode II “cater[s] specifically to fans”—Boba Fett, homestead, Jedi battles. Design meeting on CG Yoda (n.757), typography locked: “The secret is making [the new CG Yoda] look like a rubber puppet. The problem we got to last time [on Phantom Menace] was that when they made the upgrade it didn’t look like the [original trilogy] rubber puppet anymore. So then everybody complained that he didn’t look real. But they were saying ‘Well he looks better than he did before.’ Yeah they made him better but people don’t like that, they want him look like he did in the first film.”

Rotten Tomatoes post-*Sith* experiment quoted in full (n.758): original-release scores SW 79, Empire 52, Jedi 31, average 54; prequels RotS 83, AotC 65, TPM 62, average 70—“the Prequels are actually better reviewed by 16%.” **Kaminski rejects the comparison as slanted** (web sources vs *Newsweek*/*New Yorker*/*Sun-Times*). Using RT’s “top critics”: TPM 40%, AotC 37%, RotS 68%, trilogy average 48%—“better reflects the public’s collective memory.” Footnote: Kaminski’s “own study of reviews from original release rates them much higher than Rotten Tomatoes’, especially the two sequels.”

Lucas on two fan bases (n.759): “One is over 25 and one is under 25. The over 25 fan base is loyal to the first three films and they are actually in their 30's and 40's now, so that they're in control of the media, they're in control of the web… The films, which those people don't like, which are the [prequels], actually are fanatically bored by the other two… the devotion for each group is pretty equal.” (Kaminski’s transcription is slightly tangled; lock the sense, not a cleaned rewrite.)

SE theatrical: *Star Wars* $138M, *Empire* $67M, *Jedi* $45M—“the two sequels combined couldn’t equal what Star Wars still managed.” Kaminski: *Empire* later re-appraised in the 1990s as equal or superior (n.760); *Jedi* “tolerated” as *Empire*’s second half (n.761). *Empire* “a sort of accident… achieved their depth in spite of Lucas, as Kershner, Kasdan and Kurtz made the film serious, slower-paced, more introspective… against Lucas’ protests.” *Phantom Menace* “had no coattails,” “unfairly reviled,” “blindsided by the pre-release marketing that focused on the tragic and mature nature of the prequel trilogy.” Lucas (n.762): “I gave [the fans] as much as I could– I gave them a 110 percent.” **Coppola** (n.763): “George worked very hard on it… he didn’t slough it off or anything.” *Los Angeles Times* (n.765): “Even without the pre-release hoopla, The Phantom Menace would be a considerable letdown… noticeably lacking in warmth and humor… from a dramatic point of view it’s ponderous and plodding.”

Rough-cut aftermath (n.766): “It’s a little disjointed… It’s bold, in terms of jerking people around, but…” shakes his head: **“I may have gone too far in a few places.”** “It’s a very hard movie to follow and at the same time I’ve done it a little more extremely than I ever have in the past… If it’s intense for us, a regular person is going to go nuts.” **Liam Neeson**, days before premiere, announcing retirement (n.767): “Honest to god, I don’t want to do it anymore… I don’t think I can live with the inauthenticity of movies anymore. I don’t like watching them, especially my own stuff.” **Terence Stamp** (n.768): “When I walked onto the set, no Natalie [Portman]. ‘We’ve given Natalie the day off,’ they said. ‘That bit of paper on the post? That's Natalie. Deliver your lines to the paper’… The movie was a bit disappointing… it’s not possible to feel empathy when you’re watching something done digitally.”

## Autobiographical thesis; “the very thing I was trying to avoid” (pp. 350–353)

To **Lesley Stahl** (n.769): “Oh, it always hurts. It hurts a great deal… it’s like if you paint your house white and somebody comes over, 'Well that should be a green house.' Well, fine, but I wanted to paint it white… I wanted it to be a white house.” Labor of love; five-plus years; return to directing. Lucas (n.770): “Everything I write is my life… This is like a marriage. You have to be in love with this thing for at least four or five years and probably for the rest of your life.”

**Autobiographical parallels are Kaminski’s thesis unless quoted.** Lucas (n.771): “The auteur theory of film actually is very true if you know directors… in the case of somebody who writes and directs—it is my life.” Kaminski: Modesto escape in *THX*, *Graffiti*, and *Star Wars*—Uncle Owen / George Sr., Beru / Dorothy, T-16s, “wastes time with [his] friends.” Vader’s offer “parallels Lucas Sr.’s offer of his Modesto business”; “I’ve become the very thing I swore to destroy” (n.772, 2004). “Lucas’ own reconciliation had occurred only a few years prior to the writing of Return of the Jedi.” Lucas (n.773): “There is a lot more of me in Star Wars than I care to admit, for better or worse… Luke more or less is my alter ego. He can’t not be.” 1977 (n.774): “I wasted four years of my life cruising like the kids in American Graffiti and now I’m on an intergalactic dream of heroism. In Star Wars, I’m telling the story of me.” Footnote: May 7th, 2002 CNN, he rejects a direct father parallel—“my father wasn't at all like Darth Vader”; Kaminski: “the overall themes and arcs are designed at perhaps such an unconscious level that Lucas himself is unaware.” **Stephen Zitto**, 1977 (n.775): “One cynic, in advance of its completion, has called it American Graffiti in outer space.” **Hamill** (n.776): “George is Luke… We were in the desert one time—it was the scene where I had just found Artoo… ‘Do you think I should replace the restraining bolt?!?’ But George came up to me and said, ‘It’s not a big deal.’ He acted it out… I did it like that—and he said, ‘Cut. Print it. Perfect.’… After that I often felt like I was playing George.” Rebels as New Wave vs Hollywood Empire; Han “partially based off Francis Coppola” (n.777).

Prequels “chart[]” post-1983 life through Anakin (Kaminski): Chosen One expectations; sterility and adoption vs virgin birth and Palpatine’s Plagueis speech. *Wired*, 2005 (n.778), typography locked: “I’m not happy that corporations have taken over the film industry, but now I find myself being the head of a corporation, so there’s a certain irony there. I have become the very thing that I was trying to avoid. That is Darth Vader - he becomes the very thing he was trying to protect himself against.” Kaminski extends this to the divorce and the ranch: “he told Marcia that he was doing it for her… Marcia didn’t care about Skywalker Ranch… Perhaps it is appropriate that the final scene between Anakin and his wife plays out in an exact mirror scenario.” Lucas (n.779): “You can’t write a main character and not have him be a part of you and not be able to identify with him.” 2005 (n.780): “Everybody thought of Darth Vader as this big evil guy that had no heart and that he was just evil. But in the end it’s not that at all. I mean, here is a guy who has lost everything.” After release: vacation, then daily interviews, including *Rosie O’Donnell*. “Times had certainly changed.”
