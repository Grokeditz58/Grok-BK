# Kaminski, *The Secret History of Star Wars* (e-book v3.0, 2008): notes — Chapter VI: The Wreckage (pp. 218–230)

Fully read. Citations: Kaminski p. N; Kaminski Notes n. Y (Ch. VI notes start at n.461). Kaminski glosses flagged. Staff/crew/cast statements labeled by speaker. *Empire* production as “the Apocalypse Now of fantasy films,” the Kurtz split, and the nine-film plan beginning to die before Episode VI is even written.

## Production, money, Kershner vs Lucas (pp. 218–225)

Filming began in Norway, March 5th, 1979. Long Coppola/*Apocalypse Now* parallel: self-financed, personal assets as collateral, 16 weeks becoming 286 days, $13M to $31M. Coppola at Cannes (n.461): “My film is not about Vietnam. It is Vietnam… little by little we went insane.” Lucas not directing and “hardly on set,” but “it would be enough to personally change him.”

**Irvin Kershner**, suggested by **Gary Kurtz**, “character-oriented,” a “stylistic departure… which sometimes led to resentment.” Lucas “gritted his teeth” at improvisation and line changes (n.462); carbon-freezing scene “mostly improvised” (n.463, Alan Arnold); Kershner “still thinks Empire Strikes Back moves too fast” (n.464). Kershner (n.465): Fox thought he was “too old” (over 55); Lucas had been in his USC seminars; loved *Eyes of Laura Mars*, *The Return of a Man Called Horse*, *The Flim-Flam Man*; “you know everything a Hollywood director's supposed to know, but you're not Hollywood.” Kershner turned it down (“I don't know anything about special effects”); Lucas: “You think up anything you want and it’s up to Industrial Light and Magic to make it work.”

Lucas financed the film himself; *Star Wars* riches not enough. ILM move to Marin, Skywalker Ranch “would eventually cost nearly $20 million,” bank loan required. All personal assets tied to the picture. To Arnold on a set visit (n.466), typography locked: “I’m faced with a situation where everything I own, everything I ever earned, is wrapped up in this picture. If it isn’t a success not only could I lose everything, but I could also be millions of dollars in debt… It has to be the biggest grossing sequel of all time just for me to break even.” Context: *Sorcerer*, *New York, New York*, *Star Trek: The Motion Picture*, *The Black Hole* over-budget or poor; *More American Graffiti* “not the success many had hoped.” **Robert Watts** (n.467): “It was the most wearing film I’ve ever done.”

Disasters: *The Shining* accidentally burned down a stage (sixty-four sets into seven stages); Norway storms trapped the crew in their hotel; production designer / second-unit director **John Barry** “unexpectedly die[d] during production”; **Carrie Fisher** “would battle a heavy drug addiction all through production, eventually being diagnosed a manic-depressive in 1980 and undergoing rehab”; mechanical failures. Kershner (n.468): “every shot was like pulling a donkey out of a hat… Halfway through, my crew was falling apart.” X-wing out of the swamp: wood wings collapsed from water weight, ten hours for a six-second shot; gas mask with a microphone; broken arms from slipping into the swamp. Kershner called Lucas about cutting pages; Lucas: “Don’t do anything, just keep shooting.” (n.469)

**Mark Hamill** (n.470): “Empire seemed like nine months of torture… I'm supposed to be an actor, not a stuntman… I was the only human being listed on the call sheet for months… I probably had more differences of opinion with Kersh… he was an eccentric guy… He also changed his mind frequently, particularly with camera set-ups.”

**Lucas/Kurtz tension.** Lucas wanted Kurtz to restrain Kershner. Kurtz: “The director needs to do what he needs to do, that’s all.” (n.471) Lucas: “Gary never said no to anything.” (n.472) Daily calls: “scale down and speed up.” (n.473) Kurtz (n.474): “George wasn’t here… I kept telling George, ‘Look, you’ve got to let me do this my way… What we’re getting is very good. Don’t make it worse than it is.’ ” Kershner on **Harrison Ford** after a one-take effects shot (n.475): Ford asked whether he was good or whether the effects just worked; “he pointed his finger at me, wagging it, and said, ‘Now you better watch yourself.’”

They would part after *Empire*. Lucas had been “apprehensive about hiring Kurtz for Empire in the first place,” feeling Kurtz had aggravated *Star Wars* tensions especially with **Gil Taylor**, “but at Kurtz’ persuasion he gave him a second chance” (n.476). Lucas: “I suspected there would be problems and I knew I was asking for trouble.” (n.477) Kaminski: accusations that Lucas then sought “Yes Men” are “not wildly unreasonable” given the overruns with all of Lucas’s future tied up. Kurtz (n.478): “One of the arguments that I had with George about Empire was… he said, we could have made just as much money if the film hadn't been quite so good, and you hadn't spent so much time. And I said, ‘But it was worth it!’ ” Dichotomy: never again those overruns, “and yet he would never achieve the same level of critical and artistic success.” Sequels’ “primary purpose was to provide him with funding for Skywalker Ranch, an investment Kershner or Kurtz had little concern for” (n.479). Lucas to Lucasfilm president **Charles Weber**, 1980 (n.480): “The ranch is the only thing that counts. That’s what everybody is working for.” Lucas on *Empire* (n.481): “It looks pretty because Kersh took a lot of time to do it. It’s a great luxury that we really couldn’t afford. And ultimately it doesn’t make that much difference… It was just a lot better than I wanted to make it.”

Rough-cut panic like *Star Wars* (n.482): “I was extremely upset, because I felt it wasn’t working at all… I had a movie I thought was no good.” Pollock (n.483): Lucas’s recut criticized by Kershner, editor **Paul Hirsch**, and Kurtz. Lucas exploded at **Duwayne Dunham**’s amazement: “You guys are ruining my picture! You are here messing around and we’re trying to save this thing!” “It’s my money, it’s my film, and I’m going to do it the way I want to do it.” Then he calmed down (n.484): “I never got on Kersh about the fact that he was over schedule and putting a great burden on me and my life… Everything I owned was wrapped up in that damn movie.” Admitted his cut “didn’t really work very well” (n.485); followed Kershner’s suggestions; “It came together beautifully.” (n.486) Second then third bank loan; Fox had to guarantee the third, “leading to an improved distribution deal on their part (‘we’re still suffering from it,’ Lucas laments in 1983,” n.487). Budget from $15 million to $33 million (n.488).

Released May 21st, 1980; $209 million. Darker, no proper intro or resolution, some felt it “lacked the warmth and heart of the original.” *Washington Post* praise (n.493). Kershner (n.489): “It has to be slower and more lyrical. The themes have to be more interior, and you don’t have a grand climax.” Lucas (n.490): “The film was well directed, it was just differently directed.” Kasdan objected to on-set improvisations (n.491) but (n.492): “It has a quality I think Kersh gave it… It’s just not like any of the others. I really loved Richard Marquand. But he didn’t put as distinctive a stamp… He loosened up George in a lot of ways… even though it scared George at times.”

## After the hit: ulcer, Kurtz split, nine-film plan dying, Episode crawl (pp. 226–230)

By 1980 Lucas “irrevocably changed”: strained marriage, corporate downsizing/layoffs, “chronic headaches and bouts of dizziness, eventually being diagnosed with an ulcer that year.” “Only seven more Star Wars films to go!” **Miki Herman** (n.494): “I don’t know how one person has that much energy.” During production he began reconsidering the epic plan. Lucas (n.495): “I was ready to quit [after Star Wars]… I kidded myself into thinking that if I stopped directing, it would be like quitting. I thought I could just oversee it. But it didn’t work that way.” Sequel Trilogy “suddenly seemed like an unnecessary chore.” **Kurtz** (n.496): “At that time [during the making of Empire] we were still talking about what was happening with Jedi and it already was apparent that he was changing his mind with what he wanted to do with Jedi.” Collaborative-directors plan withdrawn (n.497): for the last sequel “he would choose a director more in line with his own style, be on set every single day, film much of the material himself, and for all intents and purposes become a co-director,” encouraged by the shift from adventure-of-the-week to a connected saga.

Marriage “in worse shape than ever”; Marcia wanted a family; “Their marriage would sadly not last the release of the final Star Wars film three years later.” Kurtz split “with irreconcilable differences over what had happened during Empire and over where the series was headed.” Kurtz, twenty years later (n.498): “it was kind of a mutual parting… he felt he would probably be more comfortable with someone else to handle the production chores on Jedi and I felt that I would prefer a different kind of challenge… Jim Henson had asked me to produce The Dark Crystal.” Kurtz “began to loathe the growing Lucasfilm empire,” once Lucas, Kurtz, Marcia, **Lucy Wilson**, and **Bunny Alsup**, now “Fortress Lucas,” “perhaps the inspiration for the under-construction Death Star that Lucas would write a few months later.” Lucas (n.499): “People can be pests… Everyone wants to be my friend now…and they all want something. I don’t want any more friends.” Kurtz (n.500): “The saddest thing… was the slow takeover by the bureaucracy… You couldn’t talk to George. You had to talk to his assistant. It became more Howard Hughes, in a way.” By 1983 Lucas accepted responsibility, to Pollock (n.501): “Gary did the best job he could, he made enormous contributions, but he was in over his head… If anybody is to blame, it’s me. Because I was the one who knew and stayed over here [in California] until it was too late.”

**Howard Kazanjian**, USC friend, producer of *More American Graffiti* and then *Raiders*, takes over *Jedi*. *Raiders* framed as “a sort of substitute sequel”—return to light 1930s serial vernacular after the saga had grown serious and nine-chaptered. While *Raiders* filmed in July 1980, Lucas “weighing the future.” **Harrison Ford** “fought hard to see to it that his character not return for the third instalment and instead be killed off.” Hamill and Fisher typecast; *Muppet Show*, Holiday Special. Actors contracted for only one more film. He and Marcia “would soon adopt a baby girl, Amanda, in 1981, after Lucas decided to give up his space opera” (he had previously refused a family because the trilogy was all-consuming, n.502). “could he finish up the story he had started in just one film?” The “Other” “now needed to be addressed, but with some clever writing it could be resolved.” **“It seems Lucas’ generation-spanning, nine-film epic Saga was dead before it ever got off the ground. Just two short years after he mapped out a nine-film structure, he was ending it.”**

Public still waiting: was Vader lying? was Obi-Wan lying? dark side? Han? Luke still in love with Leia? the “Other”? **Hamill** to David Packer, *Starlog* 1980 (n.503), typography locked: “George insisted [the ‘Other’] had always been part of the storyline, though he never told me who it might be. Somebody suggested it might be the Princess, but I think that would be a letdown.” Packer: “not even George Lucas knows for sure how it will emerge until it is down on paper.” Hamill again on the volcano duel already locked in Ch. III, then: “Now I wonder if it's true? … remember the Clone Wars? They could have cloned my father.” “Darth Vader is a good example of changing a character to please the people. I think, originally, if you follow classic drama, I would have to kill him in the third episode. But now he's a cult figure and, in a way, George may not want to do away with him… Ultimately, the Emperor should be the main bad guy - someone you try to get through nine movies, and in the ninth one you succeed… There has got to be something to the fact that he looks and sounds a bit like Obi Wan.”

*Empire* opens with “Star Wars Episode V The Empire Strikes Back.” An alternate title card filmed for *Star Wars*; 1981 reissue (not 1978) reads “Star Wars Episode IV A New Hope.” *Washington Post*, 1980 (n.504): “Could one ‘Star Wars’ plus one ‘Empire Strikes Back’ equal five? … When ‘Star Wars’ is reissued, probably next summer, the prints will include the subtitle, Episode IV: A New Hope. This adjustment may already be seen in the published screenplay… ‘The Art of Star Wars.’ ”

Chapter close, typography locked: the 1978 father fusion “began the first of two main events that would lead to the formation of the prequel trilogy… another event would shape and mould the focus of that story—but its effect would not come to pass for over an entire decade. That event is what we now come to, when George Lucas altered his plans for the saga and ended the series on Episode VI. I am of course talking about the humanisation and redemption of Darth Vader, and the creation of Anakin Skywalker, the fallen hero.”
