# 167 summary — Michael Kaminski, *The Secret History of Star Wars* (e-book v3.0, 2008)


## What this book is

A 2008 (third online edition) unauthorized history of how the *Star Wars* story was written, from the 1973 *Journal of the Whills* / *Hidden Fortress* treatment through *Revenge of the Sith* and the 2004 original-trilogy DVDs. Kaminski’s thesis, stated in the Conclusion and argued throughout: Father Skywalker and Darth Vader were not the same character until after the first film was released (April 1978, *Empire* second draft); there was no 1970s master plan for a six- or nine-film Anakin saga; later “Tragedy of Darth Vader / I had it all mapped out” claims are a publicity spin over intermediate versions that had already been published as films. The book is a synthesizer of Pollock, Bouzereau, Rinzler, Arnold, Hearn, Baxter, *Starlog*, *Rolling Stone*, *Time*, DVD commentaries, and many others, plus Kaminski’s own readings of the early drafts. The 939 end notes were all read; unique locks (date corrections, McCallum “you’ve been writing” parallels, the *Dark Empire* Vader-imposter veto, and others) are in `kaminski-15-endnotes.md`.

## Priority lines (Vader / Father / saga structure)

- **Ch. II, May 1974 rough draft.** Four later-combined ingredients, flagged as Kaminski’s reconstruction: General Darth Vader a minor Imperial flunkey who dies; Prince Valorum a redeeming Sith; Kane Starkiller the half-machine Jedi father; a Jedi family. The 2002/2005 “split the saga / saga of Darth Vader” claims are rebutted here (the rough draft is 132 pages, a more convoluted version of the 1977 film, not a six-film outline).

- **Ch. III, revised fourth draft.** Kenobi: the father “was betrayed and murdered…by a young Jedi, Darth Vader.” Second-draft father is still the living Starkiller; third-draft Vader is Kenobi’s fallen student, “Darth” a first name, Vader drinks from a flask. The volcano “Duel” is told to Hamill on set; Burtt’s breathing later turns the suit into life-support. A 1977 private talk still names the father Annikin, separate from Vader. Kaminski: Vader is not Luke’s father in the 1977 film; Leia is not his sister.

- **Ch. IV, December 1975 Foster meeting (also App. G).** “Han splits at the end of the second book and we learn who Darth Vader is…In the third book, I want the story to be just about the soap opera of the Skywalker family… The whole battle where Luke’s father gets killed.” Kaminski / App. G: “we learn who Darth Vader is” is the killer-of-the-father origin, later moved into the revised fourth draft, not a Father-Vader reveal. Lucas on Vader’s unexpected popularity: “That icon of evil sort of took over everything, much more than I intended.”

- **Ch. V.** Five revelations: *Empire* is still Chapter/Episode II (no prequel trilogy yet); Brackett, February 23rd, 1978, still has Father Skywalker’s ghost, separate from Vader; Lucas’s April 1978 second draft, p. 128, “I am your father,” forms the prequels and later Episode V; Leia was never the sister — the “Other” is Sequel Trilogy setup; Vader is not yet to be redeemed (*Revenge of the Jedi*). Close, typography locked, p. 214: “There is no master plan—and there never was. It was all made up as he went, and any plans laid would prove only temporary.”

- **Ch. VII, June 12th, 1981 revised rough draft.** “The boy is mine!” Unwilling servitude; both Vader and the Emperor into lava. Kaminski: “most important piece in the development of Anakin Skywalker.” Kershner, on a scaled-down *Jedi* script: “I didn’t believe it.” On the unmasking: “I was very surprised to see that he was an ordinary man in the third film.” Lucas refused a Han death: “The fact that the boy gets the girl… was a key factor.” Title-change accounts (Kazanjian / Cocks / Lucas “always Return” / Pollock) are not collapsed.

- **Ch. VIII–X.** Prequel outline “under a dozen pages” (Ch. VIII); Vaughan 1999 (Chs. IX–X and App. A): “No, that's wrong” that he had the whole thing mapped; “it’s only about seven or eight pages.” Mid-90s: Anakin displaces Obi-Wan as protagonist; Palpatine officially made a Sith; Rule of Two “a complete turnaround”; “Darth” becomes a Sith designation (see App. C). Lucas: “How does a sweet kid like this turn into something as evil as Darth Vader?” Anakin notes, typography locked: “Ages 9-20,” “Is he a mutation? Who was his father? Why was his mother outcast.” Three age timelines (Kaminski reconstruction, flagged).

- **Ch. XI, *Wired* 2005.** “I have become the very thing that I was trying to avoid. That is Darth Vader… here is a guy who has lost everything.”

- **Ch. XIII.** Art department works without a script. Mustafar: “I’ve always had this set piece: the end between Obi-Wan and Anakin.” James Earl Jones, 1990s: Vader becomes bionic “no more than five minutes at the end.” January 31st, 2003, 55-page rough draft, typography locked: Palpatine, “I arranged for your conception. I used the power of the force to will the midichlorians to start the cell divisions that created you… You could almost think of me as your father.” Later replaced by the Plagueis tale. June 2004 rough-cut, typography locked: “They didn’t understand the fact that Anakin is simply greedy. There is no revenge. The revenge of the Sith is Palpatine. It doesn’t have much to do with Darth Vader; he’s a pawn in the whole scheme.” Then the post-production rewrite: Anakin’s turn is re-shot from a Jedi-coup / power-lust decision (Anakin already present when Mace enters) to a Padme-saving Faust pact (he turns Palpatine in, then barges back). First half revised, second half not — hence the jarring jump to “I will kill all the Jedi.” To Christensen: “It’s basically Faust in the end.” 2004 (n.912): “In the [original] three films, it appeared the story is about Luke, but if you see all six films then you realise the story is about Darth Vader.” 2004 OT DVDs: Hayden Christensen inserted into the final *Jedi* scene; new McDiarmid *Empire* dialog, “I have no doubt that this boy is the offspring of Anakin Skywalker.” “Now Darth Vader is a tragic character who’s lost everything. He’s basically a bitter old man in a suit. ‘I am your father’ was a real shock. Now it's a real reward.”

- **Conclusion, 2005.** Sequel trilogy: *Empire*, “‘Never! Absolutely never!’ … ‘It was the story of Darth Vader, you know, it starts when he’s 10 years old and ends when he dies.’” MTV: “This was never planned as a nine-episode work.” Three “Tragedy of Darth Vader / always one movie” formulations (n.936–938). 1999 BBC, Coppola: Lucas says “Darth Vader, the father, was actually there, he was the father.” Coppola: “Really?”

- **App. A.** The massive pre-script “history of the galaxy” Journal never existed. The real 1973 document is Rinzler’s two-page Mace Windy / C.J. Thorpe synopsis. **Pablo Hidalgo**, *Insider* 79: “There is no 'real' Journal of the Whills— George Lucas added that to spice things up a bit.” *Annotated Screenplays*: “I eventually dropped this idea, and the concepts behind the Whills turned into the Force.”

- **App. C.** *Rolling Stone*, June 2, 2005: “Darth is a variation of dark. And Vader is a variation of father. So it’s basically Dark Father.” 1970s (Rinzler p. 172): “The early name was actually Dark Water. Then I added lots of last names, Vaders and Wilsons and Smiths, and I just came up with the combination of Darth and Vader.” Kaminski identifies a Modesto classmate, **Gary Vader** — this is Kaminski, not a Lucas quote. “Darth” is a first name (“a young Jedi named Darth Vader”; draft-three “Darth stands”) until 1999. MTV, May 9th, 2005: “When I started out, it was Darth Vader... And then [in the prequels] I started calling everybody dark something or other.” Third-draft “You're next, Blue Five... I have this feeling I know you. The Force is strong with you” is the Kiber crystal / Ashla omen, not a father hint; Vader overhears “Luke Starkiller” with no reaction. Kaminski: no silent pre-1978 Father Vader on paper. Points to “Conversations on Dark Father,” not in this edition.

- **App. D / E / F.** The Sequel Trilogy existed (Spielberg and McCallum, 1999; Arnold 1979 nine-film statements). The 1979 “Other”-centric plan, the post-1981 sixty-year-old reunion, and the 1999–2005 “media invention” denials are not collapsed. **Gary Kurtz** (IGN / Film Threat / Ens): hexology, Han killed, Leia queen, sister elsewhere, no second Death Star, no Ewoks — locked as Kurtz’s words. Kaminski: a “Super-Storyline” that “never existed at any point in time”; “some of his comments regarding the original configuration of Return of the Jedi remain valid.” **Dale Pollock**, *Washington Post* chat, May 19th, 2005: twelve 5–8-page synopses, I–VI most detailed, confidentiality on the unmade plots. Kaminski downsizes this; do not collapse with Lucas’s seven-or-eight-page outline.

- **App. G.** Rinzler 2007, built on Lippincott’s 1975–78 “Lost Interviews,” finds no “Tragedy of Darth Vader” script and no “make Vader Luke’s father?” note. The p. 111 volcanic / “walking iron lung” passage is “a word for word copy from Paul Scanlon's August 25th, 1977 interview” in *Rolling Stone*, not evidence of a late-1975 dating. The “Luke and the princess are twins” line is out of place and, Kaminski argues, comes from Rinzler’s 2006 Lucas interviews; revised fourth draft gives Luke as twenty and Leia as sixteen.

## Priority Han lines

- Ch. V conferences: Han an orphan raised by Wookiees, plus a Hemingway trader. Rinzler second-draft notes (App. C n.12): “Han Solo a wookie? Wookies talk to plants and animals.”
- App. C: Han’s name “could have come from Solo [paper] cups.”
- App. G / Worrell: “Han Solo and Luke are like twin brothers, the spiritual brother and the warrior brother with the devil-may care attitude.” **John Mollo**: medal-ceremony Luke “ought to look a bit more like Han.”
- App. B, Arnold 1979: “This character evolved into Han, the other side of Luke and an older brother figure.” Kaminski: Lucas is “clearly confusing his own drafts.”
- App. B: Toshiro Mifune inquiry; “If I’d gotten Mifune, I would’ve also used a Japanese princess, and then I would have probably cast a black Han Solo.” Interested in **Glynn Turman**. **Dianne Crittenden**: “which led George into thinking Han Solo might be black.”
- Ch. VII: Ford wanted Han to sacrifice himself; Lucas refused (“boy gets the girl”).
- App. E, Kurtz: “The resolution of that film was going to be quite bittersweet, with Han Solo being killed, and the princess having to take over as queen of what remained of her people.” Keep Kurtz’s words; do not collapse with the 1981 Ford/Lucas exchange or with Kaminski’s “Ford refused, so a backup death” hypothesis (which Kaminski himself calls not conclusive).
- Ch. XIII / App. H: a ten-year-old Han Solo on Kashyyyk in the January 31st, 2003 rough draft; removed in the April 10th, 2003 first draft.
- App. D, Worrell 1983, typography locked: “Han Solo and Leia would be together, although Lucas says, ‘They might be married, or not. We have never actually discussed marriage in this galaxy. I don’t even know if it exists yet.’”
- App. H: public *Empire* shooting script transcribes the final edit; “the carbon freezing of Han Solo… was originally scripted very differently” (Arnold). Kasdan notes, December 15th, 1981, “wookie doodle pad,” “apparently with regards to Han Solo.”
