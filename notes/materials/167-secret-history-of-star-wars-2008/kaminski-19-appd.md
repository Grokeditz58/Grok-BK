# Kaminski, *The Secret History of Star Wars* (e-book v3.0, 2008): notes — Appendix D: The Legend of the Sequel Trilogy (pp. 491–506)

Fully read. Appendix footnotes restart at 1; cite as Kaminski App. D n. Y or Kaminski p. N. Kaminski reconstructions of the 1979 “Other”-centric plan, and of the post-1980 “reunion” cover version, are flagged as such. Staff labeled. Do not collapse 1978–80 nine-film statements, the 1983 aged-cast plan, 1990s “maybe someday,” and 1999–2005 “media invention” denials. Kurtz’s hexology is deferred to Appendix E as “a confused conflation.” P. 493 contains a leftover editorial query, typography locked: “INSERT DO WE NEED ALL THIS?”

## It existed; timeline of plans (pp. 491–498)

Kaminski’s answer to “did it even exist?”: “a resounding ‘Yes!’” Lucas’s later “media invention” charge is “perplexing, simply due to the sheer volume of information Lucas has divulged.” **Steven Spielberg**, BBC *Omnibus*, 1999 (App. D n.1): “George always wanted to make nine. He wanted to the first three, then he wanted to make the prequels to that, then he wanted to make the last three. And that was something that was part of his concept.” **Rick McCallum**, same program (n.2): “Whether George only completes six of the nine-part series or he actually ever really ultimately completes the nine, it’s really nine parts of one film. Its one big saga, a saga about a family that happens to live in a galaxy far, far away.” Kurtz 1999 hexology (Vader dies in VI; Luke a full Jedi in VII; twin sister in VIII; Emperor revealed and defeated in IX) “never existed at any given time”; “confused conflation of the ever-changing storylines from 1977-1980” — see App. E.

Stand-alone first film; second-draft teaser “search for the Princess of Ondos,” “probably not seriously expected to be made.” Sequel rights as early as 1974 (Rinzler p. 25, n.3); Fisher and Hamill contracted for two sequels; Foster commissioned for the novelization plus two sequel novels under the three-film Fox contract (Rinzler p. 107, n.4). Final-draft embellishments (opening scroll, Death Star conference) made the Death Star’s destruction imply the fall of the Empire, “a sense of closure—Empire and Splinter both ignore this.” First Foster sequel scrapped as a film and released as *Splinter* (1978).

1977 interviews: James Bond-like loosely connected serial, different characters, periods, themes, directors, not necessarily chronological; possible young-Kenobi sequel; Kurtz indicated a film tracing the formation of the ancient Jedi order. Hamill approached about more than his three contracted films, “somewhat sarcastically that by the end he would be Kenobi’s age.” (The extract here contains the leftover “INSERT DO WE NEED ALL THIS?”)

Twelve films, projected completion 2001, announced around the Brackett draft; after the April 1978 father fusion, “Likely the series was changed to the nine-film, three-trilogy version.” On the *Empire* set, to Alan Arnold, typography locked (App. D n.5–6, pp. 177, 247–48): “The first script was one of six original stories I had written in the form of two trilogies. After the success of Star Wars I added another trilogy. So now there are nine stories. The original two trilogies were conceived of as six films of which the first film was number four.” “There are essentially nine films in a series of three trilogies. The first trilogy is about the young Ben Kenobi and the early life of Luke’s father when Luke was a little boy… About a year or two passes between each story of the trilogy and about twenty years pass between the trilogies. The entire saga spans about fifty-five years… The next chapter is called ‘Revenge of the Jedi.’ It’s the end of this particular trilogy, the conclusion of the conflict begun in Star Wars between Luke and Darth Vader. It resolves the situation once and for all.”

**The “Other.”** Lucas later claimed he had no idea who Yoda meant, that it wasn’t Leia or a sister, only to put Luke in danger. Draft trail, typography locked: revised second, “Now we must find another.” Third: “No…we must search for another.” Fourth: “No, there is another.” Kaminski: the fourth-draft change “Implying that somebody is already out there”; “Lucas was setting up the protagonist for the third trilogy,” likely a Force-sensitive youngster hidden at birth, not a seventy-year-old purge survivor. *Starlog*, July 1981 (n.7), typography locked: “No—possibly the robots, but there weren’t originally designed to go through the whole…nobody was designed to go through all three… Luke ends up in the third film of the first trilogy just three-and-a-half years old. There is continuity with the characters in other words, but not with the actors—and the look of the films will be different.” Kaminski reconstruction (flag): trilogy one Obi-Wan; trilogy two Luke, with Obi-Wan as mentor cameo; trilogy three the Other, with elderly Luke (not necessarily Hamill) as mentor. “The sequel is about Jedi knighthood, justice, confrontation, and passing on what you have learned” (Worrell, App. D n.8). Still alluded to in *Jedi* when dying Yoda says “Pass on what you have learned…” “Contrary to popular belief, there is no indication that an elderly Luke and company would be the protagonists of the sequels— that story point would be created much later.”

**Denise Worrell**, 1983 (n.9): specific plots for the prequels, “but he has only a vague notion of what will happen in the three films of the sequel,” “only some notes… not specific outlines.” 1980 *Time*: sequels would revolve around “the rebuilding of the Republic.” 1983: thematically “the necessity for moral choices and the wisdom needed to distinguish right from wrong.” “The third [trilogy will] deal with moral and philosophical problems… In Star Wars, there is a very clear line drawn between good and evil. Eventually you have to face the fact that good and evil aren’t that clear-cut and the real issue is trying to understand the difference” (n.10). Footnote: those moral-ambiguity comments were made about the post-1980 “reunion” sequel; Kaminski believes “thematic and tonal qualities remained more or less the same.” 1979 reconstruction (flag as Kaminski): I Obi-Wan and the fall; II Luke and the Rebellion; III the Other, Luke an elderly master, rebuilding the Republic. Plot beyond that is “anyone’s guess”; Kaminski lists possible new menaces, including an Emperor clone, noting Lucas okayed the Emperor-as-clone for *Dark Empire* (n.11). Flag the list as speculation.

*Bantha Tracks*, May 1980 (n.12 / Arnold pp. 247–8): “Originally, when I wrote Star Wars, [I] had material for six movies. After the success of Star Wars I added another trilogy.” By 1980, plan to film prequels first, then sequels—“the option of backing out.” Stress of *Empire*, marriage, actors tiring. “by 1981 he had decided to cancel the Star Wars saga, including his Sequel Trilogy.” The hanging “Other” is resolved by writing Leia in as the sister, recycling the Brackett-draft sister. “With the ‘Other’ forced to be addressed in Return of the Jedi and made into Princess Leia, the Sequel Trilogy lost its key character and story link,” shifting to a “reunion” when the characters are in their sixties, “first mentioned in *Time* Magazine in May, 1983.”

## Reunion version; quote anthology; 1999–2005 denials (pp. 498–506)

*Time*, May 23rd, 1983 (n.13, n.20): “Luke, who will then be the age Obi-Wan Kenobi is now, some place in his 60s, will reappear, and so will his friends, assuming that the creator decides to carry the epic further. Hamill and the others will get first crack at the roles—if they look old enough.” Worrell that month (n.14), typography locked: “In the sequel Luke would be a sixty-year-old Jedi knight. Han Solo and Leia would be together, although Lucas says, ‘They might be married, or not. We have never actually discussed marriage in this galaxy. I don’t even know if it exists yet. Who knows what relationship they will have? I mean, they’re together, let’s put it that way.’ … ‘If the first trilogy is social and political and talks about how society evolves,’ Lucas says, ‘Star Wars is more about personal growth and self-realisation, and the third deal with moral and philosophical problems.’” This reunion version is “roughly forty years after Return of the Jedi as opposed to twenty.”

*Rolling Stone*, June 12th, 1980 (Jean Vallely, n.15): Fox has first refusal; “Seven left.” On the Other: “Yes. [Smiling] There is another, and has been for a long time. You have to remember, we’re starting in the middle of this whole story. There are six hours’ worth of events before Star Wars, and in those six hours, the ‘Other’ becomes apparent, and after the third film, the ‘Other’ becomes apparent quite a bit.” “Do you have story lines for the seven Star Wars movies left to be done?” “Yes, twelve-page outlines.” Kaminski: “gobbledygook intended to avoid answering the question,” and “more nonsense about the amount of material.” Flag as Kaminski.

**Richard Marquand**, *Prevue*, July 1983 (n.16): “Yes, all nine parts ... if you follow the direction and project into the final trilogy, you realise you're going to meet the supreme intellect, and you think, how is it possible to create a man who has such profound cunning that he can not only control Darth Vader, but the fate of Luke Skywalker? Control the destiny of the whole galaxy? You'll be amazed!” Kaminski: “the character he describes is clearly the Emperor”; “not the original plan but the post-1980 ‘cover version.’”

Kaminski: “As infamous as it was, the Sequel Trilogy only existed in 1979 and early 1980, and possibly 1978.” Mid-80s *Starlog* (n.17, comment earlier, reprinted July 1987): “No, no books. If I do [more Star Wars stories], I will do them as movies.” By the 90s he allowed Bantam post-*Jedi* novels as “replacements.” *Wired*, May 1999 (n.18), already locked in Ch. IX: “The sequels were never really going to get made anyway… I said do sequels, because I'll probably never do sequels.” Anakin-as-prequel-protagonist “rendered [the third trilogy] obsolete.” Moral-ambiguity themes “found their way quite prominently into the prequels.” Novels set only a few years after *Jedi* left “a window” for films set decades later—why Spielberg and McCallum still acknowledge sequels in 1999; later novels moved further out.

**Quote anthology (pp. 502–504), typography locked, do not collapse.**

- *Time*, May 19th, 1980 (n.19): Episode VI “will end it, with either Luke or Darth Vader walking away from their final bout. The last three episodes involve the rebuilding of the republic. Only two of the main characters will appear in all nine films, and they are the robots… ‘In effect, the story will be told through their eyes.’”
- *Bantha Tracks* 8, spring 1980: after *Revenge*, first trilogy then last; “the ninth film released in the spring of 2001.”
- *Starlog*, July 1981: robots possibly the only continuity (quoted again).
- 1982, Baxter p. 387: “I’m only doing [Revenge of the Jedi] because I started it and now I have to finish it. The next trilogy will be someone else's vision.”
- *Time*, May 23rd, 1983: already quoted.
- Pollock, *Skywalking* first edition, 1983, p. 146: “The final three movies feature an adult Luke and the final confrontation between the rebels and the Empire. The entire saga spans more than fifty-five years; C-3P0 and R2-D2 are the only common element to all the films.” (This still places a Rebels-vs-Empire climax in the sequel trilogy; do not collapse with the 1983 sixty-year-old reunion or the 1979 Other plan.)
- *Press-Telegram*, May 18, 1983: “Now I've finished one book. And there may be two other books in my mind, but whether I start another book is not crucially important. The next book doesn't have anything to do with this book. Different sets, different actors.”
- *Starlog* 127, February 1988: “Hopefully, I will someday be doing the next three Star Wars, but I’m not sure when. The next three would take place 20 or 30 years before the films they’re celebrating here today. I’ll do the first trilogy first. There are nine [films] floating around there somewhere. I’ll guarantee that the first three are pretty much organized in my head, but the other three are kind of out there somewhere.” “Why didn’t you give Luke a girl?” “You haven’t seen the last three yet.”
- *Premiere*, September 1990: “Star Wars is a story, divided in three trilogies. It's a long movie of 18 hours, divided in nine parts… The main characters will be, besides A Young Vader and a Young Ben Kenobi, completely new.”
- *Splinter* second-edition introduction, 1994: “I began to see it as a tale that would take at least nine films to tell -- three trilogies -- and I realized, in making my way through the back story and after story, that I was really setting out to write the middle story.”
- *Unauthorized Star Wars Compendium*, January 1999, pp. 13–14: “The only notion on that was, wouldn't it be fun to get all the actors to come back when they're sixty or seventy years old and make three more about them as old people. That's how far that has gone, but the first six will definitely get finished.”
- *Juice*, May 1999: “And that got turned into doing nine films. It's six films. It's really not nine films. It's extremely unlikely that I will go on and do any more.”
- *TV Guide*, November 19, 2001: “Each time I do a trilogy it's 10 years out of my life. I'll finish ‘Episode III’ and I'll be 60. And the next 20 years after that I want to spend doing something other than ‘Star Wars’. If at 80 I'm still lively… I might consider it. But don't count on it. There's nothing written, and it's not like I'm completing something. I'd have to start from scratch. [The idea of episodes VII, VIII, and IX] was more of a media thing than it was me.”

**Mark Hamill**, September 10th, 2004 (n.21), typography locked: “You know when I first did this, it was four trilogies. 12 movies! … I said, ‘Why are you starting with IV, V and VI? It’s crazy.’ [Imitating Lucas grumble,] ‘It’s the most commercial section of the movie.’ He said the first trilogy’s darker, more serious. And the impression I got, he said, ‘Um, how’d you like to be in Episode IX?’ This is 1976. ‘When is that going to be?’ ‘2011.’ … ‘Well, what do you want me to do?’ He said, ‘You’ll just be like a cameo. You’ll be like Obi Wan handing the lightsaber down to the next new hope.’”

Misreading of *Bantha Tracks*, May 1980: “What is the third trilogy about?” “It deals with the character who survives Star Wars III and his adventures.” “Star Wars III” here is *Revenge of the Jedi*, not Episode III; not a cryogenically frozen Episode III protagonist. Zahn’s trilogy “was not meant to be a third trilogy… but they were indeed commissioned in place of the Sequel Trilogy.” 2005 (Hearn pp. 240–42, n.22): “I said that it might be fun to come back and do sequels with all the characters in their eighties, and to ask Mark [Hamill] to come back when he’s eighty. What I forgot, or didn’t realise at the time, was that I’d be eighty too! So, no, I’m not going to make another Star Wars film at eighty.” MTV, May 9th, 2005 (n.23): “I sort of played into it, but I probably shouldn’t have.”
