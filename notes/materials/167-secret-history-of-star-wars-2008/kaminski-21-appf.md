# Kaminski, *The Secret History of Star Wars* (e-book v3.0, 2008): notes — Appendix F: The Tales of Dale Pollock (pp. 515–518)

Fully read. Appendix footnotes restart at 1; all three cite the same *Washington Post* online chat, May 19th, 2005 (App. F n.1–3). Cite as Kaminski App. F n. Y or Kaminski p. N. Kaminski’s “exaggerated memory / 5–8 pages is the trilogy outline” reading is flagged as Kaminski. Do not collapse Pollock’s 2005 claim of twelve 5–8-page synopses with Lucas’s consistent seven-to-twelve-page *total* outline.

## Access, Lucas’s denunciation, the 2005 chat (pp. 515–516)

Crown asked Pollock to write “an unauthorized biography, and my big coup was getting Lucas to agree. He has never made that mistake again.” “I did more than 70 hours of interviews personally with Lucas, lived at Skywalker Ranch for two weeks, and spent time on the set of Return of the Jedi… I interviewed close to 100 other individuals who grew up with him, worked with him, or did business with him.” Lucas later denounced the book as inaccurate. Pollock: “He hated it because it’s honest, doesn’t pull any punches, and was well-researched (i.e., does not rely on only George's version of life and the universe.). I think it was upsetting to him to actually read what he said about people. The irony is that I was worried throughout the writing of the book that I was being way too nice to him, and that people would accuse me of kissing his butt. I was surprised by the vehemence of his reaction, because to me, twenty-one years later, the book is a very balanced portrait.” “the only member of the general public who spent any considerable time with Lucas during the creation of the original trilogy” aside from Alan Arnold on *Empire*; one of the few with free archive access, including “the once-mythical ‘Mace Windu’ Journal of the Whills summary.”

*Washington Post* chat, May 19th, 2005, typography locked:

On Jar Jar: “I think Lucas is as immune to outside criticism as he has always been. He doesn't care what anyone else thinks other than a very small circle of close friends. But I think JAR JAR was only there for one film (I remember seeing the character mentioned in only one story outline that I read, provided by George)and just didn't fit into the other ones. Characters come and go in the STAR WARS films. We've barely seen a Wookie again until Episode III.”

On how much was planned: “He had very specific outlines, which each ran 5-8 pages. I read them for a total of 12 films, so at least that many were planned. The most detailed synopses were for Episodes I-VI. He says he has no more STAR WARS features planned, but I still think he could change his mind, because I know he worked out stories that followed the original 3 films.”

On whether I–III were written before the OT: “I never saw actual screenplays for Episodes I-III, but there were very detailed story outlines, with certain character names, but not all. I think he started in the middle because of the excruciating set-up we all witnessed in Episodes I and II. If he had started there, we wouldn't all be on this chat room now.”

On summarizing the unmade sequel synopses: “I had to sign a confidentiality agreement to read them, so I'm afraid I cannot.”

## Kaminski’s reconciliation (pp. 516–518)

This “seems to contradict every single quote Lucas has ever said,” which put the entire trilogy-spanning outline at no more than twelve pages (Episodes I and II “only occupied roughly twenty percent”). Kaminski first suspects the 2005 comments, “almost twenty-five years after he supposedly read these treatments,” are “the exaggerated product of confusion.” He does not write them off entirely: Pollock uniquely handled the Mace/Whills document, and “the twelve-film saga was only seldom mentioned in a brief period much earlier.” Hired by a publisher, Pollock “obviously was not following minute details of the Star Wars development beforehand. His report that the series had twelve stories developed for it seems to be not of a confused remembrance of information gleaned elsewhere—he seems to report the matter unaware that Lucasfilm announced these twelve films in 1978, and hence these statements must be accurate.” Treatments he saw for VII–IX may have been the original 1979 versions; he also “had access to the sequel-sequel-trilogy—episodes X-XII, which were only developed in early 1978 and existed for a few brief months, and would have been considered apocrypha by 1982.”

Kaminski then suspects “each film having its own five to eight page outline is erroneous,” a confusion with Lucas’s “seven to twelve page trilogy outline,” Kaminski’s lower estimate being seven pages, “which he has also stated multiple times.” “with certain character names, but not all” fits a short composite outline. Jar Jar: Pollock “never identifies it with a bumbling frog sidekick, only that he remembers that name appearing.” Possible explanations: a later-renamed character, or actually “Bink” from the 1974 first draft (Deak Starkiller in the rough draft), “consistent with the fact that Pollock remembers the character only being in one story.”

Taken literally, twelve films at 5–8 pages would be “up to 96 pages (or more) of story treatments,” which Kaminski finds “hard to believe.” X–XII “perhaps cumulatively only a couple pages, and very likely not even proper treatments but unordered hand-written notes” written privately while Brackett drafted (December–February). I–VI “far more detailed than the sequels, which is consistent”; if the prequel treatment was ~seven pages for the whole trilogy, “the sequel trilogy outline was only three or four pages long.” Combined with complete *Star Wars*, *Empire*, and *Jedi* synopses plus other notes, “the total amount of pages would easily exceed forty or fifty pages… perhaps explaining why, twenty-five years later, Pollock remembers it as being much larger.” Record Pollock’s claim and Kaminski’s downsizing side by side; do not collapse.
