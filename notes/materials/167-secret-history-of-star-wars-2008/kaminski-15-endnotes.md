# Kaminski, *The Secret History of Star Wars* (e-book v3.0, 2008): notes — End Notes (pp. 421–449)

Fully read. All 939 notes (n.1–n.939) parsed from the born-digital extract. Citations here: Kaminski Notes n. Y, or Kaminski p. N in End Notes. Most notes are short bibliographic citations for quotations already locked in the chapter files (Arnold, Pollock, Baxter, Hearn, Bouzereau, Rinzler, *Starlog*, *Rolling Stone*, *Time*, DVD commentaries, etc.). This file locks only material that is unique to the notes, or that substantively corrects or expands a chapter lock. Kaminski’s own commentary in the notes is flagged as such.

Chapter ranges (approx.): Intro n.1–5; I n.6–99; II n.100–213; III n.214–269; IV n.270–358; V n.359–448; VI n.449–523; VII n.524–606; VIII n.607–642; IX n.643–705; X n.706–743; XI n.744–833; XII continues in the 780s–832 band (overlap with XI numbering in the extract headers is a Kaminski section-label slip in the notes typography, not a missing range); XIII n.834–930; Conclusion n.931–939. ## Unique locks (not already quoted in the chapter notes)

**n.1 (Intro).** Homing Beacon #223: writing started April 17th, 1973. Kaminski: first/second editions wrongly put him on Portola Drive (USC / pre-marriage); he and Marcia moved to an unknown Mill Valley house while writing *THX 1138* in late 1968 (Hearn p. 33); this edition uses Medway Avenue, the house bought just before *Graffiti*. The 1968–mid-1973 house, “wherever this was it would be where Lucas actually first wrote Star Wars,” is still unknown. The May 25th, 1973 date on the bootlegged treatment footer (stolen after release) is “expelled as myth”: Rinzler, *Making of Star Wars* p. 11, Jeff Berg gave UA VP David Chasman the typed fourteen-page treatment by May 7th, 1973.

**n.120–121 (Ch. II).** Homing Beacon #223 (April 17th, 2008) cites a 1983 *Rolling Stone* claim that he completed the treatment on May 20th; Lucasfilm’s explanation that the early-May UA copy might be an earlier draft “may or may not be correct.” Handwritten original “May 1973,” ten pages (Rinzler p. 9); typed version fourteen pages, not thirteen; in existence by May 7th. Kaminski leaves open that the bootleg May 25th footer could be the secretary’s typing date.

**n.140 (Ch. II).** **Ralph McQuarrie**, 1993 Definitive Edition laserdisc commentary: Lucas initially wanted Chewbacca based on “a lemur-like alien from some old magazine.”

**n.142 (Ch. II).** Hearn p. 80 wrongly says the film was submitted to Universal in February, “before the fourteen-page treatment even existed.” Rinzler: summer, just before *Graffiti* was released.

**n.178 (Ch. II).** Rinzler p. 18 calls a scene “deleted”; Hearn p. 33 excerpts the same scene from the first draft. Kaminski: likelier deleted after the first draft than filmed and cut.

**n.214 (Ch. III).** Pollock p. 149: Lucas gave McQuarrie comic books. 1977 interviews: fond of **Moebius** and **Druillet** / *Heavy Metal* (*Métal Hurlant*). Kaminski: this “junky ‘used universe’” also inspired Scott’s *Alien* and *Blade Runner*.

**n.243, n.270 (Chs. III–IV).** **Tom Pollock** letter to Fox requesting sequel rights as far back as September 1974 (Rinzler p. 25); formal negotiations not until February 1976, dragging into March, by which time the revised fourth draft was more self-contained. 1973 memo deal existed, but rights were not concretely worked out until the 1976 contract.

**n.275 (Ch. IV).** *Empire* 2004 commentary and 1993 laserdisc already used in Ch. IV. Additional, **Static Multimedia**, 2005: “I do things. I kill Obi-Wan off and turn him into a ghost, but then I got stuck in the second film because I still had that character to deal with and so I created Yoda to sort of be the alternate Obi-Wan. If Obi-Wan had lived, that was his part.”

**n.283 (Ch. IV).** Practical cuts (Rinzler pp. 84–85): extras recycled; scenes deleted; Mos Eisley hangar scaled down; entire Alderaan location deleted (action moved onto the Death Star); Rebel base from jungle to temple interior; Ben’s multi-leveled cave scaled to a small hut.

**n.284 (Ch. IV).** *Entertainment Tonight* online, May 30th, 2005; also Static Multimedia, September 10th, 2005: “I never intended for the back story to be told.”

**n.292.** Shooting start is March 22nd, not March 25th (Hearn p. 116 schedule; Rinzler confirms).

**n.303.** *Splinter* hardcover February 1978, first paperback April 1978 (Kaminski’s 1994 reprinting), not March as some reports.

**n.359 (Ch. V).** Steve Sansweet, *The Vault* (2007): complete reproductions of Lucas’s handwritten *Empire* treatment originals, “unfortunately, only the first four pages.”

**n.363 (Ch. V).** *Gone With the Wind* parallels: “I’d just as soon kiss a pig”; “You should be kissed and by someone who knows how.” *Empire* theatrical poster emulates the Gable/Leigh pose with Han and Leia. Foster 1975: book two would be *Gone with the Wind* in Outer Space.

**n.377 (Ch. V).** Arnold p. 144, Lucas claimed three months to write; he completed the second, revised second, and third drafts all in April 1978 (*Annotated Screenplays*). Kaminski: “unbelievably rapid progress is indicative of the success of his new storyline.”

**n.378 (Ch. V).** Hearn p. 127, cropped photo of a draft fragment: Luke is not wounded on the ledge; Vader taunts throughout, “making a remark about Obi Wan and Luke’s father, which causes Luke to shout ‘Enough! [Obi Wan] said you killed him.’”

**n.419–420 (Ch. V).** *Art of The Empire Strikes Back* (1997 reprint): “Episode II: The Empire Strikes Back” / “Star Wars Chapter II” stamps on designs dated July 20–December 21, 1978. From 1979, after the February fifth draft titled Episode V, the stamp drops “Episode II” and says only “Empire Strikes Back.” *Annotated Screenplays* lists “Episode V” first at the fifth draft and omits a fourth-draft listing (Kaminski: honest mistake; the fourth draft exists). “even when Lucas was using Episode V after draft five… there was no mention of Episode V in the revised stamp—perhaps this aspect was to be kept secret, so as to not alert office employees that three prequels were planned.” Flag the secrecy inference as Kaminski.

**n.428 (Ch. V).** Kerry O’Quinn, *Starlog*, July 1981: “There is continuity with the characters, in other words, but not with actors.”

**n.373 (Ch. V, shorter but load-bearing).** Baxter p. 270 says Brackett’s husband answered the phone; **Edmond Hamilton** died in 1977. Flag Baxter error.

**n.502, n.614 (Chs. VI, VIII).** Baxter p. 240; *Skywalking* p. 83: **Marcia** wanted to start a family around 1969; Lucas was afraid it would interfere with his work.

**n.524 (Ch. VII).** 2004 *Jedi* commentary: the film as the last third of the “original, big trilogy script,” primitives/Wookiees toppling the Empire and Death Star. Kaminski: “extremely misleading and inaccurate: that 1974 rough draft was merely a more convoluted version of Star Wars.”

**n.579 (Ch. VII).** *The Vault* p. 66: media first reported the title change January 27th, 1983.

**n.584 (Ch. VII).** *Empire of Dreams*: **Jim Bloom** suggested the cover name to avoid inflated rental costs; *Blue Harvest* “was only used while on location in California for the Endor sequences,” late in production.

**n.587 (Ch. VII).** Usenet, June 20th, 1983, user CFV: at Octocon that year, **Howard Kazanjian** still gave a presentation on *Revenge of the Jedi* and “got a lot of ‘Jedi’s don’t belive in revenge’ flack.”

**n.634 (Ch. VIII).** *US*, December 25th, 2000, Jonathan Schwartz: **Linda Ronstadt** “in the '80s she was engaged (‘ring on the finger and all’) to” Lucas. A&E *Biography*, 2001: relationship four years long, ending 1988, the year he adopted Katie.

**n.643 (Ch. IX).** Baxter’s chronology differs from Zahn/Waldenbooks: Lou Aronica–Bantam Spectra contacted Lucasfilm in 1988, heard back a year later, commissioned Zahn in 1990 (*Mythmaker* p. 388). Do not collapse.

**n.651 (Ch. IX).** *Dark Empire* first proposed 1989; originally included a Darth Vader imposter in a copy of the suit so the Empire could claim he was alive. “Lucas vetoed this.” **Tom Veitch** then suggested the Emperor return as a clone; Lucas agreed. “Supposedly, Dark Empire is one of Lucas’ favorite EU.”

**n.673, n.678 (Ch. IX).** 1992 *Lucasfilm Fan Club* 17: already committed and planning to shoot all three at once—Kaminski: the only indication of work that early, “very preliminary.” Official development announced 1993. McCallum’s “1990” (scifi.com) “I believe he is merely speaking in general terms or is simply mistaken (for starters—he wasn’t even working with Lucas at that time).” Fan Club 17 also: films would need stylistic continuity to fit as “one piece.”

**n.687 (Ch. IX).** **Frank Oz** tried to invent a Yoda history; Lucas told him it wasn’t important (*Starlog* 127, February 1988, p. 48). *Jedi* novelization p. 135: the Emperor has only a vague recollection of a Jedi named Yoda. Anakin’s *Jedi*-era backstory “only involves Kenobi”; Yoda “never mentioned anywhere else.” Kaminski: Dagobah may always have been Yoda’s dwelling, not exile; Brackett had Kenobi and Father Skywalker train there.

**n.706 (Ch. X).** Hearn p. 190: handwritten rough draft cover dated January 13th, 1995; Kaminski: rough, revised rough, and typed first draft of *The Beginning* were all completed that day.

**n.716 (Ch. X).** Late April 1995 interviews: McCallum “until George completes the storylines”; Lucas “I’m writing [the scripts], at least the first drafts myself”—implies the second draft does not yet exist and he has not decided whether he will write it or bring in another writer. Second draft “summer 1995 at the earliest, and probably it was even later.”

**n.723 (Ch. X).** Episode I *Insider’s Guide* rough-draft summary: no prophecy, virgin birth, or midichlorian scenes; Anakin is gifted, future clouded, existence “foreseen.” “The Prophecy, the Chosen One, and the midichlorians came in the later drafts—logically in the second draft with Qui Gon’s promotion.”

**n.733 (Ch. X).** Late 1996 *Making Magic* CD-ROM: still hasn’t decided if he will direct any of the new films; if he did, he would only do one, not all three. Announced through a spokesperson Wednesday, September 25th, 1996, that he would direct the first prequel (*Sarasota Herald-Tribune*, September 29th; *Contra Costa Times*, September 30th).

**n.744, 747, 749, 754 (Ch. XI).** Review chronology: AICN May 5th mostly warm; May 9th press (*Rolling Stone*, *Time*, *Newsweek*, *NYT*, *Variety*) “trash the movie.” Jar Jar critiques: “prancing and flighty nature and his effeminate voice”; **Michael Dyson**, Columbia, to CNN June 14th, 1999: “If you go back and look at cartoons from the 30’s and 40’s and 50’s, they’re full of racism… Maybe this time around in reaching back to borrow from old movies, maybe Lucas or his people had trouble separating stereotypes.”

**n.814, 815, 821 (Ch. XII).** McCallum in *Insider* #45 (conversation two weeks before Episode I opened): “George is about a quarter of the way through the script. He’ll have the script done by September.” Duncan and later Rinzler show he had not started; notes on summer 1999 vacation, script unwritten. *Insider* #44 ~April 1999: “He’s editing Episode I, but on weekends and the early mornings he works on the script”—“this does not seem to be the case.” Parallel to Episode III (Rinzler p. 31): “You said you’ve been writing.” “I’ve been thinking about it.” Hearn p. 216 cover: dated “March 13, 1999”, “1999” crossed out in red, “2000” written over. “Lucas was so busy he couldn’t remember what year it was!”

**n.827 (Ch. XII).** Leaked shooting script also titled revised third draft; addendum “Last Revision: September 5, 2001.” May be a mismatch to the body; October 2001 pick-ups (Duncan p. 207).

**n.837 (Ch. XIII).** **James Earl Jones**, *Insider* 49, May 2000: the Idaho conversation was “a few years prior”—“sometime in the 90’s.”

**n.854 (Ch. XIII).** *Calgary Sun* / IMDb Studio Briefing, March 28th, 2002, **Samuel L. Jackson**: “I told George I didn't mind dying… I just didn't want to go out like some punk. George said that was fine and he'd see what he could do about a fitting death scene.”

**n.875, 879, 881 (Ch. XIII).** December 13th, 2002 (Rinzler p. 36): still no outline. **Trisha Biggar** asks which costumes Padme will wear; Lucas: “It’s too early. I have to get an outline together,” then lists the sets. Outline “late December, 2002, or early January, 2003.” Rough draft typed January 31st, 2003, 55 pages; Kaminski thinks scripting after the holidays.

**n.898 (Ch. XIII).** Rinzler p. 82, shoot day six (a Monday): Lucas tells **Hayden Christensen** he spent the weekend rewriting the turn: “I’ve stretched it out and given you a little more to go on in terms of him pulling you in.” P. 96: Plagueis tale moved to an opera house after he realized he had already shot many office scenes.

**n.928.** 2005 “worst year for theater attendance in over a decade” (AP, June 17th, 2005); CNN Money: worst slump since 1985.
