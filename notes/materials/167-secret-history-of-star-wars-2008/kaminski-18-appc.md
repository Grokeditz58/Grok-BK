# Kaminski, *The Secret History of Star Wars* (e-book v3.0, 2008): notes — Appendix C: The Dark Father (pp. 473–490)

Fully read. Appendix footnotes restart at 1; cite as Kaminski App. C n. Y or Kaminski p. N. Kaminski’s etymology, “Gary Vader,” and the long “silent Father Vader” brief are flagged as Kaminski except where they quote Lucas or staff. This is the book’s concentrated case that (1) “Darth Vader” ≠ “Dark Father” in 1974–77, (2) “Darth” was a first name, not a Sith title, until the prequels, and (3) Father Vader cannot be shown to predate spring 1978. Close points to a further appendix, “Conversations on Dark Father,” which is **not** among Appendices A–H in this e-book v3.0.

## Part One: “Dark Father” and the name’s origin (pp. 473–477)

*Rolling Stone*, June 2, 2005 (App. C n.1): “Darth is a variation of dark. And Vader is a variation of father. So it’s basically Dark Father.” Kaminski: Lucas “didn’t start making the claim that Vader was always meant to be Luke’s father until the early 90's,” and this “Dark Father” explanation “didn’t appear in prominence until 2005.” Dutch “Vader” = father is a later coincidence, not proof of a 1970s master plan.

1974 rough draft: General Darth Vader is “Imperial Fodder,” “a tall, grim looking general,” not a Sith, not a cyborg, blown up; Kane is already the father training the hero. Even in the second draft, Sith/mechanical Vader coexists with a living on-screen father. “Since the name originated in a person verifiably separate from the father (1974’s General Darth Vader), any eventual connection… is a coincidence, whether it happened in 1975 or 1978.”

Names chosen for phonetic quality, not symbolism. **Terence McGovern** (radio voice for *THX* and *Star Wars*; *Graffiti*): driving with Lucas, “I think I ran over a wookie back there.” Lucas asked what a wookie was; “I don’t know, I just made it up”; Lucas jotted it down (*Bantha Tracks* 8, spring 1980, App. C n.2; also Scanlon, *Rolling Stone*, August 25th, 1977, n.3). Other versions exist (ad-lib in the *THX* recording); Kaminski prefers the *Bantha Tracks* version “due to its specification.” **Walter Murch**, *Graffiti* mix: asked Lucas to grab “R2-D2”—reel two, dialog two; Lucas jotted it, first page of his notes (Pollock p. 141, App. C n.4).

*Annotated Screenplays* p. 10 (App. C n.5): “The names in Star Wars were simply created phonetically.” Lucas (n.6): “As I was writing, I would say the names to myself and if I had a hard time dealing with a name phonetically, I would change it.” Rinzler p. 172 (n.7): Han “could have come from Solo [paper] cups.” Ben: “A combination of a lot of words I put together.” Leia: “I just picked that name.” Tarkin: “That was just a name made up out of nowhere.” Chewbacca: “I came up with a whole bunch of Wookie words, just changing words around, and I liked Chewbacca the best.” Footnote: phonetic kinship of “Bob Falfa” and Boba Fett.

1970s origin of the name, typography locked (Rinzler p. 172, App. C n.8): “That’s just another one of those things that came out of thin air. It sort of appeared in my head one day. I had lots of Darth this or Darth that, and Dark Lord of the Sith. The early name was actually Dark Water. Then I added lots of last names, Vaders and Wilsons and Smiths, and I just came up with the combination of Darth and Vader.” “Vaders and Wilsons and Smiths”—explicitly a surname.

Other language sources (Kaminski, flagged): taun / Japanese “town” (Tauntaun, Taune We); Jedi from *jidai-geki*; “Sith” and “banths” from Burroughs; “Coruscant” in E.E. Smith’s *Lensmen*; Hoth perhaps from General Hermann Hoth; Padmé a Buddhist lotus. 1973 brainstorm lists: Roland, Monroe, Kane, Owen, Wan, Crispin, Hayden—“particularly ironic” given later casting of Hayden Christensen.

Associative readings of the final-draft names are Kaminski’s (flag): Han Solo a loner; Leia Organa / “organic”; Luke Skywalker “daydreamer”; Obi-Wan “Holy One” (or “Obi Juan” / Don Juan, or Japanese per Nikki White, *Bantha Tracks* 6); Darth Vader “Dark Invader,” like Sidious / insidious. “Any connotation of ‘father’ is completely absent from both the phonetic quality of the word ‘Vader’ and the function and characterisation of the character within the context of the film itself.”

Dutch coincidence: “Vay-der” ≠ “fah-ther”; Dutch *vader* is pronounced “Vah-der”; “Darth” is not a Dutch word; foreign versions “Dark Vader,” France “Dark Vador,” Italy “Dart Fener.” “Lucas himself never made this Dutch connection claim, but merely stated that ‘Darth Vay-der’ connotes ‘Dark Fah-ther’ on its own.” “Dark Hater” is “a more accurate connotation.”

**Gary Vader.** Kaminski: Modesto school yearbook, “a particular fellow in Lucas’ class,” “bulky, jock-looking.” “That’s right. Lucas got the name from a high school jock. Another mystery solved.” This is Kaminski’s identification, not a Lucas quote; record it as such and do not collapse it with Lucas’s “came out of thin air / Vaders and Wilsons and Smiths” account.

## Part Two: “Darth” was a first name, not a Sith title, until 1999 (pp. 477–479)

Rough draft: *General* Darth Vader—first name + last name, “otherwise ‘General Darth Vader’ would be redundant.” Survives to the fourth draft as a Sith who is still not Luke’s father. Kenobi: “A young Jedi named Darth Vader…” On the Death Star: “Only a master of evil, Darth,” and “You can’t win, Darth.” Early-draft Sith (Prince Valorum; other Sith in drafts two and three) are not named Darth. Draft-three grammar, typography locked: “Darth, did you feel that?” / “Darth stands and stares at the crystal.” “had that word been intended as a rank or title the proper phrasing would be ‘The Darth stands.’”

After the 1978 merger: Father Skywalker “adopted a Sith name—Annikin Skywalker was no more. He was now Sith Lord ‘Darth Vader.’” Kaminski: Episode III, Anakin adopts the identity “Vader”; originally he would have adopted the identity “Darth Vader” as a whole, not “Darth” as a title. “This is why there are no ‘Darths’ in the Star Wars universe until 1999.” EU Sith before then (Exar Kun, Ulic Qel-Droma, the whole *Tales of the Jedi* series from 1993) are not Darths. After Episode I, EU fills with Darth Bane, Bandon, Malak. Lucas to MTV, May 9th, 2005 (App. C n.9): “When I started out, it was Darth Vader... And then [in the prequels] I started calling everybody dark something or other.” 1997 internet FAQ anagram “DArk lord of the siTH” is a fan theory. Interchangeable “Darth” / “Lord” Vader (like Commander Skywalker) produced the assumption.

## Part Three: no silent pre-1978 Father Vader (pp. 479–490)

The frequently cited third-draft (August 1975) line: “You're next, Blue Five... I have this feeling I know you. The Force is strong with you.” Kaminski: not a father hint. Running theme: Sith feel the Ashla growing as Luke and Ben embark; cut to Death Star, “Darth, did you feel that?”; “Something old has been awakened. The Force has suddenly grown stronger.” Similar scene when Ben uses the Force on Alderaan: “If I didn't know better I'd say we were in the presence of a Jedi knight.” The Kiber crystal, stolen back by Kenobi, is in Luke’s X-wing and “making his ship glow”; in this draft the crystal itself catalyzed Vader’s fall. Draft two already has Vader sense the crystal: “I feel the influence of the Kiber Crystal. Perhaps the Starkiller is still alive.” In draft three, after Vader’s line, “Luke's hand instinctively goes to the pocket that holds the Kiber Crystal. Its glow lights up the entire cabin.” Luke then identifies himself: “I am Luke Starkiller. Had we met earlier, you would not be here.” Vader, having overheard “Luke Starkiller” on the radio, has “not a single reaction,” continues trying to kill him—“Not yet, my bold friend.” When the Sith-omen scenes and the crystal were cut, the first half of the line went too; the film keeps only “The Force is strong with this one.”

Vader surviving is “a throwback to the old serial films.” To Foster, late 1975 (Rinzler p. 107, App. C n.10): “Vader runs off in the end, shaking his fist: ‘I’ll get you yet!’ ” Third-draft outline had a face-to-face Death Star saber duel in which Vader is killed again; cut for pacing (Rinzler p. 59, n.11); survival preserved the duel for a sequel. Lucas’s later “had Vader in mind as the father all along” claims “never, ever imply that he had ambiguously set up hints in an early draft but then eliminated them”; they claim explicit drafts / a “Tragedy of Darth Vader” / one “original big script.” “there are no ‘red herrings’ in the film.”

Rinzler second-draft notes (p. 26, n.12): “‘Jabba in prison cell’; the love relationship between Leia and Luke Starkiller is divided into ‘seven stages/crucial scenes,’ with her being crowned queen at the end; ‘Han Solo a wookie? Wookies talk to plants and animals.’” Alternate ideas “often suffixed by question marks.” First visual conception: a doodle of Wookiee-planet geography “on the back of a scrap piece of paper in early 1973/late 1974” (Rinzler p. 22, n.13). Rinzler, having also written *Making of Revenge of the Sith* and thus knowing the Tragedy-of-Vader claims, “came out totally empty-handed” of any pre-1978 Father Vader note, with “substantial proof of the opposite orthodox backstory.” Flag as Kaminski.

**Noah Henson** (quoted at length): discarded unwritten notions “are no longer applicable to any reasonable discussion as to origins, since they were never entertained seriously enough to warrant being recorded.” Kaminski: Lucas’s own claim is not “I thought of it and set it aside”; it is that the twist was designed into *Star Wars* and kept secret, including from Foster, “the writer of the sequel in which this reveal would play,” and from his own private notes—“an elaborate conspiracy… demonstrably false.” Secrecy cannot explain private 1975 Foster and Kurtz meetings, or the 1975 remark that he would like to film a prequel about the backstory in which Father Skywalker gets killed (Rinzler p. 107). “there were no blue-photocopy-proof script pages for Obi Wan's death because no one knew who the hell ‘Obi Wan Kenobi’ was.” 1977 treatment and Brackett draft still have a separate father (ghost in draft one exists “specifically to relay” the twin-sibling story point, eliminated in April 1978). December 1977 conferences still propose Father Skywalker has a twin child also training as a Jedi.

Dating, typography locked from Kaminski’s close (flag as Kaminski, not a document): “we can pinpoint the epiphany to have occurred sometime in the new year of 1978… Brackett turned in her draft February of 1978… Brackett died and Lucas was left alone with the pen… he must have arrived at the concept that the character of Luke's father could be combined with Vader, a twist facilitated by the close link they already shared and the more complicated and serious style that Empire was heading down.” “a very high degree of certainty that the concept of their fusion was not arrived at or ever considered until after that film's release, with a reasonable degree of certainty that it occurred in early 1978.”

Final pointer: “For additional debate… consult the appendix ‘Conversations on Dark Father.’” That title is **not** in this e-book v3.0’s Appendices A–H (pp. 450–533).
