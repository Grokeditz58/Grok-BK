# Kaminski, *The Secret History of Star Wars* (e-book v3.0, 2008): notes — Appendix E: The Tales of Gary Kurtz (pp. 507–514)

Fully read. Appendix footnotes restart at 1; cite as Kaminski App. E n. Y or Kaminski p. N. Kaminski’s “Kurtz is simply confused / Super-Storyline” argument is flagged as Kaminski. Kurtz’s own words are locked in full. Do not collapse Kurtz with Lucas, or with Kaminski’s four-plan chronology (1976–80). A date slip: the body calls the IGN FilmForce interview “1999,” but App. E n.1 and n.6 cite Ken P., IGN, November 11th, 2002 (same URL used in the main-text notes). Record both.

## Kurtz on *Jedi* and a nine-film hexology (pp. 507–511)

Kurtz resurfaced after “nearly two decades of silence,” partly for the late-90s renaissance, partly as producer of the indie *5-25-77*.

**IGN FilmForce** (body: 1999; n.1: November 11th, 2002), typography locked: “At that time [of Empire Strikes Back], [Lucas] always said that he had enough material for three earlier films and three later films, to make a total of nine, and there were outlined materials certainly for a later three that culminated with this big clash with the Emperor in Episode IX. So, we'll never see any of those, based on what he's said now… One of the reasons Jedi came out the way it did was because the story outline of how Jedi was going to be seemed to get tossed out, and one of the reasons I was really unhappy was the fact that all of the carefully constructed story structure of characters and things that we did in Empire was going to carry over into Jedi. The resolution of that film was going to be quite bittersweet, with Han Solo being killed, and the princess having to take over as queen of what remained of her people, leaving everybody else. In effect, Luke was left on his own. None of that happened, of course… Much, much less [of a happy ending]. It would have been quite sad, and poignant and upbeat at the same time, because they would have won a battle. But the idea of another attack on another Death Star wasn't there at all ... it was a rehash of Star Wars, with better visual effects. And there were no Ewoks ... it was just entirely different. It was much more adult and straightforward, the story.”

**Film Threat**, Chris Gore, March 5th, 2000 (App. E n.2): “So the story [for Return of the Jedi] was quite a bit more poignant and the ending was the coronation of Leia as the queen of what was left of her people, to take over the royal symbol. That meant she was then isolated from all of the rest and Luke went off then by himself. It was basically a kind of bittersweet ending. She's not his sister that dropped in to wrap up everything neatly. His sister was someone else way over on the other side of the galaxy and she wasn't going to show up until the next episode.”

**Paul Ens**, then director of Lucas Online, TheForce.net, May 26th, 1999 (n.3), reporting Kurtz at Sci-Fi Expo, Plano, TX, with daughters Tiffany and Melissa (Jawas as children in *ANH*). Kurtz left *Jedi* in part because “Jim Henson was inviting him to produce his experimental film, Dark Crystal, which he chose instead.” “Original intentions for the nine films as they were laid out BEFORE 1980,” typography locked from Ens’s list:

- EPISODE 1: origins of the Jedi Knights, how they are initiated and trained
- EPISODE 2: introduction and development of Obi-Wan Kenobi
- EPISODE 3: introduction and life of Vader
- EPISODE 4: seven different drafts; pursued buying *Hidden Fortress* rights; “At one point, Luke was a female, Han was Luke's brother, Luke's father was the one in prison (interesting point for some debates) and the film featured 40 wookies”
- EPISODE 5: shooting script “almost exactly” the film; only cuts wampas in the rebel base and “about two minutes of Luke/Yoda Jedi training with no real dialog”
- EPISODE 6: Leia elected “Queen of her people” and isolated; Han dies; Luke confronts Vader and goes on alone; Leia not Luke’s sister
- EPISODE 7: Luke’s life as a Jedi, “very few details planned out”
- EPISODE 8: Luke’s sister (not Leia) appears from another part of the galaxy
- EPISODE 9: first appearance of the Emperor

Kaminski’s restatement of “the main tenants”: hexology of Luke’s tale across VI–IX; Vader dies in VI; Rebel-vs-Empire plot continues; twin sister in VII or VIII is Yoda’s “Other”; Emperor first revealed and killed in IX. The “best conclusion” fans draw—that all of this was compressed into *Jedi* after *Empire*’s cost—is “very logical. However, what Gary Kurtz has said does not appear to be the case.” “Is he trying to re-shape the series’ history? … Not in the least. The problem is that the Star Wars storyline changed so much that Kurtz couldn’t keep up with it, and more than twenty-five years of time has blurred everything together.” Four storylines between 1976 and 1980. “Gary Kurtz is simply confused… his story is impossible! It contains elements which were valid only at separate points in time and never co-existed together.” Flag as Kaminski.

Impossibilities, per Kaminski (cross-ref App. D / Arnold pp. 177, 247–48, n.4): a continuous hexology in which the Empire survives until IX cannot coexist with the private *Empire*-set statement that three trilogies sit twenty years apart and the Rebel-vs-Empire plot wraps in VI. The Jedi sister belongs only to *Empire* draft one, while Father Skywalker was still separate, under the twelve-episode plan, and “was not reprised for draft two.” The “Other” “definitely was not Luke’s sister” until *Jedi* recycled the abandoned sister after the Sequel Trilogy was cancelled. Continuous Luke adventures into VII–IX “probably comes from the 12-film stage.” “What it appears has happened is that Gary Kurtz has combined all the various plot threads… into a sort of Super-Storyline, though some of his comments regarding the original configuration of Return of the Jedi remain valid. The version which Gary Kurtz describes never existed at any point in time.”

Breakdown (flag as Kaminski): nine-episode length from the 1979 plan; prequel trilogy from the 1978 plan; hexology and Emperor-unseen-until-the-end from the 1977/78 twelve-film plan (revealed March 1978 *Time*, “likely in place as early as late 1977”); Vader dying in VI from the 1979 plan. Sister + Other fused from two different versions, “as many fans have done themselves.”

Han’s death / Leia as queen: “harder to say for sure but it appears to have been an alternate version of the Return of the Jedi storyline, created in the event that Harrison Ford refused to appear… Lucas desperately wanted Han to live but if Ford refused he would have to be killed, leaving Leia by herself to take up the Alderaan crest.” Alternatively, Han rescued then killed at the end, changed in 1981. “this is not conclusive.” “some of his comments regarding the original configuration of Return of the Jedi remain valid.” Do not collapse the two alternatives.

## Prequels; 1977 serial plan (pp. 511–514)

Kurtz “may even be ‘selectively’ remembering the aspects which he liked the most.” He “first proposed that rather than three inter-connected trilogies, the series could be individual chapters of a single narrative,” which “the saga would ironically eventually become with Lucas’ new prequel developments in the 1990’s.” *Starlog*, July 1987, p. 41 (n.5): “If we ever got the first film off the ground… I thought it would be nice to do several episodes that fit together into one giant story.”

IGN (n.6): “Well a lot of the prequel ideas were very, very vague. It's really difficult to say. I can't remember much about that at all, except dealing with the Clone Wars and the formation of the Jedi Knights in the first place – that was supposed to be one of the keys of Episode I, was going to be how the Jedi Knights came to be. But all of those notes were abandoned completely.” Film Threat (n.7): “episode one was going to be about the origin of the Jedi and the killing off of the Sith Lords and much more kind of archetypal, political aspects. He's perfectly free to write and make what he wants to make, but because of all of that, I find seeing this film really difficult.” Ens’s Episodes 1–3 list repeated (n.8).

Kaminski: “His comments about the prequels are equally inaccurate. The fact that an entire film is supposedly dedicated to exploring the history of the Jedi is enough to dispel this—in his version, Anakin and Obi Wan are hardly in the storyline. The prequels were created specifically because of the Obi Wan-Anakin storyline” after the 1978 merger. Likely source: the post-release 1977 serial plan. Lucas, *Rolling Stone*, August 1977: “One of the original ideas of doing a sequel [was] that if I put enough people in it and it was designed carefully enough I could make a sequel about anything… One of the sequels we are thinking of is the young days of Ben Kenobi. It would probably be all different actors.” Arnold p. 3 (n.9): “[Gary Kurtz] described [Star Wars II] as ‘a new chapter in the Star Wars saga,’ because the intention is never to refer to it as a sequel for the simple reason that future George Lucas stories do not have chronological sequence.” Ens’s prequel list then reads as 1977-era separate stories (ancient Jedi; Obi-Wan and Father Skywalker in the Clone Wars; Vader appearing only after the Wars to betray and murder Father Skywalker)—combined, in Kurtz’s memory, with the later three-prequel structure. Close: “Gary Kurtz’ statements on the Sequels and Prequels are entirely unreliable, and represent a confused amalgamation of virtually every single plot point from four different storylines from 1976 to 1980. The version of the Star Wars storyline Kurtz describes never existed.” Flag the verdict as Kaminski; keep Kurtz’s quotations intact.
