### 1974 rough draft: four later-combined Vader ingredients (Kaminski reconstruction)

**Speaker:** Michael Kaminski (reconstruction of the May 1974 rough draft; not a Lucas quote).
**Source:** In the 132-page May 1974 rough draft, Kaminski isolates four elements later combined into the screen Vader: General Darth Vader, a minor Imperial flunkey who dies; Prince Valorum, a redeeming Sith; Kane Starkiller, the half-machine Jedi father; and a Jedi family. The draft is “a more convoluted version of the 1977 film,” not a six-film “saga of Darth Vader” outline. Do not collapse this reconstruction with Lucas’s later 2002/2005 “I split the saga / Tragedy of Darth Vader” claims. — Kaminski pp. 45–83 (Ch. II); see also Kaminski pp. 473–490 (App. C).
**Notes:** `notes/167-secret-history-of-star-wars-2008/kaminski-02-ch02.md`; `kaminski-18-appc.md`

### Revised fourth draft: “betrayed and murdered…by a young Jedi, Darth Vader”

**Speaker:** George Lucas (revised fourth-draft Kenobi speech, March 1976).
**Source:** In the hut scene, not only was the father a Jedi: “He was betrayed and murdered…by a young Jedi, Darth Vader.” In the 1977 film Vader and Father Skywalker remain two people; Leia is not the sister. — typography-faithful quote, Kaminski p. 84–130 (Ch. III), Notes n.250.
**Notes:** `notes/167-secret-history-of-star-wars-2008/kaminski-03-ch03.md`

### The Duel / “walking iron lung” (1977 public account; Rinzler p. 111 is the same 1977 interview)

**Speaker:** George Lucas (summer 1977; *Star Wars Poster Monthly*, late 1977). Kaminski identification of Rinzler p. 111 flagged as Kaminski.
**Source:** Lucas, summer 1977 (Kaminski Notes n.208): “It was a whole part of the plot that essentially got cut out…Vader kills Luke’s father, then Ben and Vader have a confrontation, just like they have in Star Wars, and Ben almost kills Vader. As a matter of fact, he falls into a volcanic pit and gets fried and is one destroyed being. That's why he has to wear the suit with a mask, because it's a breathing mask. It's like a walking iron lung.” First official Lucasfilm mention, *Star Wars Poster Monthly*, late 1977 (Notes n.264): Vader “almost killed by Ben Kenobi, who was understandably enraged at his disciple's fall from grace… during the fight, Vader stumbled backwards and fell into a volcanic pit… What remained of his human body was dragged out and preserved by encasing it in an outsize black metal suit -- virtually a walking iron lung… Only his heavy, rasping breath reveals the suit's true function.” Rinzler, *The Making of Star Wars*, p. 111, prints the same “walking iron lung” passage; Kaminski (App. G): “This, however, is a word for word copy from Paul Scanlon's August 25th, 1977 interview with Lucas for Rolling Stone,” not evidence of a late-1975 dating. “there still is no evidence of it being in place before 1976.” — typography-faithful quotes, Kaminski pp. 97–98 and Notes n.208, n.264; Kaminski App. G n.8 (p. 519–525).
**Notes:** `notes/167-secret-history-of-star-wars-2008/kaminski-03-ch03.md`; `kaminski-22-appg.md`

### December 1975 Foster meeting: “we learn who Darth Vader is” (not a Father-Vader reveal)

**Speaker:** George Lucas (to Alan Dean Foster, December 1975). Kaminski / App. G reading flagged as Kaminski.
**Source:** typography locked (Notes n.304): “I want to have Luke kiss the princess in the second book. The second book will be Gone With the Wind in Outer Space. She likes Luke, but Han is Clarke Gable. Well, she may appear to get Luke, because in the end I want Han to leave. Han splits at the end of the second book and we learn who Darth Vader is…In the third book, I want the story to be just the soap opera of the Skywalker family, which ends with the destruction of the Empire. Then someday I want to do the backstory of Kenobi as a young man—a story of the Jedi and how the Emperor eventually takes over and turns the whole thing from a Republic into an Empire, and tricks all the Jedi and kills them. The whole battle where Luke’s father gets killed. That would be impossible to do, but it’s great to dream about.” Kaminski: in December 1975 the film did not yet reveal Vader as the father’s murderer; that entered the March 1976 revised fourth draft (n.305). Appendix G reads “we learn who Darth Vader is” as the killer-of-the-father origin, later moved into the revised fourth draft, **not** a Father-Vader reveal. Do not collapse the Lucas quote with that reading. — Kaminski p. 131–153 (Ch. IV), Notes n.304–305; Kaminski App. G.

### “That icon of evil sort of took over everything, much more than I intended”

**Speaker:** George Lucas.
**Source:** typography locked, Kaminski pp. 145–146 (Notes n.319): “Darth Vader became such an icon in the first film… That icon of evil sort of took over everything, much more than I intended.” “Darth Vader was supposed to be a memorable villain, yes, but not as important as audiences made him out to be.” Kaminski: hence more screentime and expanded mythos in the sequel; in *Star Wars* he “has only nine minutes of combined screentime.” — Kaminski pp. 145–146, Notes n.319.

### 1977 backstory notes “about seven or eight pages”; “I never really intended it to be turned into a movie”

**Speaker:** George Lucas.
**Source:** typography locked (Notes n.239–242): “It isn’t really until it evolved into what is close to what Star Wars now is that I began to go back and deal with the stories that evolved to get us to that point.” The backstory notes were “about seven or eight pages” by the final draft: “I never really intended it to be turned into a movie… just a little backstory of what the Jedi were like, and what the Republic was like, and what the relationship of Obi Wan and Darth Vader was…and the Emperor; ‘what is the Empire?’” Keep this 1977 statement next to, and not collapsed with, the later Vaughan 1999 “seven or eight pages” and Pollock 2005 “twelve 5–8-page synopses” entries. — Kaminski Ch. ### Brackett, February 23rd, 1978: Father Skywalker’s ghost is still separate from Vader

**Speaker:** Michael Kaminski, quoting the Brackett first-draft text (February 23rd, 1978).
**Source:** typography locked, Kaminski p. 165: “in the first draft of Empire Strikes Back, Father Skywalker’s ghost appears to Luke! … The ghost of Luke’s dead Jedi father appears while Luke is training on Dagobah and gives Luke advice. Naturally, when Luke finally faces Darth there is no ‘father revelation’—he beckons Luke to join the darkside, Luke refuses, Vader attacks Luke and Luke jumps off the ledge; the point of Luke’s confrontation with Darth is that he refuses the darkside. Father Skywalker is described as ‘a tall, fine looking man,’ and is referred to only as ‘Skywalker.’ Luke takes the oath of the Jedi from his father.” Kaminski, p. 161, on Lucas’s earlier *Empire* outline: “You will notice that there is no ‘I am your father revelation’ in Lucas’ outline. Nor would there be in the first draft screenplay. This is the most crucial development in all of Star Wars’ story history.” — Kaminski pp. 161, 165.

### April 1978 second draft: “I am your father”

**Speaker:** George Lucas (*Empire* second draft, April 1978, p. 128 of that draft).
**Source:** typography locked from the script block, Kaminski pp. 166–169. Luke: “Enough! He said you killed him.” Vader: “I am your father.” “The two have been battling all through this dialog. Luke pulls away at this revelation. The two warriors stand staring at one another; father and son.” Luke: “That’s impossible. It’s not true.” Vader: “Search your feelings you already know it to be true. Join me.” Kaminski dates the fusion to after Brackett’s late-February draft and before this April second draft — “around March.” The Emperor “doesn’t become the shadowy, black-hooded sorcerer until the second draft of Empire Strikes Back—when Father Skywalker was merged with Darth Vader.” Lucas decided Ben’s 1977 tale could be read as a lie to protect Luke, “and that in a sense, Father Skywalker metaphorically ‘died’ when he became Darth Vader. It was a bit of a stretch… It also gave Lucas the option of backing out if he changed his mind after completing the film.” — Kaminski pp. 166–180.

### Mid-1990s: Palpatine officially a Sith; Rule of Two “a complete turnaround”; “Darth” becomes a designation

**Speaker:** George Lucas (on the Rule of Two). “Complete turnaround” and the “Darth as designation” dating are Kaminski.
**Source:** typography locked, Kaminski Ch. IX, Notes n.682: “One of the themes throughout the films is that the Sith Lords, when they started out thousands of years ago, embraced the dark side… they killed each other. Eventually, there was only one left, and that one took on an apprentice… there could never be any more than two of them, because if there were, they would try to get rid of the leader, which is exactly what Vader was trying to do, and that's exactly what the Emperor was trying to do… And that is the antithesis of a symbiotic relationship, in which if you do that, you become cancer, and you eventually kill the host, and everything dies.” Kaminski: “Thus was born the so-called ‘Rule of Two,’ a complete turnaround from the previous incarnation in which they were a warrior clan with many members.” “Darth,” once Vader’s first name, then Anakin’s Sith alias from 1978, “now… became a Sith designation (see appendix ‘Dark Father’).” Flag the last two sentences as Kaminski. — Kaminski Ch. ### 2005 “Dark Father” etymology vs. 1970s “Dark Water… Vaders and Wilsons and Smiths” (do not collapse)

**Speaker:** George Lucas (two accounts, years apart).
**Source:** *Rolling Stone*, June 2, 2005, typography locked, Kaminski App. C n.1: “Darth is a variation of dark. And Vader is a variation of father.
