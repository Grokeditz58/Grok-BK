# Kaminski, *The Secret History of Star Wars* (e-book v3.0, 2008): notes — Chapter I: The Beginning (pp. 17–44)

Fully read. Citations: Kaminski p. N; Kaminski Notes n. Y (End Notes begin p. 421; Ch. I notes start at n.6). Kaminski glosses flagged. Staff/crew/cast statements labeled by speaker.

## Childhood, father, crash, anthropology (pp. 17–26)

Opens after *American Graffiti*: Lucas tells an interviewer his next project is “a western movie set in outer space,” then “Don’t worry… Ten year old boys will love it.” (No endnote on this opening anecdote.)

Biography from Pollock and later interviews: Modesto, George Sr., stationery store (Kaminski twice prints “stationary”). Lucas: “I was as normal as you can get.” (n.6, Harmetz, *American Film,* June 1983.) On his father: “He was the boss; he was the one you feared.” (n.8, Pollock p. 36.) “I’ve always had a basic dislike of authority figures, a fear and resentment of grown-ups.” (n.9, Pollock p. 36.) The refusal of the family business and “I’m going to be a millionaire before I’m thirty!” (n.10, Pollock p. 38) — already familiar from earlier notes, typography here as Kaminski prints it.

**Kaminski gloss, pp. 18–19, not a Lucas quote:** “It is not hard to pinpoint the theme of Luke Skywalker fearing he would become like his father, Darth Vader, as stemming from Lucas’ issues with his own. The two Lucases are perhaps more alike than the filmmaker would wish.” Lucas to *Playboy,* 1997 (n.11, Weinraub, July 1997): “I’m the son of a small town businessman… He was conservative, and I’m very conservative, always have been.” George Sr.: “A scrawny little devil”; “He never listened to me… He was his mother’s pet… He was always dreaming up things.” (n.12–13, Clarke, *Time,* May 23, 1983.)

Fantasy diet: comics, TV, war books, westerns, pulps (Harrison over Asimov), *Metropolis* and *Forbidden Planet,* Republic serials and *Flash Gordon.* Long Arnold 1979 interview block (n.21, Arnold pp. 220–222): not much of a reader until college; *Flash Gordon* strip and serials at nine and ten; “Well, loving them that much when they were so awful, I began to wonder what would happen if they were done really well.” Comic art as sociology; Frazetta, Druillet, Moebius. Co-owned Supersnipe Comic Emporium in New York in the 1970s (Kaminski’s report). Long Kristen Brennan “Star Wars Origins” excerpts on *Buck Rogers* and Burroughs / *Flash Gordon* (n.22–23) — Brennan’s wording, not Lucas’s.

Crash, typography locked (n.24, Harmetz; n.25, *60 Minutes,* March 28, 1999; n.26, O’Quinn, *Starlog,* August 1981): seatbelt ripped; “They thought I was dead… I wasn’t breathing and I had no heartbeat. I had two broken bones and crushed lungs.” “I wasn’t just in an accident, I was in an accident that by all logic I should have been killed… ‘How did I survive? Why did I survive?’ ” He decides racing is not for him and applies himself at Modesto Junior College.

Anthropology major, typography locked (n.27, Ty Burr, *Boston Globe,* October 2005): “Well, I started out in anthropology, so to me how society works, how people put themselves together and make things work, has always been a big interest. Which is where mythology comes from, where religion comes from, where social structure comes from.” Kaminski says “It is here that Lucas first came to be exposed to the works of Joseph Campbell” — that “here” is Kaminski’s inference, not a dated Lucas citation on this page. Space as the new border of myth (n.28, Seabrook, *The New Yorker,* January 6, 1997): “Myths, stories from other cultures… there was no longer a lot of mythology in our society… Westerns used to provide that, but there weren’t westerns anymore. I wanted to find a new form… It comes from the borders of society, from out there, from places of mystery—the wide Sargasso Sea. And I thought, space.”

## USC, 21-87, writing, Zoetrope, *THX*, *Graffiti*, the space-opera idea (pp. 26–44)

**Howard Kazanjian** (n.31, Pollock p. 41): USC instructors would say, “Good morning, this is editing, and although we’re here to teach you the fundamentals of editing, you’ll never use them because you’ll never get into the upper echelons of the industry.”

Canyon Cinema / San Francisco underground: Lucas and **John Plummer**. Silberman, *Wired,* May 2005 (n.33) on Baillie, Belson, Brakhage, Conner; Plummer: “That’s when George really started exploring.” Kaminski misspells Godard as “Goddard” and Truffaut as “Trauffaut” (p. 27). Haskell Wexler; Lucas disproves the legend that Wexler got him into USC (Pollock, n.35). George Sr.: “I fought him… I didn’t want him to go into that damn movie business.” (n.37, Pollock p. 41.)

**21-87 / Arthur Lipsett, p. 29 (n.39).** Lucas: “I said, ‘That’s the kind of movie I want to make—a very off-the-wall, abstract kind of film. It was really where I was at, and I think that’s one reason I started calling most of my [college] movies by numbers. I saw that film twenty or thirty times.’” Kaminski ties *21-87* to *THX 1138* and *Look at Life*, but **does not, in this chapter, quote Kroitor’s “some kind of force” line or Lucas’s later “echo of that phrase in 21-87.”** Do not import that from Jones 166 into this chapter.

Kurosawa via John Milius; long-lens / silent-film influence (n.40). Writing, typography locked (n.41–42): “I had no intention of becoming a writer… I don’t think I am a good writer now. I think I’m a terrible writer… I hated stories, and I hated plot, and I wanted to make visual films that had nothing to do with telling a story.” “When I sit down I bleed on the page.” *Look at Life* in an animation class (n.43). Student *THX 1138 4EB* from a Murch/Robbins “Breakout” treatment. Draft, diabetes, 4-F; Saul Bass; Woodstock and Altamont camerawork; returns as TA and shoots the student *THX*. Scholarship on “*McKennan’s Gold*” (Kaminski’s spelling). Meets Coppola on *Finian’s Rainbow*.

**Coppola** (n.49): “I was working on the show and there was this skinny kid, watching for the second day in a row… I went up to him and asked who he was.” On *The Rain People* (n.50): “I’ve had it with these big Hollywood movies… I’m going to start in New York, get in a truck and drive across the United States, making a movie as I go. No planning, no nothing—just do it.” Coppola puts documentary money in the budget so Lucas can write the feature *THX* (n.51). **This, Kaminski says, is “the most earliest and primitive beginnings of what would eventually become Star Wars”** — because Coppola forced him to write. Lucas: “I come from experimental cinema… [Coppola] told me, ‘you have to learn to write, to structure.’ So it’s because of him that I got into it. He forced me.” (n.52.) Coppola’s advice: write fast, don’t polish page one (n.53). Oliver Hailey draft rejected; Lucas and Murch write the script (n.54). John Korty; Zoetrope as a Danish-style commune (Coppola, n.56–57). **John Milius** on the opening party (n.60): “There was a lot dope being smoked, a lot of sex; it was great.” **Walter Murch:** “The clatter of film was heard twenty-four hours a day.” (n.61.)

New Hollywood context: **Paul Schrader**, **Peter Guber**, **Ned Tanen** (who “would later green-light *American Graffiti* and reject *Star Wars*”) on studios leaving “kids” alone (n.63–65). *2001* as inspiration (n.66). Feature *THX* from Orwell/Huxley (n.67). Lucas: a “documentary film of the future” (n.68); unofficial DP; pan only with moving objects (n.69). **Murch** on Japanese cinema and unexplained ritual in SF (n.70): “a Japanese film would just have the ritual and you’d have to figure it out for yourself.” Thirty-five-day shoot; edited in the attic with Marcia.

**Marcia Lucas, pp. 38, 43–44 — staff, typography locked.** Married 1969, Mill Valley. “A professional editor… would be responsible for the picture cutting of nearly all of her husband’s films, and unbeknownst to most, would be one of the prime influences in the shape of the *Star Wars* films during their writing and editing.” That “prime influence” claim is Kaminski’s. Marcia: “I always felt I was an optimist because I’m extroverted. And I always thought George was more introverted, quiet, and pessimistic.” (n.73.) Lucas: “I say black, she says white.” (n.74.) **Murch:** “Marcia was very opinionated, and had very good opinions about things, and would not put up if she thought George was going in the wrong direction. There were heated creative arguments between them—for the good.” (n.75.) She was “not very pleased with *THX 1138* because she felt it did not engage the audience.” **John Milius** (n.95): “She was a stunning editor… Maybe the best editor I’ve ever known, in many ways,” moving scenes in *The Wind and the Lion* and advising Lucas, Spielberg, and Scorsese. Credits listed: *Rain People,* *Medium Cool,* *Filmmaker,* *Graffiti,* all three *Star Wars* films (Oscar on the first), plus Scorsese titles. Marcia on timing a baby: “He didn’t want the extra responsibility at that time because he might be forced into taking a job he didn’t want to take.” (n.97.)

Warners recut *THX*; Zoetrope deal cancelled. Lucas’s “chop off one of its fingers” analogy (n.76). *Apocalypse Now* with Milius, intended as 16mm *Dr. Strangelove*-style documentary satire (n.77–78); later fulfilled, Kaminski notes, in the 16mm Vietnam material of *More American Graffiti.* Footnote credits John Baxter with linking the approach to Wexler’s *Medium Cool.*

**First space-fantasy mentions, pp. 40–44.** Lalo Schifrin “supposedly has said” Lucas confided as far back as 1969 that he wanted a *Flash Gordon* type of film — Kaminski hedges with “supposedly.” First issue of *Bantha Tracks*: “as early as 1971 [Lucas] wanted to make a space fantasy film.” (n.79.) **Marcia Lucas** (n.81): “I think that damn movie was whirring through the editing machine in George’s head on the day we met… Even when he was a film student at USC, he spent a lot of his time thinking of ways to get those spaceships and creatures on the screen.” **Mona Skager**, Zoetrope / *Rain People* script supervisor (n.82): “George was watching television—and all of a sudden he started talking about holograms, spaceships and the wave of the future.” *THX* would begin with a clip of Buster Crabbe as Buck Rogers. Lucas (n.83): “I conceived [Star Wars] at about the same time I finished *THX*… I came up with the idea of doing a sort of modern mythology, like Saturday morning serials for kids. I came up with two ideas: one was Indiana Jones and the other was Star Wars.”

Cannes 1971, typography locked (n.84–85): last $2,000, Eurail, backpacks; David Picker / UA “two-picture deal for *American Graffiti* and *Star Wars.*” At that point he “hadn’t yet developed any story or content but rather the concept of a *Flash Gordon*-esque space opera” — Kaminski’s characterization. **Richard Walter** (n.86): “I’m a Jew from New York. What do I know about Modesto? We didn’t have cars. We rode the subway, or bicycles.” Huycks polish *Graffiti*: “the scenes are mine, the dialog is theirs.” (n.87.)

**Gary Kurtz**, as Lucas recalls meeting him (n.88): Kurtz and Monte Hellman visit the Mill Valley attic to see Techniscope / a Steenbeck for *Two-Lane Blacktop*; Coppola thought Kurtz right for *Apocalypse* because he had been a Marine sergeant; after Cannes, Lucas asks him onto the “hot-rod movie” instead. Kaminski: Kurtz would be “regarded as his personal manager (though his creative involvement is sometimes exaggerated).” *Lady Ice* $100,000 offer refused (n.89). *Graffiti* shot “very much like a documentary” (n.92). Coppola: “he stood ’em up and shot ’em, and [the actors] were so talented… it was just lucky.” (n.91.) Directing “dealing with people, sometimes very difficult people… I just didn’t enjoy it.” (n.93.) Pollock via Kaminski: he “long dreamed of making a space movie” that would evoke the serials (n.94).

Chapter closes, typography locked (n.99): broke again after *Graffiti*; Marcia working; “that’s when I decided that I wanted to do a children’s film… Why don’t you make some kind of *Taxi Driver* movie? … I said, ‘No, no—I think I’ll just go off in a completely different direction.’ … I’d had this idea for doing a space adventure… I picked one off and said, ‘This space epic is the one I want to do.’ Like *American Graffiti,* it was such an obvious thing that I was just amazed nobody had ever done it before.”
