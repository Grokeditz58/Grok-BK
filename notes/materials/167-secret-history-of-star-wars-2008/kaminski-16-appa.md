# Kaminski, *The Secret History of Star Wars* (e-book v3.0, 2008): notes — Appendix A: The Great Mystery of the Journal of the Whills (pp. 450–467)

Fully read. Appendix footnotes restart at 1; cite as Kaminski App. A n. Y or Kaminski p. N. Kaminski’s detective reconstruction and “edited into Bouzereau” hypothesis are flagged as Kaminski. Staff labeled. This appendix is the book’s systematic demolition of the “massive pre-script galactic chronicle” legend, distinguished from the real two-page 1973 *Journal of the Whills* synopsis Rinzler published in 2007.

## The legend versus the documents (pp. 450–456)

Popular description: a large pre-script chronicle containing all background, including the prequels; stories of the saga only a small part; written as if by historian-beings called Whills. Against the linear path already locked (Flash Gordon → 1973 *Hidden Fortress* treatment → 1974 rough draft → 1976 final). A brief two-page *Journal of the Whills* outline before the thirteen-page treatment “is unrelated to the ‘history of the galaxy’ Journal discussed here.”

First recorded in-universe use: second draft (1975) Bible-like opener, typography locked: “… And in the time of greatest despair there shall come a savior, and he shall be known as: THE SON OF THE SUNS.” “Journal of the Whills, 3:127.” Later replaced by “A long time ago, in a galaxy far, far away…” Public introduction: 1976 novelization’s two-page Palpatine/Republic history ended “—From the Journal of the Whills.” 1979 *Art of Star Wars* final-draft title: “Star Wars…as taken from The Journal of the Whills.” Reinforced by twelve-film *Bantha Tracks* claims and Arnold: “I have treatments on all nine films… I also have voluminous notes, histories and other material I’ve developed for various purposes” (App. A n.1).

*Annotated Screenplays*: 1973 forty-page “Journal of Whills,” then the rest; “in compiling this information and these background details, Lucas created a reference book he called ‘The Journal of the Whills,’ which eventually became the starting point for the Star Wars saga” (App. A n.2, n.5, n.11). Hearn: a massive tale broken into thirds, first section adapted as the first draft.

Start-date errors: Seabrook, *New Yorker*, January 6th, 1997: February 1972–May 1973 for the thirteen-page summary; Biskind and Edwards also cite 1972, often “after he had filmed *American Graffiti*”—impossible, filming was summer 1972. 1970s interviews, Pollock, and Lucas himself: writing started after *Graffiti* was completed, spring 1973. 1977 Souvenir program: began the script January 1973. *Rolling Stone* 1980 and later Rinzler: after the January 28th, 1973 *Graffiti* preview. “The February 1972 date stems from Pollock… and all subsequent reports of a 1972 date are merely due to people citing Pollock.”

## The “Mace Windu / Windy” mix-up; Rinzler’s two-page document (pp. 453–459)

Pollock p. 134 (App. A n.3), typography locked: “By May 1973, he had completed a bewildering thirteen-page plot summary. Handwritten on blue-lined paper, it tells ‘the story of Mace Windu, a revered Jedi-bendu of Opuchi who was related to Usby C.J. Thape, padawaan learner to the famed Jedi.’ With that as its opening sentence…” Those names are not in the May 1973 treatment. *Annotated Screenplays* intro (App. A n.4): “a forty-page outline entitled ‘Journal of the Whills’ about ‘Mace Windy, a revered Jedi Bendu of Opuchi,’ as told by ‘C.J. Thorpe, Padawaan learner of the famed Jedi.’ Following the outline was a list of characters.” Spelling conflict Windu/Windy, Thape/Thorpe; Kaminski notes *Star Wars* had a deleted-Anchorhead character named Windy.

Pollock’s and Bouzereau’s *summaries* of “the treatment” are the May 1973 General Luke Skywalker / princess / bureaucrats story; Mace and Thape/Thorpe never return. Bouzereau source list (p. 454), typography locked: “Partial handwritten outline entitled \"Journal of the Whills\" (no date) / Handwritten list of characters (no date) / Handwritten list of planets (no date) / The Star Wars--synopsis (no date) / The Star Wars, May 1973--treatment typed from original notes.” Kaminski: Bouzereau’s only description of the Mace document is “a word-for-word quote” from Pollock; “the main body of the book operates in ignorance to this document’s existence”; “it is my belief that they have been edited into Bouzereau’s book after Bouzereau wrote it.” Flag as Kaminski. Baxter and Hearn repeat Pollock’s single line (Hearn: 1972 massive Journal, “C.P. Thorpe,” cut to a third for the $3 million budget, then Annikin and Kane).

Pollock’s “treatment” summary is itself a “confused fusion”: May 1973 plot plus 1974 rough-draft names (princess “Leia Aguilae” / Aquilae; General Darth Vader; Prince “Valarium”; “General Luke Skywalker, one of two surviving Jedi knights (along with his friend Annikin Skywalker)”) that belong to later drafts. Kaminski first hypothesized a lost 1974 treatment (Rinzler later found a third-draft treatment).

**Rinzler, 2007** (*Insider* 92; *Making of Star Wars* p. 8, App. A n.8): a story pre-dating the May 1973 *Hidden Fortress* synopsis, titled *The Journal of the Whills*, “Mace Windy, a Jedi-Bendu or Jedi-Templer.” “A mere two pages long, written by hand,” not forty and not incomplete. Plot (brief): Mace takes an apprentice, C.J. Thorpe, later expelled from the order; serial cliffhanger, the two Jedi dispatched by “the chairman” on a secret mission. First-person, two parts: Thorpe remembering his apprenticeship; Thorpe’s greatest mission. Lucas has “never referred to its existence” except the Lippincott 1978 making-of interviews that became Rinzler. *Prevue*, 1980 (App. A n.7): “It wasn’t until I finished American Graffiti in ’73 that I actually started writing [Star Wars]. My original 14-page treatment didn’t bear much relationship to the final production, though.”

Typography of the opening line, per Rinzler: not Pollock’s “Mace Windu…as related to Usby C.J Thape” (misreading of messy printing) but “The story of Mace Windy…as related to us by C.J. Thorpe.” Footnote: QWERTY Y/U adjacency may explain Pollock’s Windu typo.

Conclusion of Parts One–Two: “there are actually two Journal of the Whills—the first is Lucas’ ill-fated two-page attempt at creating a space opera and the second is an in-universe concept of a galactic history-book which the films themselves are contained within.” “The Journal of the Whills, in the form that Lucas and Lucasfilm propound—that is, a massive tome which contained all the history of the Star Wars galaxy—does not, and never did, exist.” **Pablo Hidalgo**, *Insider* 79, p. 55, November 2004 (App. A n.6): “The whole saga is said to be taken from the Journal of the Whills. But ultimately that strange attribution is just meant to suggest that the Star Wars saga has been around for eons and has been adapted and interpreted from earlier works. There is no 'real' Journal of the Whills— George Lucas added that to spice things up a bit.”

## In-universe concept, Vaughan, 1987 Q&A, Force, *Sith* cut scene (pp. 460–467)

Lucas (Rinzler p. 9, App. A n.9): “Journal of the Whills came from the fact that you ‘will’ things to happen… The introduction was meant to emphasize that whatever story followed came from a book.” Second draft: a Holy book inside the universe, Luke as Son of the Suns. Later title: *The Adventures of Luke Starkiller as taken from the Journal of the Whills / Saga One / The Star Wars*—the movie itself “originated from this document.” 1976 novelization prologue used as a fake excerpt. “there was never any actual development done apart from the concept.” This “led to Lucas saying that there were twelve stories (and later nine)… already known to him.”

Notes binder “jokingly nicknamed The Journal of the Whills.” Kaminski believes this is the red binder in the first Episode I web doc (also used for original *Star Wars* and *American Graffiti*). Lucas (Bouzereau p. 6, App. A n.10): “I eventually dropped this idea [of using the Journal of the Whills in the films], and… the Whills became part of this massive amount of notes, quotes, background information that I used for the scripts.”

**Johnny Vaughan**, *The Big Breakfast*, July 16th, 1999 (App. A n.12), typography locked: “The way I imagine it is that George has this great big leather book, covered in dust, it's the Chronicles of Space and you've written the whole thing already… Is that right?” “No, that's wrong.” “You don't have the complete story, mapped out from the start, all those years ago?” “No.” “Okay, nice one, so you're winging it.” “No, I have a little story treatment, a little outline that says this happened here, this happened here… but the ones I'm working on now, was the back story which I'd written out, which was this was where he comes from, this was were he comes from, this is what the Clone Wars were about; it's just a little outline that goes right through the plot of the movie and where the characters came from and what they did - it's only about seven or eight pages.” Same seven-or-eight-page outline already locked in Chs. IX–X.

*Starlog* convention Q&A, 1987 (Bill Warren, *Starlog* 127, February 1988, p. 49, App. A n.13): “What is the Journal of the Whills?” “I’m not sure I can explain that. It’s where the Star Wars saga came from; it was a larger work that I had been working on, of which Star Wars was just a piece.” 1997 *Annotated Screenplays* (App. A n.14): “The stories were actually taken from the ‘Journal of the Whills.’” Kaminski: he “could not reveal that the Journal was fraudulent.” Flag as Kaminski.

Possible Tolkien source (Kaminski, flagged): *The Silmarillion* (Tolkien died 1973; book published 1977); closer analogue, the Red Book of Westmarch / first-edition *Fellowship* foreword presenting the book as a found translation.

*Annotated Screenplays* p. 6 (App. A n.15, n.17), typography locked: “Originally, I was trying to have the story be told by somebody else; there was somebody watching this whole story and recording it, somebody probably wiser than the mortal players in the actual events. I eventually dropped this idea, and the concepts behind the Whills turned into the Force.” **Mark Hamill**, *Bantha Tracks* 18, November 1982 (App. A n.16): suggested the title *The Other Shoe Drops* instead of *Revenge of the Jedi*, “a misnomer. It's really not right for Jedi to have revenge unless what we see in this part of the ‘Journal of the Whills’ was recorded by non-Jedi.” Journal subtitle “not reprised for any of the sequels.”

Second-draft Force “less mystical”; fate/destiny sat with the Whills (Son of the Suns prophecy) until the third draft and *Empire*. 1999 (Bouzereau/Duncan, *Making of Episode I*, pp. 8–9, App. A n.18): “The Force breaks into two sides: the living Force and a greater, cosmic Force. The living Force makes you sensitive to other living things, makes you intuitive, and allows you to read other people's minds, et cetera. But the greater Force has to do with destiny. In working with the Force, you can find your destiny and you can choose to either follow it, or not.” Kaminski: “The greater, cosmic Force is what the Whills became—the Will of the Force.”

*Revenge of the Sith* cut Polis Massa scene (script, novel, comic; cut “for pacing”), typography locked from the script (pp. 465–466): Qui-Gon (V.O.): “When I became one with the Force I made a great discovery… You will become more powerful than any Sith.” “The ability to defy oblivion can be achieved, but only for oneself. It was accomplished by a Shaman of the Whills. It is a state acquired through compassion, not greed.” Later Yoda to Obi-Wan: “The secret of the Ancient Order of the Whills, he studied… How to join the Force, he will train you. Your consciousness you will retain, when one with the Force. Even your physical self, perhaps.” Kaminski: “debatable if this version of the Whills should even be considered a canoninical part of the Star Wars story.” Off-the-cuff to **Rob Coleman** on the *Sith* set (Rinzler p. 72, App. A n.19): R2-D2 recounts the story to the keeper of the Journal a hundred years after *Jedi*—“private and tongue-in-cheek,” but “Lucas still was partial to his original conception of a literal Journal.”

Footnote, “Son of the Suns” in TPM / *Jedi* SE celebrations: starwars.com February 28th, 2007 podcast (App. A n.20); original Skywalker Sound recording is “merely jibberish Huttese and various crowd yellings.”
