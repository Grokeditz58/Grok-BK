# Kaminski, *The Secret History of Star Wars* (e-book v3.0, 2008): notes — Chapter XII: Stitches (pp. 354–376)

Fully read. Citations: Kaminski p. N; Kaminski Notes n. Y (Ch. XII starts at n.781). Kaminski glosses flagged. Staff labeled. *Attack of the Clones*: nine-month pre-production, late Dooku, Jango replacing Boba, clones as the Republic army (a “complete 180”), Sido-Dyas rewritten in pick-ups, Hales polish arriving too late, script finished as Lucas boarded the plane.

## Broad strokes, Tatooine, romance, clones (pp. 354–359)

After Episode I, Lucas “mulled… and didn’t start writing until the fall,” with filming set for June. **Jody Duncan** (n.781): European vacation after release, but “the movie… was never far from his thoughts… he scribbled down ideas.” Discovered Lake Como, later the romance setting. Script and designs targeted for May 2000: “only a nine-month pre-production period from the September start” (n.782) versus three years for Episode I.

Tatooine / Lars homestead links the trilogies. Shmi has married **Cliegg Lars**, leaving Anakin a step-brother, **Owen Lars**. “This dashed the previous notion that Owen was in fact Obi Wan’s brother, developed during Return of the Jedi and even present in that film’s novelisation, and restored Owen and Beru’s status as legitimate relatives of Luke.” C-3PO bought by the Larses (cut: Padme first dresses him in metal coverings). Shmi kidnapped by Tuskens; sequence “derived from John Ford’s *The Searchers*”; she dies in his arms; massacre “giving him his first taste of the darkside.” Kaminski flag: “This section would initially be a crucial turn for Anakin’s character and set up his fall… but, as we will see in the next chapter, Lucas would re-write Anakin’s arc and eliminate this angle.”

How much of II was planned in the mid-90s “is unknown.” I, *Empire*, *Jedi* “predominantly made up on the fly”; III “largely already known.” “The gap between Episode I and Episode III is still largely mysterious.” Broad strokes known (love, Clone War, army, mother dies, Palpatine’s ascent); “Lucas himself has been somewhat tight-lipped… in order to preserve the myth that all was worked out decades prior.” Episode II “seemed to serve more as a link… filling in the little blanks.” McCallum and Bocquet scouted Tunisia summer 1999 (n.783) and “several lakeside locations in northern Italy… a Naboo summer retreat” plus Caserta (n.784). Aurra Sing’s podrace cameo may have become **Zam Wesell**; “This then would indicate the assassination plot surrounding Padme existed beforehand.” Lucas called II a “romance” or “love story.” **Marcus Hearn**: wedding originally bigger and earlier; Lucas made it gradual (n.785). **Ian McCaig** (n.786): “At one point, George said the wedding might be at the center of the conflict, rather than an idyllic moment.” Footnote: Geonosis arena “very conveniently timed” with *Gladiator*—Kaminski speculation.

Boba “always tied to the Clone War” (*Bantha Tracks* 1979); Special Edition insertion “might have been made with his upcoming prequel role in mind.” Enlarged from “anonymous participant” to “central figure at the heart of the Clone War’s origins.” McCallum, *Insider* August 1998 (n.787): “He’s definitely going to play a part in Episode II… There’s a real story point for Boba Fett.” **Jango** “an entirely new character”; “it seems fairly apparent that Jango wasn’t merely added… he replaced Boba,” now a cloned son. “Boba Fett’s central role… clearly foreshadowed an important place in the next film—although, as we will see… Lucas then eliminated him.”

1980: Jedi “betrayed” by a “trap” set by the Emperor (n.788); 1977: Vader secretly assassinating, then “they tried to regroup, but they were eventually massacred by one of the special elite forces led by Darth Vader” (n.789). “The clones of the infamous Clone War were not an outside enemy… This seems to be a complete 180 from Lucas’ original plan.” Earlier versions: Lando’s clone planet; Boba and “Imperial Shocktroopers” from across the galaxy, wiped out by the Jedi; 1981 *The World of Star Wars*: stormtroopers “actually cloned human beings.” Footnote: third-draft “white legions.” Now: Palpatine engineers a civil war, controls both sides, Trade Federation among Separatists, clones are the Republic army led by Jedi Generals, later “programmed” to turn. Lucas (n.790): Order 66 as “one of those inevitable pay-offs… I was always worried in Episode II that I was giving away too much… they mention… Lord Tyranus and Count Dooku are the same person… And that Darth Tyranus is the one who started the clones. So if you are paying attention it is very easy to figure out… they’re going to be the ones that betray everybody.”

Yoda (and later Palpatine) lightsaber duels: “faithful to Lucas’ first conceptions”—first *Empire* draft, Yoda fences Ben’s ghost; *Revenge* rough draft, Emperor has a saber. “The characters’ subsequent transformation… provides merit to the criticisms.”

## Dooku invented late; noir; writing alone; Hales too late (pp. 359–371)

**Count Dooku / Darth Tyranus “invented considerably late… during the pre-production period of Episode II.”** Outline merely: Sidious fosters Separatists, possibly via a powerful politician; a new apprentice duels Yoda and Anakin; Anakin would slay him in III “as his final act towards the darkside (this would inevitably be re-structured).” First thought like Maul; unused Episode I “Sith Witch” later **Asajj Ventress** (n.791); Lucas “suggested a robot-cyborg,” later **General Grievous** (n.792). Then: a different character played by **Christopher Lee** (n.793)—“the opposite of… Maul, instead a thinking man, older… elegance and regality.” Then: “the Separatist leader and the Sith apprentice would be the same person.” Kaminski: “a similar situation and process which led to the merging of Father Skywalker and Darth Vader in 1978, or… General Darth Vader and Prince Valorum in 1974.” Disillusioned Jedi; later pick-up: he was Qui-Gon’s master (from Jocasta Nu comparing them). “In effect, Lucas combined Palpatine and Anakin into a new character.” Cut “Lost Twenty” scene quoted at length (pp. 360–361): bronze bust; “Only twenty Jedi have ever left the Order”; “The Lost Twenty… Count Dooku was the most recent and the most painful… much like your old Master, Qui-Gon Jinn… very individual thinkers… idealists… He disappeared for nine or ten years, then he just showed up recently as the head of the separatist movement.” “Organic and unplanned… entirely serendipitous.” Then “practically disappear[s] from Episode III.”

Abandoned 1978 *Empire* environments reused (n.794, *Annotated Screenplays*): Water Planet, Ttaz the Rock/Desert Planet, Garden Planet, Ton-mummd the Grass Planet, City Planet. Kaminski: Kamino and Otoh Gunga; Geonosis from Ttaz; Naboo from garden/grass; City Planet → 1981 Had Abbadon → Zahn’s Coruscant, “adopted by Lucas” (n.795). Kaminoans from 1977 “Whatnots,” “tall, thin, white, maybe similar to… Close Encounters” (n.796).

Padme family/childhood sequence cut. Kenobi’s brashness “transferred to Anakin”; Lucas “seemingly split the difference and gave him a wise-cracking, swashbuckling edge.” Anakin remade: “rosy-cheeked, optimistic boy” replaced by “a brooding and disobedient teenager-on-the-verge-of-adulthood.” Kaminski: the “how a good person becomes bad” exploration is “absent from the film—the transformation is an offscreen one… In a way, Lucas reverted back to the initial characterisation of the impatient student Darth Vader from 1977.”

Obi-Wan’s plot “fashioned after the ‘film noir’ movies of the 1940’s” because “Anakin and Palpatine’s arcs… seem to comprise the majority of Lucas’ outline” (n.797). Detective subplot: murder, hitmen, forgeries, diner “modelled after the very same one seen in American Graffiti.” Kaminski essay on noir flagged as gloss. Episode personalities “startlingly dissonant”: I “whimsical children’s fantasy,” III “operatic character-based tragedy,” II “direct emulation of pulp… Mysterious Island to War of the Worlds to Flash Gordon” (n.799). Lucas on the romance (n.800): “I wanted to tell the love story in a style that was extremely old-fashioned… much more like a movie from the nineteen thirties… a slightly over-the-top poetic style… I knew a lot of people might not buy it… more sophisticated, cynical types also don’t buy that stuff.” Footnote, **Harrison Ford**, 1978: “the film was very contemporary. The characters were very contemporary as well.” Kaminski: Lucas “reverted to his default perspective of Star Wars—1930’s serial material,” story “pulled in two different directions,” like the failed first recut of *Empire*.

Lucas, *Insider* after the 1999 New York premiere (n.801): “I’m back as a director now, and I will be directing II and III. I found going back to directing just like I hadn’t stopped.” Recap of 1970s collaborative circle; De Palma and **Jay Cocks** helped rewrite the crawl (n.802). **Coppola** (n.803): movements happen because people “cross-pollinated each other.” **Milius** on the implosion (n.804): “as soon as they got money, everyone turned on each other… Steven and George had tremendous power, and they never asked me to do anything for them.” Scripts “unsurprisingly weaker”; “Even Lucas realized that he was in trouble once he finished Episode II and had Jonathan Hales do a polish.” Art department shaped plot without a “counterbalance” of character/dialog. **Hamill**, 2005 (n.805): “He’s in his own world… like William Randolph Hearst or Howard Hughes… You can see a huge difference in the films that he does now and the films that he did when he was married… [Marcia] was really the warmth and the heart of those films… Now he’s so exalted that no one tells him anything.” **Willard Huyck**, 1983 (n.806): “When you’re that successful and you’ve been proven right too many times, you don’t give people an opportunity to argue with you because they can’t argue with success.” 1977 hope that others would carry on *Star Wars* “in a sort of ‘film school competition’” (n.807); 1981 doubts anyone else could script the prequels (n.808).

First major picture “shot without a foot of film” (Sony/Panavision HD). Footnote: prototype sensors “backfired,” more work for bluescreen; **John Knoll** quoted. Production moved to Sydney, “the first Star Wars film not shot in England.” McCallum, 1999 (n.809): “We were actually planning to do II and III back-to-back. But once George started to really talk about what he wanted in II, it was better to not try and compress them… the [technology] that can happen in two years is just extraordinary.” Lucas (n.810): three days writing, two design; later Saturdays too. Casting rumours: DiCaprio, Joshua Jackson, Paul Walker; **Hayden Christensen** announced May 12th, 2000, “just weeks before the start of photography.” McCallum, February 2000 (n.811): “We’re not putting any pressure on him… right now we don’t need a script—we just need the locations… When will the script be completed? I don’t know—whenever George hands it to me! It’s like Citizen Kane—the script was finished two days before the film started shooting.” In fact finished “the day before Lucas left for Sydney.” Lucas (n.812): “My assistant handed me that draft to proofread as I was getting on the airplane, just one week before I started shooting. Nobody on the crew had received a script yet. I had given them a rather detailed outline of what the scenes were… but that was it.”

**Jonathan Hales** (early 60s; UK TV, *Loophole*, story for *The Scorpion King*—footnote) brought in as shooting was about to start. Kaminski: “scenes were already written and locked… Hales’ main duties then would have been mainly to clean up the dialog… he simply came on board far too late to have significant impact.” **Hayden Christensen** (n.813): “[I was] definitely worried when I read the script for the first time… The dialogue was, well... I didn’t know how I could make it convincing.” McCallum at Episode I premiere, May 1999, claimed a quarter of II already written, finished by September (n.814)—“Factually, this was untrue”; Lucas “did not begin scripting until September… according to himself” (n.815). “the script never even was given an official title.”

Set construction late February; “a complete set every other week until March, and then, from March on, we had to build a set a week” (n.816); seven days, fourteen hours (n.817). Bocquet (n.818): “George had always told us that Episode II was going to be a much smaller, more intimate film than Episode I… But it didn’t turn out that way.” McCallum: “backwards,” “designing a skyscraper without building a foundation” (n.819); “Do you know what it’s like to budget a film without a script?” (n.820). Rough draft dated **March 13th, 2000** (n.821)—filming two months later; contrast *Jedi*’s nearly twelve months from Lucas’s first screenplay. Chiang remembers a draft in January (n.822). Lucas (n.823): started September, “page one… as quickly as possible… I do lots of drafts… I called the first one typed up by my assistant my ‘rough draft,’ but I’d done fourteen or fifteen drafts before that one.” Footnote: Coppola’s late-60s advice (see p. 33). Third draft to Hales while Lucas left for Sydney (n.824). Hales (n.826): “Our first story conference lasted a day and a half, and I mainly listened… I wrote the first draft. After George read it, he called me from the set in Australia and said, ‘Some of this is brilliant, some of it is not so brilliant—we’ll talk about what’s not so brilliant.’… I wrote another draft.” Lucas rewrote Hales into the final as shooting commenced (n.827). Script to crew “just three days before principle photography” (n.828).

Production June 26th, 2000, Fox Studios Australia, then Italy and Tunisia; original Lars homestead re-created. Also filmed an Episode III shot of Obi-Wan delivering infant Luke, later redone on a soundstage. Droid-factory fight created entirely in pick-ups; Kaminski notes Spielberg’s *Minority Report* car-factory parallel. **Ryan Church** (n.829): “Some of the things that are normally figured out during preproduction were actually being done during production… more typical of an [animated] feature.”

## Sido-Dyas, title, reception (pp. 372–376)

Pick-ups created “an elaborate mystery angle… that remains conspicuously unresolved: Sifo-Dyas.” Final film: a well-known Jedi who died before the order; “a red-herring.” **Original shooting script: there is no Sifo-Dyas. Sidious himself orders the clones using an alias, “Sido-Dyas.”** Script blocks locked (pp. 372–374): Lama Su, “Please tell your Master Sido-Dyas… He is well, I hope?” Obi-Wan V.O.: “I've never heard of a Jedi called Sido-Dyas.” Mace: “No. Whoever placed that order was not a Jedi, I can assure you… A clone army! Ordered by someone in the Senate perhaps... Someone's out to start a war.” Yoda: “Inform the chancellor of this, we must.” Mace: “Who do you think this impostor Sido-Dyas, could be?” Another scene: Sido-Dyas asked Kaminoans to keep it secret; Lama Su: “This army is for the Republic… We are also very much against this Count Dooku.” “The name Sido-Dyas was too obvious… you wouldn’t need a Ph.D to realise that Sido-Dyas is just a variation on Sidious.” Rewritten in pick-ups to assert the character “actually existed and was personally known,” but “Lucas was not quite prepared to deal with all the issues he raised… the central mystery of Attack of Clones basically remains unacknowledged.”

Untitled in production, “jokingly named in the script as ‘Jar Jar’s Great Adventure.’” Title announced August 6th, 2001 (n.830): “harks back to the sense of pure fun… classic movie serials and pulp space fantasy.” Just after human-cloning news. Fan reaction “generally negative.” **McGregor**, on-camera (n.831): “Is that it? That’s a terrible, terrible title.” Later: “they hadn't even told me!… it does sound like a sort of ‘Flash Gordon in A-t-t-a-c-k-O-f-T-h-e-C- l-o-n-e-s!’ But I suppose… maybe that’s just the way it should be.”

Calmer release: virtually no fast-food tie-ins, more adult marketing. Ebert: “It is not what’s there on the screen that disappoints me, but what’s not there.” *L.A. Times*: “only a teenage boy would not notice the flimsy emotions.” **Stephen Hunter**, *Washington Post*: “it’s too long, it’s too dull, it’s too lame.” **Mike Clark**, *USA Today*, on the last 45 minutes; *Time* on Yoda’s “martial artistry”; *Newsweek*: “A decidedly mixed bag.” “Considered an improvement… even if just barely” (n.832). Surpassed $300 million; IMAX truncated. *Simpsons*: Lenny and Carl, “I say Phantom Menace sucked more,” “No, I say Attack of the Clones sucked more!” Vacation in Canada, then Episode III. Lucas (n.833): “Act III is when everything comes together. That’s going to be the most fun to do.”
