# 057 / jk-04: Appendix, the Design Team, the Justin Chin interview, and back matter (PDF 399–426)

Prima *Jedi Knight: Dark Forces II* guide by Rick Barba (1997). Citations are PDF pages. **Printed page = PDF − 13** (so PDF 400 ≈ printed 387). This chunk is the most valuable part of the book for lore and development history.

## Framing (p400)
- Barba spent a week on site: "I arrived the day after code release, which means the final" … "version of the software was frozen in place." He calls Justin Chin the "project leader, writer, designer, and all-around Jedi" … "visionary".

**Development details:**
- **Rahn's monologue nearly lost (p404):** Gaber's computer crashed while he and Chin were writing Rahn's dream-sequence monologue. Gaber: "We drove like" "maniacs from my place to work repeating the speech, line by line".
- **Robel's design rule (p403):** "I’m always looking for ways" "to create the sense that this character has a real person inside."
- **Derleth's design rule (p410):** "If I have one design philosophy, it’s to script situations that constantly point back to the central theme."
  - Barons Hed draws on "the pretty clear model of Mos Eisley" plus Turkish and Greek architecture.
  - For Mission 9 he spent a day at the Chevron plant in Richmond: "Pipes, tanks, pipes, more tanks."
- **Missions 18 and 19 were once one level (p412).** They were designed by Doug Shannon, who "left the company". Stephens: "Doug did all the geometry and most of the puzzles". Shannon's cylinder "was so huge, the Jedi team decided to cut it in half" (the OCR breaks these words across lines). Compare p182: Missions 5 and 6 were also one level at first.

## Interview with Justin Chin (pp413–418)
- **Barba and Chin knew each other before (p413).** They worked together at Accolade on *Lost in L.A.* and tried to start a design studio in 1992.
- **Influences (p413):** "The Seven Samurai—and all the Akira Kurosawa" films, "which I knew deeply influenced George". Also "The Good, the Bad," "and the Ugly". The Valley of the Jedi stands in for that film's "legendary gold".
- **The Dark Jedi (p414):** "the seven Dark Jedi, sort of like the" "evil seven samurai".
- **Kyle's backstory (p414):** "Originally, he" "was an Imperial soldier, and in fact became one because he thought his" "father was killed by Rebels." He was at the Imperial Academy when Morgan Katarn was murdered.
- **8t88 (p414):** "He’s a manipulator who wants to be powerful."
- **Rahn and Morgan (p415):**
  - Rahn "as a young Jedi, was a wanderer, sort of an orphan".
  - The valley is "like Atlantis".
  - Jerec "tries to" "recruit Rahn into his fold".
  - Morgan found the valley "on another planet, which we left" "unnamed".
  - Jerec "had built his palace there in Barons Hed".
  - Rahn refuses to learn the location. He "convinces Morgan to hide the information and" "pass it on to his son".
  - Rahn "gives Morgan the lightsaber to pass on" "to Kyle".
- **Personal story (p416):** "It’s about my own childhood, growing up with my father," "then losing my father." Jason Court, who plays Kyle, "had" "a very complicated, troubled childhood himself".
- **The Maw scene (p417):** Maw "taunts him about putting his father’s head on a" "spike". Chin: "we were totally stunned by the real emotion Jason" "brought to that scene".
- **Moral meter (p418):**
  - CHIN: "Yeah, the banality of evil."
  - He credits Reed Derleth with the Ugnaught choices.
  - "A true Jedi uses the" "Force for good."
