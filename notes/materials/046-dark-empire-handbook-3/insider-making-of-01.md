# Star Wars Insider #157–159 (Titan, 2015): "Of Rebel Dreams and Dark Empires: The Making of a Star Wars Legend" by Michael Kogge, parts 1–3

These official Insider article pages were included in the xC9wHbKZ compilation. **Comparison with 038:** the 038 Insider hand-read covers issues up to #153 (plus specials), so #157–159 were never noted. Everything below is new.

## Part 1: Insider #157 (May/June 2015), pp. 44–51; cover p. 001
- **Cover p. 001:** "Lords of Misrule" (Emperor and Vader), Anthony Daniels, the 10th anniversary of RotS, Jeffrey Brown. Issue #157, display until 06/09/15. Only the cover and the article pages are in the compilation.
- **p. 44:** Veitch "saw it as the beginning of a new era". He dreamed up "sequels and prequels, epics concerning the Clone Wars, the Sith, the Jedi". Includes the DE #1 cover by Dave Dorman.
- **p. 45:** DE #2–5 covers (Dorman).
- **p. 46, "A Proposal":** on November 19, 1988, Veitch wrote to Lucas proposing a painted series "in the same fully-painted style of *The Light and Darkness War*".
  - Lucy Wilson (director of publishing) called; on Nov 29 "Lucas had given his blessing and the project was a go". By Dec 2 Veitch had a concept tentatively titled "The Jedi Chronicles". It was "an arduous three-year journey that nearly saw the comic terminated more than once". DE #6 cover.
- **p. 47:** a Kennedy panel captioned "The *Millennium Falcon*, retrofitted with the latest lightspeed ion engines, bursts out of hyperspace amidst the wreckage of a great space battle!" The opposite-page caption notes issue #4 "showed something fans had speculated about for a long time: Leia brandishing a lightsaber".
- **pp. 48–49, "Tom's Story":** Veitch's biography: Golden Age comics, EC, Kirby, Columbia, a monastery stay, underground comix, Allen Ginsberg.
  - DE panels: "The ravages of civil war! Mutinous Imperials are deadlocked with forces loyal to the Emperor's inner circle for possession of a world that once ruled an Empire!" and the AT-AT battle in which Luke's signal "came from the center of the city … near the old Imperial palace".
- **p. 50, "Cam's Story":** Kennedy's biography (Glasgow, D.C. Thomson *Commando*, *Battle*, *2000 AD*, *Rogue Trooper*, *Judge Dredd*). It reproduces the original proposal excerpt "Notes on proposed STAR WARS graphic novel":
> "Our basic idea is to use a framing device which could potentially enclose a lot of different stories about the subject that interests us most: the Jedi. … an outlaw monastic order or secret society charged with keeping alive the memory of 'the days when Jedi were numerous and the Force was honored and respected throughout the Republic.' … would be called, most probably, The Jedi Chronicles." — Insider #157 p. 50
> "our first ambition is to examine the period between the first and second trilogies, when Darth Vader rose to preeminence as 'Dark Lord of the Sith'. We are told that it was during this 20 year span following the Clone Wars that Vader 'hunted down and exterminated the remaining Jedi'--all except Yoda and Obi-Wan Kenobi. … we would want to focus on the greatness of the martyred Masters, their wise and ingenious use of the Force, their awesome exploits against the Empire, and ultimately, their heroic deaths." — Insider #157 p. 50
  - (ANALYSIS: DE began as a Purge-era "Jedi Chronicles" pitch; the 1988 framing used the "20 year span" between the trilogies.)
- **p. 51, "When Cam Met Tom (Through Steve)":** Steve Bissette introduced them in Vermont, which led to the *Light and Darkness War* pitch to Archie Goodwin at Marvel Epic. DE "would become one of the best-selling comics of the 1990s". "More to Say": DE is available under the Legends banner from Marvel; Titan is reissuing *Light and Darkness War* on June 23.

## Part 2: Insider #158, "Of Light and Darkness: The Making of Dark Empire – Part II" (Kogge), pp. 38–43
- **Covers** (the compilation has p. 001 plus alt and alt2): the newsstand "We're Home!" Han and Chewie cover (Countdown to TFA; "Make Mine Star Wars"; Lando); a subscriber Chewbacca cover; and a Han Solo cover ("Star Wars Origins … Journal of the Whills"; Celebration aftermath; Rebels season two). These are official variant covers, not fan-made.
- **p. 38:** the pitch was "a prequel to the original trilogy, not a sequel"; Veitch called it *The Jedi Chronicles*. It repeats the proposal quote dated "December 2, 1988". Image: Dorman's DE #2 cover, "a darker side to Luke Skywalker".
- **p. 39:** Lucy Wilson "rejected Veitch's proposal on the grounds that it might interfere with a prequel film trilogy George Lucas was considering making". She suggested continuing after RotJ, the "first sequel to the original film trilogy—in any form … a full year before author Timothy Zahn signed a contract to write his *Heir to the Empire* novel."
  - "The Light and Darkness War" section: Bissette introduced Kennedy to Veitch.
- **p. 40:** *Light and Darkness War* (Epic; Lazarus Jones, Lord Na, the Deadsiders) was inspired by Jung and Vietnam. Captions: "Luke takes on the cloned Emperor; the Empire's impressive battleship, the *Eclipse*". DE panel text: "You have filled the galaxy with your darkness … I have seen that ultimately the dark side will fail!" / "Jedi fool … in spite of the stories you tell yourself, I am the stronger … did I not warn you?" / "Now take the consequences of your failure—like your father before you!"
- **p. 41:** the Menteps live "in a kind of ongoing state of mystical rapport with 'the Source,' which is pure light and consciousness". Lord Na is "a send-up of the Marvel super-villains". "Deadsiders" is attributed to Burroughs and Eric Frank Russell. The series inspired James Cameron's paraplegic Jake Scully in *Avatar*.
  - "From Lazarus to Luke": the new pitch's first page asked that the villain "should be the supreme Master of the dark side from the films, Emperor Palpatine".
  - Rejected theories for his survival included that he "was not flesh-and-blood at all, but a machine", or that he was:
> "actually possessed by an evil alien intelligence. 'This entity would probably come from outside the galaxy, and it would have a parasitical relationship to the Force,' Veitch wrote at the time, and 'its political subversion of the Republic (through the agency of Palpatine), would be part of a plan to transform the galaxy into a massive anthead society, with itself at the center.'" — Insider #158 p. 41
  - (ANALYSIS: abandoned concept, not canon. Note it as development history only.)
- **p. 42:**
> "Veitch's idea that the Emperor found a way to survive by transferring his life force at the moment of annihilation into the bodies of younger clones. Palpatine's ultimate goal in the series would be to take possession of the body (and soul) of his arch-nemesis, the last of the Jedi, Luke Skywalker. … George Lucas later used an iteration of this idea in *Revenge of the Sith*, when Darth Sidious reveals to Anakin Skywalker that if they work together, they could learn the dark side secret of subverting death." — Insider #158 p. 42
  - "The Return of Archie Goodwin" (who was "Mr. Star Wars"). Facsimile of Veitch's handwritten notes; only partly legible, e.g. "Major Emperor revelation 2/3 of way in", "clone aspect not so pat", "Luke – shadow Darth Vader", "Visualization of use of Force … new ways?", "Alliance corrupted from within?", "Vader sends a signal … Emperor might want to keep + use that".
  - DE panel, Palpatine to Leia: "Nothing magical about it, my dear—primitive *hologram* technology. The old Jedi you see was part of a group that lived in the *Adega system* six hundred years ago … they took it on themselves to make a *record* of Jedi history and teachings … for the Jedi of the future … like *yourself*."
- **p. 43:** Goodwin suggested "the idea that the Empire would put somebody else inside Vader's costume". The nine-page proposal went to Goodwin on December 30, 1988.
  - Lucasfilm's one exception: "Anything to do directly with Vader was out. 'George is working on a prequel'"; a "'training hologram' featuring Vader" was allowed.
  - Goodwin left for DC in May 1989; a year later the contracts were still unfinished. "*Dark Empire* was suddenly in serious jeopardy."
  - Panels: "Okay, team—we're through the terminal gate!" and "Luke … I'm seeing him. He's *commanding* the Imperial forces—like our father!"

## Part 3: Insider #159, "Death to the Dark Side! The Making of Dark Empire – Part III" (Kogge), pp. 38–43
- **Covers:** p. 001 (Vader and Ahsoka "Jedi Rebel"; "Mysteries of the Force! How the Star Wars prequels enhanced the mythos!"; Asajj Ventress tale) and alt (Ventress subscriber cover). Official.
- **p. 38:** "Over 25 millennia … devotees of each battled in a seemingly eternal war of noble Jedi against evil Sith."
  - "Artist in Jeopardy": by March 1990 Kennedy had finished his issue #1 pages. Kennedy's pencils with Todd Klein lettering.
- **p. 39:** Kennedy was told to seek other work. Veitch suggested Andy and Adam Kubert as replacements.
  - "Visions of the Future": Bantam's novel licence created a continuity problem. Barbara Kesel: "a miniature freak-out in the office when we learned that twins were in the offing for Leia, when our story had focused more on the idea of [a] 'child.'" Lucasfilm had no Keeper of the Holocron at the time.
- **p. 40:** Veitch on Jung, "integrating the Shadow". The Byss darksiders were modelled on the Assassins' Guild under Hassan-i Sabbah, Hitler's Gestapo, and "the dark angels serving Satan".
> "the Force Storm—by explaining it was not a chaotic super weapon, but a 'function of two powerful minds focused on each other: Luke and the Emperor. It's as if a wormhole in the Force had opened between them, causing a massive release of energy.'" — Insider #159 p. 40
> "'The attempt to create a stable society based on the power of the dark side is what the Emperor—and *Star Wars*—is all about,' Veitch wrote to Lucasfilm." — p. 40
> "'The "secrets" of the dark side *must* be assimilated in order for the dark side to be finally conquered. Otherwise there is only endless combat, endless war, numberless deaths.'" — p. 40
  - DE was set "shortly after Zahn's trilogy" with a bridging crawl.
  - Images: Dorman's pencil cover rough for #1; Boba Fett and Dengar ("The Sarlacc found me somewhat *indigestible*, Solo." / "Your friend Mako has been real helpful").
  - (ANALYSIS: authorial intent says the Force Storm was produced by Luke and Palpatine together. Treat it as creator commentary, not in-universe text.)
- **p. 41:** Lucasfilm agreed to log stories on a timeline, which set a precedent for continuity.
  - "A Dark Horse to the Rescue": Mike Richardson saw the #1 art at San Diego Comic Con in 1990: "Cam is just the best *Star Wars* artist in comics. Nobody else even comes close." Lucasfilm granted Dark Horse the licence, "a 23-year chapter".
  - "Cam Kennedy's Star Wars": McQuarrie said he viewed Kennedy's art with "envy"; Kennedy traded pages with Mœbius.
  - World Devastator over Calamari panel: "Very true … many years ago the Mon Calamari were conscripted as slave laborers by the Emperor … when we resisted, our cities were attacked, to set an example".
- **p. 42, sidebar "Tom Veitch on the Light and the Dark,"** quoting DE #1's text pages:
> "'A Jedi, from the beginning, must do what most men cannot: develop a sensitivity to the very existence of the Force. … his conscious awareness must join the Force, so that the knowledge available through the Force becomes his own. … By listening, by becoming peaceful, by turning his attention to the Force, he finds that place where his individuality is joined to the knowledge and power of the Universe.' Opposed to this knowledge is the dark side, which channels the Force through anger, the will-to-power, unfettered violence, and instilling fear in the weak-minded." — Insider #159 p. 42
  - "An Empire for the Ages": DE #1 premiered in December 1991 and sold "hundreds of thousands of copies". Lucas gave copies as the annual Lucasfilm Christmas gift.
  - Script facsimile p. 10–12 (the Falcon and AT-AT on Coruscant; "Leia: I can feel the Force moving through me … guiding my hands …").
- **p. 43:** script facsimile pp. 2–3 (roll-up text; "Two Alliance escort frigates … amidst the wreckage of a great space battle"; Han and Leia "have been married for five or six years"). Climax panel:
> "With all his hatred funneled into the death storm, the malevolent genius is caught off guard by the invisible wave of *life energy* surging over him, separating him from his power— … Enveloped in light, cut off from his great rage, the Emperor finds himself unable to *control* the dark power he has unleashed— … —and the cataclysmic *storm* turns against its maker!" — DE panel reproduced in Insider #159 p. 43
  - "Dark Empire remains perhaps the most spectacular *Star Wars* comic ever produced". More to Say box.
