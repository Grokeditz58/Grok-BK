# Owner Material 038 — Star Wars Insider #1–153 plus specials (with The Lucasfilm Fan Club Magazine #1–22): The Lucasfilm Fan Club Magazine #6 (1989)

- **Source:** *Star Wars Insider* magazine (issues 23–153, 1994–2014) and its predecessor *The Lucasfilm Fan Club Magazine* (issues 1–22, 1987–1994), plus seven special editions: articles, interviews, comics and short fiction Secondary source (licensed Legends reference). Scan OCR'd on the box; the scan stays box-only and is **not** published.
- **Method:** every scanned page was OCR'd and machine-screened sentence by sentence for power-relevant statements (fights, kills, defeats, Force feats, rankings, superweapons) that name a character on our roster; each kept sentence is quoted verbatim from the OCR text and has a crop of the printed passage directly under it. Pages with nothing power-relevant are still listed with what is on them.
- **Citation:** "*The Lucasfilm Fan Club Magazine #6* (1989), p.X" by printed page ("scan p.X" where no printed number applies). **Weight:** Licensed magazine: a secondary source. Its short fiction and comics are Legends stories; its articles and interviews sum up or discuss lore and rank below the novels, comics and games they describe.

## scan p.6 (scan page 6)

Page contents: Lucas interview: his love of editing, possibly directing again, compromise, heroes (Kurosawa, Welles, Fellini), More American Graffiti as his biggest disappointment, the Last Crusade exploring Indy's father and youth, and Harrison Ford. Real-world. (hand-read)


## scan p.7 (scan page 7)

Page contents: Lucas continued: Hitler's real occult interest, the Lucas/Spielberg division of labor (Lucas on story and script, Spielberg on directing), characters evolving across drafts rather than arriving "full blown", Temple of Doom, Star Wars conceived after American Graffiti mail, the trilogy steering ten years, all characters "my favorite kids", Raiders as the most fun film. Real-world. (hand-read)


## scan p.8 (scan page 8)

Page contents: Lucas continued: Star Wars' success "a fluke"; its legacy of inspiring kids; more Star Wars "someday". He confirms the first trilogy will cover a young Obi-Wan Kenobi, a young Darth Vader and the early Jedi, with the Droids continuing throughout. He would want a Howard or Spielberg to direct; cost is the main obstacle. Real-world production statements, not feats. (hand-read)
