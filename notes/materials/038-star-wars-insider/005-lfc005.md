# Owner Material 038 — Star Wars Insider #1–153 plus specials (with The Lucasfilm Fan Club Magazine #1–22): The Lucasfilm Fan Club Magazine #5 (1988)

- **Source:** *Star Wars Insider* magazine (issues 23–153, 1994–2014) and its predecessor *The Lucasfilm Fan Club Magazine* (issues 1–22, 1987–1994), plus seven special editions: articles, interviews, comics and short fiction Secondary source (licensed Legends reference). Scan OCR'd on the box; the scan stays box-only and is **not** published.
- **Method:** every scanned page was OCR'd and machine-screened sentence by sentence for power-relevant statements (fights, kills, defeats, Force feats, rankings, superweapons) that name a character on our roster; each kept sentence is quoted verbatim from the OCR text and has a crop of the printed passage directly under it. Pages with nothing power-relevant are still listed with what is on them.
- **Citation:** "*The Lucasfilm Fan Club Magazine #5* (1988), p.X" by printed page ("scan p.X" where no printed number applies). **Weight:** Licensed magazine: a secondary source. Its short fiction and comics are Legends stories; its articles and interviews sum up or discuss lore and rank below the novels, comics and games they describe.


## scan p.1 (scan page 1)

Page contents: Cover (viewed): Jeff Bridges as Preston Tucker with the Tucker car; "Inside: Tucker, Indy III, Willow". OCR was nearly empty. (hand-read)


## scan p.2 (scan page 2)

## scan p.3 (scan page 3)

Page contents: "Collections: Foreign Star Wars Collectibles" by Adam Schultz: repackaged versus truly foreign items, the Intergalactic Trading Company, the Anakin and Yak Face figures, Palitoy's cardboard Death Star, the Marvel UK comic, Japanese R2-D2 toys and the Takara Vader. Real-world. (hand-read)


## scan p.4 (scan page 4)

Page contents: "Is It Real or a Replicar?" by Lisa E. Cowan, part 1: the 22 real Tuckers and four fibreglass replicars built by Tom Sparks and Mike Fennell on Ford Galaxie chassis. Real-world. (hand-read)


## scan p.5 (scan page 5)

Page contents: Replicar article concluded: the "Stude-Tucker" built from a 1951 Studebaker, Buddy Joe Hooker's cannon-driven three-roll stunt in one take, and the real 1948 roll-over. Real-world. (hand-read)
