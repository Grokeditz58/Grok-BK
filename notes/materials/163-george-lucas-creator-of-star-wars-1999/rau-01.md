## Ch. 1: "What's going on here?" (pp. 7–9)

- Opening night, May 25, 1977; Hamburger Hamlet; traffic jam on the way; Lucas asks, "What's going on here?" Crowds heading to the Chinese Theater. Authors' framing: Lucas had created "something that kids, and adults, needed—a modern fairy tale."
- **Lucas, p. 8 (typography locked; crop `quotes/q01-p08-fairy-tales-unknown-force.jpg`):** "There's a whole generation growing up without any kind of fairy tales," Lucas had said. "And kids need fairy tales—it's an important thing for society to have for kids." The pull-quote repeats the second sentence.
- **Authors, not a Lucas quote, same page:** "With *Star Wars*, Lucas had done it. He had created Luke Skywalker—a hero led by an unknown force who battles the dark side and wins." Lowercase "force."
- Lucas so tired he had forgotten the release date. Through the restaurant window he watched fans pour into the theater, "amazed." Authors close the chapter with the film's own formula, in quotation marks: he had created "a long time ago in a galaxy far, far away."
- Caption p. 9: Loews Astor Plaza line, "Eager crowds line up to see *Star Wars* when it was first released in 1977."

## Ch. 2: Comic books, television, and race cars (pp. 10–19)

- Birthdate is correct: May 14, 1944 (p. 11). Parents George Walton Lucas, Sr. and Dorothy Bomberger, married 1933; sisters Katherine, Ann, Wendy; housekeeper Till; mother often hospitalized. Small-town Modesto.
- **Lucas, p. 12, comics shed:** "We had so many that eventually my dad built a big shed in the back, and there was one room strictly devoted to comics, floor to ceiling," George says. Favorite: Scrooge McDuck; also Batman and Robin, Superman, *Amazing Stories*. Television: *Adventure Theater*, *Flash Gordon*.
- **Lucas, p. 14, school:** "One of the big problems I had . . . was that I always wanted to learn something other than what I was being taught."
- Racing and cars; crash June 12, 1962: Chevy into a Fiat, flipped four or five times, thrown through the open roof, Modesto City Hospital.
- **Lucas, p. 18 pull-quote / body:** "The fact that I was still alive was a miracle." Continues p. 19: "I realized that I'd been living my life so close to the edge for so long. That's when I decided to go straight, to be a better student, to try to do something with myself." *Straight* is spelled correctly here (the 2015 Rise comic lettered STRAIT). Diploma delivered to the hospital room three days later; grades not good enough for a four-year school; Modesto Junior College.

## Ch. 3: A legend and star is born (pp. 20–28)

- MJC could not match racing at first; then sociology, anthropology, literature, creative writing; a friend's 8-mm camera. Graduated MJC June 9, 1964, associate in arts. John Plummer suggested USC. Haskell Wexler may have helped with acceptance. George Sr. wanted L. M. Morris; they argued.
- **Authors, p. 22:** "George vowed to his father that he would be a millionaire before he turned thirty." Father taught "strong morals and principles, honesty, and the value of hard work." A salary-as-tuition deal. Entered USC as a junior.
- **Lucas, p. 22:** "When I got to film school, the other students said, 'You really can't make movies here. They don't give you enough film, they don't let you keep the camera for very long.'" George said. "Well, I made eight films at USC. . . . It was difficult, and there were lots of barriers, but it wasn't impossible."
- **John Plummer, p. 23:** "This was a guy who never had any direction before, and now suddenly he had asserted himself and became totally devoted to one thing, film."
- **Lucas, pp. 23–24:** "my whole life was film—every waking hour. It was all new, neat, and exciting." And: "the department never taught us much, other than the basics. They opened the door, but we had to go inside and find out for ourselves. . . . Whenever I broke the rules, I made a good film, so there wasn't much the faculty could do about it." Favorite role: editor. He believed shooting did not control the film; editing did.
- Student films named: *Look at Life*, *Herbie*, *Freiheit*, *The Emperor*, *1:42:08*. **Spielberg, p. 25:** "George Lucas was always the star of the student film festivals then."
- Graduated USC August 6, 1966, BA in cinema (correct, vs. the 2015 Rise comic's 1967). Drafted, against the Vietnam War; a physical found diabetes; rejection from the armed services at age twenty-two (no Air Force / speeding-tickets story).
- Verna Fields hired him at USIA as an assistant grip; Marcia Griffin was an assistant editor; both born in Modesto; married February 22, 1969. Returned to USC for one graduate semester in 1968; nights and weekends directed the 15-minute *THX 1138:4EB*. Columbia scholarship on *McKenna's Gold* (spelled correctly, vs. Rise's MCKENNA'S). Warner Brothers 1968 six-month observation scholarship. Only production on the lot: *Finian's Rainbow*, Coppola directing. Coppola: "We started a conversation and a friendship." Lucas joined the crew of *The Rain People*.
