# Rau and Rau, *George Lucas: Creator of Star Wars* (1999): notes part 3 — chs. 6–9 and back matter (pp. 68–112)

Source: owner's MEGA book-scan cbr `tTkERLKY`. Citations are printed footer numbers. Typography locked from the JPEGs.

## Ch. 6: Thrilling the audience (pp. 68–79)

- Indiana Jones among icons "besides Luke Skywalker and Darth Vader." The character began as Indiana Smith, named after his dog Indiana; archaeology interest from a Modesto Junior College course. 1975 meeting with Phil Kaufman, who suggested the Lost Ark of the Covenant. 1977 Hawaii vacation with Spielberg (they had first met at a student festival where Lucas won an award for *THX 1138:4EB*). Lucas motto: "We can do anything, and we can figure out how to do it for a price." Spielberg designs shots as they happen; Lucas creates films in the editing room. Spielberg suggested changing Smith to Jones because Smith sounded too common.
- Lucas imagined Indy "rebellious, but with some morals, a lot like Han Solo." "He has to be a person we can look up to," "We're doing a role model for little kids, so we have to be careful. We need someone who's honest and true and trusting." Spielberg envisioned someone "a little more sleazy"; they compromised on someone "like Harrison Ford." Lucas first looked at Tom Selleck (about to start *Magnum, P.I.*); CBS held on. Lucas wanted to try different actors because Ford had already appeared in five Lucasfilm productions; finally Ford. Plot as "either he's chasing them, or they're chasing him." Talked Spielberg down from two thousand Arab extras to six hundred. $20 million, seventy-three days, two weeks under; Lucas had final cut and he and Marcia edited.
- *Temple of Doom* 1984, Katz and Huyck. **Huyck:** "George wanted it to be really scary." **Richard Schickel, p. 75:** "The whole Lucas emphasis is on special effects, on loading the film with optical tricks that can be created only in movies, [and this] . . . opens the audience's mind . . . to the connections between a seemingly simple tale and the richer realms of myth." (Brackets and spaced ellipses are in the book.)
- **Lucas, p. 75 KEY quotation:** "On the one hand I'm doing these huge productions and at the same time I'm helping on these little productions, for my friends. They're all interesting movies, movies that I cared about and wanted to see made one way or another. Some of them were small failures, some of them were huge failures, and some were extremely nice movies. But in most of the interviews with me, and even within the company, they're passed right over, as though they never existed. But those movies may be closer to what I am than *Star Wars*." The pull-quote is the last sentence.
- Lumage in *Twice Upon a Time* (1982). Awkward grouping of Kurosawa / *Kagemusha* (1980) among "young up-and-coming filmmakers." Also *Body Heat*, *Mishima*, *Latino*, *Labyrinth*, *Captain EO* (Lucas wrote, Coppola directed, Michael Jackson starred). *Howard the Duck* (1986); a critic said "the Force was not with him." *Willow* (1988), Ron Howard; *Tucker*; *The Land Before Time*. Television: *The Ewok Adventure: Caravan of Courage* and *Ewoks: The Battle for Endor* as ABC Sunday Night Movies, 1984–85; *Ewoks* and *Droids Adventure Hour*, fall 1985–86. The Indy trilogy was completed in 1989 "though there are rumors that a fourth film is in the works." *Last Crusade* $44 million vs. *Doom* $30 million. Began as "a Chinese legend about a Monkey King in Africa," then the Holy Grail. *The Young Indiana Jones Chronicles* (1992) from Lucas's educational foundation.

## Ch. 7: Back to the war in the stars (pp. 80–86) — 1997 Special Editions and the 1999 prequel frame

- Special Edition rerelease, January / February / March 1997. **Lucas, p. 80:** he "had not been entirely happy with the films when they were first released. He now says that the original *Star Wars* 'was about sixty percent of what I wanted it to be.'" Ideas for creatures and effects were not possible in the 1970s–80s for money, time, and technology.
- Restored deteriorated film; added the Jabba scene (cut because Jabba could not be created to Lucas's satisfaction), Tatooine creatures, a "monster" cantina, more spectacular space battles. **Authors, pp. 82–83 (notable given later Greedo/Han controversy):** "Lucas made no major changes in the movies, but he feels the new effects complete the picture of the world he was trying to create." $15 million to redo, more than *Star Wars* had cost in 1977. **Lucas, p. 83:** "It's like a revisiting of the entire event. I certainly didn't expect this to happen." P. 85: "It's nice to think of [the movies] as timeless." (Brackets are in the book.)
- **Authors, p. 85 KEY nine-movie / Vader-became-evil passage; crop `quotes/q04-p85-nine-movie-vader-became-evil.jpg`:** "The summer of 1999 will see the return of Lucas's world to the big screen. *Star Wars: Episode 1* will be released, starting Lucas's return to the first three episodes in his nine-movie epic. These "prequels," or the movies that come before the three films that fans have already seen, will cover information fans have always wanted to know. How did Darth Vader become evil? Why did Obi-Wan Kenobi spend all those years hiding on Tatooine? What did Yoda do before he ended up on his swamp planet of Dagobah? What were the Clone Wars? Hopefully, Lucas will answer all of these questions along with many more in the next three films." This is the authors' framing, not a first-person Lucas quotation. "He is not only writing the new films, but he will also direct at least *Episode I*. This will be the first film Lucas has directed since the original *Star Wars*." (Numeral "1" in the title, Roman "I" later on the same page.) The Special Edition additions were "only a brief glimpse at how today's computer technology will change the look of *Star Wars*" (p. 86).

## Ch. 8: The Empire (pp. 87–93) — companies, not Sith

- LucasArts Entertainment Company, Lucas Digital Ltd., Lucasfilm Ltd. LucasArts Learning established 1978. **Lucas:** "If we can make these multimedia experiences interesting enough so that the child is the motivator, I think that's the most powerful way to learn." Games named: *Maniac Mansion* (also a 1990 TV series), *Grim Fandango*, *X-wing vs. TIE Fighter*, *Star Wars Masters*, *Herc's Adventures*. Lucas Digital = ILM and Skywalker Sound. Founding of ILM was earlier placed in Van Nuys; this chapter locates ILM in San Rafael.
- **Lucas:** "In the first decade, the only time that ILM did not win an Academy Award for special effects was when it was matched against another ILM nominee that did win." Strong on preplanning / storyboards; ILM filled a five-hundred-page notebook for *Empire* and a one-thousand-page notebook for *Jedi*. Model counts: *Star Wars* fewer than 50; *Empire* slightly over 100; *Jedi* more than 160 new. "Luke Skywalker's light saber" spelled as two words. Skywalker Sound began as Sprocket Systems in 1980. **Lucas:** "Sound is fifty percent of the motion picture experience." Led to the THX division in 1983, "THX—The Audience is Listening."
- Ebert interview, quoted at length: "I've watched lots of movies under lots of different conditions, and how a film is presented has a great deal to do with your emotional response to that movie and how much you enjoy it, how much you get out of it. . . . We've discovered that twice as many people will go to a THX theater showing the same movie in the same city in the same-quality theater. . . . They want the best possible presentation."

## Ch. 9: The moviemaking ideal (pp. 94–100)

- **Leonard Maltin:** "*Star Wars* may have started out as a mere movie . . . it changed the way movies were made." **Lucas:** "The focus of my life, the thing I care most about, was and is making movies: writing and shooting and editing films." Skywalker Ranch. **Spielberg:** "an extraordinary complex in Northern California, from where [Lucas] continues to stretch the limitations of education and entertainment."
- **Lucas on USC as a model:** "At that time the school was a tiny enclave of creative people working together and having everything we needed to make movies in a pleasant environment. It was quite a bit different from the way the studios were set up, like large factories." Bought property on Lucas Valley Road in 1978 with *Graffiti* and *Star Wars* money. "It is just a coincidence that he and the road have the same name, but it may have been fate that led him to the perfect place." Construction began 1980; buildings "to feel as if they were a part of the land—not intruding upon it," cars hidden in underground garages.
- **Lucas, p. 97:** "What I wanted to build," he says, "was a work environment that is a model of the way I think creative people should be allowed to work." He believes "writers need the support of a large research library."
- Section **HAVING THE FORCE** (pp. 97–100). *Entertainment Weekly*, October 1997, named him the sixth most powerful person in Hollywood. **Lucas, pp. 97–98:** "I'm so ordinary," he says about himself, "that a lot of people can relate to me, because it's the same kind of ordinary that they are. I think it gives me an insight into the mass audience. I know what I liked as a kid, and I still like it." 130,000 people called a toll-free number in one day before *The Empire Strikes Back*. Star Tours; National Air and Space Museum exhibit of more than two hundred Lucasfilm Archives items.
- **Authors, then Lucas, pp. 98–100 — KEY Vader / teachers passage; crops `quotes/q05-p98-good-guy.jpg` and `quotes/q06-p100-thalberg-force-has-always-been.jpg`:** "Lucas does not take the power he has over his audiences lightly. Children are often his main audience, and now as a divorced father of three children (two through adoption), Lucas can watch his movies through their eyes. Lucas tries to create an ideal world where good always wins over evil. We even find that Darth Vader, one of the most dreaded villains in history, is actually a "good guy" in the end." This "good guy" sentence is the **authors' wording, not a Lucas quote** — do not attribute that phrasing to him. The next sentence continues onto p. 100: "During his acceptance speech of the Irving G. Thalberg Award from the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences in 1992, Lucas said, 'I've always tried to be aware of what I say in my films because all of us who make motion pictures are teachers; teachers with very loud voices.'" **Authors' close:** "From the time he was a child reading adventure comic books to his present-day fame and fortune, it cannot be disputed that the Force has always been with George Lucas." Caption p. 99 typo: "Lucas's ranch in Nicasto, California" (for Nicasio). Caption p. 100: "Lucas takes his power over popular culture and young people very seriously. Here, he attends a movie premiere with his daughter Amanda."

## Chronology (pp. 101–103)

- 1944, May 14: birth (correct; George Walton Lucas, Jr.); 1962 crash, June 12, Thomas Downey High School; 1964 MJC; 1966 USC film school; 1967 meets Spielberg; 1968 graduate semester, *Finian's Rainbow*, *Filmmaker*; 1969 marries Marcia February 22, Zoetrope founded; 1971 *THX 1138*; 1972 Lucasfilm Ltd.; 1973 *Graffiti*; 1975 ILM; 1977 *Star Wars* + five Oscars listed (score, editing, art/set, costume, visual effects); 1979 *More American Graffiti*; 1980 Sprocket / Skywalker Sound, *The Empire Strikes Back*; through 1997 Special Editions; 1999 "Star Wars: Episode I released (Lucas as director, executive producer, and writer)" listed as a fact (the book's 1999 printing anticipates or records the May 1999 release).

- About the Authors, p. 112: "Even when they were kids, Chris and Dana Rau loved the feel of movie-theater seats and the smell of butter-soaked popcorn. Among Chris's favorite movies of all time were all three *Star Wars* films, and among Dana's were all three *Indiana Jones* films." They met in college and "discovered that they were the perfect match. Not only did they sometimes see up to five movies in a weekend, like Lucas himself, but they also joined forces to write the weekly movie review in the college's newspaper. This was the best movie deal they will ever have—someone paid them to see a movie a week!" Today "Chris is an eighth-grade social studies teacher, and Dana is a children's book writer and editor. They live in Farmington, Connecticut." "Someday, when they have lots and lots of money, they plan to build a THX theater in their home and decorate the walls with their movie posters and all of the ticket stubs Dana has saved over the years."
