# 163 summary — Rau and Rau, *George Lucas: Creator of Star Wars* (1999)

Franklin Watts *Book Report Biographies* juvenile / library life of Lucas, 112 numbered pages plus unnumbered front and rear matter (120 scan images). Written in the window of the 1997 Special Editions and the then-upcoming *Star Wars: Episode 1* (the book uses a numeral on p. 85 and a Roman *I* later on the same page; the Chronology on p. 103 already lists Episode I as "released"). Secondary source. Full visual hand-read of book scan `tTkERLKY`. The dirty IA epub was not quoted. Magazine cbr `4C0miTRQ` is the same incomplete scan, not a different publication.

## Priority hits (Vader / Anakin / the Force / Sith / Chosen One / power / canon)

- **Ch. 1, p. 8 — authors, not a Lucas quote, and a lowercase "force."** After the scan-verified Lucas fairy-tale lines ("There's a whole generation growing up without any kind of fairy tales," / "And kids need fairy tales—it's an important thing for society to have for kids."), the authors write: "With *Star Wars*, Lucas had done it. He had created Luke Skywalker—a hero led by an unknown force who battles the dark side and wins." Do not promote the "unknown force" / "dark side" sentence as Lucas's wording. Crop: `quotes/q01-p08-fairy-tales-unknown-force.jpg`.
- **Ch. 5 "The Force" is production history, not Force doctrine.** The chapter title is the book's most prominent use of the word. The only substantial in-text definition is the authors' sentence on p. 61 (not a Lucas quote): "Yoda and the Emperor brought more definition to the mysterious Force that Obi-Wan had introduced in *Star Wars*." Crop: `quotes/q03-p61-force-definition.jpg`. No midichlorians, no Chosen One, no Sith, no power-level comparison.
- **Nine-picture / nine-movie epic frame (authors, 1999).** Pp. 61–63: "With this second sequel, Lucas completed the middle **trilogy** of his grand nine-picture epic." P. 85: "The summer of 1999 will see the return of Lucas's world to the big screen. *Star Wars: Episode 1* will be released, starting Lucas's return to the first three episodes in his nine-movie epic. These "prequels," or the movies that come before the three films that fans have already seen, will cover information fans have always wanted to know. How did Darth Vader become evil? Why did Obi-Wan Kenobi spend all those years hiding on Tatooine? What did Yoda do before he ended up on his swamp planet of Dagobah? What were the Clone Wars? Hopefully, Lucas will answer all of these questions along with many more in the next three films." This is the authors' pre- or mid-*Phantom Menace* framing, not a first-person Lucas quotation. Crop: `quotes/q04-p85-nine-movie-vader-became-evil.jpg`.
- **Modern-myth quotation, p. 48 (Lucas, typography locked from the scan):** "When I created the original *Star Wars*, I was very interested in creating a modern myth to take the place that had been occupied by the Western. . . . Greek mythology, or mythology from any country, often takes place in an unknown area, but one that is believable to the audience. The only area we have now that is like that is outer space. So I decided outer space was a good idea." The pull-quote shortens the first sentence to "When I created the original *Star Wars*, I was very interested in creating a modern myth." Crop: `quotes/q02-p48-modern-myth.jpg`.
- **Vader "good guy" is the authors' wording, pp. 98–100, next to a real Lucas Thalberg quote.** "Lucas tries to create an ideal world where good always wins over evil. We even find that Darth Vader, one of the most dreaded villains in history, is actually a "good guy" in the end." Do **not** attribute the "good guy" phrasing to Lucas. The next sentence continues onto p. 100: "During his acceptance speech of the Irving G. Thalberg Award from the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences in 1992, Lucas said, 'I've always tried to be aware of what I say in my films because all of us who make motion pictures are teachers; teachers with very loud voices.'" The authors close: "From the time he was a child reading adventure comic books to his present-day fame and fortune, it cannot be disputed that the Force has always been with George Lucas." Crops: `quotes/q05-p98-good-guy.jpg` and `quotes/q06-p100-thalberg-force-has-always-been.jpg`.
- **Caption, p. 62 (photo of Vader):** "Darth Vader is one of the most recognizable villains in movie history."
- **ROTJ, p. 63 (authors):** "In *Return of the Jedi*, the powers of good finally achieved a solid victory in their war against the evil Empire." No Vader-redemption scene is described. The same page has a historically confused Ewok/Wookiee origin claim and the slip "Darth Vader's powerful Death Star weapon" (completed on p. 65: "which would give the Empire total control of the galaxy").
- **p. 67 (authors, not a Lucas quote):** "Perhaps the most amazing part of *Return of the Jedi* is how true it is to Lucas's original story. Lucas never changed his vision for the films no matter how successful they became. He had an idea in 1975 that was not completed until 1983, but he stayed on track the entire time."
- **Special Edition, pp. 80–83.** Lucas: the original *Star Wars* "was about sixty percent of what I wanted it to be." Authors (notable given later Greedo/Han controversy): "Lucas made no major changes in the movies, but he feels the new effects complete the picture of the world he was trying to create." Lucas on the reissue: "It's like a revisiting of the entire event. I certainly didn't expect this to happen." "It's nice to think of [the movies] as timeless." (Brackets are in the book.)
- **No canon-hierarchy discussion. No "Chosen One." "Sith" does not appear.** The Glossary (pp. 104–105) has only film-production terms (cinematographer, crew, diabetes, director, documentary, editor, grip, icon, postproduction, science fiction, score, serial, special effects, trilogy). The Index (pp. 110–112) has no entries for the Force, Darth Vader, Jedi, Yoda, the Emperor, Obi-Wan, Luke Skywalker, or Sith.

## Other Lucas statements worth keeping (filmmaking, independence)

See the chapter notes. Highlights: comics-shed and school quotes (pp. 12, 14); crash, "The fact that I was still alive was a miracle" / "go straight" (pp. 18–19; *straight* is spelled correctly here, unlike the 2015 Rise comic); USC "I made eight films" and "Whenever I broke the rules, I made a good film" (pp. 22–24); Zoetrope "putting on the brakes" / "jump off the cliff" (p. 29 area); THX "very personal kind of film" recut protest; *Graffiti* "It all happened to me, but I sort of glamorized it" and "they cut five minutes out of it, and I was furious"; *Star Wars* "too wacky" / "I will get back to you. I will see to it that you get made" (pp. 45–47); "wasn't very much fun"; delegating on *Empire* (p. 58); Indy "role model for little kids" and the p. 75 key line "those movies may be closer to what I am than *Star Wars*"; educational multimedia (p. 87); "Sound is fifty percent of the motion picture experience"; Ranch "a model of the way I think creative people should be allowed to work"; "I'm so ordinary…" (pp. 97–98).

## Factual slips and claims in the book (do not repeat as fact; record as the book's text)

- p. 32 / ch. 4: feature *THX 1138* shot in "subway tunnels and parking garages of Los Angeles" (other bios place the shoot in San Francisco / BART).
- p. 40: Universal "cut only five minutes" from *American Graffiti* (Jim Smith 2003 said 2.5 minutes — record as this book's claim).
- p. 51 caption: a snowy crew photograph is labeled "the first scenes of *Star Wars* in the Tunisia desert" (the image looks like snow, possibly later *Empire* Norway).
- p. 54 caption typo: "Millenium Falcon" (one n).
- Body p. 58: "Irwin Kershner" (wrong); caption p. 59 correctly "Irvin Kershner." The Index (p. 110) repeats "Kershner, Irwin."
- "Wookie" / "Wookies" spelled with one e throughout (pp. 58, 63).
- p. 61: "Lando Calrissian" spelled with two s's.
- p. 63: historically confused Ewok-then-Chewbacca origin: "Lucas had originally intended this group of creatures in *Jedi* to be Wookies, but he liked them so much that he created the Wookie character of Chewbacca for *Star Wars*." (Wookiees existed from *A New Hope*; Ewoks replaced a Wookiee-army idea for Endor.) Same page: "Darth Vader's powerful Death Star weapon."
- pp. 75–76: Kurosawa / *Kagemusha* (1980) awkwardly grouped among "young up-and-coming filmmakers."
- pp. 82–83: "Lucas made no major changes in the movies" (1999 book downplays Special Edition changes).
- p. 99 caption typo: "Nicasto" for Nicasio.
- p. 85 / Chronology p. 103: 1999 nine-movie-epic frame, and the Chronology lists "Star Wars: Episode I released (Lucas as director, executive producer, and writer)" as a fact in a 1999 printing.

Birthdate is correct here (May 14, 1944, p. 11 and Chronology). USC graduation is August 6, 1966 (correct, vs. the 2015 Rise comic's 1967). The post-crash "go straight" is spelled correctly (vs. Rise's STRAIT).
