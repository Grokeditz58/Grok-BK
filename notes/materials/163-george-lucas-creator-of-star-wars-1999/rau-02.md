# Rau and Rau, *George Lucas: Creator of Star Wars* (1999): notes part 2 — chs. 4–5 (pp. 29–67)

Source: owner's MEGA book-scan cbr `tTkERLKY`. Citations are printed footer numbers. Typography locked from the JPEGs.

## Ch. 4: Lucas versus Hollywood (pp. 29–42)

- Documentary *Filmmaker* on Coppola. **Lucas:** "I was very grateful to have someone of my own generation around to discuss what I was trying to do." On the partnership: they "were like two halves of a whole. I was always putting on the brakes and he was always stepping on the gas. It was good for me, because it loosened me up and got me to take more chances. I realized that you can jump off the cliff and survive 99 percent of the time."
- American Zoetrope, Coppola sole shareholder, Lucas VP. Announcement: "The essential objective of the company is to engage in the varied fields of filmmaking, collaborating with the most gifted and youthful talent using the most contemporary techniques and equipment possible." Coppola wanted a grand studio; Lucas wanted "a nice little house." Downtown San Francisco warehouse, 1969. Warner gave Zoetrope $3.5 million to develop five of seven projects, including *THX 1138:4EB*. *Apocalypse Now* conceived with John Milius. Salary $15,000.
- **Lucas, on making the feature *THX*:** "I realized that I might never get the chance again to make this totally off-the-wall movie, without any real supervision." The book calls *THX 1138* a "science-fiction documentary" according to Lucas. Setting: an underground, computerized, drug-controlled society in the twenty-fifth century; shot in "subway tunnels and parking garages of Los Angeles" (**possible slip**; other bios place the feature shoot in San Francisco / BART). Robert Duvall lead.
- Warner thought it too strange, did an in-house re-edit, canceled the Zoetrope deal. **Lucas:** "They told Francis we were going to have to pay back all the money they'd advanced," "It was a dire time." Warner ended up cutting only four minutes. **Lucas:** "The cuts didn't make the movie any better; they had absolutely no effect on the movie at all," "It was a very personal kind of film, and I didn't think they had the right to come in and just arbitrarily chop it up at their own whim." *THX 1138* out 1971, mixed reviews; *Newsweek* "an extremely professional first film." Biggest commercial failure, one of his favorite films. After *THX*, he and Coppola "had to go our separate ways."
- *Graffiti* motivation: he feared a reputation as "a cold, weird director, a science-fiction sort of guy who carried a calculator. And I'm not like that at all. So, I thought, maybe I'll do something exactly the opposite." *New York Times*: "It all happened to me, but I sort of glamorized it," "I went through all that stuff, drove the cars, bought liquor, chased girls. I think a lot of people do, which is the whole idea behind the title—a very American experience."
- United Artists $10,000, then rejected; Katz and Huyck, then Universal; Coppola co-producer with Gary Kurtz; $750,000, twenty-eight days. Cast: Ron Howard, Richard Dreyfuss, Cindy Williams, Suzanne Somers, Harrison Ford. Originally suggested eighty songs; Universal wanted five; settled on forty-five. San Rafael $300/night, then Petaluma; $25/night for period cars. Wanted Marcia to edit; Universal put Verna Fields with her. Set up Lucasfilm Ltd. for taxes. Preview January 1973; Coppola shouted at executives to buy the film. Universal "cut only five minutes" (Smith 2003 said 2.5 minutes — this book's claim), released August 1973. **Lucas:** "They wanted it shorter . . ." "so they cut five minutes out of it, and I was furious." Grossed $50 million vs. $750,000 cost; stayed in theaters two years. *Los Angeles Times*: "one of the most important films of the year." Ready for a space drama that would grow into *Star Wars*. 1997 Modesto *Graffiti* statue photo, p. 41.

## Ch. 5: The Force (pp. 43–67) — production history, not Force doctrine

- After *Graffiti* he beat the millionaire-by-thirty goal by two years. Victorian house in Marin became Lucasfilm Ltd. Original draft settings: a desert, a jungle, "a strange city in the clouds." Researching science fiction, fantasy, world myths, comics. First draft by 1974.
- **Lucas, on taking the risk:** he thought *Star Wars* might be "too wacky for the general public. I just said, 'Well, I've had my big hit [with *American Graffiti*], and I'm happy. And I'm going to do this crazy thing, and it'll be fun.'" Universal and UA turned it down; Fox showed some interest. By 1975 he had completed "the screenplay to the middle portion of a giant space epic." Hired Ralph McQuarrie (the authors also credit McQuarrie with *E.T.* and *Cocoon*). **McQuarrie:** "He wanted people to look and say, 'Gee, that looks great, just like something on the screen.'" Fox start-up money, then bought in.
- **Authors + Lucas, p. 47:** "He divided the *Star Wars* story into three episodes and set the second two aside. The whole story would take several movies." He gave up a good portion of the director's fee for sequel and merchandising rights. "When he set the other two parts of the story aside to make *Star Wars*, he said, 'I will get back to you. I will see to it that you get made.'"
- **Lucas, p. 48 — modern-myth quotation, typography locked; crop `quotes/q02-p48-modern-myth.jpg`:** "When I created the original *Star Wars*, I was very interested in creating a modern myth to take the place that had been occupied by the Western. . . . Greek mythology, or mythology from any country, often takes place in an unknown area, but one that is believable to the audience. The only area we have now that is like that is outer space. So I decided outer space was a good idea." The pull-quote shortens this to "When I created the original *Star Wars*, I was very interested in creating a modern myth."
- Casting: Carrie Fisher, Harrison Ford, Mark Hamill as Luke Starkiller. **Authors' claim:** "(His character's name wasn't changed to Luke Skywalker until the first day of shooting.)" **Hamill:** "There were guys literally everywhere, in age from sixteen to thirty-five." **Ford:** "George wanted and hired strong personalities." Alec Guinness as Obi-Wan. Tunisia, March 1976, eleven days; only Hamill and Guinness made the trip. Letter to Marcia: "I forget how impossible making movies really is. I get so depressed, but I guess I'll get through somehow." Caption p. 51 claims the snowy crew photo is "the first scenes of *Star Wars* in the Tunisia desert" — **likely a caption error** (looks like snow, possibly later *Empire* Norway). Elstree cheaper. Lucas wanted things "faster and more intense."
- **Peter Mayhew:** "My first impression was that he [Lucas] was an average sort of guy," "He didn't look like the image of a Hollywood director." **Ford:** "Very little time was wasted. George didn't have an authoritarian attitude like many directors. . . . He was different. . . . He encouraged our contributions."
- ILM in a Van Nuys warehouse; John Dykstra; first feature to use a computer to control the special-effects camera; staff mostly under thirty, some under twenty; no dress code. Caption p. 54 typo "Millenium Falcon" (one n). Chewbacca's voice from angry walruses; laser blasts from hammers on antenna wire. John Williams: "Why don't you let me write our own classical music?" Lucas says making *Star Wars* "wasn't very much fun." Opened on thirty-five screens. "Star Wars: The Cantina Band" topped pop singles. LucasArts Licensing; 10 Oscar nominations, 5 wins including Marcia for editing; shared profits even with ILM housekeeping. Never doubted a sequel.
- *More American Graffiti*: Vietnam, drugs, death, war, and sex; commercial failure. *Empire* work started 1978. **Lucas:** "I was very nervous when I started the second film." Middle story of three, headed for dark dramatic moments. Kasdan draft (also *Raiders*); ready early 1979. **Lucas:** "As a director, I wanted to do everything," "It's very hard for me to delegate things to other people. Well, the best way to do that is to take one more step back and be forced to delegate everything. And see if I could stand it." "Chewbacca is still my Wookie and R2-D2 is still my little robot." ("Wookie" one e in this book.) Hired "Irwin Kershner" in the body, p. 58 (wrong); caption p. 59 correctly "Irvin Kershner." Shot Norway (Hoth), Elstree, ILM California.
- **p. 61 KEY Force sentence (authors, not a Lucas quote); crop `quotes/q03-p61-force-definition.jpg`:** "Yoda and the Emperor brought more definition to the mysterious Force that Obi-Wan had introduced in *Star Wars*." Lando Calrissian spelled with two s's. Lucas spent $33 million of his own money; the film made $365 million worldwide.
- *Return of the Jedi*, May 25, 1983, six years to the day. **Authors, pp. 61–63:** "With this second sequel, Lucas completed the middle **trilogy** of his grand nine-picture epic." (The sentence breaks across the p. 62 Vader photograph.) "He also gave his fans a brighter conclusion than they had with *The Empire Strikes Back*. In *Return of the Jedi*, the powers of good finally achieved a solid victory in their war against the evil Empire." No Vader-redemption detail is given. Caption p. 62: "Darth Vader is one of the most recognizable villains in movie history."
- **Historically confused Ewok claim, p. 63:** "Lucas had originally intended this group of creatures in *Jedi* to be Wookies, but he liked them so much that he created the Wookie character of Chewbacca for *Star Wars*." Record as the book's claim. Same page, authors' slip: the plot "is centered on stopping the construction of Darth Vader's powerful Death Star weapon," completed on p. 65, "which would give the Empire total control of the galaxy." Caption p. 64: "Lucas poses with the cast of *Return of the Jedi* and director Richard Marquand (far right)."
- Speeder-bike scenes on Endor as "a perfect example of Lucas's faith in technology." He did not want to direct; brought in Marquand and Kasdan. **Kasdan:** "I liked George, and he was always under pressure—they were always starting movies before they had scripts." "George understood that you could integrate the effects into the story so that they formed part of the story in a way people had never really tried before."
- **Authors, p. 67, not a Lucas quote:** "Perhaps the most amazing part of *Return of the Jedi* is how true it is to Lucas's original story. Lucas never changed his vision for the films no matter how successful they became. He had an idea in 1975 that was not completed until 1983, but he stayed on track the entire time." Public question: "When is he going to make more?" They would have to wait more than fifteen years.
