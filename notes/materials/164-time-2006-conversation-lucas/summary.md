# 164 summary — Corliss, "A Conversation with George Lucas" (*TIME*, 20 March 2006)

Short primary interview, two-hour phone call, excerpts prepared for a *TIME* story on the future of movies. The owner's file is a 10-page 2019 Word reprint of the 20 March 2006 piece (`5alyBBAQ`), not a magazine scan. Full hand-read. Mostly distribution, digital exhibition, multiplexes, 3-D, "retirement," hobby filmmaking, *Indiana Jones 4*, and pre-visualization. Little Vader / Force doctrine.

## Priority hits (Vader / Anakin / the Force / Sith / Chosen One / power / canon)

- **No Force doctrine, no Vader-power or Chosen One statements, no canon hierarchy.** Lucas does not discuss the Force, the dark side, Sith philosophy, or Anakin's fall in these excerpts.
- **Corliss introduction, not a Lucas quote (TIME PDF pp. 2–3):** Lucas "could have gone back, or on, to making the gnarly little independent movies he has talked about, to increasingly incredulous listeners, for 35 years. Yet he has extended the original Star Wars trilogy not just to the three episodes he made in the past decade but to an Ewoks TV show and, now, a Star Wars cartoon series for cable TV and a rerelease of all six chapters in 3D. He is a father who feels obliged to raise the children he sired. Luke and Anakin... ...and Indiana Jones." Do not treat "Luke and Anakin" as Lucas's wording.
- **"Sith" appears once, as a film title, TIME PDF p. 10.** Lucas: "I let him do some pre-vis on the last film, on Sith. And he loved it, and he did it on War of the Worlds." This is about pre-visualization on *Revenge of the Sith*, not Sith doctrine.
- **"Retired" means no more Star Wars movies, TIME PDF p. 7.** Lucas: "I mean, I'm old enough and I'm kind of retired..." Corliss: "What do you mean, you're kind of retired?" Lucas: "In that I don't have to do Star Wars anymore. I don't have to make money any more. I can just waste it. I call it hobby filmmaking, where you just get to do what you want to do, and you don't have to worry about what anyone thinks about it." After the TV series, "I'm going to do my own little movies." "Basically, you have to accept the fact that it's going to be the land of THX (the movie), and worse."
- **Star Wars television / 3-D, TIME PDF p. 6.** Lucas: "Now the television show I'm working on, the Star Wars television show, is 3D." He means "the old-fashioned 3D," "with glasses and everything." He "had no idea" at the time of making the movies. "I was very much against 3D until I saw this new process."
- **Indy 4 compared to *The Phantom Menace*, TIME PDF p. 8.** Lucas: "Well, I've been working on Indy 4 for ten years. So I've been more involved, so no matter how you count it on this one I'll be more involved than I'll have ever been on the other three put together." "All we can do is hurt ourselves, all it's going to do is get criticized. I mean it's basically Phantom Menace we're making. No matter how you do it, no matter what you do, it won't be what the other ones were in terms of the impact or the way people remember them."

## Other Lucas statements worth keeping (industry, digital, exhibition)

See corliss-01.md. Highlights: *Star Wars* and *Jaws* money let theater owners build multiplexes, which then needed filling by Miramax-scale companies (HOW BIG MOVIES MAKE LITTLE MOVIES). Digital prints vs. $20–30 million print costs; a $1,200 / $200 conversion scheme "no one's bought in yet" (THE DOYEN OF DIGITAL). Day-and-date is "inevitable"; theaters remain a "social experience," like football in 20-below weather (DAY AND DATE). "I compose it for the big screen, I don't worry about the little screen"; "I am a giant proponent of giant screens. But I accept the fact that most of my movies are going to be seen on phones" (BIG SCREEN, LITTLE SCREEN). Form-experimenting and "my own little government subsidy" (RENEGADE AND RETRO). Spielberg will "win" the film-vs-digital argument on Indy 4 because "He's the director" (INDY 4). Home "larger servers"; pre-vis "almost like a video game"; "Directors can just sit down like a writer and direct their movie on a desk" (FAVORITE TOYS). Closing: "Unfortunately, we live in a new world where all the fun things are gone. Everything is virtual."
