# 169 summary — Arnold, *Once Upon a Galaxy* (1980)

Unit-publicist journal of the making of *The Empire Strikes Back*, written as a dated diary (March–November 1979; signed January 1980). High value for contemporaneous Lucas, Kurtz, Kershner, Ford, Hamill, Fisher, Guinness, McQuarrie, Prowse, Williams, Burtt, Hirsch, and other staff/crew/cast statements. Not a later reconstruction. Staff/crew/cast get the same full literal-quote treatment as Lucas. Arnold glosses (the Cannes “forever young” line he wrote for Kershner; “I don’t think he will play [Solo] again”; Yoda’s “Third Eye” / 800-year speculation; carbon-freeze “experimenting” on Han; “puck in an elfin landscape”) are flagged as Arnold’s.

## Conflicts recorded, not collapsed

- Kurtz, Oslo, early March 1979: never refer to *Empire* as a sequel “because future George Lucas stories do not have chronological sequence.”
- Lucas, Thursday, July 19: “The first script was one of six original stories I had written in the form of two trilogies. After the success of *Star Wars* I added another trilogy. So now there are nine stories… the first film was number four.”
- Lucas, Monday, October 29 (Marin): “essentially nine films in a series of three trilogies. The first trilogy is about the young Ben Kenobi and the early life of Luke’s father when Luke was a little boy… some twenty years before… *Star Wars* and *Empire*… the entire saga spans about fifty-five years… story treatments on all nine… *War and Peace*… cut it in half… each half into three parts… six movies. After the success of *Star Wars* [conversion-garbled] I added another trilogy but stopped there… ‘Revenge of the Jedi’… conclusion of the conflict begun in *Star Wars* between Luke and Darth Vader.”
- Lucas, Friday, August 3: “The first one was about Luke’s father, and the second one took place at a later time and was mainly about Luke.”
- Lucas, Thursday, August 23: first script “was their [Vader and Ben’s] story”; originally an older brother and younger brother rescue “their father, an old Jedi”; Ben “developed from the father figure into a friend of the father”; Vader started as “a kind of intergalactic bounty hunter,” then “a grotesque knight”; “from the early concept of Darth Vader as a bounty hunter that Boba Fett came.”
- These stay separate from one another and from Kaminski 167, Jones 166, later Lucas denials, and any later “always nine / always the father” reconstructions.

## Priority lines (not a substitute for the chapter files)

- Ford invents “I know” against Kershner’s “I’ll be back… It’s almost contractual” (Thursday, June 21 cordless-mike tape); Fisher confrontation the same day.
- McQuarrie: Lucas wanted “a tall, black figure with fluttering robes, incredibly strong but graceful and majestic”; “fisherman’s helmet”; “George acted as a close guide”; Vader “a great deal like my sketch.”
- Prowse: “preserve Darth Vader’s anonymity at my expense”; “When George Lucas asked me why I wanted to play the villain, I said, ‘Because he’ll be remembered.’”
- Hamill: “the dark side of the Force is very seductive… which is what has happened to Darth Vader”; later, Yoda’s “there is another” could let Lucas “discover a long-lost sister or brother of Luke’s,” and Han’s carbon freeze leaves Ford “in limbo.”
- Characters “all came out of one composite-Luke was the composite”; “Should he be a she?”; Leia and Han split out of Luke; Han a “cynical foil”; Chewie from Indiana the husky; R2-D2 named from Walter Murch’s “reel two, dialogue two.”
- Force: Kenobi’s “energy field” quoted; Lucas: “a concept of religion based on the premise that there is a God and there is good and evil… distill the essence of all religions… nondenominational… I believe in God and I believe in right and wrong.”
- July 27 (already isolated): folklore, “America has no modern fairy tales,” Western as last myth, television “amoral,” “I was trying to design a modern fairy tale,” *THX* “a parable… rather depressing.”
- Williams: “Grand Imperial March” for Vader; Love Theme for Leia and Han; late-nineteenth-century romantic model so the music would sound familiar against unearthly images.
- Kershner will not direct “Revenge of the Jedi”: “The *Star Wars* saga is George’s life.”
