# 169 — Elstree: Monday, March 12 through Thursday, May 31

Citations: Arnold, “EMI Studios, Elstree,” [journal date]. Typography locked from the XHTML, including conversion slips. Arnold glosses flagged. Staff/crew/cast same treatment as Lucas.

## March 12–30

Monday, March 12: first unit home from Finse; Hamill stays for glacier. Lucas in London “treading snow” — artificial snow in the Rebel command center, Stage 1. Norman Reynolds on process; typography locked, Lucas’s brief: “George Lucas gave us our terms of reference on *Star Wars* and we have abided by them. ‘Everything,’ he said, ‘must relate to Earth, to what we know about our own planet.’ This prevents us from getting carried away into realms of science fiction. The *Star Wars* pictures are not science fiction. We’re making adventure stories… We try to create a strangeness from familiar things.” Snowspeeders; Imperial snow walkers “machines that look like huge animals.”

Tuesday, March 13: Kershner on Norway (“I had icicles in my beard… I found Gary’s presence good”). Norwegian dailies please Lucas, Kurtz, Kershner; telex that second unit has established Mark on the glacier. **Stuart Freeborn** interview: craft of secret; masks as saga develops; little make-up on Guinness (“He had a nice tan and his own beard… Every true actor’s face is plastic”).

Wednesday, March 14: dialogue revisions; sixteen-week live-action schedule “nobody I talk to is confident.” **Peter Suschitzky**: VistaVision for blue screen, “adaptations of old Technicolor cameras.” **Robert Watts**: “Gary Kurtz’s and George Lucas’s trans-Atlantic view of what is happening is vital. Theirs is the view from the bridge.”

Friday, March 16: glacier −33°; Falcon hold, Han flirting with reluctant Leia. Monday, March 19: medical-center tank (400 gallons, British Aerospace perspex); Time’s Eric Amfitheatrof interviews Kurtz; sets closed — “Keeping wraps on motion pictures of this kind is as vital as… space research.”

Tuesday, March 20 — **Ralph McQuarrie** interview, typography locked. THX then *Graffiti*; Fox cautious. “George had ideas about how his picture should look. In fact, I think the look of the picture was more interesting to him than the plot… he wanted to get what he could see in his mind onto the screen.” Twenty or twenty-two illustrations for *Star Wars*, thirty so far for *Empire*. Vader: “I started to sketch Darth Vader on the information that George wanted him to be a tall, black figure with fluttering robes, incredibly strong but graceful and majestic, too. I thought he could wear something like a fisherman’s helmet, an enclosing helmet. So I started to sketch various shapes and designs. And George acted as a close guide on everything, eliminating this or that sketch.” “R2-D2 ended up like my original sketch. And, although somewhat changed, Darth Vader was a great deal like my sketch, too.” “George was the inspiration… I enjoy fact much more” than SF reading.

Thursday, March 22: Billy Dee arrives as Lando. Hamill, three years to the day after *Star Wars* started: “It was a long time ago in a galaxy far, far away.” Monday, March 26: Lucas and Kurtz tour Robert Stigwood (record rights). Hamill on first meeting Guinness in Tunisia (Lady Guinness’s mosque sketch torn up; “local art critic”). Tuesday, March 27: Hoth HQ under attack; Austrian TV; robots only “in character.” Lucas returns to California. Arnold asks Kershner if he felt inhibited; typography locked (garbled lead-in): “It was like having the toymaker present, but he didn’t play with the toys.” Wednesday, March 28: art-deco Bespin residential interiors; “circular windows that reveal panoramas of the city floating amid the pink, plumy clouds of the gas planet.” Thursday, March 29: dead Tauntaun in the ice-cavern corral. Friday, March 30: Wampa-through-wall shot fails first take (“the Whole thing… anOther try”; mid-word capitals locked). Kershner: “In this scene we’re trying to suggest more than will actually be seen. We need to use the audience’s imagination as a means of giving color to our coloring book.” Script-supervisor log: “wall too strong for ice monster to break through. Also, the man inside the monster suit has been there three hours and is suffering from stomach cramps.”

## April

Monday, April 2: merchandising visit (General Mills and British subsidiaries); Vader among characters “represented in a range of functional” products (cookie jars, etc.). Tuesday, April 3: Kurtz dinner for Kershner and four principals; Kenny Baker / Prowse / Des Webb (Wampa). Billy Dee on being called “a black actor”: “I long for the time when I’ll be thought of simply as an actor.” Wednesday, April 4: plan to rent space for an eighteen-foot walker foot crushing a snowspeeder is dropped; sequence to miniature in California. Thursday, April 5: move to Cloud City; Kurtz directs a second unit. Billy Dee agrees Lando “is well conceived and capable of a lot of development… starting out as a character you want to see get kicked in the teeth yet becoming in time one you’re rooting for.” Friday, April 6: Norway second unit returns (only five really good days, 12 March–3 April); frostbite, flu; rescue skiers in an igloo / a hole under the snow. Tea ladies (this span) serve “a platoon of snowtroopers… a robot, Darth Vader, and the Wampa Ice Creature.”

Monday, April 9: Ugnaughts. Tuesday, April 10: Carrie flu; schedule changes; house leased from Eric Idle. Wednesday, April 11: “Scenes preliminary to Luke’s confrontation with Vader on the planet of Bespin.” *Star Wars* reissue in ~70 territories (not Norway, still first-run; not Italy, summer). Rough-cut ~12 minutes: “a more mature look to Mark, Harrison, and Carrie.” Thursday, April 12: **Jeremy Bulloch** as Boba Fett; costume “period swagger… Richard II,” Old West belt, scalps. Tuesday, April 17: after a four-day Easter holiday, back on Cloud City interiors; Carrie rested at “the village of Slaughter in the Cotswolds”; Ford had been bedridden with flu, now “well and working… watched by his two young sons.” Wednesday, April 18: media inquiries “average around 130 a week.” Thursday, April 19: Kershner Cannes line, typography locked, **written by Arnold**: “The *Star Wars* saga is pure fantasy, an exotic trip, a magical landscape familiar as our dreams. It speaks directly to that part of us that is forever young.’ Well, that sounds good — and I should know because I wrote it for him.” Friday, April 20: full-scale Falcon floated onto the new stage on hoverpads (“twenty-threeton,” hyphen missing).

Monday, April 23: Hamill hung upside-down in the Wampa lair; “It’s fun.” Tuesday, April 24: Kurtz on Bespin interiors; Kershner on Vader’s Star Destroyer; **Yoda** in Freeborn’s shop, “at least 800 years old” and a “Third Eye” are Arnold’s speculation — “As yet no one has been permitted to see him.” Wednesday, April 25: BBC radio likens *Star Wars* merchandising to Dickens / *Pickwick Papers* “secondary exploitation.” Thursday, April 26: Vader summons bounty hunters (lobster head, reptile, eight-foot war machine) for a reward on Han. Friday, April 27: Vader’s aides Michael Culver, Julian Glover, Kenneth Colley, Michael Sheard — theater actors (Wolfit “cooking”). Monday, April 30: eight Artoos (two for Baker, three dummy, three remote); one “nearly exploded” near a 5,000-volt oscillator.

## May

Tuesday, May 1: Rebel hangar on the Star Wars stage; “no fewer than 59 carpenters.” Wednesday, May 2: Vader’s meditation sphere; Lucas, “describing the traumas of the final stages of completing *Star Wars*”: “The buck stops at opticals.” Thursday, May 3: UK general-election polling day; technicians vote after call. Friday, May 4: Thatcher victory ad in the *Evening News*: “May the Fourth Be With You, Maggie. Congratulations.” Tuesday, May 8: Kurtz baby shower for the Hamills; Arnold: “When Luke Skywalker adds the sword of fatherhood to his armory of lightsabers. Do we tell the fans?” — Mark’s baby, not a saga beat. Wednesday, May 9: Stage 8, snowspeeder cockpits vs walkers “inserted in the blue screen” at ILM; “pieces of a jigsaw puzzle.” Thursday, May 10: Lucas at Wallingford House with Kurtz, Kershner, and Hirsch; Arnold declines to write only “from [Kershner’s] angle.”

Friday, May 11 — **Kershner** on the schedule (never approved the original): “we have too many gadgets… Not disastrously, but we have fallen behind… There is no such thing as a perfect shot… I hate the word compromise… Flexibility is the essential factor.” “George and Gary know that I pressure myself.” Normally he would not show producers more than rushes; he changed his mind: “I’m not dealing with studio departments… George is a very fine editor.’ So… George is taking back to California with him fifty minutes of footage (the battle scenes)… There is no ego in this picture.”

Monday, May 14: Leia to tortured Han: “What are they doing to you?” Carrie and a predatory journalist (Arnold). Tuesday, May 15: John Barry returns to second unit after being replaced on *Saturn 3* (“It is like coming home”). Lucas and Kurtz lunch Guinness at Neal’s; whether he will return as Kenobi is unconfirmed; percentage rumor (John Mills on TV) flagged as chit-chat; Arnold wonders about a hologram or voiceover. Wednesday, May 16 — **Peter Mayhew**: seven feet two; hospital orderly; Lucas and Kurtz called him; Chewie “furry and lovable but pretty fierce,” “a heart of gold”; “400year-old Wookiee” (hyphen missing). Thursday, May 17 — **Carrie Fisher** interview (studies, Central School, *Shampoo*, Sarah Lawrence); more personal than character.

Friday, May 18 — **Paul Hirsch** (with Marcia Lucas and Richard Chew on *Star Wars*; solo editor to finish after Marcia left for *New York, New York*). George hired him after *Phantom of the Paradise*; mid-shoot call from Marcia. ILM: “I’ve never worked on anything this complicated.” Lucas: “That’s all right. Nobody has.” May 1977 San Francisco preview, incomplete answer print: audience “went crazy”; hyperspace “jump for joy.” Leaving, Hirsch asked George what he thought. “Well, I guess we won’t have to change anything after all,” he said. And we didn’t.” *Empire* “more polished”; Kershner “moving the camera quite a lot. In *Star Wars* the camera hardly ever moved.” Videotape to “the DLM people” (conversion slip for ILM).

Monday, May 21 — **Billy Dee Williams**: Lando “a scoundrel and a hero… a survivor… You think at first he’s a bad guy, all bad, but he ends up not being such a bad guy.” “Martin ಗ್ಧter King” = Luther (garbage cluster). “An actor is an actor. I’m bored with the race problem.” Tuesday, May 22: Star Wars stage opens (ice hangar, full-scale Falcon); no press/Prince Charles; fan-club winner Matthew Pack. Kershner: “outer space is colossal and where can you go to film that? You have to build.”

Wednesday, May 23 — **Brian Johnson** (special effects): digital film printer, electronic models, NASA rapport; “If every successive production is going to try to outdo its predecessor’s effects, films are going to cost a fortune. On *The Empire Strikes Back* we’re trying to streamline the process.” “I couldn’t agree more” that effects must not diminish story. Tuesday, May 29 — Kershner rejects “puppeteer”: “I feel wonderful making this film… It deals with archetypal situations and characters in which good and evil are clearly defined… legend, myth, and magic… There’s more to this story than meets the eye.” Not a stepping-stone picture. Wednesday, May 30: *Variety* “EMPIRE” GUARANTEES $26 MIL PLUS; Arnold flags complacency. Lucas: “We’re trying to make the best picture we can.” Thursday, May 31 — **Dave Prowse**, typography locked: “When *Star Wars* opened I thought I had the raw end of the deal. It seemed to me there was a concentrated effort to preserve Darth Vader’s anonymity at my expense. Yet I had played him and he had become a cult figure. I was determined to get the credit for it.” Hired press agents, answered fan mail, appearances unmasked. “I didn’t think they would change a winning team.” “When George Lucas asked me why I wanted to play the villain, I said, ‘Because he’ll be remembered.’” “Is Darth Vader a fit man?” “Put it like this — he’s single-minded… You’ve got to be strong to be evil. In any case, the devil looks after him.”
