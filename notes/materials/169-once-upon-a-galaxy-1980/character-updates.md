### McQuarrie: “a tall, black figure with fluttering robes” / “fisherman’s helmet”

**Speaker:** Ralph McQuarrie, interviewed by Alan Arnold; Lucas’s brief as reported by McQuarrie.
**Source:** “I started to sketch Darth Vader on the information that George wanted him to be a tall, black figure with fluttering robes, incredibly strong but graceful and majestic, too. I thought he could wear something like a fisherman’s helmet, an enclosing helmet. So I started to sketch various shapes and designs. And George acted as a close guide on everything, eliminating this or that sketch.” “R2-D2 ended up like my original sketch. And, although somewhat changed, Darth Vader was a great deal like my sketch, too.” Same interview: “George had ideas about how his picture should look. In fact, I think the look of the picture was more interesting to him than the plot.” — typography-faithful quotes, Arnold, “EMI Studios, Elstree,” Tuesday, March 20.

### Hamill: “the dark side of the Force is very seductive… which is what has happened to Darth Vader”

**Speaker:** Mark Hamill, interviewed by Alan Arnold.
**Source:** “Luke will be tempted by what he has to undergo. It was made clear in *Star Wars* that the dark side of the Force is very seductive, that it is easier to achieve your goals by allowing anger and aggression to guide you, which is what has happened to Darth Vader. As the saga continues there will be constant changes in all the characters. Luke certainly isn’t the same boy now as he was in the first film.” — typography-faithful quote, Arnold, “Norway,” Thursday, March 8.

### Kershner: the same Force used for evil; “It’s possible to fight because you love, not just because you hate”

**Speaker:** Irvin Kershner, interviewed by Alan Arnold (Sunday, March 5).

They do not descend to the level of the enemy… It’s possible to fight because you love, not just because you hate.” “These films are morality plays… They are also fairy tales.” — typography-faithful quotes, Arnold, “Norway,” Sunday, March 5.

### Prowse: “preserve Darth Vader’s anonymity at my expense” / “Because he’ll be remembered”

**Speaker:** Dave Prowse, interviewed by Alan Arnold.
**Source:** “When *Star Wars* opened I thought I had the raw end of the deal. It seemed to me there was a concentrated effort to preserve Darth Vader’s anonymity at my expense. Yet I had played him and he had become a cult figure. I was determined to get the credit for it.” “When George Lucas asked me why I wanted to play the villain, I said, ‘Because he’ll be remembered.’” On whether Vader is a fit man: “Put it like this — he’s single-minded… You’ve got to be strong to be evil. In any case, the devil looks after him.” — typography-faithful quotes, Arnold, “EMI Studios, Elstree,” Thursday, May 31.

### Lucas, August 3: “The first one was about Luke’s father, and the second one… mainly about Luke”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, interviewed by Alan Arnold, Friday, August 3. Do not collapse with the July 19 or August 23 statements, or with later “always the father” reconstructions.

### Lucas, August 23: “Darth Vader and Ben were developed in my first script-it was their story” / bounty hunter to grotesque knight / Boba Fett

**Speaker:** George Lucas, interviewed by Alan Arnold, Thursday, August 23. Do not collapse with the August 3 “first one was about Luke’s father” line or with later prequel reconstructions.
**Source:** “Darth Vader and Ben were developed in my first script-it was their story. When I wrote the second script it was later in time and Luke was the focus. I wanted to develop an essentially evil, very frightening character. He started as a kind of intergalactic bounty hunter, evolved into a grotesque knight, and as I got deeper into the knight ethos he became more a dark warrior than a mercenary. Again, I split him up and it was from the early concept of Darth Vader as a bounty hunter that Boba Fett came.” — typography-faithful quote, Arnold, “EMI Studios, Elstree,” Thursday, August 23.
**Notes:** `notes/169-once-upon-a-galaxy-1980/arnold-04-elstree-august-september.md`

### Lucas, August 23: rescue “their father, an old Jedi”; Ben “developed from the father figure into a friend of the father”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, interviewed by Alan Arnold, Thursday, August 23. Keep separate from the Vader-and-Ben “their story” paragraph above and from later Anakin/Obi-Wan tellings.
**Source:** “Originally, the story was about an older brother coming to find his younger brother, who’s living on a farm, so that together they can rescue their father, an old Jedi. The older brother is a battle-hardened warrior. This character evolved into Han, the other side of Luke and an older brother figure. Ben Kenobi developed from the father figure into a friend of the father.” Same interview, on Kenobi’s image: “This character started out as Luke’s father and became the friend of Luke’s father. I wanted a character who was an old warrior, very stately, a father image for Luke… He wasn’t meant to be Christ like, but rather a thoughtful and intelligent man with a noble bearing-a symbol of goodness and mystical power.” — typography-faithful quotes, Arnold, “EMI Studios, Elstree,” Thursday, August 23.
**Notes:** `notes/169-once-upon-a-galaxy-1980/arnold-04-elstree-august-september.md`

### Lucas, October 29: nine films / three trilogies / “early life of Luke’s father” / “Revenge of the Jedi” concludes the Luke–Vader conflict

**Speaker:** George Lucas, interviewed by Alan Arnold, Monday, October 29, Marin County. Do not collapse with the July 19 nine-stories statement in this same book, or with Kaminski 167, Jones 166, or later denials. Conversion slips in the *War and Peace* paragraph are locked as printed.
**Source:** “There are essentially nine films in a series of three trilogies. The first trilogy is about the young Ben Kenobi and the early life of Luke’s father when Luke was a little boy. This trilogy takes place some twenty years before the second trilogy which includes *Star Wars* and *Empire*. About a year or two passes between each story of the trilogy and about twenty years pass between the trilogies. The entire saga spans about fifty-five years.” “I have story treatments on all nine… Originally, when I wrote *Star Wars*, it developed into an epic on the scale of *War and Peace*… So cut it in half, but it was still to big, so each half into three parts. I then had material for six movies. After the success of *Star Wars* but to write and finish them. Mostly, it will be a I added another trilogy but stopped there, primarily because reality took over… So I’m still left with three trilogies of nine films.” “The next chapter is called ‘Revenge of the Jedi.’ It’s the end of this particular trilogy, the conclusion of the conflict begun in *Star Wars* between Luke and Darth Vader. It resolves that situation once and for all.” — typography-faithful quotes, Arnold, “Marin County, California,” Monday, October 29.

### Lucas on the Force: “there is a God and there is good and evil” / “nondenominational”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, interviewed by Alan Arnold, Thursday, August 23. No new `the-force.md` file; this is entered on the Vader file as creator commentary on the Force, including Vader’s side of it.
**Source:** “The Force evolved out of various developments of character and plot. I wanted a concept of religion based on the premise that there is a God and there is good and evil. I began to distill the essence of all religions into what I thought was a basic idea common to all religions and common to primitive thinking. I wanted to develop something that was nondenominational but still had a kind of religious reality. I believe in God and I believe in right and wrong… ‘Do unto others . . .’ is the philosophy that permeates my work.” — typography-faithful quote, Arnold, “EMI Studios, Elstree,” Thursday, August 23.
**Notes:** `notes/169-once-upon-a-galaxy-1980/arnold-04-elstree-august-september.md`

### Kershner: Yoda sequence is “the heart of the picture” and leads to a “very ritualized” Luke–Vader duel

**Speaker:** Irvin Kershner, interviewed by Alan Arnold, Monday, August 6.
**Source:** The Yoda sequence “expresses the philosophical content of the picture… In these scenes between Yoda and Luke, the Force is best understood as something very real. We learn how it is used, where it comes from. The Jedi training program and disciplines are explained. The concept of the Force’s use for good rather than evil is promulgated. Finally, these scenes lead to the big confrontation duel between Luke and Darth Vader, a fight that is very ritualized, and based on the disciplines these scenes with Yoda reveal.” Luke’s choice to stay or leave: “What is more moral: to try to save the world or to attempt to rescue the friends closest to your heart?” — typography-faithful quotes, Arnold, “EMI Studios, Elstree,” Monday, August 6.
**Notes:** `notes/169-once-upon-a-galaxy-1980/arnold-04-elstree-august-september.md`

### Williams: “Grand Imperial March” for Vader, “a more important role in this film”

**Speaker:** John Williams, interviewed by Alan Arnold, Saturday, November 17, Los Angeles.
**Source:** “There will be a new piece of music for Darth Vader who plays a more important role in this film. ### Ford: “a cynical space pirate… not really a cynic” / “The third dimension is me”

**Speaker:** Harrison Ford, interviewed by Alan Arnold, Wednesday, March 7, Finse.
**Source:** “It’s George’s creation. He gave himself a beautiful princess in Leia and a callow youth in Luke. He gave himself a sage warrior in Ben Kenobi and he gave himself a character called Han Solo, who is described as ‘a cynical space pirate.’ But he’s not really a cynic and the phrase ‘space pirate’ doesn’t really indicate what he does… He’s the most realistic character and consequently, in a way, the easiest one to play… I was just part of the equation George dreamed up.” “I never thought of the character as having only two dimensions until the critics said so. And they’re wrong. The third dimension is me.” — typography-faithful quotes, Arnold, “Norway,” Wednesday, March 7.
