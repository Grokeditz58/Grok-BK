# 169 — Elstree: Wednesday, August 1 through Monday, September 10

Citations: Arnold, “EMI Studios, Elstree,” [journal date]. Typography locked. Arnold glosses flagged. Four further Lucas interviews (Fri 3 Aug, Fri 10 Aug, Thu 23 Aug; Guinness/Lucas Fri 24 Aug) plus Hamill wrap, Kershner, Guinness.

## August 1–17

Wednesday, August 1: Luke’s fall from the Cloud City ledge ruined in the lab (stoppage in the processing bath). Kurtz: Mark did the first part of the stunt; a double did the specialized fall. Peter Diamond: Mark doing “ninety-five percent of all his own stunts.” Sidney Ganis telex on unauthorized stills; Kurtz: they came from the teaser trailer / lab reject prints, not from unit stills. Still count: 24,000 color, 22,000 b/w.

Friday, August 3 — **Lucas interview 3**, typography locked (no room of his own; cutting room / Kersh’s room / Dagobah storyboard; plaid and denims). UA two-picture deal at Cannes after *THX* (Picker); UA dropped *Graffiti* (to Universal under option) and declined the space fantasy; Fox via Ladd because of *Planet of the Apes*. “I was given $10,000 to write the script, and it took me two years to do it.” *THX* salary “only about $15,000” over three years. Marcia “giving us the financial Support I needed” (capital S as printed) while editing for Scorsese. Turned down *$100,000* to direct *Lady Ice*: “If I had said yes, I would have abandoned *Graffiti* and I would probably be nowhere today.” “Once I decide I am going to do something, I just don’t let anything stop me.” Met Kurtz during *THX* (Steenbeck in the Mill Valley attic; *Two-Lane Blacktop* / *Apocalypse Now* then *Graffiti*). Lucasfilm “founded in 1970.” After *Graffiti*’s success: “I wrote a screenplay I didn’t like much… The first one was about Luke’s father, and the second one took place at a later time and was mainly about Luke.” Independent production: “Star Wars wasn’t a Fox production… my production company made it.” Merchandising: “After the film’s success I had to have a merchandising company to ಇಂಗ್ಡಂ! things” (garbage: ≈ handle). Quality standard vs Fox: turned down a junk-jewelry half-million. ILM founded 1974; Cantwell then Dykstra; Brian Johnson and Richard Edlund now. Fox didn’t support ILM at first: “for the first six months I was supporting it out of my *Graffiti* earnings… reimbursed for my ಙ್ಗhat investment of between $400,000 and $500,00.” (garbage: ≈ that / $500,000.) Wanted San Francisco; started in L.A. “for two-and-a-half years. Then I moved it to San Francisco.”

Monday, August 6: Yoda debut, Frank Oz. **Kershner**: the sequence is “the heart of the picture… the philosophical content… We learn how it is used, where it comes from. The Jedi training program and disciplines are explained. The concept of the Force’s use for good rather than evil is promulgated. Finally, these scenes lead to the big confrontation duel between Luke and Darth Vader, a fight that is very ritualized… ” Luke’s choice to stay or leave: “What is more moral: to try to save the world or to attempt to rescue the friends closest to your heart?” Yoda “conquered his senses, his fears, his anger, his ego.” Wednesday, August 8: printed take — Luke “I am not afraid”; Yoda “You will be, my son… You will be.” (lock “my son” as printed). Snake up Mark’s leg; Animal Actors list. Thursday, August 9: Kenobi’s definition of the Force quoted; foreign titles (Lord Darth Fener / Dark Vador).

Friday, August 10 — **Lucas interview 4**, typography locked. Motion-control: “Before our use of computer-controlled cameras, it had not been possible to pan on flying objects in the way we did in *Star Wars*.” “Concealment, after all, is the art or artifice of it.” Fantasy “firmly rooted in the implausible… realizing dreams”; some things “just can’t be filmed.” Predicts theatrical video when resolution matches film. *Star Wars* post: four weeks over, Fox “cut me off,” then relented; editor “didn’t work out”; start from scratch, three months behind. ILM: “a year and $1 million and had only come up with one acceptable shot.” “John Dykstra is basically a camera technician… obsessed with the research work.” Lucas to Edlund and Muren plus George Mather; storyboards and B&W dogfight footage as guides. “We never really got R2 to move more than two or three feet.” Christmas 1976 trailer: “DLM knuckled down” (slip for ILM). First non-crew screening, San Francisco, May 1977: “Star Wars works. That was my primary reaction.” “Hollywood is like a grandiose high school… dedicated to making deals.” Ranch in Marin: “film research facility… like making movies in a ranch environment… a community of people working together.” Future: “Nonlinear films, basically, films that would use neither plot nor characters to convey emotional content. Pure film.”

Wednesday, August 15: Kenobi on Dagobah as “a vision, a ghost, an angel” (Kurtz); Guinness still unconfirmed (eye operation; tax-march to Downing Street). Friday, August 17: Frank Oz / Miss Piggy tea; Muppets history; “I wouldn’t like to be that famous.”

## August 20–31

Monday, August 20 — **Mark Hamill** interview, typography locked: endurance (wires, snow, tank, snakes; fourteen head-bumps). Conflict with Kershner on the carbon-freezing set: “Don’t do that expression… It was all terribly childish.” Electricians’ party “Go home, Yank.” “The percentage George gave us of *Star Wars* was probably thirty times our salary. He didn’t have too it.” (slip). “*Star Wars* was a celebration.”

Tuesday, August 21 — **Paul Hirsch**: first four reels, Lucas started the second cut; “too much exposition… pretty much solved.” Target 110–120 minutes; ~120 hours shot, ~60 printed. Suschitzky “soft, luminous.” “Intimate knowledge of *Star Wars* isn’t necessary.” Then a second heading **“Wednesday, August 21”** (48 words; Soyuz 33) — lock as printed.

Thursday, August 23 — **Lucas interview 5** (formative years / characters), typography locked. Flash Gordon newspaper strip and serials at nine and ten; “Tommy Tomorrow”; “loving them that much when they were so awful, I began to wonder what would happier if they were done really well” (slip: happier ≈ happen). Wells, Orwell, Verne, Huxley in college; *Metropolis*, *Forbidden Planet*; Uncle Scrooge; Frazetta, Druillet, Moebius. “It is difficult to pinpoint… but it was about 1973.”

Characters, typography locked: “They all came out of one composite-Luke was the composite, which is another way of saying they came out of me… I was dealing with two opposites, and these are the two opposites in myself-a naive, innocent idealism and a view of the world that is cynical, more pessimistic… Should he be a she? At one point, I was going to have a girl at the center. Luke Skywalker might never have been; he might have been a heroine. Leia came out of Luke, so to speak, just as Han did, as the opposite of Luke. Han Solo evolved from my wanting to have a cynical foil for the innocent Luke.”

“Originally, the story was about an older brother coming to find his younger brother, who’s living on a farm, so that together they can rescue their father, an old Jedi. The older brother is a battle-hardened warrior. This character evolved into Han… Ben Kenobi developed from the father figure into a friend of the father.”

Vader: “Darth Vader and Ben were developed in my first script-it was their story. When I wrote the second script it was later in time and Luke was the focus. I wanted to develop an essentially evil, very frightening character. He started as a kind of intergalactic bounty hunter, evolved into a grotesque knight, and as I got deeper into the knight ethos he became more a dark warrior than a mercenary. Again, I split him up and it was from the early concept of Darth Vader as a bounty hunter that Boba Fett came.”

Lando: “a kind of alter ego for Han… more of a rogue, gambler, and con artist than a fast-shooting, fast-talking type.” Chewbacca from Indiana, the Alaskan husky, “Marcia drives a little station wagon… this big fuzzy dog looking like he’s copilot… I’d put Indiana’s spirit in the next one.” R2-D2: Walter Murch mixing *Graffiti*, “George, get me R2-D2” = reel two, dialogue two; C-3PO invented to be compatible; Abbott and Costello / tall and skinny vs short. “There is, I think, a common thread through fairy tale, myth, far-away adventures, and Westerns… a timeless feel.” Casting: saw thousands himself; Mark’s “innocence”; Harrison tested against others despite *Graffiti*; Carrie “eighteen but she appeared to be thirtyish… a young senator.” Kenobi “wasn’t meant to be Christ like, but rather a thoughtful and intelligent man with a noble bearing.” Force: “I wanted a concept of religion based on the premise that there is a God and there is good and evil. I began to distill the essence of all religions into what I thought was a basic idea common to all religions and common to primitive thinking. I wanted to develop something that was nondenominational but still had a kind of religious reality. I believe in God and I believe in right and wrong… ‘Do unto others . . .’ is the philosophy that permeates my work.” Churches using *Star Wars*; “the amoral influence of television.”

Friday, August 24: Guinness confirmed. Lucas: “Sir Alec is Ben Kenobi. He’s the real thing.” Role simplified “to reduce the strain.” Guinness, typography locked, via Arnold: 1975 *Murder by Death*; unsolicited script, “Good God, it’s science fiction!”; Robert Moore, “Lucas is a film man”; dialogue “pretty ropey” but he kept turning pages. Lunch: “I liked his quiet diffidence. I trusted him.” Original description “an old desert rat”; Lucas “some slight connotation of Gandalf in the Tolkien tales.” “I never lost faith… ‘Lucas doesn’t know what he’s doing,’ they’d say… ” Success: “It was a darn good story dashingly told… Failure has a thousand explanations. Success doesn’t need one.” Sequel: “During the making of *Star Wars* George and Gary asked me if I would reappear in a sequel. I told them, ‘Yes, absolutely.’… eight months ago I developed wretched eye trouble… I felt that if I could help them out of a difficulty, I must do so.”

Sunday/Monday, August 26/27: SEACON, Brighton; Kurtz presents a Hugo to *Superman* (Christopher Reeve); a nearby voice: “Space opera! Junk movie!” Friday, August 31: Dagobah wrap picnic (Meredith Kurtz). **Hamill**, typography locked, on a third film: “George Lucas is very clever… He’s not going to compromise himself by making us indispensable… look at what’s happening to Harrison… George has left him in limbo in this one… ‘He’s in a perfect state of hibernation.’ So George has given himself the option. Han is not vital to future stories.” “Ben Kenobi says to Yoda: ‘Luke is our only hope.’ Yoda shakes his head: ‘No,’ he says, ‘no, there is another.’ So, you see, George could write me out… they could easily discover a long-lost sister or brother of Luke’s living in another part of the galaxy. They could just say Luke is off somewhere looking for Han Solo… It’s the legend that lives on, not necessarily the character.”
