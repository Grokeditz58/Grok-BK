## Chapter 4 — Marin County, California

Monday, October 29: Arnold visits Lucas at a redwood-paneled study on the outskirts of San Anselmo; Lucas drives him back in “a well-worn twelve-year-old Chevrolet.” Arnold suggests Marin might make him remote; Lucas: “If I want a super-stimulating environment I can always fly to New York… I don’t have to live there to be aware of them.” Fine cut on *Empire* finished; “I’m leaving for Japan. I’m executive producer of a film being directed there by Kurosawa. But I’ll always return to Marin.” Arnold’s parting gloss, flag as Arnold: “In his woodland setting he seemed a figure from a fairy tale, a puck in an elfin landscape.”

**Lucas interview**, typography locked. Drawn to Marin after USC: “I decided I would come back to northern California… liked it well enough to settle here.” “I like Marin’s rural quality.” Holdings: “three offices in San Anselmo including an editing room. In San Rafael we have Industrial Light and Magic… build the ranch facility which will consolidate all these units.” Lucas Valley, “a 2,000-acre holding”: “It was already named Lucas Valley… the only piece of land that seemed appropriate… financially within my reach.” Ranch is “purely a ‘think tank’ concept and largely an environment in which writers can create. It will have a library, editing rooms, a place to do the music and the rerecording. It will not be a place to make movies place to think.” (slip locked). Northern California group: Korty, Ritchie, Robbins, Barwood, Murch, Coppola / Zoetrope, Kaufman. “As a group we have all traded ideas and helped each other over the years.”

**Nine-film / three-trilogy claim — do not collapse** with the July 19 interview in this same book, or with Kaminski 167, Jones 166, later Lucas denials, or Kurtz’s Oslo “no chronological sequence” line. Typography locked:

“There are essentially nine films in a series of three trilogies. The first trilogy is about the young Ben Kenobi and the early life of Luke’s father when Luke was a little boy. This trilogy takes place some twenty years before the second trilogy which includes *Star Wars* and *Empire*. About a year or two passes between each story of the trilogy and about twenty years pass between the trilogies. The entire saga spans about fifty-five years.”

“I have story treatments on all nine. I also have voluminous notes, histories, and other material I’ve developed for various purposes. Some of it will be used, some not. Originally, when I wrote *Star Wars*, it developed into an epic on the scale of *War and Peace*, so big I couldn’t possibly make it into a movie. So cut it in half, but it was still to big, so each half into three parts. I then had material for six movies. After the success of *Star Wars* but to write and finish them. Mostly, it will be a I added another trilogy but stopped there, primarily because reality took over. After all, it takes three years to prepare and make a *Star Wars* picture. How many years are left? So I’m still left with three trilogies of nine films. At two hours each, that’s about eighteen hours of film!” (conversion slips locked as printed: “still to big,” “but to write and finish them. Mostly, it will be a I added another trilogy.” Sense is recoverable as: after *Star Wars*’s success he added another trilogy, then stopped because of time.)

“The next chapter is called ‘Revenge of the Jedi.’ It’s the end of this particular trilogy, the conclusion of the conflict begun in *Star Wars* between Luke and Darth Vader. It resolves that situation once and for all. I won’t say who survives and who doesn’t, but if we are ever able to link together all three you’d find the story progresses in a very logical fashion.”

Friday, November 2: Kershner at the Kurtzes’ Sausalito house (they are in L.A.) pulling LPs for a temp track for John Williams (Stravinsky, Shostakovich, Hindemith, Copland, Prokofiev, Bliss; Prokofiev’s *October*). “John has a strong feel for what he wants.”

Saturday, November 3 — **Kershner** last interview, typography locked. Puget Sound island retreat. L.A. and Washington as places “seeking power, and power corrupts the soul.” Film is collaborative: “I don’t believe filmmakers are artists in the sense that a writer starting with a blank page or a painter with a blank canvas is an artist.” But “George Lucas is one, a conceptual artist in a very profound way. That doesn’t necessarily mean that his work is profound… he is unique in what he does.” Guidelines: “a script I helped to polish… maintain the rhythm started by the first film, but my film would be different.” Asked about directing “Revenge of the Jedi”: “The *Star Wars* saga is George’s life. It is the thing that he has created. I came in on an interim basis. I’d like to do a film that represents more of my own attitudes.” “If I had *Empire* to direct over again… Almost everything I would make a different picture if I were starting now.” “I felt a pride in the picture, a great responsibility because I know what it meant to George because of the way it was financed.”

Monday, November 5 — **Ben Burtt**, typography locked. Dismayed not to have been at the spotting session with Williams, Lucas, Kershner, and Kurtz. Space fantasy: “a clean slate… Sound in fantasy can function somewhat like music.” Hired for a year of library work before *Star Wars*. “George Lucas and I agreed that electronic sound had been overused in fantasy and space films. We decided to use organic sounds instead… actual sounds-bulldozers, landslides, trains.” Lightsabers: “electrical hums and the sounds of a TV picture tube between channels, all greatly magnified.” Planet destruction: “several military explosions, thunder, and impact on metal.” Rebel vs Empire: Falcon “loose and rusty”; Empire “more organized, more martial.” Chewbacca: “fragments of many different animal sounds: bears, badgers, camels, and other mammal sounds.” Hired by Kurtz out of USC.

Wednesday, November 7: ILM, San Rafael, ~seventy people, “about one-fifth of *The Empire Strikes Back* will have been filmed-without actors.” Magician-trademark plaque. Asteroid-belt Falcon vs TIE sequence shown to Lucas; technicians not satisfied; back to benches. Arnold’s Méliès / *A Trip to the Moon* (1902) close.

## Chapter 5 — Los Angeles, California

Saturday, November 17 — **John Williams** at home (Arnold’s cab lost in the hills). Typography locked: “*Empire* will require 107 minutes of underscore, although some of this will involve quotes from my original score for *Star Wars*… about 102 minutes of new music… mid-January.” “a chameleon.” “I would like to rest after *Empire*.” Themes: “a new theme for Yoda… piquant… more profound, more noble… a new theme that could be called the Love Theme developing from the love interest between Princess Leia and Han. There will be a new piece of music for Darth Vader who plays a more important role in this film. In *Star Wars* he had what you could call a musical fragment, but in the new picture there will be a Grand Imperial March… new thematic material for the ice-planet battle sequences. The Force Theme from *Star Wars* will be more widely used and developed. Finally, the *Star Wars* March, which is associated with Luke and the good side of the Force, will be newly presented.” Conscious model: “late nineteenth century, romantic orchestral scores… the music should have a familiar emotional ring so that as you looked at those strange robots and other unearthly creatures… the music would be rooted in familiar traditions.” Recording “something like a quarter of a million dollars” with the LSO; four-sided LP “which the Robert Stigwood Organization will issue when the movie opens.”

## Postscript

Typography locked. Arnold, more than thirty years in the business, “never considered it nor the people who work in it to be particularly glamorous.” Freelance crews, long hours, no pension. “it is undeniably a special business… a product that may become legendary, or totally forgotten, or-as is more usual-simply run-of-the-mill.” Hollywood Boulevard Walk of Fame. Dedication: “And it is to these people who make films such a warm and exciting business that this journal is dedicated… largely, if not completely, this is a structure based on talent. No amount of financing, packaging, or promotion is a substitute for the plain fact that in the beginning is the word, the idea-it all begins with once upon a time.”

## About the Author

Typography locked. London-born journalist; British government press officer in Egypt and the U.S.A.; four years a foreign correspondent in New York; “thirty other countries.” Books: *The Incredible Sarah*, *Valentino*, and *How to Visit America*. “some forty films” (Monroe to Minnelli, Dietrich to Caine). Mentions Eleanor Roosevelt, Lillian Hellman, Edward R. Murrow. Closing quote, typography locked: “They gave me perspective… They taught me that success isn’t tangible, though in show business some people make the mistake of believing that it is.”
