# 160 summary — Shields, *George Lucas* (2002)

Chelsea House YA biography in the *Behind the Camera* series. 112 pages, 2002, written with *Attack of the Clones* just out and Episode III expected in 2005. Secondary source. Full hand-read.

## Priority hits (Vader / Anakin / the Force / Sith / Chosen One / power / canon)

- **The Force, pp. 20–21 — not a direct Lucas quote.** Photo caption, p. 20: the high-school crash "ultimately led to his concept of The Force,’ which he thought up while convalescing in the hospital after the accident." Body, p. 21: "According to those close to Lucas, it was during the weeks of rehabilitation that that he began thinking about “the Force,” the energy of life often referred to in the Star Wars trilogy." The doubled "that that" is in the book. The Lucas quotes on the same page are about being given "an extra day," becoming "more aware of myself and my feelings," and trusting his instincts — they do **not** name the Force. Do not promote the hospital-origin claim as a first-person Lucas statement.
- **What *Star Wars* is about, p. 55 (chapter epigraph, attributed to George Lucas):** "I would rather see us be a positive force in the universe than a cancer. We have the knowledge to be either one. That, in essence, is what Star Wars is about. We are both good and evil, and we have a choice."
- **Friendship / compassion vs. "what is negative," p. 57:** Lucas, on sticking to "the folk side of things" and avoiding sex and violence: "My films aren't that violent or that sexy. Instead, I'm dealing with the need for humans to have friendships, to be compassionate, to band together to help each other and to join together against what is negative."
- **Cynicism, p. 56:** "You can learn from cynicism, but you can't build on it."
- **Vader** is named as an unexpected 1977 icon (pp. 10, 12, 14), as the Empire's "enforcer" in a fairy-tale outline (p. 58: change Vader to a dragon and children would recognize the story), and in a THX costume parallel (p. 40: "black-armored Darth Vader confronts Luke Skywalker and Princess Leia, both garbed in white"). On *Empire*, Shields (not Lucas) writes that Kershner treats the characters as human beings: "Luke is clearly afraid when he confronts Darth Vader alone" (p. 67). No power-level, Chosen One, or Sith-doctrine statements.
- **No canon-hierarchy** discussion. No "Chosen One." "Sith" does not appear as a term of lore.

## Other Lucas statements worth keeping (filmmaking, independence, digital)

See the chapter notes. Highlights: p. 12 tired/stressed at the Mann's opening; p. 13 "We just fell on the floor"; p. 18 father / "I will never be president of a company"; p. 30 hating script writing and plot; p. 33 / 40 on Warners recutting *THX*; p. 41 "If we're not careful about the way we conduct our lives…"; p. 48–49 Academy of Achievement "dark period" writing *Graffiti*; p. 59–60 "I'll make this into three movies"; p. 61 "I refuse to command other people"; p. 62 to Pollock, after exhaustion, he would stop directing; p. 67 "I'm a filmmaker, not a director… I might be a toymaker if I wasn't a filmmaker"; p. 85–93 digital revolution, recutting movies on the Internet, independence.

## Factual slips in the book (do not repeat as fact)

- p. 14: "Hans Solo."
- p. 61: "Carrie Fischer."
- p. 47: typo "Eucas."
- p. 71: marriage described as "18 years" at the 1983 filing; they married 22 February 1969 (~14 years).
- p. 71 and p. 23 caption of Rise (separate title): Shields implies three adopted children at the time of the divorce; only Amanda (1981) was adopted during the marriage (Katie 1988, Jett 1993).
- p. 73: *Toy Story* dated 1997 (the film is 1995).
- Index (p. 100 area): Alec Guinness listed under *American Graffiti* — an index error (the body correctly places him in *Star Wars*, p. 61).
- Contents page lists "Index 180"; the index actually begins around printed p. 100.

Birthdate in the chronology is 14 May 1944, which is the standard date.
