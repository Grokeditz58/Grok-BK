## Ch. 1: Star Wars (pp. 7–15)

- Opening caption, p. 7: C-3PO and R2-D2 as two of the "strange new characters" introduced in 1977.
- Chapter epigraph, a Fox spokesman "days after the opening of Star Wars in 1977": "It's madness . . . The lines at the theaters are unbelievable."
- p. 7–8: Today Lucas is known for the *Star Wars* trilogy and Indiana Jones; ILM and "Sky walker Sound" (space in the PDF) "count 28 Oscars to their credit." Educational and gaming software, including *Curse of Monkey Island* and *Jedi Knight*, are mentioned as award-winning.
- 25 May 1977: 33-year-old Lucas and Marcia sit in a Los Angeles restaurant watching the Mann's Chinese ticket office. Shields says Lucas had "spent two years scripting Star Wars, The Empire Strikes Back, and Return of the Jedi" even though he disliked scriptwriting, "a year directing and shooting," then cutting with Marcia. Premiere "at only 40 other theaters nationwide" besides Mann's (later "a little more than three dozen screens").
- Fox buzz is bad; Warners had already been "burned" by *THX-1138* (Variety: "Abstract, handsomely stylistic but sluggish," p. 9). Friends' screening: Spielberg and De Palma "said nothing. Marcia burst into tears" (p. 9). Alan Ladd, Jr. is "behind the film one hundred percent." Irwin Allen after an early screening: "There's no stars, there's no love story, what are they clapping at?" (p. 9).
- p. 10: "practically no one was expecting anything on the order of Darth Vader, talking robots, leaps into hyperspace, and light sabers." David Weitzner (Fox VP, worldwide advertising): "It was the first film I'm aware of—and I started in the movie business in 1968—that had a complete marketing manual: research, flow charts and everything." Theater-chain seminars in fall 1976; Richard Einiger: "Everybody that attended was confused by words like ‘R2-D2' and ‘C-3PO' and ‘Darth Vader,' because it was fresh and original" (p. 12). Five TV commercials instead of print ads; ticket price given as $2.75.
- **Lucas, p. 12**, sitting in the restaurant: he felt "tired, stressed, and thinking about nothing but all of the things I hadn't done and should have done."
- The line forms at Mann's. **Lucas, p. 13:** "We just fell on the floor" with amazement.
- 14-year-old Robert Cartegna (Warwick, New Jersey) saw it three times in the first week; opening-scroll / blockade-runner reaction quoted.
- *Washington Post* review headed "A Spectacular Intergalactic Joyride" is excerpted at length (p. 13). *New York Daily News* 3½ stars.
- First six days $2.5 million; "biggest hit ever in 50 years at the Mann's Chinese Theater"; nine days $3.6 million; Fox stock $11.75 to $21; mid-summer the public is "crazy for Luke Skywalker, Hans Solo, Princess Leia, and of course, Darth Vader" (p. 14 — **Hans** is the book's typo). Carter jealous that Amy could watch it in the White House theater. Late September, $100 million.
- Closer, p. 15: "a quiet kid from Modesto, California who said that when he was a teenager, he only went to movies to chase girls."

## Ch. 2: The Wanderer (pp. 17–23)

- Epigraph: AP, Dennis Kamstra (locker partner) at the 20th reunion in 1982: "the kind of wimp you used to slap around with a towel."
- Birth: Modesto, **14 May 1944** (p. 17; chronology agrees). Family on a walnut ranch; stationery store provided the income.
- **Lucas, p. 18:** "My father provided me with a lot of business principles, a small-town, retail-business ethic ... I guess I learned it." Conservative outlook from his dad. At 18 he refused to take over the store: "I told him, ‘There are two things I know for sure. One is that I will end up doing something with cars, whether I'm a racer, a mechanic . . . whatever, and two ... I will never be president of a company.'"
- D+ average at Thomas Downey High School. History books "from Ancient Greece and Alexander the Great, up to the Civil War—the Monitor and the Merrimac"; fiction: *Kidnapped*, *Treasure Island*, *Huck Finn*, *Swiss Family Robinson*. No TV until he was 10. Westerns as the seed of *Star Wars* "good guys" vs "bad guys." John Wayne. **Lucas, p. 19:** "I only went to movies to chase girls. It took years before good movies got to my town—and foreign films? Never."
- Fiat Bianchina; "suicide doors."
- **p. 20 caption (Shields / caption writer, not a quoted Lucas line):** "George Lucas’ primary ambition was actually to work with cars, whether it was racing them or working on them. Unfortunately, a near-fatal car accident in high school ended that dream abruptly, but ultimately led to his concept of The Force,’ which he thought up while convalescing in the hospital after the accident." Typography locks the odd "The Force,’".
- Three days before graduation: broadsided; tree uprooted and moved two feet; thrown through the open roof; seatbelt "mysteriously broken," which the police said saved him from being crushed. **Lucas, p. 21:** "every day now is an extra day. I've been given an extra day so I've got to make the most of it."
- **p. 21 body, attributed to "those close to Lucas," not to Lucas in the first person:** "According to those close to Lucas, it was during the weeks of rehabilitation that that he began thinking about “the Force,” the energy of life often referred to in the Star Wars trilogy." (Book typo: "that that.") Then a direct quote: "The accident made me more aware of myself and my feelings. I began to trust my instincts. I had the feeling that I should go to college, and I did."
- Modesto Junior College: "realistic goals… setting the goals of getting decent grades… was a big goal, and I focused on that" (p. 21). Anthropology, sociology, psychology. Then USC cinematography, which he enrolled in thinking it was like photography. **Lucas, p. 23:** "And once I started in that department, I found what it was that I loved and was good at. And I realized I could do it very well, and that I enjoyed doing it. It really ignited a passion in me, and it took off from there. After that, I didn't do anything but films."

## Ch. 3: Film School (pp. 25–31)

- Epigraph, Lucas: "If somebody gave me a hundred feet of fdm, I made a movie out of it." ("fdm" is a PDF typo for "film.")
- Pollock, *Skywalking*, quoted: the change was dramatic. **Lucas via Pollock, p. 26:** "Suddenly my whole life was film—every waking hour. It was all new, neat, and exciting." Hollywood craftsmen averaging age 55; **Lucas, p. 26:** "A bit of history opened up like a seam, and as many of us who could crammed in." He recalls classmates complaining they couldn't get a class or materials.
- Animation class; 32 feet of film; "two dozen awards" for the student project (Shields's figure).
- Four "strengths" Shields lists:
  1. Ordinary-viewer instincts. **Lucas, p. 27:** "I'm so ordinary that a lot of people can relate to me, because it's the same kind of ordinary that they are. I think it gives me insight into the mass audience. I know what I liked as a kid, and I still like it."
  2. "Sense of structure and visual continuity" (a classmate's phrase), including cockpit/meteor perspectives in the *Star Wars* films.
  3. Sound and music as glue (*The Emperor*; later *American Graffiti*). **Lucas, p. 28:** "The sound and music are 50 percent of the entertainment in a movie." This, Shields says, leads to ILM and Skywalker Sound.
  4. Comfort with technology. Student film *1:42:08* as "a hymn to his love of cars" — "The characters are the cars, not the drivers" (p. 29).
- Weakness: shy, machines vs. people; goes ahead on set even if assistants don't follow. **Lucas, p. 30:** "I did terrible in script writing classes, because I hated script writing. I hated stories, and I hated plot, and I wanted to make visual films that had nothing to do with telling a story." Method: "Mostly I just followed my inner feelings and passions, and said ‘I like this, and I like this,' and I just kept going to where it got warmer and warmer, until it finally got hot, and then that's where I was." A talent is "something you can lose yourself in—something that you can start at 9 in the morning, look up from your work and it's 10 o'clock at night—and something that you have a natural ability to do very well."
- 1966 USC BFA (chronology also places the student-film prize in 1967–68). Moves to San Francisco rather than staying in L.A. *THX-1138: 4EB (Electronic Labyrinth)*: Shields says "funds chipped in by family members," "20-minute tale," first place at the 1967–68 National Student Film Festival, Warner Bros. scholarship to observe *Finian's Rainbow*.
