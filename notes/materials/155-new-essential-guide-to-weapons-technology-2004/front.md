## S011 — Introduction (printed xi)
Opens on the lightsaber as “uniquely and recognizably *Star Wars*” and “the glowing, elegant weapon of the Jedi.” Argument: SW tech is “both fantastic and oddly familiar” (Han’s blaster ~ gunslinger’s pistol; Chewbacca’s bowcaster ~ crossbow; both fire “bolts of pure energy”; Jango Fett’s armor fuses “medieval concepts and modern weaponry, yet the suit enables him to fly”). Book is a “wide cross section” of devices from films, comic books, role-playing games, novels, video games, and other sources — not a complete catalog.
**Timeline:** Covers weapons and technology “up to the final days of the Yuuzhan Vong invasion, as described in the most recent installments of the New Jedi Order series.” Invasion “begins about twenty-five years after the Battle of Yavin.” As of writing, “the Yuuzhan Vong invasion has yet to be completely repelled.” “Key events yet to be detailed in *Episode III* … cannot be addressed here.”
**Vital Statistics** (starts here): each major entry has a list.
DAMAGE TYPE (WEAPONS), printed definitions:
- **Blaster Energy:** concentrated, high-particle beam; can affect organic and inorganic materials; often accompanied by intense heat.
- **Laser Energy:** much more powerful form of blasterfire, designed to take out starships and other large targets; also accompanied by heat.
- **Sonic Energy:** physical damage created by concentrated sonic waves.
- **Ion Energy:** affects only electrical systems and components.
- **Heat or Cold Energy:** usually at the cellular level, from extreme temperatures.
- **Electrical Energy:** electrical discharges; often disrupts neurological functions.
- **Kinetic:** solid physical object, or a device that replicates being hit by one; usually blunt trauma.
- **Explosive:** violent explosion, often coupled with kinetic or other forms of damage.
- **Piercing:** a sharp object, or a small fast-moving projectile, penetrating a target.
- **Slashing:** long cuts or slices, usually from blade attacks.
- **Poison:** toxin introduced to the victim’s system; effects from blindness to death; category includes poison gases.
- **Lightsaber Energy:** unique energy generated by a Jedi’s lightsaber; sometimes emulates excessive heat or slashing weapons.
DEFENSE TYPE (DEFENSES): primary form of damage against which the device provides protection; types same as above. Continues on xii.

## S012 — Vital Statistics continued + Historical Perspective start (printed xii)
Remaining vital-stat fields (left column):
- **OPTIMUM/MAXIMUM RANGES:** range (usually in meters) at which the device is most effective, then maximum operating range. Printed example: “A blaster, for example, is most accurate at 30 meters, but can be fired successfully at a target up to 120 meters away.” Some general equipment items have an “effective range” covering the area within which the item is most useful.
- **REACH (MELEE WEAPONS):** distance by which the weapon increases the wielder’s reach. Example: a simple blade may only provide a few centimeters of additional reach, while a polearm can provide a meter or more.
- **COVERAGE (DEFENSES):** extent of the area protected by the device.
- **SIZE (EQUIPMENT):** approximate height and/or length, listed in metric values.
- **WEIGHT (EQUIPMENT):** approximate weight of the device.
- **PRIMARY MANUFACTURER:** company most responsible for designing and building the device.
- **AFFILIATION:** group, individual, or individuals with which the device is most often associated; if used by multiple organizations or characters, this section only lists the most prominent.
**Art:** large conical device (banded cylinder with red stripe, dark viewports, pointed tip) occupying the upper right — not captioned on this page.
**Historical Perspective** (starts lower left; text wraps around the art; does not finish on this page):
- History of the galaxy’s “wondrous and diverse technologies is astonishingly complex”; distinct origins of specific devices and weapons “are often impossible to trace.”
- In general technology advanced at a “steady, albeit relatively slow pace,” marked by “a handful of dramatic periods of rapid technological progress,” almost always “forced by conflict.”
- Beyond simple tools, wheels, and other primitive inventions, “the most significant technological breakthrough was undoubtedly the blaster.” Unknown where blasters first appeared; “some scholars believe that the first energy weapons were developed on Coruscant (which is widely recognized as the center of the galaxy).”
- “It is certain that, as in the case of arrows and stone knives, blasters and similar devices developed independently on multiple worlds at roughly the same time.” Assumed these advanced ranged weapons were first conceived for self-defense and perhaps hunting, but “quickly became the primary tools in war and conquest.”
- “There can be no argument that the next—and perhaps most important—major technological leap was the discovery of hyperspace travel.” Ability to travel between star systems “ushered in a new age of scientific discovery.” Explorers forced to invent tools for harsh/hostile new worlds; useful devices from specific cultures “were quickly identified, and soon spread across the galaxy.” Warlords and conquerors “who had previously been relegated to single systems were suddenly capable of attacking neighboring civilizations as well, often with devastating results.”
- Advent of hyperspace technology also allowed formation of the Old Republic. After years of unchecked anarchy, “saner systems prevailed.” Beginning with Coruscant and the Core systems, leaders agreed to form a unified government “based on the ideals of freedom and democracy.” Over the next “several thousand standard years,” the Old Republic reached out to other civilizations, “trying to bring enlightenment to all of the galaxy’s inhabitants.” Not all known systems became part of the Old Republic; “large sections of the galaxy remained torn by violence”; the government’s existence “ensured some semblance of order and allowed a steady flow of technology from the Core Worlds to the Outer Rim.”
- Old Republic “introduced unparalleled prosperity and contentment, but over time this led to complacency.” While it encouraged scientific research and an open exchange of ideas, “the relative absence of conflict meant that civilizations did not need to find new ways to fight one another, or defend themselves.”

## S013 — Historical Perspective conclusion (printed xiii)
Continues mid-sentence from xii (“And again, during these times, there appeared dramatic changes in technology.”).
- **Empress Teta / Unification Wars** (example of conflict-driven change): more powerful handheld weapons and ground vehicles for large-scale battles; combat-ready personal shields and planetary defenses introduced; widespread use of dangerous battle droids; advances in the Jedi Order’s lightsabers.
- **Sith invasion** of the galaxy, “some four thousand years before the Battle of Yavin,” “served a similar function.” Aside from these few conflagrations, “the galaxy remained relatively unchanged for centuries at a time.”
- **Clone Wars** + introduction of an Army of the Republic = next major period of advancement. Under former Jedi Count Dooku, the Confederacy of Independent Systems created an army “unlike anything the galaxy had ever known”: “a unique collaboration of diverse weapons, vehicles, and droids that challenged the safety of the Old Republic.” In response the Old Republic assembled its own army “by leveraging mysterious cloning technology to create a near-limitless supply of troops.” Both sides “enjoyed immense wealth, funding the construction of even more fearsome weapons of war.” The Clone Wars “pitted history’s two largest armies against each other” and “spread to nearly every inhabited world,” again pushing progress “in most fields of technology, especially starship weaponry.”
- **Galactic Civil War:** the vast armies of the Clone Wars were “largely replaced by the concept of the ‘superweapon.’” The Death Star, “which has its origins in the Clone Wars,” was “a massive space station capable of destroying an entire planet. Nothing like it had ever been encountered.” To combat Imperial inventions such as the Death Star, the Rebel Alliance “was forced to use existing (and often outdated) technology in innovative ways,” including “the acquisition of cutting-edge starfighters such as the X-wing.” Empire: large army and a fleet of Star Destroyers “bristling with weapons.” Alliance “embraced everything from simple proton torpedoes and stubborn astromech droids to life-saving bacta tanks,” and “was perhaps the most versatile army in history”; its ability “to find—or invent—the right tool for any job introduced a wide number of new or improved technologies to the galaxy.”
- **Post-Empire / New Republic:** galaxy “again settled into a period of slow technological growth.” NR did push starfighter and starship technologies “in order to combat Imperial Remnant forces.” Empire’s research “was largely focused on a seemingly endless string of experimental weapons of mass destruction.” “Engineers and designers on either side did not change their core philosophies until the galaxy was introduced to the terrifying Yuuzhan Vong.”
- **Yuuzhan Vong:** “an alien race from outside the galaxy who invaded the New Republic.” Advance “exceedingly violent, claiming untold millions of lives as they conquered world after world.” They “introduced the galaxy to mind-boggling new concepts.” “The Yuuzhan Vong’s technologies are a bizarre combination of organic and inorganic engineering; thus they have challenged the perceptions of the galaxy’s inventors.” Throughout the conflict “the New Republic was constantly forced to adapt or be destroyed and its scientists are discovering many amazing devices used by the Yuuzhan Vong.” Closing line: “Only by developing increasingly more effective weapons and versatile countermeasures can the galaxy hope to survive future challenges on the level of the Yuuzhan Vong invasion.”
**Art (uncaptioned):** large olive-brown inverted conical/faceted vessel or emplacement with a dish-like upper housing, side-mounted gun barrels, and a dark spherical object suspended from the pointed lower tip. No printed caption or callouts on this page.

## S014 — Major Manufacturers start (printed xiv)
Intro: devices “ranging from handheld comlinks capable of transmitting messages across the stars to personal blasters designed to blow attackers out of their boots.” “Literally hundreds of thousands” of companies design/manufacture weapons and other technology; “dozens are considered on the cutting edge of their respective fields.” Scale ranges from small operations “like Quagga’s Garage, which provides equipment to mechanics on Tatooine,” to galaxywide firms “with manufacturing plants, supply outlets, and subsidiaries on hundreds of planets.” Impossible to catalog all; “here begins a list of the most prominent, influential, or innovative organizations responsible for the galaxy’s wondrous technology.”

**Arakyd Industries** (logo: stylized black/white winged-teardrop mark).
- Largely known as a droid manufacturer (among creations: the Imperial probot) but has made “strong inroads into the areas of starship and weapons technology.” Success in those fields “has stemmed from its strong share of the droid market”; many of its weapons systems “integrate advanced droid brains to enhance performance.” “Arakyd’s designs have always been heavily geared toward combat applications.”
- Founded by industrialist **Velitz Arakyd** and his children on the planet **Vulpter**, “some nine thousand standard years before the Battle of Yavin.” As he laid the groundwork, Velitz “vowed to eliminate all competitors, through whatever means necessary.” Initial droid models “were prepped for war, despite strict Old Republic laws regulating the manufacture and sale of such automata.” Droids and “well-designed Arakyd weaponry inspired fear in Arakyd’s rivals, which reluctantly agreed to become Arakyd subsidiaries.”
- Continued “bullying competitors, even after the company amassed enough wealth to buy other companies outright.” Tactics “typified by the violent hostile takeover of Viper Sensor Intelligence Systems (VSIS) during the Clone Wars.” “The Imperial probot is just one product of the Viper–Arakyd ‘partnership.’”
- Feared for business tactics, but also formed “a number of valuable alliances.” Shortly before the Clone Wars, Arakyd “became an official member of the Techno Union.” During the Clone Wars, “along with the other members, Arakyd donated weapons, droids, and equipment to the Confederacy of Independent Systems.” Officials “were careful to keep themselves removed from actual battlefields, thus many retained powerful connections within the Old Republic.”
- Rise of the militaristic Empire “proved extremely profitable for Arakyd.” Through “political maneuvering, bribery, and selective elimination of his remaining competitors, Velitz Arakyd positioned himself as the Empire’s primary droid manufacturer.” The company “proved its loyalty and soon began receiving contracts for weapons and vehicles as well.” During the Empire’s reign, Arakyd “focused on expanding its business to include prisoner-control devices, such as nerve disruptors and interrogator droids.”
- At the dawn of the New Republic, “Arakyd retired from public view for a short time in order to regroup.” After a change of leadership, it began producing “security droids, personal security devices, and legal firearms for the consumer market.” Those endeavors “have proven moderately successful, but still account for only a small fraction of annual output.” Despite the company’s history, “Arakyd’s new board of directors convinced the New Republic of its loyalty to the current government.” “Arakyd worked closely with the New Republic, continuing its weapons and droids programs until the Yuuzhan Vong invasion.”

## S015 — Manufacturers continued (printed xv)
**Athakam MedTech/RSMA (end):** “The main corporation, however, perfected medical treatments for Imperial stormtroopers.” Strategic partnership with **Rhinnal State Medical Academy (RSMA)**, “which provides research for most of Athakam’s inventions.” Together they “have produced some of the galaxy’s most advanced microsurgery computers, cybernetic implants, hyperbaric medical chambers, and scanning devices.”

**Baktoid Armor Workshop / Baktoid Combat Automata** (logo: orange inverted-triangle/chevron device).
- **Baktoid Armor Workshop (BAW)** “was originally a vehicle manufacturer owned by the Trade Federation.” The Trade Federation “funded Baktoid’s research in order to build its secret army for an invasion of Naboo.” The company delivered “the terrifying AAT assault tank, an unstoppable transport known as the MTT, and a small fleet of other armed military vehicles.”
- After the Battle of Naboo, BAW “was held largely responsible for the invasion.” The Trade Federation, “in an attempt to distance itself from the short-lived occupation of Naboo, began dismantling Baktoid.” “Within months, most of Baktoid’s manufacturing facilities and designs had been transferred to other Trade Federation subsidiaries.” Shortly before the Battle of Geonosis, the Trade Federation “closed down the last Baktoid Armor Workshop plants on Foundry, Ord Cestus, Telti, Balmorra, and Ord Lithone.”
- “Although the company no longer exists, much of its equipment can still be found throughout the galaxy, particularly in the Outer Rim.” Designs “may have been ugly to some; however, they proved durable.” “Baktoid weapons have been known to survive for decades with only minor maintenance.” Designs “have resurfaced through other manufacturers.” “In order to supplement dwindling cash reserves on the eve of the Clone Wars, the Trade Federation began selling Baktoid designs to the highest bidders.”
- Sister company **Baktoid Combat Automata (BCA)**. The two “shared a common point of origin and board of directors,” but “could not have been more different: BAW was a publicly known company, whereas BCA was created and run in complete secrecy, presumably to protect the Trade Federation’s invasion plans.” BAW “focused on vehicles and large, mounted weapons”; BCA “designed illegal battle droids and a variety of personal blasters and blaster rifles.” “Baktoid Combat Automata did not suffer the same fate as its parent company.” When the Trade Federation “made a show of dissolving Baktoid, it preserved BCA, ensuring that the company could go on to produce equipment used at the Battle of Geonosis.” “By keeping a low profile, BCA has managed to survive well into the era of the New Republic, though its new designs are fewer and farther between.”
