# 171 — Chapter Six: Shadows in the Light (May to June 1979)

Print pp. 154–193 (EPUB anchors `page154`–`page193`; print TOC opening p. 154). Rinzler ESB pp. 154–193. Staff/crew/cast same treatment as Lucas. Rinzler glosses flagged. The February 20, 1979, shooting-script carbon-freeze dialogue, the June 21 on-set trailer revisions (Arnold’s cordless-mike transcript), and the script supervisor’s final continuity notes are kept strictly separate. Ford’s later remark that “the original line was, ‘I love you, too’” is not collapsed into the printed Feb. 20 script, which reads differently.

## Opening (p. 154)

Yoda still secret. Hamill works a prototype: “it’s amazing how his face comes to life… He also bears a resemblance to Stuart Freeborn… George… said, ‘Well, Stuart would make a great Yoda.’ ” Lucas: “We’ve been shooting tests and having a lot of discussion about Yoda… particularly when they were doing the sculpting of his head.”

## REVERSALS (pp. 154–155)

Nos. 46–49, May 9–14: snowspeeder cockpit traveling matte (Hoth battle); Cloud City corridor and dining room, Scs. 363–364 (Vader traps Luke’s friends). John Morton as Luke’s gunner “Dack (later changed to Dak for legal reasons).” Small fire on the *Falcon* set. *Variety*, same day, reports Fox saying *Empire* is “guaranteed $26 million from exhibitors.” Kurtz: “it just said to them that it doesn’t matter what the picture is like… I think that’s a negative.” After marketing and distribution, “ *Empire* would still have to clear at least $50 million to make a profit.” Sequel comparables (*Beneath the Planet of the Apes*, *Godfather II*, *Jaws 2*, *The Spy Who Loved Me*) are listed as caution, not as a forecast.

Hangar and swamp will not both fit the Star Wars Stage; a temporary extension “something like 60 feet or so” is planned because the *Falcon* “takes up almost a quarter of the stage” (Reynolds). Production says first unit will start on the stage Friday “regardless of our schedule status”—Kershner must shoot while construction continues.

Saturday: Lucas lunches with Alec Guinness at Neal’s, Covent Garden. Arnold: “I cannot yet get a definite statement as to whether Sir Alec will re-create his role as Ben Kenobi.” Lucas returns to the US with footage. Kershner first refuses to show a producer anything beyond rushes, then changes his mind and screens “everything I’ve cut, 50 minutes.” “George took exception to only one scene, only because I ended it on a certain shot… Gary had some very astute remarks, but they were minimal.” Hirsch: “Kersh is moving the camera a lot more. In the first film, the camera hardly ever moved.” Kershner gives Lucas the Norway battle footage “to go through it all and rough it together.” Arnold is incensed when a journalist “worked her way into the studio under false pretenses, and poor Carrie was her victim… a sort of rape—which is how Carrie herself described the encounter.”

## SECRET BY DESIGN (pp. 155–157)

Nos. 50–54, May 15–21: *Falcon* cockpit; large prison cell (Chewie fixes C-3PO; Han vs. Lando). John Barry, depressed after being replaced by Stanley Donen on *Saturn 3* (“It is like coming home”), is hired as second-unit director. Kurtz: “To work as a second-unit director with Kersh is very difficult anyway, because he has a tendency not to like anything that he hasn’t shot.” Second unit with Williams and Hamill: Lando rescuing Luke from the underbelly of Cloud City.

Kershner: “I filmed many shots of Solo as he is being tortured. There were flashes of electricity everywhere.” Prowse: “We had a scene where I have Han Solo in a torture chamber, but I have no idea how he got there! … everything is so secretive—no one will tell you a thing.” *The Register* (Santa Ana) would report Prowse’s habit of “giving away plot secrets.” Hamill: “He doesn’t mean to… He just has this real child-like quality to please.” Kershner: “David talks his head off.” After Prowse revealed to *Starlog* (June 1979 issue) “that Boba Fett was after Han Solo and that a disfigured Vader would be unmasked,” Lucasfilm tracked leaks: “Harrison Ford, one leak; Carrie Fisher, one leak; David Prowse—nine leaks.” Prowse admits: “I’ll always say, ‘Whatever happens, don’t mention this to anybody.’ But I am responsible for more leaks of information on this picture than anybody.” Hamill: “So they gave David pages of dummy scripts on this movie. He was saying lines I knew were not real. But he didn’t know I knew.”

Caption, Stage 2, July 6, 1979 (between takes after the Luke battle): Vader, “(to himself) ‘Ben cannot help you now, young Jedi! (to the stormtroopers) Bring my ship in!’ In postproduction, this line would be changed to a command for his shuttle.” (Wording here is “young Jedi,” not the earlier revision’s “my young Jedi”—record both, do not collapse.)

## EVACUATION (pp. 158–164)

Nos. 55–59, May 22–29: Star Wars Stage, main hangar deck (Leia’s speech; Han dismounts tauntaun; Han and Chewie fix the *Falcon*; droids by hangar doors; Leia worries about Luke). About 67 Rebel men and 10 Rebel women extras, £2,047.50 per day; Kershner “always complaining that it didn’t appear to be enough people” among ~130 extras. Dendritic salt with magnesium sulfate sparkle; Bruton, “something approaching 50 tons of salt.” Planned Prince Charles press opening dropped because they are so far behind. Fan-club winner Matthew Pak visits; Charles Champlin (*Los Angeles Times*) writes the stage is “second in size only to the James Bond Stage at Shepperton.”

Daniels overplays a “hurricane” against airplane-propeller wind; Kershner asks him to be “a bit more subtle.” Fisher’s ion-cannon debrief: “that set probably cost millions of dollars… there had to be one master shot where I did the whole speech without any cutaway point.” Kurtz: she was “very distraught” about both the troop speech and the night scene waiting for Han and Luke, “upset and almost crying”; noise “like an industrial factory”; taken out twice. Kershner shooting “completely out of continuity” while they build at night; salt jams gadgets. Lucas on set designs: “some… are improvements and some of which I’m not that crazy about.” Caption: a laser ice-cutter “which Luke would’ve used to battle wampas in scenes that were never filmed.”

EE daily: Mayhew, as Chewbacca, speaks lines later replaced by Wookiee roars, including “Where the hell have you been?” Kershner: “As the doors slam shut, I had Chewbacca scream in agony. That was a decision I made during filming.” Daniels: “playing around with Han Solo, see, I can’t bully him like I used to bully Artoo.” EE audio (cafeteria, during photography): Kershner “defends Anthony Daniels and the others against someone referring to them as ‘cardboard characters.’ ”

## MAY ILM: NIGHTS AND JETS (p. 165)

May 4 notes: Lucas to finish remaining storyboard breakdowns; 29 asteroid elements; asteroid crater set; half-size *Falcon* completed. Potatoes used as asteroids “just for laughs” (Ralston); “if you know where to look, they’re hilarious.” Bloom split shift May 11 (Muren days, Ralston 3 PM–midnight). Johnson films cloud plates from a Learjet with Astrovision, renting a VistaVision camera from Dykstra/Apogee. Empireflex finally running May 29 after a faulty motor. Model shop: “easier to start from scratch on the Darth Vader Star Destroyer rather than modifying an existing model.”

## TRAGEDY (p. 166)

Nos. 60–61, May 30–31: hangar (Luke and Dack take off; Han told Luke is missing; ceiling cracks above the *Falcon*). ILM unimpressed by tauntaun footage: “They sort of goofed on the eyes” (Muren). Thursday: John Barry “complained of feeling unwell,” collapses in Watts’s office; temperature 105; “by 2 AM, June 1, he was dead.” Cause: infectious meningitis; “a mystery how he contracted what was normally curable, and why no one else on the crew caught it.” *Variety*: death “with a suddenness that, had it been written into a film plot, would have been called implausible dramatic license.” Arnold: “the entire company is in shock.” Reportedly “a disease that he’d picked up in North Africa.” Hamill: “He was one of the kindest, friendliest people in the world.” Kurtz: contact tracing; “fortunately, nobody else got ill.” Lucas: “John was doing a fantastic job. The film would have been much better if he’d lived.”

## BLUESCREEN FEVER (pp. 166–168)

Nos. 62–68, June 1–11: *Falcon* cockpit (mynock, escape from slug, Lando system, pursued by Star Destroyers, Leia turns the *Falcon* around); second unit on the Star Wars Stage; Stage 4 carbon-freezing chamber, Sc. 376 pt. (Vader inspects the chamber). Harley Cokliss (caption later spells “Cokeliss”—record both) starts as second-unit director; Kurtz directs second unit for a week in between. Cockpit built larger than in *Star Wars*; “when Lucas found out, he was less than thrilled.” Kurtz: “the single worst set we had as far as the actors were concerned.” Hamill: “I have this theory that the bluescreen gives off rays that penetrate the brain and make you go crazy. Harrison really flipped out once, picked up a saw and started sawing through the console…” Fisher on screaming at a bluescreen corner. Covering shots a month later, same scenes, “very frustrated.” Hamill: “Actually, Harrison and I never fought in either picture. It was Carrie and I who had the screaming matches… She’s a devil.”

June 11: shooting stops at lunch for John Barry’s funeral, St. Paul’s, Grove Park Road, 3 PM; cremation 4 PM. Arnold sees Kurtz, Reynolds, Watts, Sharman, Kershner, “Stanley Kubrick and Stanley Donen.” Day 68 of shooting, 22 days over; McQuarrie flies back to LA. Hangar systematically destroyed for bog-planet construction around the *Falcon*. Kershner: “Some days I would come onto the set and find that an ice corridor had become a swamp overnight.” Kurtz disappointed in hangar production value: “More than half the material was probably focused in so close that you didn’t see the scope of the set.” Script-page caption: recent dialogue changes to Sc. 337, “in which Han Solo decides to pay a call on his old friend, Lando Calrissian.”

## HELTER SKELTER (pp. 168–171)

Nos. 69–72, June 12–15: carbon-freezing chamber, Sc. 379 pt. (Han put in carbonite); landing platform, Sc. 353 (Lando greets Han, Leia, et al.). Need to finish all *Falcon*-centered sets so the ship can be “deconstruct[ed]… for the next film.” Kershner: Lucas calls every couple of weeks; “All I’ve ever heard from him is, ‘I love them.’ ” June 14: Fisher unwell, early wrap. June 15: stomach pains and spasms, two injections; crew rumor she “had been up all night.” Fisher: Eric Idle’s “Tunisian Table-Cleaner”; “Harrison came over and the Rolling Stones came over, and I think we stayed up most of the night. So when we arrived at Cloud City, we were very happy.” Williams “would say dirty things… something unmentionable as he kissed my hand.” Ford improvises: written line when Lando eyes Leia was, “She’s traveling with me, Lando. And I don’t intend to gamble her away, so you might as well forget she exists.” On set Ford substitutes “the simpler, ‘You old smoothie.’ ”

## BUGGED (pp. 172–187) — carbon-freeze day, June 21, and the on-set rewrite

Nos. 73–76, June 18–21: asteroid cave / mynocks; landing-platform firefight; Stage 4, Sc. 379 pt. Hamill maps the duel: “I drew a figure of Darth Vader and put the different hit marks, right hip, left head.” June 21: 24.5 days over; first unit shoots Han into hibernation. Suschitzky lights from underneath through translucent floors; “like working in a Turkish bath.” Kershner: rubber gaskets burn, everyone evacuated; “The little people started getting ill… I was sick the whole time I worked there.” Watts: “the words *freezing chamber* are completely wrong because it was like a sweaty jungle.” Lucas: “Kersh, more than anyone else, decided to use an abstract look to this set… this hellish place, which is right in the middle of heaven, so to speak. It’s more steam than physical reality.” Arnold persuades Kershner to wear a cordless mike all day (June 21, 1979)—the transcript that follows.

### February 20, 1979, shooting-script text as printed here (pp. 172–173)

Keep this exact wording distinct from later versions:

VADER
Put him in the carbon freezing chamber.

BOBA
What if he doesn’t survive? He is worth a lot to me.

VADER
The Empire will compensate you for the loss.

LEIA
No.

[C-3PO complains as] Chewie attacks the stormtroopers …

HAN
Chewie, no! Stop it, Chewbacca! … Save your strength for another time, Chewie, when the odds are better.

Chewie barks a doleful farewell.

HAN
Yeah … I know … I feel the same way … Keep well. (turns to guard) You’d better chain him until it’s over.

Han takes Leia in his arms and she gives him a passionate kiss.

LEIA
 … I love you. I couldn’t tell you before, but it’s true.

HAN
Just remember that, ’cause I’ll be back.

### Trailer and set, June 21 (Arnold transcript, pp. 173–187)

Kershner wanted to talk to Ford the previous day, couldn’t reach him. Parenthetical later remark: “Larry Kasdan had written some very good scenes, but there was still the interpretation… The interpretation is what kept me awake nights.” Kershner wants the scene “like an execution”; “I’ve been to real executions… They strip people halfway down… gas chamber… black shorts.” Discovery: “You [Han Solo] have no way of knowing that you’re the one that they’re going to do anything to.”

Symmetrical set with no back: all reverses shot in the same physical direction (Kurtz). Kershner and Reynolds plan the gantry: “As Luke comes to this point, this is the first time that he sees Vader… we fill up the frame with his head and there is Vader, just taking the last few steps.” Medical-set window (round vs. square); Kershner turns off the recorder for “sort of secret material” (Luke’s hand).

Trailer, 11 AM. Kershner: “The Carbon Freezing Chamber is complex technically and dramatically… one of the few drama scenes in the picture. It has to do with love, hate, extreme fear; it has to do with anger.” Ford: last saw the group in the cell; “I know that I’m doomed, more or less… if a bounty hunter doesn’t mean that he’s deadlier than shit… then we’ve lost the dramatic value of the bounty hunter… I’ve got a debt to Jabba the Hutt… this guy is going to bump me off unless I can talk him out of it. I have never had the opportunity.” Kershner: “what we’ve learned is that Jabba doesn’t want you hurt. He wants you delivered intact. But you don’t know that.” Why Leia and Chewie are brought: “so that you would not make any problems… they’ll say, ‘Okay, we’re going to kill them.’ ” Ford cites “George’s line, ‘Give me a good fight any day over all this hiding and freezing …’ … That is the definition of Han’s character.” They add a beat with Lando: “What’s the plan now, pal?” / “What are you up to now, buddy?” Ford wants manacles off in front so the carbonite pose can work. Kershner on the later carbonite portrait: he wanted “the exact moment you realize that it’s actually happening, and you’ve got to try to stop it!”

On the Leia–Han exchange: Kershner, “No, no, no, I’m not going to do that” (the longer script declaration). Ford: “I think she ought to just say, ‘I love you,’ as I’m passing by her.” Kershner still has “Just remember that, because I’ll be back.” Ford: “If she said, ‘I love you,’ I could say very nicely, ‘Yeah, I know. Don’t worry, I’ll be back.’ ” Then, after Kershner insists “you’ve got to say, ‘I’ll be back’ ”: Ford, “But if she says, ‘I love you,’ and I say, ‘I know,’ it’s beautiful and it’s acceptable and it’s funny.” Kershner laughs, “Right.” Ford still wants “Don’t worry about this”; Kershner: “you don’t know whether this is the end or not.” Ford: “I’m not worried about myself anymore; I’m worried about her.” Kershner considers holding Vader out “until the end… ‘Put him in the Carbon Freezing Chamber.’ ” — then rejects it: no place for Vader to walk; “I hate a scene where the bad guy waits and finally says, ‘Enough of this horseshit.’ ” They settle “What’s up, pal?”; Chewie “Look after her” (Ford typo in transcript: “Look afte her”); “as I turn, she can say, *sotto voce … ‘I love you.’*” Kershner writes: “Leia: ‘I love you.’ And Han says, ‘I know.’” Then still voices “Yeah, I know. I’ll be back.” as he writes—do not treat that spoken string as the final locked line.

To Williams: Han, “What’s up, pal?”; Lando, “You’re being put into carbon freeze”; “Then why are they here?” / “To keep you polite”; Leia, “You know that could kill him”; Lando, “I’m powerless.” “The whole scene was based on ignorance before and I want it to be based on knowledge.” Fisher asks about declaring and kissing; also wants to slap Lando. They try a slap; she actually hits him; Williams: “Don’t hit me like that! … fake it.” Fisher feels rewritten “behind my back… ‘It’s the bimbo again.’ ” Kershner (later): “I regarded that scene as entirely his… That led to a little tension with Carrie… Professional jealousy is very healthy… But it was his scene.” “Carrie began to feel that she had two directors.” Kershner to Tomblin: “Some of the actors are all angry at each other… Carrie went crazy.” Ford later: “Directing is a job Kersh attacked with great courage.”

Lobot (John Hollis): “short for lobotomy. Although in the fifth draft, he had at least one line, it had been decided that the assistant was a mute.” Kershner: “The idea of Lando communicating with Lobot through his headset did not exist in the script… I added that on the set.” Lucas parenthetical on Kershner’s piecemeal masters vs. a single wide master so “the cast and crew sometimes wouldn’t see the whole thing played through in one piece.” Prowse, mid-chaos, offers Kershner *Fitness Is Fun* (Harrods signing Saturday). Bulloch’s Fett helmet steams up. Takes: “Three-seven-nine, take two”; print four and five. Hamill enjoyed watching Ford and Fisher “in that ‘Spencer Tracy–Katharine Hepburn way.’ ”

Ford after the fact (keep distinct from the Feb. 20 script and from the trailer improvisation): “The scene where I am about to go down the chute to be carbonized—well, the original line was, ‘I love you, too,’ but I felt that the other way was Han Solo’s way of saying, ‘It’s not over.’ … We never even shot ‘I love you, too.’ We just went ahead. It gave George pause. He had not written the scene with a laugh. But that laugh opens you up emotionally… there’ll never be a payoff for the scene without a laugh.” Fisher on dailies: her “I love you” body-miked at the right level, then Ford “a loud echo: ‘I KNOW!’ Well, the cast and crew laughed for about 15 minutes… it is something very sad.”

## CONTINUITY DIALOGUE (p. 188)

“After all of the many revisions and brainstorming sessions, the script supervisor’s final notes read as follows when Han is frozen in carbonite:”

INT. CLOUD CITY—CARBON-FREEZING CHAMBER

THREEPIO
If only you had attached my legs, I wouldn’t be in this ridiculous position. Now, remember, Chewbacca, you have a responsibility to me, so don’t do anything foolish.

HAN
(to Lando)
What’s going on … buddy?

LANDO
You’re being put into carbon freeze.

Boba Fett moves away from the group to Darth Vader.

BOBA FETT
What if he doesn’t survive? He’s worth a lot to me.

VADER
The Empire will compensate you if he dies. Put him in!

(Chewie howls and attacks; Threepio: “Oh, no! No, no, no! Stop!”; Han: “Stop, Chewie, stop! Stop!”; Threepio: “Yes, stop, please! I’m not ready to die.” “Vader has evidently nodded to the guards to let him go.”)

HAN
Hey, hey! Listen to me. Chewie! Chewie, this won’t help me. Hey! Save your strength. There’ll be another time. The Princess—you have to take care of her. You hear me? Huh?

Chewbacca wails a doleful farewell. Kiss.

LEIA
I love you!

HAN
I know.

Caption: Ford posed for the carbonite portrait, “later combined with a standin’s body.”

## IT’S A MAD, MAD, MAD STUDIO (pp. 189–190)

Nos. 77–80, June 22–27: carbon-freezing chamber continues; second unit, Hoth plain and *Falcon* cockpit. Monday, June 25, was to be the last of the original 76 days; production is 26 days over. Hamill son Nathan Elias born 1:50 AM Monday “after Hamill’s grandfather and his favorite author, Nathaniel Hawthorne” (*Buffalo Evening News*). Hamill tells Kurtz he is not coming in; later agrees to one bluescreen jump (walker foot crushing the snowspeeder). Champagne toast on set. He breaks/smashes the bone of his right thumb (later also called a sprain; Friday Dr. Collins says left thumb “unfit”—record the conflict, do not collapse). “We shut down the first unit for four days.” Hamill angry Kurtz didn’t use a double; Kurtz summons a stunt double and wigmaker; Hamill “ordered everyone from his dressing room.” Insurers Bayly, Martin, & Fay Ltd. notified. Stage 4 card games. Kershner uses the halt to prepare.

June 27, also a non-shooting day: “Laddie was leaving Fox.” Kurtz had known of Ladd–Dennis Stanfill conflicts, expected them after the first of the year; “Our biggest concern is how it affects the distribution of *Empire*.” Ladd (via Aubrey Solomon’s Fox history): he and partners Gareth Wigan and Jay Kanter leave after contract talks fall apart. Ladd: “The criticism over the deal with Lucasfilm is what ended my tenure at Fox, really… I stomped out of the boardroom and said, ‘I quit.’ ” Ganis: Fox took the deal because of *Star Wars* and because “how do you not take the deal?” Lucas: “It’s like having your closest ally suddenly change governments… we know that the studio people are not giant fans and that the board is not that happy with us.” After Ladd, Ashley Boone heads foreign and domestic distribution; Sandy Lieberson, production. End-of-span tally: “SETUPS: 922; SCS. COMP: 201/468; SCREEN TIME: 92M 38S/130M.”

## JUNE ILM: ON THE TAUN (pp. 191–193)

Mid-June: eight-foot Star Destroyer completed; Vader’s ship due July 16; three snowspeeders by month’s end; first walker due July 1. Decision not to build Cloud City as a miniature: “paintings, cutouts, and foreground 3-D pieces.” Oxberry arrived; toilets “across the street—install ASAP.” June 25: “Kerner Co. over budget. Department heads urged to stop spending and hiring”; Bloom wants an 8 AM start. Muren steps back to supervise; Ralston and Don Dow split Camera 1. Doug Beswick builds the tauntaun armature in aluminum. Walker-against-bluescreen test discarded (“the matte edge looked funny”). Jon Berg recalls Gallegly comparing Lucas to Disney, then, asked about the plan after *Empire*: “Throw us all back in the gutter where he found us.”
