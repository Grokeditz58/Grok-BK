# 171 — Chapter Two: The Gathering Force (December 1977 to July 1978)

Print pp. 30–63 (first EPUB anchors are `page29` then `page30`; print TOC opening is p. 30 — recorded, not collapsed). Rinzler ESB pp. 30–63. Staff/crew/cast same treatment as Lucas. Rinzler glosses flagged. The February 17, 1978, Brackett first draft and the April 1, 1978, Lucas second draft are kept strictly separate from each other, from the November 1977 treatment (Chapter One), and from later Kasdan drafts. The father-reveal first appears in Lucas’s *handwritten* second draft and is omitted from the typed copy sent to Alan Ladd Jr.

## Opening (pp. 30–31)

Charlie Weber “transitioned from consultant to president of Lucasfilm in late 1977.” Richard Tong: hired “because the licensing income was burgeoning.” Lucas: “Even though he was more in the stock market and real estate, he did know how to put things together.” Weber: “a tiny mom-and-pop company with huge potential resources.” Kenner deal, but holiday 1977 “fans had very little to buy”; “empty boxes that purchasers could redeem for four action figures the following year.” Company run from trailers “across the street from the Universal lot… for the first 18 months.” Budget “growing from $8 to $10 million.” Lucas: “I’m willing to take the risk, because I started with nothing. Five years ago I had nothing.” He uses *Star Wars* earnings as collateral, “keeping about $50,000 a year for living expenses.”

Points: Guinness “another quarter of a net point”; Hamill, Fisher, and Ford “a quarter of one net point” each after working “on salary for very, very little.” Reports: Guinness “around $2,880,000”; the three principals “about $320,000.” Joe Johnston “one-tenth of 1 percent (which resulted in $131,037 as of 1983).” Fisher: “It changed my name from Carrie ‘Debbie Reynolds’s daughter’ Fisher to Carrie *‘Star Wars’* Fisher.” Ford: “I worked for peanuts in the first one, but I knew it would be smart to be in that picture… I’m the kind of person who thinks he’s rich if he just got a check in the mail for $5,000, but my accountant doesn’t think of me as rich.” Hamill: “The percentage George gave us of *Star Wars* was probably 30 times our salary. He didn’t have to do it.” Lucas: the biggest change was after *American Graffiti*; in San Anselmo “it’s business as usual.” Hamill: “Maybe he has a whole room full of jeans and sweatshirts now.” Lucas bought “an old Ferrari” and did home improvements: “I’d been very poor up until four years ago, but I was very happy before.” End of 1977: about $127 million domestic rentals, “about $12 million for Lucas” after taxes. Kurtz attends the UK premiere December 26. Alan Arnold compares the London clamor to crowds awaiting Dickens’s *Pickwick Papers*.

## BLITZKRIEG IN THE SNOW (pp. 31–32)

December 8, 1977: Fox lawyers ask MCA to halt *Battlestar Galactica*, “referring specifically to work being done at the ‘ILM facility.’ MCA refused.” McQuarrie, December 7, begins “Metal (Vader’s) Castle.” “That was the first production painting I made… I put a couple of figures struggling along in the snow in the foreground, for scale, with the wind blowing.” Later caption: “Left over from earlier concepts of Vader’s home, the spires in the final painting are part of a hidden Rebel base, which was a converted ‘ice castle,’ according to the artist.” McQuarrie on pressure after the first film’s success: “I kind of froze up for a little while… I had to do away with all that business of, *It’s an important thing*.” December 9 meeting: Lucas “asking to see Vader’s castle in a lava environment.”

Tauntaun: McQuarrie started “with a dinosaur kind of look, but George wanted it to be a two-legged critter… I thought it was going to be used in the middle of the desert. As it turns out, it’s an animal that has to function in the snow! So I took back the beast and winterized it.” Johnston: “George actually told Ralph a few times to think of a big rat… Ralph’s sketch looked like a big rat with human legs!” Johnston rejects disguised military tanks; proposes a two-legged conveyance (drawing 0034); Lucas “wanted something larger.” Johnston finds a U.S. Steel brochure with Syd Mead’s “four-legged walking truck”: “That’s where the initial walker idea came from.” Also the General Electric Walking Truck by R. Mosher (1968). Lucas: “The walkers were inspired by *War of the Worlds* more than anything else… Where the Martians walked in machines like giant spiders. I was trying to come up with a way of making this battle different.” Snow-creature Johnston sketches stamped “Wonderful” by Lucas.

## THE *FALCON* AND THE EGG (pp. 32–34)

January 4, 1978: Lucasfilm buys Lankershim Boulevard property for $1,050,000 — “the Egg Company,” across from Universal. Early 1978 structure: Weber president; John Moohr VP finance (the runner-up for Weber’s job); Kazanjian VP of Medway (non–*Star Wars*); Kurtz VP of production on *Star Wars*. Black Falcon, “about 30 employees by the end of 1978.” Lucas: “The name Black Falcon was a take on the 1940s comic strip *Blackhawk*… *We’re the underground licensors*.” On quality vs. Fox: “Some junk jewelry manufacturers came by and said, ‘We’ll give you half a million dollars.’ And we said, ‘No, we don’t want that.’… I didn’t want to have *Star Wars* junk in every five-and-dime store in the country.”

## A SECOND COMING (pp. 34–36)

January 25, 1978: *The Hollywood Reporter* leaks the title, “but word did not spread.” Lucas on TV executives copying scripts. Guinness, who had said “Yes, absolutely” to a sequel: “I’ve developed wretched eye trouble. It’s threatening to blind my left eye. Specialists told me that under no circumstances must I go into bright light. So I’ve sort of withdrawn, feeling I had no option.” Lucas: “One of the challenges was I had to replace Obi-Wan Kenobi. I wanted something like Guinness; I wanted to transfer his performance into Yoda… I wanted someone very old and very unassuming.” February: Johnston’s first Yoda sketches, “from a kind of miniature Santa Claus to a green-skinned, large-eared, three-toed alien.” Johnston: “I remember George saying that Yoda was 400 years old.” Lucas: “The head is slightly too large for his body, which makes him look like an 800-year-old baby and, perhaps, more attractive.” Johnston: “we sort of combined a leprechaun and a troll and a gnome.”

## A KINDLY SORCERER (pp. 34–38)

February 17, 1978 memo from Lucas to Kurtz: “No percentage points in the picture can be allotted to anyone… without a written approval by myself”; stay in contact with Weber on budget; anything more than 10 percent over budget needs Lucas or Weber. Rinzler (flag): Lucas had been “disappointed with some developments that Kurtz had not helped resolve” on *Star Wars*; he had considered Kazanjian as executive producer and Watts as producer; “Kurtz had pleaded his case… Against his own misgivings, Lucas had acquiesced.”

Lucas: “I’ve retired from directing. If I directed *Empire*, then I’d have to direct the next one and the next for the rest of my life. I’ve never really liked directing. I became a director because I didn’t like directors telling me how to edit, and I became a writer because I had to write something in order to be able to direct something. So I did everything out of necessity, but what I really like is editing.” Candidates: Alan Parker (interested, wanted his own project next); John Badham. Kurtz: many directors “shy away… if a sequel works, the original director gets the credit; if it doesn’t work, you get the blame… The worst thing that could have happened would have been to engage a director who didn’t believe in the material.” EE audio (Rinzler ESB EE, Chapter Two): “Lucas on the evolution of his role from writer-director to writer-producer” (Arnold, 1979).

Irvin Kershner: USC connection; loved *THX 1138* and *American Graffiti*; Lucas cites *Raid on Entebbe* and friendship with Haskell Wexler; Kershner had judged the 1968 National Student Film Festival where *THX 1138.4EB* won. Kurtz had shot second unit on Kershner’s Public Health Service VD film. First approach at a bar (Kurtz, Perrier; Kershner “a stiff beer”), then Hamburger Hamlet on Sunset: “Would you be interested in doing the next *Star Wars?*” Kurtz: “Kersh isn’t cynical.” Lucas at Universal commissary: “it was the middle act of a trilogy… if this one worked, he could foresee a whole series that would go on for years.” Lucas, when Kershner asked why he didn’t direct it himself: “ ‘You’ll see …,’ I said. These films are infinitely harder than other kinds of movies. You end up feeling like a harassed corporate executive.” Lucas: “He said, ‘Boy, I couldn’t get up in the mornings after a while; six months of shooting is very, very hard.’ ” Kershner: “I told George that the only way I’d do the film is if I felt I could top the first one… He said, ‘It’s not a sequel. This is a continuing saga.’ ” Kershner: “I love the fact that it’s really for children, or as George says very aptly, ‘for the child in all of us adults.’ ”

McQuarrie “City in Clouds,” January 16–18, 1978; “Initially an Imperial prison in an early *Star Wars* script, the floating metropolis returned.” McQuarrie: “You may note it bears a bit of resemblance to the mothership in *Close Encounters*, which I also created.”

## THE NEGOTIATION (pp. 36–39)

Kershner is 54; music, painting under Hans Hoffman; B-24 bombers in England, WWII. Shared interests: Kurosawa, Renoir, philosophy and religion. Kershner on Christianity, Islam, Buddhism, and being a Jew “more… because other people consider me a Jew than myself.” Lucas: “Quite a number of churches have used *Star Wars* as a way of getting young people into the church… certain religious ideas… more general good and evil concepts.” Official offer February 13; “film by” credit disputed — Kershner wanted *An Irvin Kershner Film* at the front; Pollock: same opening credits as *Star Wars* (company logos only), desired wording in the end credits. Kershner: “George assured me that money was not a problem—within limits, of course. He further assured me it would be my film completely.” Deal memo February 15; “firm commitment.” Kershner saw *Star Wars* only twice (Academy screening with his 10-year-old son; once on a Movieola): “I didn’t want it to enclose me too much.” “a very satisfying thing about the film was knowing who was good and who was bad… But the film appeals to the subconscious in all of us… That’s a step toward assuming responsibility for oneself, which is what the heroes in *Star Wars* seem to be doing.”

## MISFIRE (p. 39)

Ned Brown delivers Brackett’s first draft to Andrew Rigrod on February 21, 1978 (script dated February 17). Rigrod suggests Bunny Alsup retype it; Brackett due $16,666 (after taxes $12,770). *Variety* February 24: Fox “definitely” getting the sequel, Kershner directing, principals returning, “it is not expected that the Alec Guinness character […] will reappear”; “the second of 12 films”; worldwide grosses $208,934,715 as of February 22. February 25, 3 PM, Beverly Hills Hotel: Lucas, Kershner, McQuarrie; “Both of them were concerned. Brackett’s draft did not have the right feeling for a *Star Wars* movie.” That month Lucas signs Lawrence Kasdan for *Raiders*. Kasdan (later): “Leigh had written something that was of a different era… she hadn’t quite gotten what it is about George… I think she just was in an entirely different mode.” Kershner: “The ending didn’t work. There were sequences in the snow that really didn’t work.” Lucas: “My thoughts during the story conference weren’t fully formed, and I felt her script went off in a completely different direction.” Rinzler (flag): “Although Brackett’s script was faithful to Lucas’s treatment in almost all superficial respects, the dialogue and actions of the personnel seemed to belong in another movie, particularly those of Darth Vader.”

Lucas’s handwritten edits on the first 40 pages: Solo, “There isn’t enough life on this ice cube to fill a small space cruiser”; “ice castle” becomes “ice caves”; names “Baron Rabatt”; new Solo/Leia exchange: “I wish you’d never found out about him.” “He’s your father,” Leia replies. “Not really … he never adopted me.” (This is the *stepfather* plot, not Vader.) Luke “hanging upside down” in the “snowman cave”; “force field protects base from above attack”; Minch intro “more Buddhalike. ‘So you’ve come at last.’ ” Halfway in, comments become a scrawled “No” beside whole scenes. “George called up to talk to her about it, but she was in the hospital” (Kasdan).

## First-draft summary — Brackett, February 17, 1978 (pp. 40–42)

Rinzler ESB pp. 40–42. *Star Wars Sequel*, screenplay by Leigh Brackett, from the Adventures of Luke Skywalker by George Lucas. 128 pages. Keep this draft’s identities uncollapsed.

Ice Planet opening; Solo: “I doubt if even God remembers where he hung this star.” Willard: “Since we destroyed the Empire’s Death Star, many more systems have found courage to join the rebellion. At the last count, one thousand and twenty-six.” Leia gives Han a mission: “We’ve been in touch with your stepfather … Ovan Marekal is the most powerful man in the galaxy next to the Emperor … through his Transport Guild.” Ben’s voice to Luke in the ice cave: “Remember the Force, boy. Open your mind to it, open your heart.” Luke slashes the snow monster and “the creature melts back into the ice formations.” C-3PO finds a crystal in the lightsaber hilt.

On “the city-planet of Ton Muund, Darth Vader searches for the Rebels.” After a lead in the Granida Cluster: “The coordinates, man! The coordinates!” Vader on Luke, typography locked:

“[Luke Skywalker] disabled my fighter and sent me spinning off into space with all systems dead, even the radio … but I knew. I knew when he destroyed the Death Star, using the Force to find the target. I had much time to consider Master Skywalker while I was waiting to be rescued. He’s too much like his father …”

Han to Luke after the Force fails against the snow monsters: “You’re not a Jedi Knight, and you never will be.” Snow monsters force evacuation; Imperial “tank-type crawlers”; C-3PO “encased in ice (he later thaws out).” Vader: “He lives. Leave me, you incompetent idiot.” Vader attacks Luke telepathically in his “spacer”: “What’s the matter? I can’t breathe …”; R2 jumps to hyperspace. “Goodbye, Luke Skywalker,” Vader says, “believing he has killed Luke.”

Bog planet: Minch — “Skywalker. Skywalker. And why do you come to walk my sky, with the sword of a Jedi Knight? … I remember another Skywalker.” Minch summons Ben: “By the Force, I call you!” They fence; “Minch is clearly the superior.” Vader’s castle: “black iron that squats on a rock in the midst of a crimson sea.” Minch: “The dark side of the Force! Luke, you’re in greater danger than I realized. Even untrained, you’re far more powerful than I.” Emperor on a screen, “draped and hooded in cloth-of-gold”: “Skywalker is more dangerous than even I had realized. Remove him this time or I shall remove you.”

Han, Leia seek Lando Kadar “on a world called Hoth—‘I think the name means “cloud,” ’ Han remarks.” (In this draft Hoth is the cloud world, not the ice planet.) Leia disguised as “Ethania Eredith.” “Bahiri, chief of the White Bird clan of the Cloud People.”

**Luke’s father appears as a ghost, distinct from Vader.** “Luke is now able to summon Ben, and the latter appears accompanied by Luke’s father, who reveals to Luke that he has a sister.”

SKYWALKER: “I sent you both away for your own safety, far apart from each other.”
LUKE: “Where is she? What’s her name?”
SKYWALKER: “If I were to tell you, Darth Vader could get that information from your mind and use her as a hostage … Luke, will you take from me the oath of a Jedi Knight?”

Ben, Minch, Luke, and his father cross sabers; Luke swears to “dedicate my life to the cause of freedom and justice” (Lucas wrote “No” next to this passage). Vader’s temptation (Lucas also wrote “No”): “You’re in love with Leia. You don’t want to lose her to Han Solo … but you will, if you lack the courage to use the strength that’s in you… If you join with me, nothing can stand against us … The Empire is a passing phase. We would rule instead. You and I. The Emperor is a harsh master. You would not be.”

Lando: “I’m a clone. Of the Ashardi family. My greatgrandfather wanted many sons and he produced them from the cells of his own body… But since the wars, there are not many of us left and we try not to attract attention.” Vader “doesn’t imprison Han, Leia, and the others; he simply lets them go about their business, though they’re not allowed to leave the orbital city.” Stormtroopers kill Bahiri; Lando changes sides. Duel: Luke uses the dark side “to get the upper hand, though Vader is more powerful in the end.”

VADER: “But you’ve had no training in the dark side. You simply felt more power in it, and you used it for revenge, for hate, for the sake of being able to say that you, Luke Skywalker, had destroyed the great Darth Vader … So, once more, little Jedi. Will you join me?”
LUKE: “I’ve wasted and thrown away all the careful teaching I was given. I betrayed my trust. I broke my oath. I would prefer to die.”
VADER: “You’re a fool, Luke. Nevertheless, it can be arranged.”

Luke jumps down a vent shaft and onto the *Falcon*. Refuge on “a jewel-like planet, Besspin Kaalida.” Han leaves with Chewbacca “to persuade his stepfather to join the Alliance”; Leia “is now in love with Han.” Luke salutes the departing *Falcon* with his lightsaber. **No “I am your father.” Luke’s father and Vader are two people in this draft. No carbon-freeze of Han. No Jabba in this draft (Lucas later notes the debt was left out here).**

## DRAFTED (pp. 43–45)

Brackett hospitalized; Lucas writes the second draft himself. “I had no script and I didn’t have anyone to write the damn thing.” Johnston storyboarding a snow battle with no script; Lucas writes shots in. Brian Johnson arrives at ILM March 13, then is loaned to *Alien* while *Empire* is being revised. Yoda realization: *Darby O’Gill* forced-perspective rejected as “too restrictive.”

Leigh Brackett dies March 18, 1978, at Lancaster Community Hospital, age 60; press release March 22, “a long bout with cancer”; ashes to Kinsman, Ohio, beside Edmond Hamilton. Estate contract August 25, 1978.

Lucas finishes the second draft April 1, 1978 — “incredibly fast when that six weeks is compared with the three years he’d spent writing his first *Star Wars* scripts.” Lucas: “I found it much easier than I’d expected, almost enjoyable… The risk I took was putting the action and adventure up front and then switching to a more personal film where people were going to be philosophical and worrying about emotional issues.” “I wanted to do something that was a little bit more grown-up in terms of the entertainment value, which meant more realistic and more of a fear factor… I was very adamant that it be one movie, that both films have the same sensibility.” On structure: in the treatment Ben told Luke to leave Yoda; “now Luke must choose between an instinctive emotional attachment and the completion of his training.” Lucas: “Luke’s decision to rescue his friends is the right one, but his methodology is wrong.” The stormtrooper massacre is cut: “I decided to move him closer to the dark side in the next film, not in this one.” Lando “selling out his friends to save his city” is “a little like Han’s development in *Star Wars* but also like Luke’s, where fate steps in.”

Han’s debt to Jabba the Hutt, “which had figured at the end of the treatment but was left out of the first draft, was reinstated”; bounty hunters added. Lucas: “There were quite a few films made about bounty hunters in the Old West. That’s where that came from.” Boba Fett: “When I was writing the early scripts for *Star Wars*, I wanted to develop an essentially evil character that was frightening. Darth Vader started as a kind of intergalactic bounty hunter in a space suit and evolved into a more grotesque knight… The Boba Fett character is really an early version of Darth Vader. He is also very much like the man-with-no-name from the Sergio Leone Westerns.” Rinzler (flag, but historically tying drafts): “The earliest version of the mercenary was Prince Valorum, a Black Knight of the Sith, who had appeared in Lucas’s rough draft for *Star Wars*, where he was employed by Vader to track down the heroes.” Fett also inherits the super-stormtrooper (McQuarrie as early as October 1977); by March 1978 “a more or less completely designed galactic mercenary,” prioritized for the holiday television special and for Kenner. Lucas on naming: lists in “a couple of little books.” Yoda: not from the list; “kind of like a little Dalai Lama”; backward syntax because “an accent isn’t going to work here.”

Handwritten title page: *Empire* is “Episode II”; typed draft: *Star Wars:* Episode V *The Empire Strikes Back* by Leigh Brackett, from *The Adventures of Luke Skywalker* by George Lucas. Registered with the MPAA in March as “Episode II (Two): The Empire Strikes Back.” Kurtz: they had “got cold feet at the last minute and took that out” of the first film: “Fox was worried, and, to be perfectly honest, we were worried, too. People wouldn’t have understood… *What happened to the first three?*”

**Father-reveal secrecy, typography locked:** “Another difference between the handwritten script and the typed version was an omission in the latter of the second draft’s major revelation: that Darth Vader was the father of Luke Skywalker.” Lucas to Ladd, on the title page of the typed copy (with Johnston and McQuarrie art, revelation left out): “For Laddie, Here’s a rough idea of the film; May the Force be with us! George—P.S. Best read listening to the *Star Wars* album.” Lucas: “The issue of Luke’s father I kept pretty quiet for a long, long time. I didn’t tell anyone, not even Kersh. I just couldn’t risk it getting out.”

## Second-draft summary — Lucas, April 1, 1978 (pp. 46–49)

Rinzler ESB pp. 46–49. 121 pages. “the typed version, which has been mistakenly referred to as a third draft.” First roll-up in the process, typography locked:

“After the destruction of its most feared battle station, the Empire has declared martial law throughout the galaxy. A million worlds have felt the oppressive hand of the Emperor’s agents in their attempt to crush the growing Rebellion. As the Imperial grip of tyranny tightens, Princess Leia and the small band of freedom fighters search for a more secure base of operations …”

“The ice planet is now called Hoth.” (Name moved off the cloud world of the Brackett draft.) Han must leave: “I’ve got to pay off Jabba the Hutt or I’m a walking dead man… there will be just too many [bounty hunters] to stop … Remotes, Gank killers, even Bell Cambos.” To Leia: “They say I kiss very well. But don’t worry, I’m not going to kiss you here—you see, I’m quite selfish about my pleasures and it wouldn’t be much fun for me now. I’m going to wait for you to grow up a little more. I’m sure we’ll meet again.” Probe robot; Luke hung by ankles; Ben: “Think the saber in your hand,” then “Go to the seventh moon in the Dagobah System. You will learn from the one who taught me: Yoda.” Han shoves Luke into a dead tauntaun (“an old Native American trick, according to Kershner”). Wampa not yet named in the summary’s early cave beats (Rinzler earlier said the second draft names the snow monster a wampa); medical robot Too-Onebee; humiliation scene cut. Vader “first appears on a Star Destroyer (he’s no longer seen in his home).” Walker approach: “thump-thump, thump-thump.” Luke is Rogue Leader; Dack killed; tow cables; Luke rappels up and grenades a walker. General Rieekan “mortally wounded.” Stormtroopers open the wrong door onto snow monsters. *Falcon* hides in an asteroid crater.

Yoda: “a blue creature … dressed in rags… The creature also speaks in a backward syntax: ‘Harm I mean you not.’ ” “On and off, all your life guess I.” Vader talks via hologram with “Sate Pestage, ‘Grand Visor to His Eminence, The Emperor.’ ” The Emperor says “the ‘son of Skywalker’ could be turned to the dark side.” Han/Leia kiss; mynock; “I am not a committee!”; space slug. Bounty hunters: “Bossk, Tuckuss, Dengar, and Boba Fett.” Yoda in backpack: “Once you’ve embarked on the dark path, it will dominate your destiny …” Cave tree. Lando Calrissian, Bespin, Cloud City “art deco” and “half of the troops, as well as citizens of the city, are black, including Lando.” Alien subplot cut. Yoda and Ben warn Luke not to go:

BEN: “He is our only hope.”
YODA: “No, we must find another.”

Han tortured; Fett worries Solo may be “too damaged for the reward”; Leia “tortured off screen.” Carbon Freezing Chamber tested on Solo: “I’ll be back.” Luke: “I know you’re with me, Lord Vader, I can feel your presence. Show yourself, or are you afraid?” Vader: “The Force is with you, Skywalker, but you are not the master yet.” “I feel your anger, good, let yourself hate me.” “I destroyed your family. I destroyed Kenobi.” Then, typography locked from the handwritten draft (caption: “Lucas’s handwritten second draft pages reveal for the first time Darth Vader’s avowal to his son”):

VADER: “The Force runs strong in the Skywalker line, you must use the dark side. Together we would be the most powerful. Stronger with the Force than even the Emperor.”
LUKE: “Never will I join with you.”
VADER: “We will rule the galaxy as father and son.”
LUKE: “What?”
VADER: “Old Kenobi never told you what happened to your father did he?”
LUKE: “Enough! He said you killed him.”
VADER: “I am your father.”
LUKE: “That’s impossible. It’s not true.”
VADER: “Search your feelings; you already know it to be true. Join me.”

Luke jumps; Vader “slumps in disbelief.” Weather vane; Ben’s voice to Leia: “Luke, help Luke.” Rendezvous with the Rebel fleet. Handwritten ending (cut in the typed version): Lando will “return with Han or die”; Leia: “You know it’s Han I love don’t you?” Luke: “Yes … But I have been swept into another sphere. Han is better for you …” Kiss on the forehead; *Falcon* into “the sea of stars.” McQuarrie painting of “Fett (in white armor) and Vader taking the heroes prisoner.”

## FORCE THREE, SHADOW FOUR (pp. 50–51)

Early March reports of script conferences with Hamill, Ford, Fisher. Army Archerd quotes Ford: “The sequel to *Star Wars* will be a completely different film… It has a stronger plot—and some exotic locations.” Ford had “verbally signed on”; he had not optioned a second film initially: “I had no difficulty deciding I would do part two… I also felt I had a moral obligation.” Hamill and Fisher already optioned for second and third; Ford “uncommitted to a third.” Ford on *Force 10 from Navarone* (“a piece of total dogshit”) and a cameo in *Apocalypse Now* as Colonel “Lucas.” Daniels on being written out of publicity: “See-Threepio is entirely mechanical… I have to be honest and say it hurt.” Oscars April 3, 1978: six Oscars listed. Vader appearance without Prowse (Kermit Eller in the suit). Prowse: “I object to not being asked to do the more important appearances… I really felt that when *Star Wars* came out, I had the raw end of the deal… Twentieth Century–Fox was categorically refusing to issue any pictures of me out of the mask.” He learned of Jones’s voice from Russ Meyer clippings. Prowse, typography locked, on fan mail: “The eroticism lies in the fact that he’s big and black… Darth Vader is my body and movements, and he’s James Earl Jones’s voice. The women send photographs, mind you, and it gets to be quite pornographic.” Guinness on “dotty” mail wanting a guru. Prowse hires press agents, “ran afoul of the studio’s public relations department”; “As I turned to evil, she [his wife] turned to God.” Mayhew, Baker, Daniels (“*impersonal* appearances”) between films.

## ESCAPE FROM LA (pp. 51–53)

April in California: John Barry opts to direct *Saturn 3*; Norman Reynolds becomes production designer, Barry consultant. Location scouts: Central Africa for the bog planet; Finland, Sweden, Lapland, Norway, Canada, Alaska for ice; Finse recommended, confirmed mid-May (Kershner had not yet seen it). *Time* article: Lucas helping cut *Apocalypse Now*; Lucas quoted: “I’ve always thought that sooner or later, somewhere down the road, I will go back and [direct] another [*Star Wars* film]… about 20 years from now.” ILM moves north as “The Kerner Company,” 3210 Kerner Boulevard, San Rafael; legal paperwork March 14, 1978. Jim Bloom hired as assistant producer to move ILM. Johnston comes immediately; “Dykstra convinced the rest… ‘Stay with me and we’re gonna make our own effects facility and we can compete with ILM.’” Muren first contact April 1978; Edlund and Peterson recruited later. McQuarrie reads the second draft April 4. Kershner signs April 26; DGA waiver sought for end-credit / “film by” in paid ads. Kershner: “We negotiated for three months, became mortal enemies, finished the negotiations, and became friends again.”

## XANADU IN THE SKY (pp. 53–54)

Internal budget April 27, 1978: $15,494,475, “up from $10 million.” Lucas turns 34 on May 14. Duwayne Dunham, Steve Starkey hired. Lucas purchases “1,700-acre Bulltail Ranch from the Soares family on Lucas Valley Road” (sometimes reported as 1,882 acres; road named for John Lucas, 1882). Plans for Skywalker Ranch: vineyards, Victorian house, editing studios, sound, libraries, recreation. Tong buys it in his name “because George wanted to enjoy anonymity.” Lucas: “I’m very overextended right now.” June 1978 picnic/tour; Bloom: “this *Citizen Kane* kind of wonder in his eyes.” Lucy Wilson compares it to Manderley in *Rebecca*.

## SUPERTROOPER (pp. 54–55)

June 8: deal memos for Ford, Fisher, Hamill; Ford for *Empire* only; Hamill and Fisher also for a third “and also for *Star Wars IV*—an additional film beyond the first trilogy.” All three sign June 15. That day Kasdan, in San Anselmo with his *Raiders* first draft, is asked to write *Empire*: “Would you like to write the movie?” “How do you know you want me?” “Well, I just get a feeling about people… If I hate *Raiders*, I’ll call the whole thing off.” Kasdan: “I’m really strong in people… we wanted *Empire* to have deeper characterization, more complex psychology.” Burtt June 27 memo; Sprocket Systems. June 28: 20-minute all-white Boba Fett screen test, costume worn by Duwayne Dunham; wrist flamethrower “his arm caught on fire because of a propane leak and that idea was scrapped.” Johnston: “Originally, Boba Fett was part of a force we called supertroopers… That eventually evolved into a single bounty hunter.” Leone’s *A Fistful of Dollars* inspires Fett’s “pancho” (Johnston no. 300, June 1978). Chapter II production meetings June 28–30.

## VALLEY OF THE WALKERS (pp. 55–56)

Muren: walkers “began as an analogy to the kind of unwieldy armament and tactics the US military forces had used in the jungles of Vietnam.” Lucas and Kurtz meet Ray Harryhausen early 1978; Muren: “I don’t think they ever expected Ray to actually take the job.” Stop-motion chosen; Tippett on Muren and Johnston pushing a “quasi-animalistic” look so strobing “would still be okay.” Berg engineers the armature (“a minor miracle of engineering”); $500 check July 22, 1978, for the prototype. English unit considers a man-in-a-suit tauntaun; “Phil and I failed at talking them out of it” (Muren).

## SIZZLING AIR (pp. 56–57)

June: Fox civil action against MCA, Universal, and ABC over *Battlestar Galactica*. Kazanjian: “We immediately sued… they were using ILM’s equipment.” Lucas: “if I wanted to do a TV series of *Star Wars*, I couldn’t. They’ve already spoiled the television market.” Glen Larson: “There’s no question it’s a very big gamble… and we are very vulnerable.” John Knoll, 15, spends a day at Van Nuys (snow planet for *Battlestar*; Muren testing *1941*): “If they could do it, I could do it.” First July 4 picnic at the ranch; gate combination *1138*; “maybe 25 people.” (Dunham’s hot-air-balloon crash is attributed here to “the second picnic.”)

## PREDICTIONS OF FANTASY (pp. 57–59)

*Star Wars* re-released July 1978, 1,700 then 2,000 theaters. July 1 loan-out contract, Lucas to Chapter II Company: $10,000 plus five gross points as executive producer; $10,000 for the story treatment; “nothing for his script.” Decision to build a huge new stage at Elstree rather than go to Africa or reuse the condemned Shepperton stage; construction starts early August; “somewhat complicated deal with EMI.”

Kasdan, late June or early July, multi-day conference; “Kasdan was given only Lucas’s draft from which to work.” Kasdan: “We discarded Leigh’s script, which we never treated as the basis for anything.” Lucas: page-by-page with Kersh; “I’m the encyclopedia… I knew what the third movie was going to be.” Kasdan: “There are some things you just don’t talk about: certain units of measurement and any and all references to the planet Earth… Kershner and I, neither of us knew” the larger saga. Kershner on moving past naturalism; quotes a 1926 Huxley interview on film making “the most unbelievable thing possible” believable. McQuarrie’s paintings “stoked us all.” Cloud City: “round buildings because, if they were square, they’d wind up looking just like the Black Tower at Universal Studios”; Autry’s *Radio Ranch* and the Emerald City as antecedents. Dagobah: “The trees were like the ones in the swamps of Louisiana.”

Lucas, typography locked, on the second-act problem: “*Empire* is a rather sad story. It’s the second act and has all the problems of a second act… I was struggling and struggling and struggling to get to a climax that would feel satisfactory, but whose resolution is left hanging in the air. I had blown up the Death Star in the first one, but I’d stolen it from the third and put it in the first to give me an ending… But with the second one, I had no real climax other than the fact that Vader was his father and that it was more of a psychological movie.” Kasdan to turn out about a fifth of the script every two weeks.

## MCQUARRIE’S METHOD (pp. 60–63)

McQuarrie on his role: “I was hired to give George Lucas a chance to get as close as he could to the ideal look for things he had been dreaming about getting into his films… he draws quite well. But George concentrates more on the ideas and lets me get them into shape.” Materials, color, and time (cel vinyl, designer’s colors, airbrush; “I will never do a painting at high noon”; *Empire* paintings from one to seven days, “average is two or three”). “I’ve done a lot of dreaming in my life—and now I’m getting a chance to put it all to work.” Section includes a “Drawing of Darth Vader” and later paintings of Vader’s bridge / Star Destroyer bridge (July 18–20, 1978).
