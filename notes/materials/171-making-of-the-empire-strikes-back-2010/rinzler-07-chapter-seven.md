## RUNNING ON FUMES (pp. 194–197)

Reports 81–82, Thursday–Friday, June 28–29: main unit no shooting; second unit, *Falcon* cockpit. Friday, June 29: “Ford wrapped his role as Han Solo, after shooting ever more *Falcon* cockpit shots against bluescreen.” Hamill still injured; production “30 days behind schedule”; “morale hit its lowest point.”

Ford: “I’m terribly tired and that’s about all I have the energy to feel at the moment. Probably later on I’ll have more complex feelings about it, but right now I’m just relieved that it’s over.” Hamill: “Harrison was waiting by his telephone for Gary to see the rushes and make sure there were no hairs in the gate so he could get out.” Arnold to Hamill: “Harrison hardly said goodbye to you… he couldn’t get away quick enough.” Kershner: “He just couldn’t wait to leave, even though he loved doing the picture… The actors were at the point of collapse and exhaustion by that time.”

Kurtz: “one week when I was sort of assaulted by everybody at the same time. Harrison, Carrie, and Mark for various reasons.” Fisher: nightmares of “half robots, half people… going to lunch with midgets and giants every day.” Hamill: “I don’t care; I hate this movie and everyone in it; I just want to go home.” Kurtz on Carbon Freezing Chamber: “serious problems with both Mark and Carrie with Kersh in terms of communication. They were talking to me in the middle of the night.” Fisher: “Gary, are you producing now? Is this producing?” Ford: “I’m totally out of my mind, but I am trained to be reserved… I try to lock myself in with good things to do.” Actors fear “the acting isn’t as important as the technical parts.” Kershner: “Harrison works very, very hard when he’s acting. But when he’s finished, it doesn’t really affect or change his life… he’s not ambitious in a way that some actors are who will carry their work home with them.”

Hamill: “I’m working with Darth Vader, whose voice will be done by another actor… It’s like the ultimate test as far as using your imagination.” Daniels on R2: “He has all the charm of *Star Trek: The Ashtray*.” Prowse: “It’s no trouble to offer a menacing presence when you’re told to hurry to the set, fully dressed, only to stand around for two hours… I get madder and madder! … The costume is quilted leather, weighs 48 pounds. The mask fogs and I sweat unbelievably.”

Lucas on not directing: “It’s been a unique experience to write down something the way I thought it should be, explain to the director how it was supposed to be done, describe the ambience, and then have it come out completely different from the way I’d thought of it. It’s an interesting experience and I can now see why screenwriters go crazy.” Kurtz: Kershner’s style “is entirely different from George’s and he has a harder time adjusting to problems.” Watts: “Gary didn’t form a relationship with Kershner… he is, I suppose *introverted* is the right word… Gary does not have the common touch.” Kershner: “I didn’t really work with Gary that much… a very friendly relationship with him and that’s about it.” Kurtz: the relationship “did fray a bit”; his argument with Lucas was that overages were justified because “it has to be better or it’s not going to work.” Lucas: “I think Kershner is feeling similar to the way I felt on the first film… compromising day by day and dying a thousand deaths.”

Oz drops Yoda’s head and cracks it; Freeborn, “I think I need a drink.” McQuarrie: Ivor Beddoes “had to go back into the hospital… He had cancer.” (Rinzler: “Beddoes would pass away in 1981.”) Construction short of plasterers and carpenters; *Flash Gordon* starting. Watts: first film “had no aura… This time around, everybody was conscious of what they were living up to.” EE video: Freeborn on Yoda, early July 1979, “even speaking as the Yoda puppet.” EE audio: Diamond on Bob Anderson (Arnold, August 20, 1979).

## DUELING (pp. 197–199)

Reports 83–84, July 2–3: Stage 4, carbon-freezing chamber, Scs. 384 & S391 (Luke battles Vader). Hamill pronounced fit; duel resumes. Freeborn gets toluene in his eye, hospital; “Harrison Ford boarded a plane for LA.” Facing Hamill is Bob Anderson as Vader: Britain’s senior national fencing coach, 30 years; *Star Wars*; Errol Flynn and Douglas Fairbanks. Anderson: “That uniform was terrible. I’m six feet one, and I had three extra inches on my helmet and two inches on my boots… Quite often, I could see only Mark’s feet, so I was doing it virtually blindfolded.” Diamond: Anderson “trains the British Olympic team” and appeared in 1948 and ’52 Olympics; “the best man in the country… for this particular swordfight.”

Lightsaber blades: front-projection material, glow widened in animation. Kershner: “a man whose face you can’t see and who’s twice as big as his adversary… a swordfight, which is sort of an anachronistic idea… except it’s a laser sword, which, if it touches something, immediately atomizes it.” Hamill nearly faints from hyperventilation plus steam. Tempers: Hamill vs. Kershner over the director acting out faces; “I don’t even want to see this movie!”; Kershner, “Why even shoot it?” Kurtz now has them talk at the start of each day. EE dailies, July 20, 1979: Luke duels Anderson on the carbon-freeze set.

## YANKEES, GO HOME (pp. 200–201)

Reports 85–92, July 4–13: East Landing Platform corridors (Leia and Chewie in laserfire; Lando orders evacuation; run up *Falcon* ramp; R2 fog); medical centre, star cruiser (droid works on Luke’s hand; Luke says good-bye to Lando); *Falcon* cockpit, S431 (Luke thinks about Vader), U430 (Luke rebukes Ben). Pound’s rise vs. dollar “threatened to tack on $800,000 or more.” Bog planet being built in seven weeks instead of fourteen. Welch: “112 tons of plaster… 23 or 24 trees, at four or five tons apiece… 48,000 meters” of scrim; water area “65 feet by approximately 45 feet with 3-foot depth.” Water-tank lid blows through the tarpaulin.

July 4 crew screening of the Show Reel plus 90-second teaser of McQuarrie paintings (cost $12,061.45; “did not contain the potentially confusing ‘Episode V’ as part of the film’s title”). Hamill arrives late; crew, after drinks: “We don’t call it Independence Day, we call it Good Riddance Day”; “Go home, Yank.” Mayhew sent home: 90-degree heat, 15-pound yak wool and mohair, plus “35 pounds of metal on your back.” Kershner: “Poor Chewbacca almost broke down… I extended the range of Chewbacca… I made him more vulnerable; we show him frustrated, exasperated, angry. These things make him *human*.” Mayhew had studied “a monkey family.”

Day 90, July 11, 33 days over: last shot, medical center, bluescreen. Kershner had written Lucas earlier: “Whenever I see an amputee… they wonder how it is not to have any feeling, any sensitivity in the fingertips.” Rewrite: medical droid pokes Luke’s prosthetic fingertips, which react. Kershner: “The audience had to know that Luke has feeling in his hand. That way, even though he has a mechanical hand, it isn’t creepy when he puts his arm around Leia at the very end of the film.”

## JULY ILM: ANIMATICS (pp. 202–205)

Lucas has animatics made so he can cut movement, “very Sergei Eisenstein… movement across the screen.” “Leica reel… real cheap stick animation. On the first film, I couldn’t even afford to do that.” Hirsch/Kershner first cut of snow-battle dailies; Lucas rough-cuts with animatics; new boards with Johnston on the KEM. Mid-July: model shop finishes “Vader’s Star Destroyer”; “new tauntaun legs turned out great.” Caption: Paul Huston on the *Executor* model, “Darth Vader’s flagship.” July 4 company picnic: Dunham arrives by balloon “and crashed, nearly setting the forest on fire.”

## THE GODFATHER (pp. 206–207)

Sunday, July 15: Lucas returns to London. “Suddenly, showing up every few weeks or so didn’t work anymore. I had to be there every day and I had to be helping Kershner.” Financing is collapsing. Kazanjian blames Weber, Moohr, and “a lot of it was Gary Kurtz’s.” Weber: three Bank of America entertainment loan officers “almost shell-shocked”: “We have to pull your loan on Friday; we’re at a million-dollar payroll.” Weber: “You have $50 million [sic] in advances in the Fox coffers.” New credit manager: “your budget’s doubled, so it’s an automatic.” (Rinzler’s “[sic]” — do not collapse with later Boston figures.)

Lucas: “It wasn’t going to be $25 million, it was going to be closer to $30 million… the bank said, ‘No, we’re not giving you another dime.’ So I couldn’t finish the movie… I was afraid I was going to have to go back to Fox and beg forgiveness. I would have to give them the movie, and then I wouldn’t have my freedom.” Delaying tactic: pay everybody every other week. “we couldn’t let the cast and crew realize that we were in financial trouble.” Kershner: “I had no awareness of what was going on behind the scenes financially.” Black Falcon loans another $525,000. “According to one source, Twentieth Century–Fox, aware of the situation, was threatening to call in the completion bond and take over the film.” — flag as unsourced.

Lucas: “All the money I had made from *Star Wars* was committed to this film, plus more, but I didn’t want to go to Fox and give them the movie because I’d have to give all the rights back… It was my learning that when you play with the big boys, it’s no holds barred.” Kurtz: two years of inflation; “For the cast, it was like a 500 percent inflation rate.” Overages: “8 or 10 sets” added; about seven or eight weeks over, “half… technical problems… the other four weeks… the way Kersh worked.” Kershner: “Lucas had financed this picture himself and there I was shooting a guessing game with millions and millions of his money at stake.” Lucas: “Kersh is the one in the driver’s seat, and he just isn’t of the same school as I am… a very loud ticking clock.”

## HUMAN MISSILE (pp. 207–211)

Reports 93–98, July 16–23: Stage 1, reactor control room, Luke knocked through the window. Hamill duels Anderson; Lucas checks in: “Hiya, kid… Congratulations.” “he was very interested in making sure that when we lock blades with the lightsabers, we had a proper amount of tension.” “He only tells you when you do things wrong.” Air-cannon missile of metal and wood “quite heavy”; Diamond: “if it had hit anyone at all, they stood a chance of being killed.” Hamill “literally shaking with fear.” Kurtz: Mark was “fully 15 feet away from the closest piece of glass.” Colin Skeaping’s backward somersault through the window, no wires.

Lucas and Kubrick watch *Eraserhead* (1977) at the studio. Lucas takes first three or four reels for a second cut while Hirsch continues first assembly; “our greatest difficulties” in the opening exposition. Lucas: “Always in the beginning of the movie you have to explain what’s going on… It’s counter to being entertaining.” Hamill to Arnold: “George is not real generous with praise”; after seeing swordfight dailies, Lucas “said that it’s terrific. He said it’s going to be the ‘Battle of the Century.’ ” Captions: Hamill also duels with Prowse in the reactor room; Vader holds on to avoid being sucked out.

## ELEVENTH HOUR (pp. 211–213)

July 13–20 wraps: Billy Dee Williams (39 days), Carrie Fisher (68 days), Anthony Daniels (50 days). Fisher’s mother phones that “somebody was out to kidnap Fisher”; bodyguards for her last days. “Then Carrie and Harrison were finished and went home, and it was just me left,” Hamill.

July 23: “Luke’s training montage on the bog planet was shortened, with nearly all of the before-and-after lessons cut out, including the sequence in which he first fails and then succeeds in cutting a metal bar into several pieces with his lightsaber. Nearly all of the Yoda–Luke scene had also been reworked by this date and new pages inserted into the shooting script.” Record as July 1979 shooting-script inserts, not a new numbered draft. Lucas: “because the bog planet is going to be so difficult, I’ve rewritten it… getting it down to a reasonable number of shots that we can actually accomplish in two and a half weeks.” Kershner: need “compression on the bog planet… naturalistic time as opposed to montage time.” Lucas pushes Kershner to shoot an “overall master.”

Obi-Wan ghost still unresolved: “It’s up in the air at this particular point in time,” Lucas says. “We’re not quite sure what Alec’s situation is in terms of his health, but we’re hoping that he’ll do the picture. We could find somebody who could do his voice with makeup. Ben Kenobi can still be there without Alec Guinness, it’s just that we prefer to have the real thing.” Beddoes boards of cut tests (bar, bog jump, seekers) “were cut from the film in July 1979.”

## THE GODFATHER II (p. 214)

Report 99, Tuesday, July 24: Stage 9, tree cave, Sc. 341 (the test). Weber calls Bill Thompson at the Bank of Boston; “we literally did the deal on the back of a napkin and he funded the payroll.” Lucas: “switch banks in a period of something like 10 days, but they were willing to go the extra $5 million.” “Revolving credit” with First National Bank of Boston, signed and dated July 24, “refinancing the original loan to the tune of about $31 million, with the bank loan at $27,670,000—and with Fox guaranteeing the $3 million above the $28 million” in return for “principal terms of a distribution agreement… regarding the distribution rights with respect to the second theatrical motion picture sequel to *Star Wars*.” Lucas: “I paid Fox just a little bit more money. But they didn’t get any of the licensing and they didn’t get any of the sequels.” Second-sequel distributor split “more favorable to” Fox than *Empire*; Fox retains “10 percent of merchandising ‘participation.’ ” Because Lucasfilm itself guaranteed the $27 million, “if *Empire* didn’t at least make its money back, Lucasfilm was now on the chopping block.” Caption: Beddoes, February 1979, “There has been three or four conceptions of the tree of evil.”

## VISCIOUS VADER (pp. 214–216)

Reports 100–102, July 25–27: tree cave; snow walker (Luke throws bomb inside); Stage 1 gantry platform and pinnacle, Scs. 398, 400 (Vader cuts off Luke’s hand). Thursday, July 26, Day 101, 39 days over; nearly a week on Sc. 400, “in which Vader tries to seduce Luke to the dark side of the Force.” Hamill backs away on a “plank nine inches wide, 30 feet above the ground, with two wind machines going full-blast.” Kershner working “almost 50 feet up.” Diamond: Hamill did “about 95 percent of the actual fight himself.” Hamill falls once, “tuck and roll”; later “made a member of the British Stunt Union—not just a belt buckle, but a full membership.”

Prowse to Arnold (keep complete): “You say in the film that Vader triumphs, but I don’t know whether he triumphs because they haven’t given me a copy of the script. I’ve got my speeches, but I haven’t got a clue as to how my speeches fit into the rest of the script or what’s happened prior to me arriving in the picture or what happens after I depart from the picture. I don’t know what’s going on.”

“While Prowse, given his penchant for revealing secrets, was kept in the dark, Hamill was debriefed by Lucas and then Kershner, who called over the actor not long before cameras rolled: ‘I met with Mark, and said, “Uh, you know that Darth Vader’s your father.” “Wha—?” “David Prowse will be saying stuff that doesn’t count, forget it. Use your own rhythm compared to what he’s doing.” ’ ”

Hamill: “They took me aside and said, ‘This is what he’s going to say.’ ‘You don’t know the truth, *Obi-Wan* killed your father.’ ” — this is the dummy line Prowse is to speak; do not treat it as a real story beat.

Lucas: “I told Mark, ‘Don’t tell anybody—especially don’t tell David Prowse—but I want you to be able to know, to be able to act appropriately.’ And then Kersh worked the scene with him.”

Kershner: “I love when Darth Vader says, ‘The only way you’ll ever beat me is with hate.’ It’s a lie and the kids know it. The last thing Ben says is, ‘Remember, don’t use hate.’ It’s the most important thing in the film.”

Prowse: “I didn’t know if James Earl Jones was going to do the voice again… they did go into a sound studio to re-record my lines for guide-tracks… it was hard to sound as villainous as I did when I was wearing the Darth Vader costume. I tried to sound as much like James Earl Jones as I could.”

Hamill (keep the early story distinct from the reveal just told to him): “At the time we filmed *Star Wars*, I had no idea Darth Vader was my father. I don’t think Alec Guinness did, either, because in the scene where I ask him who my father was, he hesitated. I don’t know how George made him do that. I didn’t hear him saying, ‘Maybe you don’t really want to tell him.’ But it’s tricky. I remember very early on asking who my parents were and being told that my father and Obi-Wan met Vader on the edge of a volcano and they had a duel. My father and Darth Vader fell into the crater and my father was instantly killed. Vader crawled out horribly scarred and, at that point, the Emperor landed and Obi-Wan ran into the forest, never to be seen again. Now I wonder if it’s true? Remember the Clone Wars? They could have cloned my father. It’s all speculation at this point …”

Tree-cave captions: specially rigged Vader costume and mannequin for the decapitation; “His symbolic ‘unmasking’ was filmed in inserts thanks to a mold taken of Hamill’s face.” Cast of Hamill’s hand for the cut-away.

## HEADACHES (pp. 216–218)

Reports 103–104, July 30–31: junk room (Chewbacca finds C-3PO parts); gantry continues. Saturday, July 28: Weber arrives; Bank of Boston, “How high is this gonna go?” Monday: Kershner “hit in the head by a cable gun”; headaches for days. First three reels screened a third time; exposition streamlined. Example: Luke’s escape from the wampa’s lair delayed so Han/Leia’s ice-corridor argument and C-3PO’s message to Han now play back-to-back. Lucas shoots “a bridging sequence… Mark putting on some of his flying gear and talking to a robot” (caption: “Lucas directed this new scene of Luke and the medical droid”). Medical-center scene moved earlier; pickup of Luke leaving for the battle. Lucas on second-unit wampa cave: “they shot thousands and thousands of feet of film… that was no good… I had to go back and reshoot it all. It was grim.” Hirsch: “At this point we’re thinking of opening in space. We’ll see an Imperial ship dispatching a sort of probe to a planet below and when the probe lands, we encounter Luke.”

## BAD OMEN (pp. 219–223)

Reports 105–107, August 1–3: gantry, Vader cuts off Luke’s hand; pickups. August 1: previous day’s shot of Hamill falling from the gantry into the reactor shaft “was damaged in the laboratory” (stoppage in the bath); insurance claim, $10,000 deductible; overnight art shift. Ganis telexes about unauthorized stills in US magazines; Arnold: “they aren’t coming from here.” Kurtz on Guinness: “No, not yet… He’s still resting in the country. George is going to meet with him again sometime later in the month.” August 2: Mayhew wraps after 76 days; Hamill has strained his back; 45 days over. Friday: “Prowse completed his role as Vader after 53 days worked.”

August 3: *More American Graffiti* opens “to poor reviews and even poorer box office.” Lucas: “We really don’t know whether *Empire* is going to turn out to be another *More American Graffiti*… If it should be a *Heretic*… or a James Bond film, I will lose everything.” Kurtz: “I don’t think in the history of the film business, a sequel has ever done better business than the original. Maybe it never will.” Lucas (keep complete): “I’m faced with a situation where everything I own, everything I ever earned, is wrapped up in *Empire*. If it isn’t a success not only could I lose everything, but I could also end up being millions of dollars in debt, which would be very difficult to get out from under. It would probably take me the rest of my life just to get back even again. Everybody says, ‘Oh, don’t worry, the film will be a huge success,’ but if it is just one of those mildly successful film sequels, I’ll lose everything. It has to be the biggest-grossing sequel of all time for me to break even.”
