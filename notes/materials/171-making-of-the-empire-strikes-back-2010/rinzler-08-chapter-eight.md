# 171 — Chapter Eight: Yoda Lives (August to September 1979)

Print pp. 224–246 (EPUB anchors `page224`–`page246`; print TOC opening p. 224). Rinzler ESB pp. 224–246. Staff/crew/cast same treatment as Lucas. Rinzler glosses flagged. “There is another,” the August 24, 1979, Guinness/Yoda dialogue reassignment, and Hamill’s 1979 speculation about a “long-lost sister or brother” are kept distinct from earlier draft wording and from later identifications of the “other.” Kazanjian’s “Gary was taken off of the movie” is not collapsed into Lucas’s “I didn’t really take Gary off.”

## PHOTO FINISH (pp. 224–226)

Report 108, Monday, August 6: Stage 9, Yoda’s house, Sc. 292 (Luke becomes pupil). Frank Oz begins to puppeteer Yoda; two reptile handlers on set. Kershner: “the bog planet may be the most difficult sequences of the whole film… like a long-distance run. You have to sprint at the end.” Hamill’s nickname “Mark Hamster” from a bluescreen treadmill; “iguanas and anacondas everywhere.” Dudman: Yoda “literally down to the wire,” all-night Freeborn family / Bob Keen team; “nobody had ever done it before.” Kurtz: head “only went together completely over the last weekend.”

Kershner: “I hated Yoda. He scared me.” Lucas: “if that puppet didn’t work, the whole film was going to fail. If it’s a Muppet, if it’s Kermit running around, the whole movie will collapse under the weight of it.” Oz’s hand positions (thumb mouth, middle finger brow, index/fourth upper palette); Kathy Mullen, Wendy Midener, David Barclay; Rachel Hunt “hand double and chaperone.” Mollo: Yoda’s costume a variant of Ben’s Jedi outfit; raw silk from India (Kershner has a leftover jacket made for himself). Oz’s private biography: “The guy’s probably 800 years old… This was no longer his time. He came from a gentler world.” Reynolds blushes when Yoda calls “Norman, Norman.”

Oz uses “any old voice” on set, to be dubbed later: “whether they use my voice or some other voice they want to dub is up to them.” Hamill bumps his head 14 times standing up in the tiny house (last-minute idea). Suschitzky crawls “as one would in a mine at the coalface.” Three hours of rehearsal per line. Hamill’s requested gag: Miss Piggy pops up — “Feelings? You want feelings? Get behind this couch…” Kershner “could joke about everything else, but not Yoda.” Lucas: “Right up until the moment where he was on film and talking, it looked like it was going to be a disaster… We didn’t know until we saw rushes from the first day.”

## SNAKES AND HUNTERS (pp. 226–230)

Reports 109–113, August 7–11: Yoda’s house; exhaust pipe and weather vane, S407 (Luke falls onto vane); bog clearing, Sc. 283 (Luke meets Yoda). Midener treated for a rash. Weather-vane unipod “30 or 40 feet up”; Kershner: “Mark was literally killing himself… attached to a tiny safety wire… which would have broken had he fallen—and he knew it.” King snake crawls up Hamill’s leg during “I won’t fail you, I’m not afraid” / “You will be, you *will* be.” Mike Culling / Animal Actors: Basel the boa, Peter the Python; “something like a hundred creatures.” Still no word on Guinness. Night shift on the bog. Trees of tubular steel and plaster; “old-man’s beard” vines from 20-odd miles around. Real earth, mud, water; escaped snakes; birds, bugs, spiders. Temperature inversions hold mineral-oil fog in a horizontal layer. Hamill cannot wear a mask; Oz and Kershner can. Pythons ~22 feet / 200 pounds; anaconda ~24 feet.

Hamill’s earpiece picks up Radio 1 and the Rolling Stones’ “Fool to Cry.” Oz under the floor: “Kersh would talk to me, but he wasn’t talking to me, he was talking to Yoda.” Conflicting accounts of whether Hamill could hear Yoda: Kershner says they rehearsed with a speaker, then “he’d have to do it blank”; Hamill: “the earpiece never worked, so it was abandoned… that whole relationship with Yoda had to be developed without dialogue.” Record both, do not collapse. Hamill also acts to a tin bucket (“Squeak, squeak” / “Beep, beep”) and sometimes greets the radio-controlled R2 while Baker drinks tea in the corner. EE daily, mid-August 1979: Luke with Oz-voiced Yoda.

## SKYWALKER’S CHOICE (p. 231)

Reports 114–119, August 13–18: bog clearing, S368 (Luke leaves Dagobah), “That is why you failed,” “Do or do not,” Luke tries to raise the X-wing, Luke has a vision. Monday–Wednesday through August 15: Sc. S368, “Luke’s decision to leave and Yoda’s revelation that ‘There is another.’ ” Nothing shot in sequence; X-wing moved overnight. Kershner: “This is a film of life and death, of great urgency… we’re dealing with kings and queens and emperors… In making his decision to rescue his friends, Luke reveals his character flaw *or* his character strength… He leaves his training before he’s finished—at the expense of a greater vision for the good of all… he feels it’s more important to save his friends at this moment.” Lucas: “he screws up and everything turns bad because of his emotional decision… His attachment makes for a very selfish decision.” Hamill bitten by a shedding, “blind” king snake at the moment Ben says, “You would become an agent of evil, like Lord Vader”; eight takes.

## THE OMINOUS OTHER (pp. 232–233)

Kershner: farewell to Yoda is also the audience’s farewell; “To me, Yoda is a Zen master.” Sixteen or seventeen takes, more than any other scene, because Ben will be added later; camera locked. Hamill on “another”: “It didn’t sit so well with me at first… people would think I was pulling a $5 million holdout… George insisted it had always been part of the storyline, though he never told me who it might be. Somebody suggested it might be the Princess, but I think that would be a letdown. She has too much power already. I mean, it’s like she’s the only woman in the galaxy. If you don’t hit it off with her, you become a monk.” Asked if he could be written out: “Well, look at what’s happening to Harrison… This is very classified information, but Harrison isn’t sure if he wants to do the next one, so they found a way of doing away with him and yet keep him. ‘He’s in a perfect state of hibernation,’ says Billy. So let’s hypothetically say that I gave them a lot of trouble and they didn’t want to use me—they could very easily discover a long-lost sister or brother of mine residing somewhere in another part of the galaxy, and then just refer to the fact that Luke is off searching for Harrison.” Flag as 1979 speculation, not as a locked identification of the “other.”

Lucas: “The problem with these kinds of movies is if you have a hero that can’t be killed, then where’s your drama? What I’ve done is I’ve said, ‘Well, this guy can be killed—don’t worry, he’s not the important one. There is another.’ It’s a cheap trick, but it works.”

Guinness still in doubt a day later; recuperating from an eye operation, yet two weeks earlier “he led a protest march to 10 Downing Street” over theater-seat taxes (Arnold is “puzzled”). Lucas: “I spent last evening with Alec… trying to get it cut down so there’s not that much strain on him because of his illness. I think we’ve worked it out; I think he’ll do the picture… he *is* Ben Kenobi and I wanted to have the real thing.” Report 119: 49 days over; Oz wraps after 12 days as Yoda to fulfill another commitment. EE audio: Hamill on “the clever expendability of *Star Wars* characters” (Arnold, 1979).

## X-WING IN POSITION 2 (pp. 234–236)

Reports 120–122, August 20–22: Luke climbs from the cockpit, looks for R2, R2 spit out. The pool is “absolutely foul”; at least one crew member falls in. Handlers put a six-foot alligator on Hamill’s lap in the cafeteria; “they didn’t even use it in the end, after they changed the script.” Oz already gone; Hamill now matches over-the-shoulder Yoda close-ups from weeks earlier, using a Moviola. Kershner on pressure: deep breathing, “30 seconds” of floating above the studio. Hamill exhausted at home with newborn Nathan.

## A FOUL WIND CHANGES (pp. 236–237)

Reports 123–124, August 23–24: R2 is okay; Luke tries to lift the X-wing. Thursday: Jeremy Bulloch wraps Boba Fett after 19 days (last scene in his ship’s cockpit, second unit). Friday, August 24: “Sir Alec Guinness confirmed that he would reprise his role as Ben Kenobi”; Arnold sees newly printed dressing-room nameplates. Lucas: “His doctors say he is getting better every day.” Kershner needs “just some shots against bluescreen.” Guinness: “It was clear that if I could help them out of a difficulty, I must do so. It was as simple as that.” “New script pages were issued that shortened his role; some lines were cut and some—‘Only a fully trained Jedi Knight, with the Force as his ally, will conquer Vader and his Emperor’—were given to Yoda.” Record as August 24, 1979, shooting-script inserts, not a new numbered draft.

Lucas and Hirsch leave Saturday; 52.5 days over. Kazanjian arrives “the last two weeks of filming”: “Gary was taken off of the movie; he stayed there, but he was taken off the movie. I was reporting to the bank every day, as was Charlie Weber. So I took over the last two weeks… every day… ‘We’re on schedule and we’re on budget.’ ” Lucas: “I put Howard on the picture, but I didn’t really take Gary off. Bob Watts and Howard Kazanjian were the ones that actually did most of the work from that point.” Do not collapse these two accounts.

## AUGUST ILM: TRASH TRAWLERS (p. 237)

August 6: bottom of Vader’s Star Destroyer ready; official name-change announcement back to ILM still being arranged with lawyers; “For now, we are ILM, a division of the Kerner Company.” August 16: completed shooting Vader’s ship, the *Executor*. Peterson: “Vader’s six-foot-long ship, which had about 150,000 lights in it… George wanted it huge.” Neon needs “some god-awful 20-second exposure per frame”; sawdust visible in dailies (Ralston). Trash-diggers, gas siphoning, security guard coming. Harrison Ellenshaw leaving Disney for ILM mattes, October 1, front projection. Animated probot omitted; George wants the full-sized probot against snow. Minutes: “If we use computer-generated animation, we should talk to Ed Catmull …” July 1979: Lucas established the Computer Research and Development Division at Sprocket Systems under Catmull (picture editing, sound editing, high-resolution graphics workstation, laser printer). Johnson on the “digital film printer” and electronic models; “With regards to *Empire*, it might be possible to include one or two shots, I don’t know.”

## A BOG BASH (p. 238)

Reports 125–128, August 28–31: Luke approaches the tree; runs with Yoda in backpack. Hamill: “I felt left out… nostalgic for the grand old days on the Death Star.” Friday wrap party on the unfinished bog set; 12 people walk into the swamp; Kershner, “It was like Armistice Day.” Hamill countdown labels under his shirt. Stigwood/Lucasfilm joint venture announced (in the works since May) for record rights; rumor that Mick Jagger would score the sequel, reported by *Daily Variety*; “RSO denied the report.”

## THE RETURN OF OLD BEN (pp. 238–241)

Reports 129–131, September 3–5: weather vane (Luke calls for Ben, calls for Leia, falls from vane); black velvet (Ben Kenobi), V31 (“Luke, you’re our only hope”), S368 (“I cannot interfere”). Kershner “greatly dissatisfied” with Dagobah compromises and second-unit work “which is never the same in my eyes.” Wednesday, September 5, 8:30 AM: limousine brings Guinness; “his contract, for one-quarter of 1 percent of *Empire*’s gross receipts, is dated September 10.” Stage 4, Franciscan-style cloak and cowl, two cameras on black velvet. Guinness: “I get exhausted whatever I do… I’m not as passionate about it anymore… As T. S. Eliot once said about writing, ‘Every sentence is a struggle…’ ” He fluffs twice in rehearsal; in takes, “a speech that in itself had no great profundity was given the ring of wisdom.” Between takes he studies a little handwritten book of his lines “with little markings over the words where he wanted to change inflection or emphasis.” Wrapped at 1 PM; home two hours later. Arnold: “In less than a single day, he had earned a small fortune—but at the price of a lifetime of experience.” Hamill, not on camera, feeds Luke’s lines. Caption typo “reknown.” Script-page caption: dialogue for Guinness, Hamill, and Yoda “had been revised by Lucas and Kershner on August 24, 1979.”

## KERSHNER’S 6 1/2 (pp. 241–242)

Reports 132–133, September 6–7: Luke standing on hands; X-wing raised from the swamp. Guinness’s single day is also Hamill’s 100th and “the official end of principal photography”; second unit continues with Kershner still directing, “linguistics made for budgetary reasons.” Friday, 55 days over: last Hamill shots of the X-wing rising. Dressing-room doors already labeled for *Reds* (Jack Nicholson, Diane Keaton, Warren Beatty). John May’s *Official Collector’s Edition* report of the day. Hamill quip: “I’m under contract and overexposed.” Kershner: “I spent eight or nine hours on the bog planet one day, for six and a half seconds of film.”

## DEEP MINCH (pp. 242–246)

Reports 134–144, September 10–24. Kershner returns to the States September 9; they screen the assembly. “Even the 27 weeks doesn’t really seem too bad” (Kurtz). Tuesday, September 11: Hamill completes his role after 103 days; family leaves September 13. Baker wraps September 14 after 60 days (underwater R2). Deep Roy filmed in the “Perspective Yoda” suit, walking on his knees, for long shots. Last pickups Monday, September 24: Luke-on-Hoth insert and haunted-tree coverage, Joe Gibson doubling Hamill; wrap 4:40 PM.

Lucas favorites: “Artoo peeking through a window warms the cockles of my heart” (Kershner’s idea, special rig, “tippy toes”); “when Lando’s driving… Leia and Chewie turn and look at each other… a very funny little moment for me, between woman and Wookiee.” Watts: storage built for the next film, including the sectioned *Falcon* and X-wings. Hirsch: about 400,000 feet printed, ~80 hours, “a ratio of 40 to 1.” Official close: 21 extra scenes; average 11 setups and 55.5 seconds of screen time per day; footage exposed “541,584 feet with 274 sound rolls”—“yet 204 scenes remained to be completed by ILM.” Lucas: “it’s turning out much more like I imagined than the first one… a real collaborative endeavor between Kersh and I where he had a lot of freedom to direct the film… and I tried to support him.” Tally: “SETUPS: 1,571; SCS. COMP: 264/468; SCREEN TIME: 132M 57S/130M.” (Screen time already over the 130-minute target.) EE: Fisher interviewed by Michel Parbot.
