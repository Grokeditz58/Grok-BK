# 171 — Chapter One: The Summer of *Star Wars* (May to December 1977)

Print pp. 2–28 (EPUB anchors `page2`–`page28`; print TOC opening p. 2). Rinzler ESB pp. 2–28. Staff/crew/cast same treatment as Lucas. Rinzler glosses flagged. Draft identities and the November 28–December 2, 1977, story-conference transcript are recorded, not collapsed with later drafts or with the filmed *Empire*. The conference transcript, Rinzler notes, “runs 51 typed pages. Although Leigh Brackett participated, the transcription records only Lucas’s explanations.” The title *The Empire Strikes Back* is already on both the typed transcription and the November 28, 1977, treatment.

## Opening (pp. 2–3)

*Star Wars* “had opened in 32 theaters on May 25, 1977, and then expanded, slowly, into several hundred more.” Alan Arnold, in *Once Upon a Galaxy*, sets his journal in that summer; technicians who had worked on the film “could not explain the fever.” Robert Watts, in northern Afghanistan, opened *Time* “straight onto a bunch of color pictures from *Star Wars*. I thought, *Bloody hell!*”

Ralph McQuarrie, walking down Hollywood Boulevard past Grauman’s Chinese: “I picked it up and saw that it was a bubblegum wrapper with Darth Vader on it. I thought, *Gee, now I’m one of those people who make those things. It’s part of life now*.” An astronaut told Richard Edlund “that he believed it all and was glued to his seat.” Alec Guinness: “It was a darn good story dashingly told, and beyond that I can’t explain it. Failure has a thousand explanations. Success doesn’t need one.” Mark Hamill: “*Star Wars* tumbled out in the summer of 1977 and just went cuckoo… I was like a kid in a candy store. *Gee! All these groupies*. I don’t feel I dealt with that very successfully.” Carrie Fisher: “Very little children even believe Princess Leia is a real human being who lives in outer space.” Harrison Ford: “What *Star Wars* has accomplished is really not possible. But it has done it anyway. Nobody rational would have believed that there is still a place for fairy tales… But the need was there; the human need to have the human condition expressed in mythic terms.” John Williams: “It’s stimulating to hear people whistling your tunes.”

Lucas had returned in June from Hawaii, where he had already “enticed” Spielberg onto *Raiders of the Lost Ark*. Lucas, after taking Coppola to a regular San Francisco theater: “That was probably the first time I saw it with a real audience… by the time you get that far down on a movie, you’re so numb and so tired and so emotionally involved that it’s very hard to jump up and down and get excited. You feel good, but it’s a very quiet kind of thing.” Bunny Alsup, on previews: “I was personally calling college campuses and asking, ‘Would *anyone* like to go see this movie?’ ”

## BADLANDS (pp. 3–4)

Lucas stopped doing publicity; people arrived “with scripts… asking for money or, occasionally, making threats.” Rinzler (flag): making the first film had been “a four-year horrific seat-of-the-pants experience—one Lucas never wanted to live through again. But he had always envisioned a grander, very different film from what he’d ended up with, so a sequel would allow him to finish the saga.” Lucas: “It took so much effort just to get up to speed in order to make the first film and create this great world that I didn’t have the time to have any fun, to run around in it… In the first one, you are in a foreign environment—you just don’t know what’s going on—and it was the same for the author as it was for the audience. So I always felt if I went back to those environments using the same characters, I could make a helluva better movie.”

Media rumors, typography locked as Rinzler paraphrases them: “two *Star Wars* sequels had been shot while the first film was being made. The second movie, reportedly, would decide who gets the girl and feature a new battle against Darth Vader and his followers. The third movie would have Ben Kenobi return and try to restore the Jedi Knights…” Fox said officially that no work had been done. Lucas “stated publicly several times that he was retiring from the director’s chair.” Lucas: “You end up not being happy anymore and working yourself to death… It put me in a bad place personally.”

## DISAPPEARING MAGIC (p. 4)

Summer 1977: Pollock, Rigrod, and Bloom begin negotiations with Fox (right of first negotiation and first refusal). In 1976 they had procured sequel rights and a 50–50 licensing split. “Not a single employee of ILM was on the payroll as of June 1977.” Key former members reorganized in the Van Nuys warehouse as Apogee (founding members listed: Dykstra, McCune, Shepherd, Alexander, Miller, Peterson, Edlund) and rented Lucas’s equipment for Universal’s *Galactica*. Steve Gawley, Lorne Peterson, Edlund (“We got hornswoggled into doing this project with Glen Larson”), Joe Johnston, Ken Ralston. Dennis Muren had already left in March 1977 for *Close Encounters*. Lucas “was going to have to start a visual effects company for a second time.”

## BUILDING THE EMPIRE BENIGN (pp. 4–6)

Attorney Douglas Ferguson becomes vice president of administration after his secretary tells him “a fellow named George Lucas wanted to talk to me” and he says, “I don’t know any George Lucas.” Black Falcon would take merchandising; other subsidiaries, *American Graffiti* sequel and *Radioland Murders*. Lucas: “The business side of the film industry I don’t much like… the corporate environment in Hollywood isn’t any different from the corporate environment in the energy business. It’s all about making money and it’s all about making deals and it’s all about screwing this person or that person. It doesn’t have anything to do with making movies.”

Jane Bay, Fourth of July call to Tom Pollock, hired as office manager; starts August 16, 1977, at Park House. Lucas: “Nobody wants to invest in the esoteric craft of visual effects… We were basically carving an industry out of the wilderness here.” In the LA office in 1977: only Charles Lippincott and Carol Wakarska Titelman. Lucas: “At the time, there were no toys—it was literally T-shirts and posters.” Lippincott on the Fox kids’-records soundtrack pressing only 10,000 copies: “Needless to say, stores were empty.” Park House is “this old Victorian house that was filled with filmmakers” (Ritchie, Robbins, Barwood, Ballard, Dalva cutting *The Black Stallion*; “the basement is where Ben Burtt created the original sound effects library for *Star Wars*”).

Caption, typography locked, of an early saga outline (image, p. 6): “A very early outline for the *Star Wars* saga in which the original film is actually Episode Six. Episode I is a ‘prelude’ and the Clone Wars trilogy takes place in Episodes II through IV; Episode V would have been an ‘Epilogue/Prologue.’ ” Another notes page: “a ‘lost sister’ and a ‘long range goal’: the ‘Emperor/Empire must be destroyed.’ ”

## RECAPTURING THE MUSE (pp. 6–10)

McQuarrie is working for Larson / *Galactica: Saga of a Star World* (script dated August 30, 1977). Lucas asks Universal not to call it *Star World;* not to use Starbuck and Skyler; not to call robots “droids.” McQuarrie: “they had an emperor, stormtroopers, rocket fighters… it was beginning to look like a *Star Wars* rip-off.” The title becomes *Battlestar Galactica*; “many other changes were not made.”

Lucas on Dykstra (do not collapse with later accounts): “I think we would have gotten rid of him except for the fact that there were about five key people that worked directly with John and were very close friends of his… But there were some very upsetting times, a lot of yelling and screaming going on.” Anonymous internal notes on why Lucasfilm looked abroad for an effects supervisor: Trumbull committed; “John Dykstra was not asked to continue in his position because his personality did not mesh with that of Lucas”; Harryhausen, Linn Dunn, L. B. Abbott, Frank Van Der Veer ruled out. Brian Johnson, approached during *Space: 1999*, is hired for *Star Wars: Chapter II* (call in August 1977); he will coordinate California model work and English floor effects.

**Early Lucas notes (undated yellow pads; Rinzler flags that pages are undated and reshuffled, “which makes analyzing their development difficult”).** First page of an early outline: “Mention lost sister trained Jedi … Luke learn of main plot: Emperor/Empire must be destroyed; restore the Republic.” A *Chapter II* note that “may actually have been written for the first film, as it mentions the Kiber Crystal,” but “contains the first reference to what would become a new character”: “Luke unconscious—awakes to find Bunden Debannen (Buffy). Buffy very old—three or four thousand years. Kiber crystal in sword? Buffy shows Luke? Buffy the guardian. ‘Feel not think.’ You can become a physical manifestation of the Force—weapon in the continual struggle of the two sides of the Force to control the netherworld of which we are a part. Luke the chosen one—the human Buffy.” Another: “Learn the way of the Force. Luke: ‘Will you teach me?’ Teacher: ‘It would’ve been better to begin this when you were much younger. It’ll be harder for you now and it’ll take much longer.’ ” Another: “A repulsive, threatening figure can magically change into a most helpful friend. Talks to trees—shadows move and disappear. He [teacher] was Mynoc. Human-computer. (Vader?) Swordmasters.”

Ben’s fate in early notes, typography locked: “Ben is not ‘dead.’ ‘I have worked with several of JenJerod’s clones.’ Ben in netherworld cannot stay there forever; helpful in blocking areas of the Force to Vader and Emperor.” And: “Somewhere the good father (Ben) watches over the child’s fate, ready to assert his power when critically needed. Father changes into Darth Vader, who is a passing manifestation, and will return triumphant. Luke travels to the end of the world and makes sacrifice to undo the spell put on his father. He succeeds and happiness is restored.” Do not collapse this “father changes into Darth Vader” / “spell put on his father” note with the later filmed reveal, or with the November 1977 conference as excerpted below, which does not contain a father-reveal line.

“Luke crash in beginning (scar on face)” is considered to explain Hamill’s appearance after the January 1977 car accident. Hamill’s account of the crash (wrong freeway, BMW, about 70, car flipped). Kurtz: Hamill, after hours of surgery, said, “Oh. I’m sorry I got delayed. As soon as I get out of here this afternoon, we can go.” Hamill on Diana Hyland’s visit and later death. Lucas: “My feeling was some time has passed between films. Luke has been in the Rebellion fighting, so the change was justifiable.” Hamill, months later over dinner: “I asked George what he would have done if I’d been killed in the car accident […] Would somebody else have finished my part? He said, ‘No.’ There’d be a script change that would have found a long-lost brother or sister, something genetic, so that the Force would be with them.”

## DOMESTICATED FOX (pp. 10–11)

Martin A. Grove, *Los Angeles Herald Examiner* (1980): six months before release Fox tried to off-load *Star Wars* to German tax-shelter specialists; on May 2, 1977, a package sale to Bel-Aire Associates (*Star Wars* at $12 million with *The Other Side of Midnight* and *Fire Sale*) fell through. Lucas finances *Chapter II* himself. Tom Pollock: they arranged Bank of America financing, presented it to Fox, “They said yes very quickly.” Lippincott: Lucas “had complained in June about how, if they wanted to see another *Star Wars* movie, they had better start paying them, not talking.” Alan Ladd Jr.’s salary “went from $182,885 to $563,000.” Warren Hellman, Fox board, on the sequel negotiation: “ ‘We have to give George the rights to his characters.’ But we had a bunch of lawyers who said, ‘You can’t simply give away property rights!’ We voted and I think it was a six-to-five vote, because somebody had made the point that you can’t own his children.” (Same “can’t own his children” board-vote story as notes 170, here attributed to Hellman in this book.) Lucas: “The thing Fox did not expect was that I would pay for it and that was what shifted everything… I came in and said, ‘All you’re going to do is distribute.’ ”

The Chapter II Company is created to isolate risk (Richard Tong). Contract signed September 21, 1977, “100-page”: negative cost fixed at $8 million; “Producer [Chapter II] shall have ‘final cut’ of The Film.” Gross-receipt splits: producer 52.5% / distributor 47.5% first $20 million; 70.5 / 29.5 up to $60 million; beyond $100 million, 77.5 / 22.5. Licensing management reverts to Black Falcon Ltd. as of July 1, 1978 (incorporated February 27, 1978), 80/20 then 90/10 from April 1, 1981. Studio has “no creative control,” except Ladd sign-off “understood as a formality.” Principal photography start January 1979; release May 1, 1980. Press leaks: *Variety* says Fox’s distribution fee would be 27 percent; *Chicago Tribune* figures for a $4 ticket.

## RETURN OF THE PRODIGAL SONS (pp. 11–13)

Kurtz books Elstree and visits the *Superman* company to reassemble department heads: John Barry, Norman Reynolds, Stuart Freeborn; David Tomblin replacing Tony Waye as first AD. Watts: Barry “had his own script which he was hoping to get financed,” would decide by the end of March. McQuarrie contract October 1, 1977, after meetings September 14 and 21; he leaves *Galactica* and turns down Bakshi’s *Lord of the Rings*. Sketches for the Wookiee planet, snow armor, and a castle begin October 5.

Lucas notes on Vader, typography locked: “Once you’ve embarked on the dark path it will dominate your destiny. Don’t choose the quick and easy successes. Vader had abandoned himself to forces which were carrying him away—forces of dark and destructive violence.” McQuarrie, EE audio caption (Rinzler ESB EE, Chapter One): talks about his creative process (Arnold interview, 1978). McQuarrie: “We worked quite a lot on the Wookiee planet, which was to be the home of Chewbacca… the look of the surface of a planet where the Imperials have their base.” “Chewbacca as young Wookiee,” November 27, 1977; “whorrwaarrs (according to Lucas).”

Planet list in Lucas’s notes: “Wookiee planet Kasshook, Kazzook (Ganaararlacc)—huge trees; Gas planet (Hoth)—cities on piers, art deco, giant manta-like creatures in mist; Ice Planet, snow storms; Water planet (Acquis); a Dock/Desert (caves) planet; Garden planet (Besspin-Kaaleida); City Planet (ton-muund)—Death Star like, only bigger and can’t move; Grass Planet (ibbana); Bog Planet (Dagobah), fog-mist, swamp-jungle.” Note that in this list “Hoth” is the gas planet, not yet the ice planet. McQuarrie: “George said just off the top of his head, ‘I’d like to see a metal castle in the snow.’ George was looking for a place to put Vader’s office.”

Rinzler gloss, flagged: “In one entry, Lucas seems to have reconstructed how he arrived at the name Darth Vader—a combination of the words *dark, death, invader*.” Caption to the notes image: “Lucas’s notes that led up to the name of his villain: ‘Dark’ and ‘Death’ became ‘Darth,’ and ‘Invader’ was shortened to ‘Vader’—Darth Vader.” Record as Rinzler’s reading of that notes page, not as a dated Lucas interview line. McQuarrie drawings: “Darth Vader outside his castle” and “Vader inside his castle,” December 1977; later, “Following instructions from Lucas, McQuarrie placed Vader’s castle amid boiling lava (no. 35), December 1977.” Joe Johnston hired November 1977; snow battle and armored speeders already known; “At first they considered using existing military tanks.”

Plot notes, typography locked: “Streamline beginning—Leia to Vader to Luke. Streamline attack plot … Han thinks about Leia. Leia about Han … Threepio blown apart, transported in pieces… Han Solo, go on mission and never come back. End with Luke thinking about him … Luke develops Force, becomes powerful knight … Luke falls into trap—Vader gets upper hand; Luke barely escapes… Act I: Love story; into large threat (Emperor).”

Lucas, September 1977 transcript: he must hire writers for both the *American Graffiti* sequel and the *Star Wars* sequel; start dates already exist. Psychology-of-children notes (Bettelheim is cited later by Lucas himself): “Need symbolic images which reassure them that there is a happy ending, solution to the Oedipal problems … What the evildoer wishes to inflict on the hero should be the bad person’s fate.” Lucas: “I feel strongly about the role myths and fairy tales play in setting up young people for the way they’re supposed to conduct themselves in society… If I wasn’t a filmmaker, I think I might be a toymaker.” Caption: McQuarrie cover illustration with Vader “center stage,” worked October 31 and November 4, 7, and 14, 1977 (book published March 1978). July 1977 manuscript title page of Alan Dean Foster’s *Splinter of the Mind’s Eye*; Lucas had met Foster October 13 and 20, 1976; the story “included Yuzzum or *Yuzzem*.”

## MYSTERY ADS (pp. 13–15)

Jane Bay tracks weekend grosses for about 25 major theaters. *Variety*’s sequel announcement is only a small October 12 item (start date January 1979). Park House Fridays are potluck; Lucy Wilson: “Jane, Chrissie, and I were called the Park Way Princesses.” Chrissie England is hired from an anonymous ad after she matches the phone number to Lucasfilm in the Marin phone book; she later becomes secretary to Lucas and “did all of the cease-and-desist letters and just all the fan letters.” Doug Ferguson and Lucas reject a search firm; they advertise in *The Wall Street Journal* without naming Lucasfilm and hire Charlie Weber (official start September 12), who first said, “Who’s George Lucas?”

## SCREENPLAY NOIR (pp. 15–19) and the story conference

Weber’s official start September 12. Lucas: “three rules in the movie business: script, script, script” (Weber’s account of Lucas’s real-estate analogy). Story conferencing for *Chapter II* begins November 28, 1977, with Leigh Brackett ($50,000 flat fee). Lucas: “I was a big fan of the old John Wayne Westerns and Howard Hawks’s movies. And she knew science fiction and she said, ‘Okay, fine.’ ” He “preferred to collaborate with screenwriters, feeding them ideas and brainstorming.” Ideas taking shape: a visit to the Wookiee planet; a new alien species; “two new characters—the Emperor and an unnamed gambler.”

Lucas on the gambler: “I wanted to bring in someone from Han’s past… I wanted to round out Han Solo’s character a little bit. The ‘gambler’ used to hang with Han, but is a different kind of person, more of a rogue and a con artist type than a fast-shooting, fast-talking type like Han.” Influences Rinzler flags: Frank Herbert’s *Dune* (guilds “tinged with religious aspects”); Hawks’s *The Thing from Another World* (1951) and the Ice Kingdom of Mongo *Flash Gordon* strips (March 12, 1939–April 7, 1940) on the ice-planet scenes.

After Thanksgiving, Lucas walks Brackett through already numbered scenes. Conference ends December 2. In the treatment Lucas names “Minch Yoda.” A Lucas note: “Yoda tells a fairy tale to Luke: ‘We are not material. We are luminous beings who are tied together by the Force. The light field does not exist. Energy does flow.’ ” Lucas: “When you are born, you have an energy field around you. You could call it an aura. An archaic description would be a halo… When you die, your energy field joins all the other energy fields in the universe and, while you’re still living, that larger energy field is sympathetic to your own energy field.”

Rinzler gloss, flagged: “A key element that Lucas often revisited during the conference was the identity of Luke’s father, which changed as the plot developed and the needs of the story became more obvious.” The transcript summary as printed in this chapter does **not** quote a father-reveal line or spell out those changes; do not invent them here. Both the transcription and the treatment already bear the title *The Empire Strikes Back*.

## Story conference transcript summary (pp. 20–25)

Rinzler ESB pp. 20–25. “The transcript runs 51 typed pages… the transcription records only Lucas’s explanations… themes and concept development are here combined.” All quotations below are Lucas in that November 28–December 2, 1977, transcript, as Rinzler excerpts them.

**PLOT DEVELOPMENT.** “*American Graffiti*’s mode of writing”: each character a plot, a main plot and three sub-plots; “60 scenes, about two pages per scene”; aim 110–120 minutes, “100 to 105-page script. Short and tight.” “The basic premise is that Luke is drawn into his training due to a problem that develops during the first act. The final act is his revenge on Darth Vader’s forces. That’s the surface story. But we know that the whole thing from the beginning is a huge trap.” “The film was originally set up as a spooky movie, as a horror kind of film; now the emphasis is on the love story… It would be interesting, a good idea, if in this one Vader tries to tempt Luke.” Ice Planet opening; “They’re saying, ‘We’ve got to get rid of the Emperor,’ which we never said in the other film.” After the Death Star, “another thousand systems have joined the Rebellion.” A lurking “Thing,” “fish-like—something that swims in the snow”; “knights riding around on giant lizards”; Luke hurt at the start so “Leia nurses him back, which creates the false impression that she loves him.” “Darth Vader is only interested in Luke’s friends as bait… Han and Leia and Vader all sitting down to dinner together—the evil Count having dinner with his enemies… the gambler invites them to dinner and they come into the room and there is Vader… nothing personal. Maybe Vader explains to them that he has no interest in them.” Ending image: “The movie ends with Luke and Leia looking up at the stars wondering if they’ll ever see Han again …”

**HAN SOLO DEVELOPMENT.** “Han is coming to grips with accepting responsibility. When he runs away, he’s on some kind of mission. Only one man can do this mission… it is key to setting up the Republic government in exile… we think maybe there’s going to be a movie about him.” A “stepfather,” “like a J. P. Morgan,” “head of the transportation union,” “about the fifth most important guy in the galaxy,” guild “almost a religious kind of thing.” “There is also a whole section about how Han got tied up with Chewie. It has to do with Han being orphaned and landing on the Wookiee planet and being raised by the Wookiees.” The stepfather is “a Hemingway-esque character… In his trading, he came across Han and took him under his wing for eight or nine years—until they had a falling out… Han swore he would never talk to him again.” The trip is “on the other side of the galaxy”; the stepfather’s place is “completely hidden… but Han knows where it is.” Wookiees may return “even if Chewbacca is lost in this one with Han Solo.” Foster’s sequel novel “has Yuzzum.”

**LOST SISTER DEVELOPMENT.** “I also want to develop Luke’s sister. The idea is that Luke’s father had two children who were twins. He took one of them to an uncle on one side of the universe and one to the other side of the universe, so that they would be safe… She also becomes a Jedi… Eventually in some episode, not this one, we could cope with Luke and his sister, and how she is the female Jedi and he is the male Jedi.” “Luke gets his awareness of his lost sister through the Jedi training.”

**LOVE STORY DEVELOPMENT.** *Doctor Zhivago* ice imagery; “Han would state his position, sulks off… It’s not until later that we realize that Leia doesn’t love Luke.” “A way to visualize it is to see Han as Rhett Butler, Leia is Scarlett, and Luke is Ashley.” “the Errol Flynn kiss.” Chewbacca “jealous”; Threepio “thinks the whole emotional thing is silly.”

**OBI-WAN (“BEN”) KENOBI DEVELOPMENT.** “I may bring back Ben eventually; I’ll have to bring back his voice. May also bring back the ghost of Ben, not the person of Ben.” “Some of the Force came from Carlos Castaneda’s book *Tales of Power* [1974].” “Ben will explain to Luke that he will gather all these powers, but he can’t use them for evil or he will succumb to the dark side of the Force. If you use it for evil, it will start using you… The good side is a passive side and the bad side is an aggressive side.” A Lucas note quoted here: “The mood of a warrior calls at once for control and abandon; the Force commands you and obeys you—unity of opposites.”

**TEACHER OF THE FORCE DEVELOPMENT.** “The original idea was to have that person be an alien… (I decided to keep him [Ben] very noble after casting Alec Guinness.)… a crazy little nitwit that scurries around like a rat… Maybe he is the one who trained Ben.” “Let’s make him small, so he’s about 28 inches high. Maybe he’s slightly froglike… We could possibly have Jim Henson of the Muppets work with us… It should be like Kermit the Frog, but an alien…” Lesson: “It would have been easier if we had started when you were much younger, which is the way it was with Ben.”

**LUKE SKYWALKER DEVELOPMENT.** “we are going to throw him into this devotion of becoming the perfect Jedi Knight.” “We will begin to deal with the roots of the dark side of this Force in this film… The danger is that he will become Vader. We know now that Vader is out to get Luke.” “We need to take him to his lowest point ever.” A hologram crystal might pop from the sword handle; Artoo would identify “a crystalline holographic memory system containing three million two hundred fifty-four bits of information.” A set-piece: “Luke polishes off 137 stormtroopers. Vader comes in and they have a giant duel… The fight is psychic because, at the same time, Vader is trying to convince Luke to come over to his side of the Force.”

**PLANETS.** Water Planet, underwater city; Bog Planet “like *Hound of the Baskervilles*”; City Planet “the center of the Empire. Sooner or later, the Empire will have to be shown, but I probably won’t have to in this Trilogy or this film.”

**NEW ALIENS.** “tall, thin, white ethereal aliens… a very noble creature, but threatening.”

**DARTH VADER DEVELOPMENT.** “Where do we pick up the story with Vader?… Being alone might not be a good idea. We need to get a sense that he’s back with civilization, that they found him.” After the snow-planet attack, isolation. “Vader is really afraid of the Emperor. That’s the only thing Vader is afraid of. The best way to set up a super-villain is to take the biggest villain you’ve got and make him afraid of the super-villain. The Emperor is even more powerful than Vader. He’s the classic devil character, a hooded, dark figure—you can’t even see who he is.”

“Vader started getting fascinated with the dark side of the Force and was lured into it. He didn’t tell anybody, as he became an evil person. The evil Force was starting to take over the galaxy—it was in control of the Emperor… No one knew that he had been seduced, but he went around killing all these Jedi in secret. He killed a bunch of them and trapped others in a situation where they were all destroyed; only a few escaped. One of them was Ben.”

“Vader is completely consumed by the evil side of the Force. He is an instrument of the Force rather than having his own free will in terms of what he does… When we kill him off in the next one, we’ll reveal what he really is. He wants to be human—he’s still fighting in his own way the dark side of the Force. He doesn’t want to be a bad man, but he is. He can’t resist it. He’s struggling somehow to get out of what he is, struggling with his humanity.”

“Darth Vader’s prime purpose is to get Luke… He doesn’t really care about anybody else. He’s trying to smash the Rebels, but that’s secondary.” Environments: “a little castle on a rock in the middle of the ocean,” or “a tall, dark tower, very narrow in a lava flow, dark, red, and burning, almost like hell… gargoyles surrounding him. His pets.” Then “a gray, macabre, cold steel box and it’s the Emperor. The Emperor tells Vader to get Luke—he is the last of the Jedi and must be stopped. Vader is saying he’s not a Jedi yet.” Or a Flash Gordon viewscreen.

**VADER-VERSUS-LUKE DEVELOPMENT.** “I’d like the end conflict to be a moral conflict, in addition to a physical one… The real drama is whether Luke will become Darth Vader or not. We have tested him and we know he is weak. He really had to struggle to stay good. He has the same potentiality that Darth Vader had.” Ben, as ghost: “Vader has more power than you can imagine. When he and I met, we fought on such a level that there didn’t appear to be much of a battle; it appeared to be a swordfight, but it was a battle of our wills that was really going on in the beyond.” Lucas wanted an “*Exorcist* kind of battle” “with lightning or electrical bolts, and throwing things around the room” (he “did in the Alan Dean Foster book, which is what I wanted to do in the first one with Ben and Vader”). Achievement levels: “Luke is now a level 2 and Vader is a 4; ‘I was a 6 and the Emperor is a 6, and he’s on his way to becoming a 10’… Luke has to destroy the Emperor.” In the fight, “Vader is using the dark side… even as Luke gets the upper hand, Vader knows that he’s winning because he’s getting Luke to use the dark side of the Force… The more he’s winning, the more he’s losing.” Set piece: a shaft “down to the reactor”; Luke zips through a vacuum tube and dangles “above the exit port of the city.”

**EMPEROR DEVELOPMENT.** “When you get rid of the Emperor, the whole thing is over. The final episode is the restoration of the Republic.” “He’s not as dramatic as Vader, but is more sinister. Vader is just one of his lap dogs.” “How about if we don’t see his face? He’s just a hooded figure, reminiscent of Ben. In the end, the Emperor does exactly what Ben did; he can also transform himself. As Ben becomes the personification of the good side of the Force, the Emperor is the bad. Another way to treat the Emperor would be as a bureaucrat, Nixon-ian in his outlook, a Wizard of Oz type.”

**THE GAMBLER DEVELOPMENT.** “He’s a slick, riverboat gambler type of dude. Han Solo is a rather crude, rough and tumble kind of guy; this guy will be a very slicked down, elegant, James Bond–type… more in the Mr. Spock style of thinking… An emotional Spock… They’re both underworld characters.” “pro Empire, but he thinks he’s smarter than the Empire… in the end, he realizes that he must join the Rebellion.” “Maybe he could look human but not really be human. He’s possibly a clone… The Princess doesn’t trust him because of that… If we set him up as a clone, maybe in one of the other Episodes, we can have him run across a clan of them who are all exactly like him… We’ll assume that they were slightly weird in their own way and were partly responsible for the war… about 700 countries and he’s from one of the ruling clone clans.” “a Rudolph Valentino character, 1930s hair, slicked back. Wears white sport coats, white carnations…”

**C-3PO AND FRIENDS DEVELOPMENT.** Threepio “gets completely blown apart and we put all the pieces in a box… sympathy for a cardboard carton.” “We might be able to tie it in with Vader. Have Vader take his heart and smash it or turn it into an alarm clock or something.” Chewbacca as “Threepio’s protector” and the one who carries the bag of parts.

## Treatment summary, November 28, 1977 (pp. 26–28)

Rinzler ESB pp. 26–28. Nine-page typed treatment by George Lucas, same date as the conference opening. Summary, not the full treatment text; keep it distinct from the conference transcript and from later drafts.

Luke on a “large snow lizard (Taun Taun)” is “bashed in the face by a snow monster,” a prisoner with “a talisman that hangs around his neck, which was given to Luke by Obi-Wan Kenobi”; “He feels he must go to the planet described on Ben’s talisman.” Han tells Leia “he must pay an old debt or he will be hunted down.” Luke “uses the Force to escape.” Snow creatures attack the base; they cut the power; a water main breaks, “freezing troops like statues.” Luke “tries to use the Force, he fails and almost causes Han’s death. Luke is humiliated.” Then the Empire attacks: “The great snow battle of Hoth is fought by armored landspeeders (helicopter-like) and giant mechanical horses (tanks). The huge lumbering walking machines carry Imperial troops.” “Vader lands and ‘oversees the battle.’ ” Han, Leia, Chewbacca, and C-3PO escape in the *Falcon*; wounded Luke passes out; R2-D2 pilots to a swamp crash.

Luke meets “Minch Yoda,” gives him food; the creature “eventually claims to be a Jedi Master, the teacher of Ben Kenobi.” The Empire is waiting at the Rebel rendezvous; the *Falcon* hides in an asteroid cave. “Minch uses Luke’s laser sword to kill a monster”; Luke practices “with the sword and seeker balls.” C-3PO and Chewie “comment on the strange relationship between Han and Leia. Chewbacca is out of sorts about the whole thing—jealousy.” A desolate floating-city ruin and an attack by “strange, alien creatures,” then another floating city where they meet Lando. C-3PO “is blown to pieces by stormtroopers without the others noticing.” Luke “has a dream about Darth Vader, and Ben tells him it is time to leave the planet.” Dinner: “when the door swings open to the dining room, ‘it reveals Darth Vader!’ ” Vader “tells them he’s not really interested in them, but is using them to get Luke.”

Luke befriends noble aliens (ax fight, spares the chief), arrives at the city “riding a large manta-like flying creature,” walks into the trap knowingly. Lando “says he’ll help… as he’s had enough of Vader.” The alien chief “gives them the peace sign and approaches, but is shot down.” “Vader appears in the midst of the battle and confronts Luke: the two verbally assess each other’s powers … In this battle, both Luke and Vader use their ESP powers. Lightning bolts flash about the room, and they pick up objects with their powers and throw them at each other.” Reactor room with “a giant propeller that threatens to ‘mince’ both of them. Vader has the upper hand.” “Vader pleads with Luke to join him … For some reason, Vader is reluctant to kill Luke and would rather turn him to the Dark Side of the Force. Vader finally swings his death blow, but Luke jumps off of the narrow ledge and falls down the endless shaft.” Han blasts through a blast-shield door and they rescue Luke. “In the end, Han has to leave ‘to pay an old debt.’ ” Sunset ending with twin suns and the *Falcon* disappearing; Han gives Leia “a long kiss.” **No father-reveal, no “I am your father,” and no carbon-freeze of Han in this treatment.** Do not collapse with later drafts.
