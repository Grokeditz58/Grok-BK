# 171 — Chapter Five: Studio Clouds (March to May 1979)

Print pp. 120–153 (EPUB anchors `page120`–`page153`; print TOC opening p. 120). Rinzler ESB pp. 120–153. Staff/crew/cast same treatment as Lucas. Rinzler glosses flagged. Late March 1979 dialogue revisions and added end-scene beats are recorded as changes to the February 20, 1979, shooting script, not as a new numbered draft. Arnold 1979 interviews stay distinct from later reminiscences (including Fisher’s later published writing).

## Opening (p. 120)

Return from Finse: Kershner, “When we got back to Elstree Studios, we’d all been through something together… the location was a good way to start the picture.” Watts: technically one day late in studio, “still on schedule, because we’d achieved one day of location work with Harrison Ford that had been scheduled for a stage.” Stage 3 fire still crowding construction; Star Wars Stage “had no doors… half the walls were incomplete, so it was bitterly cold… soup twice a day… labor problems” (Welch). Commutes: Ford 30 minutes from a nearby hotel; Mayhew picked up 6:45 AM; Fisher in St. John’s Wood; Hamills in Chelsea (“where Margot Kidder stayed during *Superman*”). Kershner runs by “a beautiful lake by the studio… right across the street from Stanley Kubrick’s house,” five hours’ sleep, “almost a vegetarian.”

## A METAL MENACE (pp. 120–123)

Nos. 8–9, Tuesday–Wednesday, March 13–14; Stage 8—*Falcon* main hold; Scs. 205 (escape from Hoth), 236 pt. (asteroid field), 289 pt. (love scene). Arnold: Kershner arrives 7:30 AM with “shadowy marks of frostbite below his eyes.” Lucas arrived over the weekend (St. James Club); meeting with Kurtz and Hirsch. Hirsch cuts on a horizontal table, not an upright Moviola. Tuesday evening: Lucas, Kurtz, Kershner, and “key technicians” view Norwegian rushes, “pleased,” telex additional shots to Finse second unit.

Kershner starts with “a love scene between Princess Leia and Han Solo in the *Falcon*… two very vulnerable human beings, each trying to communicate with each other—and they’re thwarted at the last second by another piece of machinery that gets in the way.” C-3PO’s interruption of the kiss “was ad-libbed on set; in the script, Leia simply moves away from Han.” Also improvised: “Han’s attitude toward the droid’s advice about the *Falcon:* feigned non-interest followed by implementation.” First on-camera scenes for Daniels and Fisher.

Daniels delayed signing “until the last moment”: “If it hurts banging your head the first time, you’d have to be insane to bang it a second time… they preferred to pretend that the robot was not a human… this time, I would be paid reasonably, and… I really like Threepio.” (Contract details “wouldn’t be worked out until July 30.”) New armor: 11 parts instead of 20, “lighter, with no cutting edges”; luggage trolley to the set. Kershner: “I wanted to make Threepio a real pain in the rear… a combination of Mary Poppins and Felix in *The Odd Couple*… if you knew a person like See-Threepio in real life, you’d turn and run in the opposite direction.” Lucas: “The first film centered on the droids… One of the challenges was going to be keeping them front and center.” Daniels: Threepio “doesn’t quite understand what kissing is… looking at the script, that Threepio’s role has become slightly more like talking scenery.”

Baker occupies two of eight R2 units, now with two-way radio (“Before… I didn’t know whether they said, ‘Cut!’ until somebody hit me on the head with a hammer”). Hamill: “Only when he waddles is Kenny inside.” Arnold already hears that the original 16-week live-action plan “will take considerably longer.” Kurtz adds “four or five weeks right at the beginning of shooting.” Watts drops the Ministry of Defence protest after *The Sun* photos prove to have been taken from the ground, not the helicopter.

## GEAR FOR CHAOS (pp. 123–124)

No. 10, Thursday, March 15; main hold; Scs. 289, 297 pt., 299 pt. Hamill fitted at Berman’s “along with ‘4 pigmen and 2 small men’”; fencing with Diamond and Bob Anderson, “who would double as Vader in the duel.” Lucas: the crew “knows what kind of film we are making.” Kurtz: “close to 700 people.” Welch: construction averaging 300 vs. 220 on the first film; “one of the biggest rigging pictures that’s been done for a long time.” Bruton: props “Almost 100 percent more… pieces of aircraft, Tupperware… junkyards.” *Greeblie*: “a word coined on *Star Wars*… I think in fact George Lucas coined it” (Bruton).

## LAST-MINUTE WORDS (pp. 125–126)

No. 11, Friday, March 16; main hold; Scs. 297, 299, 430 (escape from Cloud City), S409, T.431 pt. Kasdan arrives for a visit. 5:30 PM “wampa explosion test.” Sunday morning: Lucas and Kershner meet the actors on script changes; afternoon “a *very* lengthy meeting… going over the entire script, making mostly dialogue changes, quite extensive” (Alsup), typed Monday. Kurtz: “Right up to the day of shooting, in fact, slightly after shooting, we kept modifying the script… we did add 8 or 10 sets after that time.”

End-scene tweaks recorded here (shooting-script revisions, not a new numbered draft): Luke hanging below Cloud City “first calls for Ben, but Vader, standing on a landing platform says, ‘Ben cannot help you now, my young Jedi!’ So Luke calls for Leia.” Aboard his ship, “now called the *Executor*, Vader asks Admiral Piett, ‘And their hyperdrive is deactivated?’ ” When the jump fails:

LUKE
Ben, why didn’t you tell me?

Luke is almost unconscious with pain and depression. He is defeated.

LUKE
(to himself)
I won’t be able to resist him.

After R2 enables the jump, Vader leaves the bridge, “his hands behind his back in a contemplative gesture.” Kershner: “It was essential to have Artoo be the one who fixes the hyperdrive. If it had been Lando, for instance, it would have been flat.” On the star cruiser, Luke’s new line: “I’ll see you on Tatooine … Take care, my friends. May the Force Be With You.”

## NDE NO. 1 (pp. 126–127)

No. 12, Monday, March 19; Stage 1—medical center; Scs. 42–43. Bacta tank: 400 gallons of chlorinated water; British Aerospace; 7′ 6″ high, 3′ 8″ diameter, “the largest of its kind ever made.” Scripts describe red liquid; “the water turned out to be blue” once Hamill was in it. Suschitzky lights the tube with an army searchlight and a mirror above; the heat “shattered the mirror above the set—not once, but twice!” Kershner: “Mark almost got killed… if he had been in the tank, I don’t know that he would’ve survived.” Hamill on a wire harness and scuba gear: “peaceful… My character was delirious and thrashing about… having a nightmare.”

Hamill on the recovery scene: Ford’s line “was supposed to be, ‘That’s two you owe me, Junior.’ But he didn’t say it … He gave me a little kiss—which had everyone falling on the floor. That’s the side of Harrison people don’t see—he’s a very funny guy.”

## THE RELATIONSHIP (pp. 127–132)

Nos. 13–14, March 20–21: medical center Sc. 57 pt.; ice corridor Sc. 15 pt. First two-set day; Report No. 14 lists production one day behind. Ice cavern: CO2, mist, steam, compressed air, glycerine; salt on the walls “got into our lungs, our pores”; heated mineral oil for fog; 100 degrees in fully enclosed corridors. Hitachi video monitor because Kershner “got lost in them, literally, for the first few weeks.”

Kasdan returns to the States; Lucas stays on set. Arnold asks if Kershner feels “inhibited or compromised”: “Not at all… like having the toymaker present, but he didn’t play with the toys.” Lucas: “Once in a while… I get a little restless as if I were directing, wishing I could go in there and get it done. But I like what Kersh is doing creatively… I much prefer that somebody else do the work.” Johnson: “George would be over his shoulder whispering this and that. Kersh did not always agree, so two versions were shot.” Kershner (source typo “Kerhsner” in one attribution): he had told Lucas he “couldn’t work if he would be looking over my shoulder all the time. After all, I had done time with Jon Peters.” Lucas “does not stand over me… he’s taken care of parts of the script that needed compressing.” Lucas: Kershner “wanted to give the film a slightly more serious tone… without taking it completely out of the Saturday matinee feeling. He wanted to get deeper into the characters and make the jokes a little less flippant.” Kasdan: Lucas “didn’t particularly like telling people what to do… he had reinvented himself as the producer-mastermind… trying to guide them into exactly what George wanted… without making them feel controlled.”

EE video: March 21, 1979, medcenter daily with extended dialogue; Daniels and Mayhew speak through masks; Kershner makes a “beep, beep” for Han at the automatic door.

## ALTERNATING RHYTHMS (pp. 132–133)

Nos. 15–16, March 22–25: Command Center, ice cave; Scs. 14 pt. (Han has to leave), 45, 46. 101 on set. Jeremy Bulloch fitting as Boba Fett (20 minutes to dress); half brother of Robert Watts, 35. Watts: “If the suit fits, the part’s yours.” Bulloch first mistakes the Wookiee scalp for a hairpiece; Lucas, on set: “You look fantastic… As far as I’m concerned, you’re fine.”

Fisher faints, “possible allergic reaction to the steam and spray paint”; “At one point I only weighed about 85 pounds… I was sort of the crew mascot… ‘If they want me, then I get a piggyback ride.’ ” Visitors: Penny Marshall, Rob Reiner, Treat Williams, Harvey Keitel. Billy Dee Williams arrives TW 760; elevator scenes on Cloud City omitted that Thursday. Williams after dinner with Lucas: “They’re a much more quiet people… People, to me, are like mirrors.”

Hamill’s fencing: “I just threw down my sword and said, ‘I can’t do this!’ ” Diamond’s “fatherly pat,” citing Errol Flynn. EE video: Hamill rehearses the carbon-freeze duel with Bob Anderson, “who will be Darth Vader in that sequence.” Separate still: Hamill also rehearses with Prowse. EE interview (Michel Parbot): Hamill and Ford on Lucas vs. Kershner directing styles. Kershner uses weekends for “rhythmic unity.”

## ENTER: DARTH VADER (pp. 133–135)

Nos. 17–19, March 26–28: command center and ice corridor; Scs. 14, 53, 15 pt., 115, 178, T205 (Vader enters base). Lucas flies home Tuesday evening; Bruce Boa completes General Rieekan. Cave-in / bombardment wrecks the carefully built ice set.

Wednesday, March 28: “after being on call the two previous days, David Prowse shot his first scene as Darth Vader, who enters the crumbling hideout.” Kershner: “I wanted the audience to be propelled into the story, so I designed a special shot to reveal each and every one of the characters.” Prowse: “The greatest bit was when they shot my big entrance… The two stuntmen went running through the hole, but, because they couldn’t see through their masks, they tripped over the polystyrene blocks and fell. I started to make my dramatic entrance… but I couldn’t move—one of the actors behind me had stepped onto my cape. I tried to get away and my cape came off and I fell on top of the two stuntmen.” Fisher flubs “Evacuate the rest of the ground patrol” on the first take of the collapsing-ice scene. Caption: a medical droid examines a dead tauntaun “killed by invading snow creatures (a plot point that would not make the final cut).”

## INSIDE THE MONSTER (pp. 136–137)

Nos. 20–24, March 29–April 4: main ice tunnel; Scs. 25, 180, 182, 44 (R2 encounters wampa, felled by a bazooka blast). Jack Purvis dress rehearsal as “Chief Hogman.” Baker on the Mini Tones / Purvis: “it’s been a little bit dicey… He’s a pig man, I think.” Wampa-through-the-wall fails on the one-take attempt (11-foot creature; operator’s arms “only half the length”). Des Webb in the suit three hours, “suffering from stomach cramps”; bazooka-hit delayed. Bank of America visits, “alarmed by rising costs.” Hamill wrist injury; Fisher home with a cold. Alsup: Kenner deadlines vs. the movie; Watts, “Are we making a movie or are we making toys?” Japanese-restaurant dinner for Kershner and the four principals. Deleted wampa-in-the-base scenes (wall break, Rebel attack, bazooka, innards). End-of-span tally: “SETUPS: 156; SCS. COMP: 28/445; SCREEN TIME: 18M 55S/130M.”

## MARCH ILM: HILARITY AND INNOVATION (p. 138)

March 12: Empireflex shipped to Norway. Expansion plans March 17; “Need more room!” New optical printer “top priority”; Edlund “stick my neck out… half million dollars or more”; four-head beam-splitter; telecentric optics from WWII tank periscopes “to do peerless mattes.” Muren and Nicholson “tried to stop this project.” 18-foot mechanical walker-foot plan in England scrapped; sequence reassigned to ILM. Early walker animatics include “a very spry machine that jumped.”

## SECOND SNOW UNIT (pp. 139–141)

Reports 8–29, March 12–April 3, Finse; Peter MacDonald directing; Bloom responsible for second-unit effects shooting; 43 extras; stuntmen Colin Skeaping and Bob Anderson. Johnson: “Norway’s worst weather in 150 years” (earlier Kershner had said “100 years”—record both, do not collapse). Probe-crash explosion: eight sticks of dynamite; radio battery knocked off at the plunger; Bloom’s “Ready when you are, C. B.” miss; they blow it anyway after the sun leaves. Extras with frostbite; students build igloos next to the gun emplacements overnight at 30 below. Wesscam opening plate: auto-rotation from 15,000 feet, 2,500 feet a minute, camera leveling “at between 3 and 6 feet.” Han Solo double ambushes the probe; Skeaping doubles Luke falling from the walker (April 1). Second unit supposed to be three weeks, “there for eight” (Bloom). Gifts: gold lockets inscribed *Star Wars*; hotel staff give pewter cups, *Thank you, Star Wars*. Last day: a huge avalanche, “a sign that spring had arrived.”

## TALKING TRASH (pp. 142–145)

Nos. 25–30, April 5–12: Cloud City corridors and living quarters; Scs. 352, 354, 355 (C-3PO destroyed), 361, 380 (Luke sees Fett), 381, 360. Five days over, then 6.5. Bulloch’s first day, April 10, Sc. 380: “transporting Han Solo in carbonite and shooting at Luke Skywalker.” Kershner: “Imagine you’re walking down the street in a Western town. He’s quick, but stealthy.” Costume / two pints of lager / can’t leave the suit. Fisher excused with influenza and bronchitis; moves to a house “leased to her by Eric Idle.” Ford sees a doctor, “didn’t miss any days.” Rinzler quotes Fisher’s later writing on “hallucinogens and painkillers… mind relievers and pain expanders”—flag as later reminiscence, not a 1979 Arnold interview. April 12: special crew films 41 McQuarrie paintings for the teaser (50 feet each, 1:1.85).

Hirsch on Kershner’s “fluid style… mini masters that overlap at certain key points.” McQuarrie: “George, perhaps, is more of an artist, where Kersh sees things from another point of view, a dramatic one concerning the motivation of the characters.” Kershner: “tone is everything… True discipline is from within… ultimately, is what the film I’m making is about.” He talks to Artoo, Threepio, and costumed Chewbacca as if they were the characters; “I finally started talking to my garbage cans at home.” Fisher: “Leia is not a real character. She is more of a caricature and is somewhat one-dimensional… In this film, though, Leia develops more. Kersh directs us as actors more than George did.” Ford (keep complete): “I never knew that my character only had two dimensions until critics told me. I don’t play the third dimension, because it wasn’t the author’s or the director’s choice to do that. But it’s there. It has to be there. I don’t have to play it because it’s me. It’s part of me, so the simpler I keep myself, the more it shows. This is a simple kind of enterprise and as such, the motives of the people involved are generally pretty straightforward. I enjoy playing this character. That’s it for me. I cannot conceive of a negative aspect to doing this film.” Easter: Fisher to the Cotswolds; Kershner to East Anglia (old USAF station); Ford “Bedridden with the flu,” security alarm outside the window: “If I’d had a laser gun I would have shot at it.” Ford: “I don’t really have a social life… I just haven’t got the energy for it.”

## IMMORAL LIZARD (pp. 145–150)

Nos. 31–40, April 17–30: Cloud City shootout and Chewie-strangles-Lando; ice gorge (Luke upside down); Vader’s Star Destroyer (orders to the Hoth system; asteroid field; Vader realizes Luke is gone; death of Captain Needa); Vader’s chamber; bounty hunters; *Falcon* “attacks” and disappears. Media inquiries “around 130 per week.” Living-quarters flirt/argue: dialogue revised March 22, then further revised by Kershner and the actors before shooting; Ford’s two young sons watch. April 19: 10.5 days over; Johnson leaves for ILM. Ford holiday in Tangiers; Fisher to Dublin then the States. 23-ton *Falcon* floated onto the Star Wars Stage on hoverpads. Hamill hanging upside down “really hard on my back”; Kurtz directs remaining ice-gorge days after Kershner’s one first-unit day.

Bounty-hunter scene: “one mercenary called Tuckuss as late as the shooting script would have his name changed to Zuckuss, perhaps because *tuckus* in Yiddish means ‘ass’ ”—Rinzler’s “perhaps” is a gloss. Kershner invents the clawed toes hanging over the pit: the admiral’s disgust “is not that it’s some alien creature, but that it’s an immoral creature… That was not in the script… The Imperials are all very pure looking and very clean; they’re all humans… They work for money. Even the Imperials think they’re doing good for the galaxy. There’s no such thing as people desiring to be evil. They’re evil for a purpose. They want to do good.”

Kershner on the meditation-chamber reveal: “I shot this scene very carefully. All you see are scars on the back of Vader’s neck for half a second. I imagined that beneath the mask, Vader was hideous: His mouth was cut away and he had one eye hanging low.” Prowse wears “a specially fitted bald-cap made by Freeborn’s team” for the damaged-skull shot. Lucas on Needa’s death: “We had to keep reminding the audience that Vader was evil and the easiest way to do that was to have him kill anyone in sight.” “Darth’s line, ‘Apology accepted,’ was improvised on set (or later in post).” — keep Rinzler’s parenthetical uncertainty; do not collapse to one place. Prowse: “When I was wearing the mask, people had difficulty hearing me, so I used to change my dialogue. I’d take a line like, ‘The asteroids do not concern me, I want that ship,’ and change it to, ‘Hemorrhoids do not concern me, I need a shit.’ ” EE May 3, 1979, daily caption (later wording, shorter): Prowse “will later say that he would change his line on some takes, for a laugh, to, ‘Hemorrhoids do not concern me.’ ” Record both; do not collapse.

## APRIL ILM: STOP-AND-GO (p. 151)

Bloom returns April 13 to run ILM after Norway second unit overruns; becomes general manager “from May or so of ’79” when Gallegly leaves. 57 Kerner employees April 6; first composited asteroid test “too many things happening.” Empire camera back from Norway, “did not function properly.” Norway plates “weren’t in any kind of order”; Muren needed to generate about 80 shots from a 20-shot plate sequence. Motion-control unit sent to Norway “never quite worked.”

## CREATING MAN (pp. 152–153)

Nos. 41–45, May 1–8: Star Destroyer collision; Vader prepares to board the *Falcon*; enter the asteroid field; Vader’s chamber, Sc. 294 (Emperor). May 1: 15 days over, “only about one-third through principal photography.” May 4: Ford returns from Morocco with a gastric infection; Hamill sent home ill; 17 days over. Same day, Margaret Thatcher elected; *The Evening News* half-page ad: “May the Fourth Be With You, Maggie.”

Kershner vs. schedule: robots “never really worked”; last-minute “adjustments, not changes of mind.” Kurtz: Kershner’s “built-in mania for wanting to tie everything together in a shot”; pressuring him “makes it worse… he’ll sit there and brood for about two hours.” Kershner never approved the schedule: “there is no realistic schedule with a film like this.” Watts: leeway “stemmed back even to the fire on Stage 3.”

Lucas arrives May 6. Internal memo to Weber, Kurtz, Kazanjian, Moohr, and Ganis: “the official budget figures to be released […] should be referred to as direct costs [of] $17 million.” “The reality was much more.” Lucas: “I discovered that they had started going over schedule… It began to be too much to work on the film while trying to create this company.” Kazanjian on Weber and Moohr: “They were the new big shots on campus… they didn’t know filmmaking.” EE audio: Lucas on producing pressures and escalating costs (Arnold, 1979, during principal photography). Lucas (complete, keep numbers distinct from the $17 million public figure and from Kurtz’s earlier $16 million [sic] in Oslo): “I had basically put everything I had into *Empire*. I took the money, everything I had from the first movie, and rolled it over into the second movie. The first one was budgeted at $13 million and then Fox said, ‘Make it for 7,’ but the thing came out to 11. The second one was budgeted at $20 million, but, about halfway through, it became very apparent that it wasn’t going to be 20. It was way over schedule and it had not been thought through carefully on the economic side. So we had to go back to the bank and get more money… ‘Are you sure we can get this done for $25 million now?’ And he said, ‘Yes, we’re absolutely sure we can get it done.’ ” Then: “when you’re actually standing there, saying to yourself, *Okay, it’s my money*, and shelling it out, you’re really betting that you can bring it in on schedule and on budget—and then you’re betting that people will go see it.”

May 7: Fisher back; Welch night shift, 100+ craftsmen including 59 carpenters, to finish the hangar. Kurtz family baby shower for the Hamills at Wallingford House; Arnold sees Lucas, Kurtz, Kershner, and Hirsch “framed in glass, each very different physically, all bearded, like figures in a Degas painting… The film is behind schedule, but their demeanor reflected no such anxiety.” End-of-chapter tally: “SETUPS: 585; SCS. COMP: 108/468; SCREEN TIME: 54M 38S/130M.”
