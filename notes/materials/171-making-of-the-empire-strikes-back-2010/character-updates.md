### November 1977 conference: “whether Luke will become Darth Vader or not” — no father-reveal

**Speaker:** George Lucas, November 28–December 2, 1977, story-conference / treatment material as quoted by Rinzler.
**Source:** “I’d like the end conflict to be a moral conflict, in addition to a physical one… The real drama is whether Luke will become Darth Vader or not.” The conference/treatment has no “I am your father” reveal. Do not invent a father-reveal at this stage. — typography-faithful, Rinzler ESB p. 14–28 (Chapter One transcript/treatment summaries).
**Notes:** `notes/materials/171-making-of-the-empire-strikes-back-2010/rinzler-01-chapter-one.md`

### Brackett first draft: Luke’s father appears as a ghost, distinct from Vader

**Speaker:** Leigh Brackett, February 17, 1978, first-draft text as quoted by Rinzler.
**Source:** “Luke is now able to summon Ben, and the latter appears accompanied by Luke’s father, who reveals to Luke that he has a sister.” SKYWALKER: “I sent you both away for your own safety, far apart from each other.” SKYWALKER: “If I were to tell you, Darth Vader could get that information from your mind and use her as a hostage.” Do not collapse this ghost-father Skywalker with Vader. — typography-faithful, Rinzler ESB p. 40–42.
**Notes:** `notes/materials/171-making-of-the-empire-strikes-back-2010/rinzler-02-chapter-two.md`

### Handwritten April 1, 1978, second draft: first “I am your father… We will rule the galaxy as father and son”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, handwritten second-draft dialogue as quoted by Rinzler (April 1, 1978).
**Source:** typography locked from the handwritten draft, in draft order: VADER: “The Force runs strong in the Skywalker line, you must use the dark side. Together we would be the most powerful. Stronger with the Force than even the Emperor.” LUKE: “Never will I join with you.” VADER: “We will rule the galaxy as father and son.” LUKE: “What?” VADER: “Old Kenobi never told you what happened to your father did he?” LUKE: “Enough! He said you killed him.” VADER: “I am your father.” LUKE: “That’s impossible. It’s not true.” VADER: “Search your feelings; you already know it to be true. Join me.” This is the first appearance of the father-reveal in the ESB drafts. — typography-faithful, Rinzler ESB p. 48–49.
**Notes:** `notes/materials/171-making-of-the-empire-strikes-back-2010/rinzler-02-chapter-two.md`

### Lucas on Needa: “We had to keep reminding the audience that Vader was evil”

**Speaker:** George Lucas; Rinzler on “Apology accepted.”

Patricia Blau later, transcribing: “I heard Darth Vader on the tape say, ‘Apology accepted.’… at that exact same moment Darth was using the Force to choke his lieutenant to death!” Do not collapse the improvisation note with the transcription note. — typography-faithful, Rinzler ESB p. 148–149, 338–339.

### On-set briefing: “Uh, you know that Darth Vader’s your father.” Dummy line for Prowse: “Obi-Wan killed your father.”

**Speaker:** Irvin Kershner, reporting the briefing of Mark Hamill not long before cameras on the gantry; George Lucas.
**Source:** Kershner: “I met with Mark, and said, ‘Uh, you know that Darth Vader’s your father.’ ‘Wha—?’ ‘David Prowse will be saying stuff that doesn’t count, forget it. Use your own rhythm compared to what he’s doing.’” Hamill, on the dummy line Prowse is to speak (do not treat it as a real story beat): “They took me aside and said, ‘This is what he’s going to say.’ ‘You don’t know the truth, *Obi-Wan* killed your father.’” Lucas: “I told Mark, ‘Don’t tell anybody—especially don’t tell David Prowse—but I want you to be able to know, to be able to act appropriately.’ And then Kersh worked the scene with him.” Distinct from the 1978 handwritten reveal and from Jones’s later first reading. — typography-faithful, Rinzler ESB p. 202–204.
**Notes:** `notes/materials/171-making-of-the-empire-strikes-back-2010/rinzler-07-chapter-seven.md`

### Hamill’s earlier-told story: a volcano duel; “They could have cloned my father”

**Speaker:** Mark Hamill, recalling what he had been told at the time of *Star Wars*, kept distinct from the gantry briefing.
**Source:** “At the time we filmed *Star Wars*, I had no idea Darth Vader was my father. I don’t think Alec Guinness did, either, because in the scene where I ask him who my father was, he hesitated. I don’t know how George made him do that. I didn’t hear him saying, ‘Maybe you don’t really want to tell him.’ But it’s tricky. I remember very early on asking who my parents were and being told that my father and Obi-Wan met Vader on the edge of a volcano and they had a duel. My father and Darth Vader fell into the crater and my father was instantly killed. Vader crawled out horribly scarred and, at that point, the Emperor landed and Obi-Wan ran into the forest, never to be seen again. Now I wonder if it’s true? Remember the Clone Wars? They could have cloned my father. It’s all speculation at this point …” — typography-faithful, Rinzler ESB p. 202–204.
**Notes:** `notes/materials/171-making-of-the-empire-strikes-back-2010/rinzler-07-chapter-seven.md`

### The Emperor: Marjorie Eaton tested, then Elaine Baker in a Rick Baker appliance, voice by Clive Revill, chimpanzee eyes

**Speaker:** Rinzler; Mark Hamill; Ken Ralston.
**Source:** “Eaton was filmed at ILM as the Emperor, though her test didn’t prove satisfactory; she was replaced by Elaine Baker in makeup, with the Emperor’s voice later supplied by actor Clive Revill.” Hamill: “It’s interesting because the Emperor was an actress, dubbed with a male voice, and monkey eyes superimposed… There is certainly something strange about him. This has got to be due to the fact that he looks and sounds a bit like Obi-Wan.” Ralston: “To make the hologram of the Emperor, we shot an actor in makeup with the eyes blacked out… We wound up shooting the eyes of a chimpanzee, then matchmoving the eyes of the actor and rephotographing it from a TV screen. Actually, that was true of all the hologram shots.” Caption: candidates for the Emperor’s eyes included “a cat, a chimpanzee, and those of assistant accountant Laura Crockett (the chimp won out).” The printed Appendix bills only “Voice of Emperor: Clive Revill.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler ESB p. 308–309, 354.

### Kershner’s survey: children “up to the age of about seven didn’t believe that Darth Vader was Luke’s father. They think he’s lying.”

**Speaker:** Irvin Kershner; a journalist as quoted by Rinzler; caption.
**Source:** “I found that children up to the age of about seven didn’t believe that Darth Vader was Luke’s father. They think he’s lying. Above the age of seven, they accept it—and it sends a chill up their spine.” “Audiences were stunned by Vader’s claim, but many were divided over whether he was telling the truth.” “Only Lucas knew for sure what was going to happen, and he wasn’t telling anyone.” Caption: *Dynamite* “asked readers whether Darth Vader was really Luke’s father.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler ESB p. 339.

### Lucas: “basically a castration scene”; kids “will simply deny that Vader is Luke’s father”

**Speaker:** George Lucas.
**Source:** “I was very concerned about the ending, that it might be too intense for small children. That is a pretty intense moment and basically a castration scene. But I spoke to a number of psychologists who basically said that most kids, if it’s too intense, will simply deny that Vader is Luke’s father. But I was also concerned about leaving kids hanging.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler ESB p. 339.
**Notes:** `notes/materials/171-making-of-the-empire-strikes-back-2010/rinzler-11-chapter-eleven.md`

### Hamill: “originally… I would have to kill him in the third episode. But now he’s a cult figure”

**Speaker:** Mark Hamill.
**Source:** “Darth Vader is a good example of changing a character to please the people. I think, originally, if you follow classic drama, I would have to kill him in the third episode. But now he’s a cult figure and, in a way, George may not want to do away with him. Ultimately, the Emperor should be the main bad guy, someone you try to get through the nine movies—and in the ninth, you succeed.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler ESB p. 339.
**Notes:** `notes/materials/171-making-of-the-empire-strikes-back-2010/rinzler-11-chapter-eleven.md`

**Speaker:** Irvin Kershner.

**Speaker:** Lawrence Kasdan.
**Source:** “The Jedi Knights are enlightened warrior-priests… The idea of having an evil father and a good father is very common in mythology… we have within us the dark side and the light, good and evil, the devil and the angel.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler ESB p. 339.

### Lucas, 1980: prequel stories exist “where Darth Vader came from, the whole story about Darth and Ben Kenobi… before Luke is born”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, 1980 interview material as quoted by Rinzler; Rinzler gloss on the shifting 12-/9-film plan.
**Source:** “The prequel stories exist—where Darth Vader came from, the whole story about Darth and Ben Kenobi—and it all takes place before Luke is born. The other one—what happens to Luke afterward—is much more ethereal. I have a tiny notebook full of notes on that. If I’m really ambitious, I could proceed to figure out what would have happened to Luke.” “There are six original stories that were written—really, seven; one was an odd film. But six original—two trilogies—and they’re complete and they were written really during the first one. When I wrote the first script, it was part of those six films; and then, after the success of the film, I added another three, another trilogy after this.” Lucas, on the shifting sequel count: “People were always asking, ‘Are you going to do *more* sequels?’ Sometimes I got carried away.” Rinzler gloss (flag): “While *Empire* was at one time part of an ephemeral 12-film plan, by the time it was released, the number had been reduced to 9—though it sometimes resurfaced as 12. Depending on who was being interviewed when, the details and time periods would subtly shift.” Do not collapse the shifting counts. — typography-faithful, Rinzler ESB p. 351.

### Ford: “I never knew that my character only had two dimensions until critics told me.”

**Speaker:** Harrison Ford.
**Source:** “I never knew that my character only had two dimensions until critics told me. I don’t play the third dimension, because it wasn’t the author’s or the director’s choice to do that. This is a simple kind of enterprise and as such, the motives of the people involved are generally pretty straightforward. I enjoy playing this character. That’s it for me. I cannot conceive of a negative aspect to doing this film.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler ESB p. 146–147.

### Ford later: “the original line was, ‘I love you, too’”; “We never even shot ‘I love you, too’”

**Speaker:** Harrison Ford, after the fact.
**Source:** “The scene where I am about to go down the chute to be carbonized—well, the original line was, ‘I love you, too,’ but I felt that the other way was Han Solo’s way of saying, ‘It’s not over.’ … We never even shot ‘I love you, too.’ We just went ahead. It gave George pause. He had not written the scene with a laugh. But that laugh opens you up emotionally… there’ll never be a payoff for the scene without a laugh.” Do not merge this later memory with the printed February 20 text (which is not “I love you, too”). Script-supervisor continuity records “I love you!” / “I know.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler ESB p. 178, 188.

### August 1980: “Harrison Ford had agreed to play Han Solo one more time.”

**Speaker:** Rinzler; Harrison Ford; George Lucas on *Revenge of the Jedi*.
**Source:** “One big question about the third film had already been answered for the public in August 1980: Harrison Ford had agreed to play Han Solo one more time.” Ford: people disappointed with the ending “contradict themselves every time, because their next comment is always: ‘I can’t wait for the third one.’ ” Lucas: “The next chapter is called *Revenge of the Jedi*… I won’t say who survives and who doesn’t.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler ESB p. 347–348.

### Lucas on the first poster: “I want to do a poster of Han Solo and Princess Leia like the *Gone with the Wind* poster.”

**Speaker:** Sidney Ganis, reporting Lucas; Roger Kastel’s painting chosen.
**Source:** Ganis: “the guiding light for the campaign was George… ‘I want to do a poster of Han Solo and Princess Leia like the *Gone with the Wind* poster.’” First advertising would feature Han and Leia, followed by posters featuring Darth Vader—“a ‘conscious’ strategy devised by Lucas.” The first theatrical one-sheet is inspired by the 1967 *Gone with the Wind* re-release poster (designed by Tom Jung, painted by Howard Terpning); six artists re-imagine Han and Leia as Rhett and Scarlett; Roger Kastel’s painting is chosen. — typography-faithful, Rinzler ESB p. 323–327.

### Lucas: “I always knew I’d have a problem with *Empire* because it was the second act, a down movie, and didn’t have an ending.”

**Speaker:** George Lucas.
**Source:** “I always knew I’d have a problem with *Empire* because it was the second act, a down movie, and didn’t have an ending. I had to get from number one to number three… In the second film, once we introduce the ‘other,’ it creates tension over whether Luke’s going to die or not. There is also the question, *Is he going to become like his father?* That’s what the real conflict is.” Rinzler gloss (flag): “While Lucas did succeed in creating perhaps the single most memorable cliffhanger in modern times, *Empire* is, as he says, an incomplete work. It would find its final form only when linked with Episode VI.” Han’s unresolved carbonite cliffhanger is part of that incomplete second act. — typography-faithful, Rinzler ESB p. 348.
