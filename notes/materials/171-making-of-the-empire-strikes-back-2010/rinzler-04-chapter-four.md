## Opening (p. 104)

With days before filming, Lucas, Kershner, and Kasdan are still making script changes. December 5, 1978, revisions already included: the *Falcon*, “upon leaving Cloud City, no longer blasted its way out through hangar doors.” A fifth draft “which introduced the ‘hogmen’” is dated February 20 (see the p. 105 sidebar, summarized below).

Kershner: “I made up those little people… We had a big argument about it, because George said, ‘Gee, maybe it’s wrong to have them.’ And I said, ‘Why?’ ‘Well, because we’re saying that the little people are slaves.’ I said, ‘They’re hogmen. They’re not slaves, they’re workers.’ When Chewbacca finds Threepio in the junk room, I made that scene up, just to have fun with it.”

Harrison Ford: “Basically, I just work here, you know what I mean? In fact, I didn’t get the script until three weeks before we started shooting.” Ford is in London and is not scheduled to go to Norway. He, Hamill, and Fisher arrive around February 17. Hamill trained four months in karate, fencing, kendo, and bodybuilding “because I do a lot of strenuous stunts on the bog planet.” Peter Diamond: “Mark has to progressively become a fantastic acrobat and gymnast… From a stunt coordinator’s point of view, you couldn’t have asked for a better actor.”

After *Saturday Night Live*, Fisher plans *Mr. Mike’s Mondo Video* with Michael O’Donoghue (“He calls me the Hollywood Devil Baby”) and *The Blues Brothers* with Belushi and Aykroyd. Hamill married Marilou York in December 1978; first child due in June. Hamill on marrying when she was three months’ pregnant: “So now it looks like we had to get married and the whole thing is so distasteful to me. I thought, *Fine—if it brings that many people that much unhappiness, I will get married*.”

Wednesday, February 28: Kurtz, Hamill, Fisher, and Arnold arrive in Oslo (Scandinavia Hotel). Arnold finds dinner conversation “somewhat strained” and blames himself as publicist. March 1 press conference: principal photography to start Monday. Fisher came “out of curiosity, not to take part in the filming, because the scenes at Finse do not require her.”

## Oslo press conference, March 1, 1979 (p. 106)

Kurtz on budget: “It should be approximately $16 million [sic]. Part of that is just inflation in the three years since the first one was made.” (Rinzler’s “[sic]” — earlier internal figures already put the budget well above this public number; do not collapse.)

Hamill: “I never thought of *Star Wars* as science fiction, I thought of it more as fantasy-fairytale…” Fisher: “I’d only read *1984* and *Dune*… *Star Wars* is very myth-oriented, with the main characters including a Princess and a wise old man, you know.”

On being connected to *Star Wars*: Hamill, “I don’t really care… I’d like to do other things, but I haven’t found a great driving force to separate myself from the project. It’s fun.” Fisher: “And terrifying.”

Why Lucas isn’t directing: Kurtz, “Well, George has retired, let’s put it that way.” Weather: “we want some bad weather… if we have two solid weeks of whiteout, that wouldn’t be good.”

Reporter: “I read that in the sequel that Princess Leia would choose between Han Solo and Luke Skywalker. Will that happen this time?” Kurtz: “We’re thinking about it. A lot of the story elements are still—” Fisher: “I can’t decide.”

Guinness: Kurtz, “His character probably will. I’m not really ready to say yet what happens with his character. We’re still working on that aspect.” Asked if Guinness has been asked: “Yes.” Fisher on offers: “I’ve had a lot of dating offers from 12-year-old boys.”

The group leaves Oslo for Finse in stages (Kershner, Mayhew, Tomblin March 2; Hamill, Fisher, Arnold March 3). Six-hour train; Marilou’s basinette, medical supplies, and “enormous teddy bear”; no porters; wrong platform. Johnson: “maniac Laplanders who just ski on horizontal slopes.” Hotel “like living in one huge sauna,” windows “iced solid.” Seventy-person crew, two to a room. Camp Sharman at 3,000 feet, Camp Kurtz on the summit, four heated huts each. Watts: “the weather conditions were appalling.” Kershner: “it was Norway’s coldest winter in 100 years… I ran two miles a day for months before it, because I knew what to expect.”

## Fifth-draft summary — Kasdan, February 20, 1979 (print p. 105)

Rinzler ESB p. 105. “Multicolored 157-page shooting script.” Keep this draft distinct from the October 24, 1978, fourth draft.

- Crawl changed from “a million worlds have felt the oppressive hand of the Emperor” to “a thousand worlds.”
- “Now Luke talks to Han before being attacked by the wampa.” (Fourth draft had no such dialogue.) Instead of Rebel troops inspecting the dead tauntaun, a medical droid does that work.
- Ben no longer tells Luke in the snow that “You are the only hope.”
- When the walker is downed, no Imperial/Rebel trench skirmish; “an airspeeder blows up the Imperial machine.”
- Ice corridors: droids pass a door with an *X*, “behind which ice monsters are contained.” Threepio: “See, your whistles upset [the Ice Monsters]. They’re being enticed into the trap by high-pitched sounds. No, I told you they can’t get out! Now will you hurry up? I don’t know why I bother …” Artoo “tells the Ice Monsters off.”
- Caption: “In his copy of the fifth draft, Lucas changed the name of Vader’s ship from *Avenger* to *Executor*.”
- Cut: R2 alone on Dagobah warming “several of his utility arms in front of the thermal heater”; and “the scene where Vader talks with Pestage before speaking with the Emperor; a new shot reveals the back of Vader’s scarred head.”
- Luke’s anger at flying remotes replaced by a shorter running-and-jumping scene with Yoda; upside-down-on-thumb scene also cut.
- Tree: after a shallow moat, “another physical guardian” — hanging vines “snap at him like lobster claws” attached to “a monstrous creature who stares down at him—Luke enters the tree.”
- At Yoda’s house, R2 rises on “toes” to peek in a window; when Luke is tested by “three glow-ball seekers,” one targets the droid, “who falls out of frame with a shriek and a clunk.”
- Cloud City: “a new scene has Chewbacca rescuing C-3PO’s parts from incineration by hogmen in a junk room. Lando accompanies Han and Leia across a plaza, but their dialogue has changed: Lando flirts with Leia and Han is jealous.”

The printed fifth-draft summary does not restate or alter the father-reveal; do not invent a change here.

## CALAMITOUS COLD (pp. 107–110)

Report No. 1: Monday, March 5, 1979; Ext. Plain of Hoth, Ice Gorge; Scs. 23, 31 (Luke staggers out of gorge, falls unconscious). Principal photography begins a 76-day schedule, finish date June 22, 1979. Start: Luke escaping the wampa’s lair. On hand: Hamill, Denis Lawson (printed “Dennis Lawson”) as Wedge, Mayhew, Des Webb (snow creature); last three CNU (called-not-used). Transport: eight Snocats, two Trac-Masters, four snow scooters, one Ratrac, a 16-person passenger sledge, 10 equipment sledges.

No trains have reached Finse for two days; snow tunnels collapsed; “We are isolated in a wilderness of snow” (Arnold). Two avalanches. Lucas’s advice to Kershner: “Don’t expect things to work.” Buried trench dressing and pipes; a local avalanche-finder with “a water-divining stick.” Kershner: “You know what the biggest problem was in working there? Going to the bathroom! We had on seven layers of clothes—we were dying!” Acetate film brittle and razor-sharp; Coast Guard rotating glass plate “didn’t work with snow.” Lenses must stay cold, camera bodies warm. Eyepiece clouds a third of the way through a take; bare skin freezes to metal, “You have to take a razor blade and slice away the skin to get it off.” Whiteout: poles every ten feet, ropes to track vehicles. Tape recorder stuck; Kershner “began to do what primitive peoples do, which is you remember everything.” Stay outside all day (7:30 to 5:30 or 6). Frozen soup and “spaghetti flambé” at minus 25°F.

After day one, Arnold/Hamill/Suschitzky conversation (1979, contemporaneous): Suschitzky, “It was diabolical… It’s just been on the news here”; three guys lost in the blizzard. Hamill on playing freezing to death: “It’s real eerie… Just walking in this stuff is murder… you’re praying for one take”; stinging “like when you put Merthiolate on a cut.” First escape-from-ice-cave shots taken “only a few steps from the hotel.”

## CALLING HARRISON FORD (pp. 110–111)

Snowed out, Bloom proposes bringing Ford a week early to shoot what, after the Stage 3 fire, had been planned for Leeds Studios, “obviate us from having to go out to Leeds” (Watts). By the time Ford reaches Oslo, three avalanches have cut the rail line. He trains as far as Geilo, then two taxi rides to Ustaoset, 23 miles from Finse. Bloom: they send “a bottle of vodka with our Australian location manager” so a snowplow driver will come. Ford: “My snow scenes were supposed to be shot at the sound stage in the studio… I found myself whisked away to join them with no preparation, wearing a costume built for conditions on the stage. Another one of those bizarre experiences in life.” Midnight arrival: Ford, the snowplow driver, and the location manager “all rolled off the train completely drunk from having finished off this big bottle of vodka.” Watts: “When Harrison saw me, he said, ‘Call my agent.’ He was kidding me, but he knew that what we were doing was not standard practice …”

## RIDE ’EM, TAUNTAUN (pp. 111–112)

No. 2, Tuesday, March 6: Scs. 31–32 (Han arrives on tauntaun, finds Luke); second unit Sc. 8 (wampa attacks Luke). Ford “dazed” but on call 7:30 AM; Arnold finds him “urbane, self-assured, and charming.” Progress Report: “snow hitting camera lenses, plus unsuitable sun conditions.” Ford: “It was so cold that no equipment could be operated outside for more than three or four minutes. It all froze up. I kept asking myself how I got into the whole mess. But I couldn’t have left even if I’d wanted to—there was no way out.” Hamill in the snow begins to “shake involuntarily” within a minute. Two tauntauns: full body, or head/neck/back only with a technician inside; gas tubes for breath steam; wires for head and eyes. Weight of skin and head “really tiring” in the prone bent position (Tomkins).

## PSYCHOLOGY (p. 112)

No. 3, Wednesday, March 7: Scs. 10 (wampa drags Luke), 29 (Han rides tauntaun). Three principals on location create bonds. Arnold: a cockney carpenter checks Hamill taking a second glass of orange juice — “Equality is the rule.” Kershner’s “relaxed tension”: “You can’t let go of the tension, but you don’t want to feel tension.” Humor, icicles in the beard; Kurtz’s “tremendous endurance.”

## HOTEL NOWHERE (pp. 112–114)

Nos. 4–5, Thursday–Friday, March 8–9: Scs. 5, 7, 33 (Luke takes off goggles; Luke on tauntaun; Han saves Luke). Fisher returns to London. Four or five scenes “right outside the hotel”; if the camera turned around, “you’d have seen a big hotel behind you” (Bloom). First of 35 mountain-rescue skiers arrive as battle extras; donation to the Norwegian Red Cross. Caption: man-in-suit tauntaun “tried out on location in Finse, with hilarious if not film-worthy results.” Des Webb in the wampa costume “could walk no more than a few feet without taking what looked like a painful fall.” Early Beddoes boards: the monster “could tunnel through snow.”

## INVADER (pp. 115–116)

No. 6, Saturday, March 10: Scs. 210 (Luke with pilots, X-wing), 51 (Han and Chewie ambush probot). Weather clears only the second time; they reach Camp Sharman. Watts: “the only location we’d picked on the recce in January that we were able to shoot” — ice-slope matte of Luke walking toward his X-wing as the *Falcon* flies over. Probot on disguised tracks, radio-controlled rotation of body and head (Johnson). Painter Gordon Wright slips, breaks two ribs, sent to Voss. Kershner: “1,000-foot drops in the ice… I’ve seen men with snowshoes just disappear—*with snowshoes*—just drop away.” A *Sun* photographer in a British army helicopter buzzes the unit; Watts protests to the Voss army base. A “little old lady on skis” from a local paper is welcomed and introduced to the principals. Ford departs for London at 8 PM.

## THE STOIC (pp. 116–119)

No. 7, Sunday, March 11: Scs. 208 (Luke watches *Falcon* fly off), 158 and 156 (Luke pulls supplies from / emerges from crashed snowspeeder). First unit wraps 5:10 PM; Monday, most fly SK 513 back via Bergen; Hamill and Webb stay for second unit. Watts: “one break of clear weather for half a day. The rest of it was all a compromise.” As they board the train, “the snow was stopping and the sun was coming out.” Official tally: “SETUPS: 52; SCS. COMP: 7; SCREEN TIME: 7M 39S.” Friday, March 9 caption: Kershner films “Han warming Luke in the innards of a deceased tauntaun.” EE video: unused Finse probot daily (no audio specified as printed daily). EE audio: Hamill “discusses Luke’s character arc from film to film” (Arnold, 1979).
