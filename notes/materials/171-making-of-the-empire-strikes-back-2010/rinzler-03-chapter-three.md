# 171 — Chapter Three: The Art of Doing (July 1978 to February 1979)

Print pp. 64–103 (EPUB anchors `page64`–`page103`; print TOC opening p. 64). Rinzler ESB pp. 64–103. Staff/crew/cast same treatment as Lucas. Rinzler glosses flagged. The August 1978 Kasdan third draft and the October 24, 1978, Kasdan fourth draft are kept strictly separate from the Brackett first draft and the Lucas April 1, 1978, second draft (Chapter Two).

## Opening (p. 64)

July preproduction: ILM recruiting, third-draft work, walker prototype. Muren visits three times; Ken Ralston flies up with Muren and Berg, hears Lucas’s ranch plans (“This is Lucas Valley Road—no relationship to me”), jumps the fence to the empty dairy farm: “Sign me up!” Peterson prefers San Francisco to LA. Edlund: they moved “every damn thing out of ILM on Valjean… we called ourselves The Kerner Company because we didn’t want to have kids going through the trash looking for model shop sweepings. We were deities to a lot of people, so we had to keep a low profile.” Berg demonstrates walker-leg mechanics in his Silverlake shop.

## MYSTERY OF THE RASCAL SAGE (pp. 64–66)

July 19 deal memo: Kasdan paid $40,000 for the “rewrite” and $20,000 for the polish. After about 25 pages, a “hairiest” conference: Kasdan: “the way George works is that he never tells you what he likes, just what he doesn’t… His silence was my only reward.” Lucas: Kershner and Kasdan “challenged *everything* in the script… if they had a great idea or they had a point, then it immediately went into the script.” Kasdan on the first fifth: “You have to establish the dynamics of the romance that follows… We don’t know what the relationships are between Leia, Han, and Luke.” Lucas: “I had a rudimentary version of Yoda… You can speak and act using Larry’s dialogue for Yoda. He mainly made the dialogue better and the characters more consistent.” Kasdan: “Boba Fett is a real villain. Lando starts out a villain and develops… a very strong, positive alien [Yoda].” Kershner first thought Yoda “should be eight or nine feet tall with a big beard, like an oversized Moses… a Zen master, almost god-like. But that was too much of a cliché.”

Visual responsibility moves from Johnston to McQuarrie. Lucas: “about four months before coming up with a design we liked.” McQuarrie found Johnston’s sketches “kind of puffy, lacking in bone structure” and wanted “more of an ascetic look.” Practical options: Muppet (“How are you going to shoot a close-up of a Muppet? It’s just a piece of cloth and Ping-Pong balls”); “a monkey dressed up in a costume” (Freeborn’s *2001* baby-monkey stories; Kershner: “Can you train a monkey? No, you can’t train a monkey”); stop-motion (Lucas: “can you get the face…? It’s impossible. We wanted a combination of Kermit and the skeletons from an old Ray Harryhausen movie”); a midget; a child. “We were ready to give up!” Kasdan: “Yoda is the lead samurai from *Seven Samurai*… He’s the mentor figure who gets the whole picture.” Caption: Johnston “Minch Muppet” measurement, 18 inches, July 1978; a monkey is outfitted with cane and mask.

## INDUSTRIAL STRENGTH (pp. 67–69)

August 4: title officially released. Hamill: “I love the title of *The Empire Strikes Back*. It’s got that gaudy Saturday-afternoon quality that’s important for us.” Burtt: “It seemed like such an extreme-sounding pulp title. But that’s what we were making: a big version of those old serials.” Watts returns to Finse; ILM bank loan discussion: $180,000 construction, $50,000 optical lenses, $27,000 high-speed VistaVision camera; “nearly $1 million.” Payroll starts: Rodis-Jamero August 7; Tippett August 8; Edlund and Muren August 16; Peterson August 17; Bruce Nicholson August 16; Huston and Gawley September 5. Bloom on esprit de corps; Tippett: “kids in a candy shop.” Berg’s appearance: “Hell’s Angels biker,” then “pussycat.” ILM still under construction: “There was no electricity in the building” (Muren). Gawley drives trucks of equipment up Highway 5; *More American Graffiti* occupies one bay.

## KING TAUNTAUN (pp. 69–70)

Tippett takes over tauntaun design: “This isn’t going to work” (Johnston). Lucas decides against a man-in-a-suit for the creature shots. Tippett: “the first time probably since *King Kong* [1933], stop-motion animation was actually being used in a big-budget motion picture.” Only an old Dykstraflex in mothballs. Muren: “The task of delivering *Empire* in two years just seemed insurmountable.” Fake wall built overnight to hide unpermitted construction from a building inspector. Lucas “lends a hand and a hammer.”

## THE ROAD TO OZ (pp. 70–73)

Early August: Kasdan delivers the third draft; McQuarrie reads it August 10; script meeting August 18. Kazanjian: “if it had gone to the Writers Guild for arbitration, I don’t think Leigh Brackett would’ve gotten a story credit. I said that to George and he said, you know, from his heart he felt she should get a co-writing credit on it, so he gave that to her.” Lucas: “I didn’t like the first script, but I gave Leigh credit because I liked her a lot. She was sick at the time she wrote it and she really tried her best.” Kasdan tightens and expands dialogue; Yoda’s “Try not. Do.” Lucas: “The philosophy of doing, putting your all into something, instead of just trying, is a philosophy that goes through all of my movies.” Asteroid-cave line: Han, “I think you like me because I’m a scoundrel. I think you haven’t run into enough scoundrels in your life.” Vader moved “from the deck of a Star Destroyer into his ‘private cubicle.’” Insert “A”: Vader cuts “Luke’s arm off at the elbow!” Insert “C,” end of story: “Luke’s lower left arm is exposed, revealing metal struts and electronic circuits, similar to Threepio.”

Lucas asks Jim Henson about a realistic puppet; Henson, busy on *The Muppet Movie*, recommends Frank Oz. Oz: “Jim called me into his trailer… showed me a sketch of Yoda—and it felt right.” Kurtz rejects monkey and marionette; meets Henson and Oz in New York; measurements of Oz’s hand. Lucas planned to dub Yoda in post “as he had for Darth Vader,” hiring Oz for puppeteering only; Oz signed on to learn how they made movies, research for *The Dark Crystal*. “a new kind of mechanical puppet.” Kershner: “I wanted control of the face, the emotions, the body.” Brian Johnson: “Yoda is going to require a lot of hard work… to become believable.” Storyboards of the Hoth battle “including the death of General Veers” (cut later; flagged already in the EE List of Enhancements).

## Third-draft summary — Kasdan, August 1978 (pp. 74–79)

Rinzler ESB pp. 74–79. 130 typed pages. Roll-up and opening “essentially the same as in the second draft,” with new relationship beats. Leia’s first words: “You’re leaving?” Han: “Well, don’t get all mushy on me. So long, Princess.” After the ice creature, Luke may also be going: Leia, “That’s just great. Why doesn’t everyone take off?” Medcenter needle prompts Leia to kiss Luke. Modified exchange:

LEIA: “Why you low-down, stuck up, half-witted, scruffy-looking nerf-herder.” [Previous draft: “self-centered Beacon Eater.”]
HAN: “Who’s scruffy-looking?”

After Rieekan’s death, Leia pauses in grief; C-3PO urges her on. *Falcon* stalls: “Would it help if I got out and pushed?” Asteroid field: “You don’t have to do this to impress me.” Vader has “his own Star Destroyer with a personal chamber.”

Yoda meeting: “There’s something familiar about this place…” “Away put your weapon. I mean you no harm.” “Wars don’t make one great.” “I have trained Jedi for 800 years… Adventure, excitement. A Jedi craves not these things.” Ben: “He deserves a chance. And we need him.” Yoda: “A mistake I am making. Obi-Wan has been wrong before.” “Forget you must all your old measures. Unlearn, unlearn” (Lucas: “You must unlearn all …”). “Judge me by my size do you?” Side-by-side Lucas/Kasdan Force lines: Lucas, “Not material are we. Luminous beings are we, tied together by the Force … Yes. There are two of you … your body and your energy.” Kasdan: “Luminous beings are we … (Yoda pinches Luke’s skin) Not this crude matter.” “Try not. *Do, do*.” “That is why you fail.” Luke: “Come on, you little flying bastards!” Yoda: “Anger, fear, aggression! The dark side of the Force are they.” Dark-side-stronger beat, Lucas vs. Kasdan: Lucas, “Not stronger, aggressive. The attacker hateful. Tempted you will be, by the easy way …”; Kasdan, “No, no. Easier, quicker, more seductive.”

Boba Fett’s “weird starship” appears as the *Falcon* drifts with the garbage. Cave tree: Luke “now actually enters with his lightsaber—and fights Vader, whose ‘decapitated head fades away, as in a vision.’” Lando: “Why you slimy, double-crossing no-good swindler … am I glad to see you.” “It’s the fastest hunk of junk in the galaxy.” Lucas note: “Was Lando’s ship?” Yoda may think it “wiser to sacrifice Han and Leia for the greater good.” Lando: “This deal’s getting worse all the time.” “I’ve got a bad feeling about this.” PA announcement to the city (Lucas cut): “Sorry it had to end this way, my people! So long!” Luke now communicates telepathically with Leia “without Ben’s intervention.” Ending: “They’ll find Han. I know they will … I’d go with them. But I have a promise to keep.” McQuarrie “Freezing plant” and carbon-freeze / “Sword fight on antenna” paintings. The summary does not re-quote the father-reveal; do not invent a change here.

## BLACK FALCON SOARS (p. 80)

September 1978: theatrical *Star Wars* ends except the Westgate, Portland (76 continuous weeks into November). ABC begins *Battlestar Galactica*. Black Falcon loans “more than $400,000” to Chapter II and “over $100,000” to ILM, plus more. Two-year projection: ILM ~$400,000/month; principal photography “over $2 million per month”; Chapter II monthly deficit “between $5 million and $25 million by the end of 1979.” “If *Empire* didn’t perform at the box office, the company would definitely be ruined.” October 17: contract between American Chapter II and Chapter II Productions Limited (England). Kazanjian on Lucas financing an “$18 million film” and the ranch (no bank would lend; if bankrupt “the only thing the ranch would’ve been good for would’ve been a Catholic school or a monastery”). Lucas: “The only reason we’re continuing to do commercial films at all is to be able to finance the ranch.”

## ACTION ON A FROZEN WORLD (pp. 80–82)

October 13: “New York City Landos” screen tests — Howard E. Rollins, Terry Alexander, Robert Christian, Thurman Scott. October 24: Kasdan finishes the fourth draft. Kershner: “Larry contributed, George contributed, I contributed, but it’s a very, very complex thing.” Kasdan: “It was a constant battle between character and action… George has to be dragged toward that moment of respite, but he will do it.” Script 163 pages vs. third draft’s 130, “thanks to complicated action scenes that had been storyboarded and then described in words… not significantly changed from the previous except in hundreds of subtle ways.”

## Fourth-draft summary — Kasdan, October 24, 1978 (pp. 81–82)

Rinzler ESB pp. 81–82. Shot-by-shot breakdown; new aerial “helicopter” plates in the Hoth opening. Luke has no dialogue with Han before “a wampa belts him (an early name for the snow monster was ‘penocha’).” A wampa appears from the hangar wall and chases R2. Ion cannon on Star Destroyers for the Rebel escape. On Dagobah, “the Creature speaks to Ben, triggering Luke’s realization” (instead of a gradual awareness). Yoda’s pipe becomes a “Gimer Stick.” After Luke leaves: instead of Yoda saying “We must find another” (Lucas had crossed out “find” and written “search for”), “now the small Jedi Master says, ‘There is another.’” Caption of Lucas’s revisions to that Yoda/Ben exchange. Keep “There is another” in this draft distinct from later filmed wording and from the conference’s lost-sister talk.

Caption/EE video, typography locked: first public appearance of Boba Fett “in the company of Darth Vader” at the San Anselmo Country Fair Parade, Sunday, September 24, 1978; Duwayne Dunham in the Fett costume; “The man inside the Vader costume would need a saline solution after collapsing in 94-degree heat.” Johnston painted the armor “like he had scavenged parts… little braids of hair, almost like a collection of scalps.” Bruce Nicholson posed in it for photos.

## ELEGANCE AND PLANNING (pp. 83–85)

Chapter II Productions Ltd. opens at Elstree. Late October: John Mollo back, “with a script and some clothes left over from the original space fantasy” (Arnold, now unit publicist; Lippincott left in June). Stormtrooper armor “stronger materials.” Kershner arrives London October 28 (Davidoff Havana cigar, Peter Noble column). McQuarrie October 29–30, reads the fourth draft; begins reworking boards with Ivor Beddoes (start date July 3; *Red Shoes* mattes; *Superman*; “started in the business as a song-and-dance man”). Johnson full-time in November after *Alien*. Star Wars Stage: 250 × 122 × 45 feet, “overall capacity in excess of one and a quarter million cubic feet.” November 9: Mayhew and Prowse legalized. Prowse: “They told me that if I dawdled any longer, they would simply get someone else to play the role, so I signed… Having no real identity in the films gives you a terrible feeling of insecurity. You are always aware that you are dispensable.” Hirsch hired to start England March 5, 1979, to assemble as they shoot. Kershner inspects Finse in early winter, helicopter to the glacier “6,000 feet up… 20-degrees-below”; “I went to Finse and it was perfect.” Huey ignition trouble; they walk back around the lake.

## REVENGE OF THE BOOB TUBE (pp. 85–87)

November 15 Gallegly letter: Edlund full time, construction “coming to a conclusion.” Gene Whiteman, Conrad Buff hired. MCA/Universal countersue Fox in November; Nicholson: *Galactica* “had done a show which was based on a snow planet. So George was upset that they had ripped him off.” November 17: *Star Wars Holiday Special* on CBS — “how Black Falcon and Lucasfilm had yet to hone their approval processes”; “The one bright spot… an animated segment produced by Nelvana Studios that introduced bounty hunter Boba Fett to the United States.” Same day: Mickey Mouse 50th with Chewbacca and R2 (party hat). Thanksgiving turkey tradition begins from Jane Bay’s 1977 staff dinner; “from ’78, we have had this tradition of George giving a Thanksgiving turkey to each of his employees.”

## ESOTERIC MODELS (pp. 87–88)

Miki Herman starts weekly ILM notes, November. December 8: Muren decides to shoot asteroid explosions live, then rephotograph in roto — “soon became a staple at ILM.” Roto shop under Peter Kuran opens January 1 (Adam Beckett “had died in a fire in the interim”). Model shop must build a new “Star Destroyer 11,000 feet long; Darth’s [new] ship is 16 miles.” Kurtz had told Peterson the first film’s models were already packed: “Well, it didn’t work out that way at all!” Lucas: “It’s important that this film have the quality of the first one so it doesn’t look as though we are skimping… That’s the challenge.” Muren: “make the work better for the same price or less as the first film.” Tauntaun hair: “calves that were mistakenly in the womb of the mother cow when she was slaughtered”; Tippett sculpts a 12-inch mock-up; England builds an eight-foot close-up model. Walker look “more or less left up to” Johnston once McQuarrie is in England.

## ROGUE LEADER (pp. 88–89)

Billy Dee Williams contract December 15. An early Lucas story note: “Actor—Billy Dee Williams—Cloud City leader.” Kershner: “really looks like a Mississippi riverboat hustler. Billy can do that charm fantastically.” *Jet*: “how do you turn down *Star Wars*?” *The Oakland Post*: Williams had “qualms” about a “token role”; Sid Ganis “vehemently denied charges of tokenism claiming that they were merely looking for a romantic hero… it wasn’t written for a black actor.” Williams: he and Kershner talked Zen; Kershner “wanna introduce some Zen here, because I don’t want the kids to walk away just feeling that everything is shoot-’em-up.”

## HIDDEN BONANZA (pp. 89–90)

December 22 internal budget: $21,473,328, “up from $18 million and now doubling the original $10 million,” set construction “more than $3.5 million.” January 1979 payroll 350; HQ moves from Park House to Ancho Vista; Park House becomes Lucas’s home. Kenner “around $100 million” the previous year; Vader “piggy banks and pencil sharpeners” among the merch list. Ferguson on building an internal legal department. Licensing rules (Weber): “nothing to do with alcohol… we didn’t do any vitamins. We didn’t want to do anything that promoted pill taking.” Ganis hired via Coppola’s recommendation, starts January 3. Attic toy hoard (Bay, Bloom, Starkey, Artoo cookie jars). By March 1979 toy sales “passed $200 million”; Kenner and Random House deals timed to *Empire*’s release.

## MODERN LIGHTS (pp. 90–91)

ILM: 33 people by January 1979; Ralston and Tom St. Amand midmonth. “Vader ship” assigned to a trio of model makers. Johnston: within six weeks they needed a second floor. Patricia Blau starts January 8 after Gallegly fires two women over a joke purchase order (furs, jewelry, drugs). Bloom: Watts UK group, Bloom American group. Lucas publicly: “Fortunately, on the special effects side, we’re very secure.” Only Dykstraflex and old Technirama at first; motion-control cameras; high-speed camera; “ILM Empire” VistaVision reflex for Norway, heaters for 30 below. Ralston: “When we found out we had to do snow shots in bright daylight, we were petrified!” Photography start February 12.

## ALBION ADVENTURES (pp. 92–93)

Elstree offices in high gear after the holidays. Oslo office January 16; Ratrac January 17. January 21: James Earl Jones confirms return; agent Lucy Kroll, “He does not wish to receive billing” — Rinzler flags this as “an odd request, given that the actor had already been publicized as Vader’s voice during the *Star Wars Holiday Special*.” Base salary “doubled from the first film to $15,000 for half a day’s work, plus a small percentage of the gross.” Peter Suschitzky signs; he had been approached about photographing the original *Star Wars*. Full-sized tauntaun: costume version and mechanical (Johnson, Freeborn, Bruton). McQuarrie: Norman Reynolds takes over Cloud City interiors, making McQuarrie’s “rounded Jell-O mold forms” “a little more rigid, more crystalline.” Kershner on his knees for hours over miniature sets. Reynolds “Darth’s Chamber” and “Insect Robot”; McQuarrie, January 1979: “These are thoughts inside Vader’s head.” Painting of “Han as prisoner on Cloud City.”

## NASCENT CLAY (pp. 93–94)

Freeborn on Yoda: Lucas needed to see a design that afternoon; Freeborn modifies a sculpture of his own head, “added ridges… as George had described the character as very wise.” “A lot of people say Yoda looks like Stuart Freeborn.” Lucas: “Yes! That’s it.” “I also gave the little fellow Einstein’s eyes… I wanted to give him a little mustache… so I compromised by creating the shape of a mustache on his bottom lip. It’s very subtle, but it’s there.” Wendy Midener from Henson’s group; Oz, Kathy Mullen. EE audio: Oz on the puppet (Arnold, 1979). McQuarrie on Dagobah: “giant petrified trees… They tend to look like big insect legs.” Kershner: a full year of LA preproduction; “it would have been nice to have *two* years.”

## BUILDING A UFO (pp. 94–95)

Ogle Design (Reliant *Scimitar*; had built Luke’s landspeeder) does snowspeeders, gun turrets, gantry control. Full-sized *Falcon* needed — first film’s was “a half-size prop built into a wall.” About 65 feet diameter, 80 feet with the mandible, 23 tons, 16 pie sections, hoverpads. Marcon Fabrications, Pembroke Docks. Mr. Wilson (archive tapes, January 1979 interview): locals think “Marcon is constructing a spaceship”; UFOs “generally about.” Arnold: “I suppose it’s possible that eventually spaceships really might be made here.”

## UP IN FLAMES (pp. 95–100)

White Horse Toy Company / Tony Dyson / Andrew Kelly: five or eight improved R2 units (Johnson: radio-controlled honeycomb, 32 pounds); Baker’s second fitting, more movement, less heat. Johnson shows Kurtz two-to-three-leg conversion; Kurtz “still very skeptical,” then surprised. January 24, 1979: Stage 3 burns during Kubrick’s *The Shining*; fire trucks blocked by cluttered driveways; “by morning the stage was a smoldering ruin.” Arnold: “virtually two soundstages were denied to the *Star Wars* team. Time-wise, it is doubtful the picture will recover.” Watts: already worried because Kubrick was over schedule and “the worst winter since 1963”; Star Wars Stage behind; entire building and shooting schedule reassessment. Lucas: “We also knew that Stanley would use that as a way of further delaying things so he could think about his film.” Watts: a Han Solo blizzard scene will go to Leeds Studios “because we have insufficient space at EMI.” Beddoes boards of the Luke vs. Vader duel: “originally, the reactor shaft had a noticeable updraft.” Fett’s ship: Rodis-Jamero from a radar dish; “George thought it was elliptical, so that’s what it became.”

## TECHNICAL TRIALS (pp. 100–102)

January 26: parts of ILM still need heat. Other effects films floundering (*Star Trek: The Motion Picture*, *Meteor*). February 5: Muren films an elephant at Marine World ($3,467.54) — “I believe it was the same elephant that was used as a bantha,” Mardji — as walker locomotion reference; horse and rider on Stinson Beach for the tauntaun gait (Suzanne Pasteur, March 8 caption). Two-foot *Falcon* (“half-sized pirate ship”) because the four-foot original was “too heavy.” Photography of asteroid sequence and “the Vader ship” begins February 1979 on Dykstraflex and Technirama. VistaVision camera christened the “ILM Empire.” Revised storyboards of the opening, “now ‘Chapter V,’” by Johnston, early 1979.

## FEROCIOUS FINSE (pp. 102–103)

January 25 recce to Finse the day after the fire. Ten containers by sea Felixstowe–Oslo–train to Finse; three flown London–Bergen; two VistaVision cameras (one “Empire flex”), Panavision, Arriflex, wind machines, props. Alan Tomkins flown out when Les Dilley falls sick: “Crikey, I never realized this amount of snow existed!” Johnson’s acetylene-oxygen gas-gun flashes as laser guides. Paint freezes in the pots. February 20: helicopter permission — first aerial filming permission Norway granted to a foreign-registered aircraft (Ronald C. Goodman, Wesscam on an Aerospatiale Lama; *Superman* flying-scenes crew). Construction crew February 25; trenches February 28. Chapter ends with McQuarrie concepts for “Boba Fett’s Gang,” swamp creatures, and “Luke’s encounter with dream Vader” inside the tree, early 1979.
