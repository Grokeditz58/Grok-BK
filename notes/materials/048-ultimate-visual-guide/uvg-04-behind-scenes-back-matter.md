# Star Wars: The Ultimate Visual Guide (DK, 2005): Behind the Scenes, Expanding the Universe, Timeline, Index, Acknowledgments, back cover (pp. 112–144 + covers), hand-read notes

Ryder Windham, *Star Wars: The Ultimate Visual Guide* (DK, First American Edition 2005; ISBN 0-7566-1420-1; foreword by Ian McDiarmid; Lucasfilm continuity supervisor Leland Chee). This is an official licensed DK/Lucas Books title.
Citation convention: "UVG p. NN" is the printed page number. Text in quotation marks is verbatim from the page. [KEY] marks power-scaling or lore-critical passages, and [sic] marks printing errors exactly as printed.

- **p112-113** BEHIND THE SCENES (section opener): Lucas quote: "Normally, a director is concerned mainly with character and with telling the story. In the Star Wars films, that is important, but equally important are all the details. They're like little time bombs all over the set, thousands of them, and if you don't catch one, it could do you in." And: "I took something that was not very well regarded, a kind of esoteric, technical-cult enthusiasm, and recognized it for the art it is and gave it a showcase where people could really see and appreciate the artistry." Photos: TPM Mos Espa set, McQuarrie painting, AT-AT stop-motion.
- **p114-115** George Lucas: "Born in Modesto, California, in 1944"; he "began to wonder what would happen if [the visual effects] were done really well. Surely, kids would love them even more." Hidden Fortress; ILM. Selected filmography 1965 Look at Life → 2005 ROTS. Learning from experience: THX 1138 vs American Graffiti: "I realized it works a lot better to make a positive, spiritually uplifting film." Difficult shoot (Tunisia 1976). Ralph McQuarrie: Star Wars "might never have been made" without him; caption: "At his audition for C-3PO, actor Anthony Daniels was reluctant to play a robot until he saw this painting and felt empathy for the characters." LUCAS'S ALTER EGO: Luke from "two opposites in myself—a naive, innocent idealism and a view of the world that is cynical, more pessimistic." Rick McCallum; Lucas cameo "as Baron Papanoida."
- **p116-117** The Star Wars Saga: script begun 1972; "developed into an epic on the scale of War and Peace, so big I couldn't possibly make it into a movie. So I cut it in half, but it was still too big, so I cut each half into three parts. I then had material for six movies." "The saga, originally the adventures of Luke Skywalker, finally evolved into the story of Darth Vader." EP IV: "In Lucas's original script, Obi-Wan Kenobi (Alec Guinness) survived his duel with Darth Vader. Preproduction was already underway when Lucas decided to kill Obi-Wan, whose death dramatically increased the threat of both Vader and the Death Star." EP V: Kershner "the second movement of a symphony". Three directors. EP VI: Kasdan 2000: "Jedi was a much tougher movie to pull off"; Hamill "it was the end". Reinventing Jabba (Declan Mulholland). EP I: script begun "November 1, 1994"; "Directing is like a war". EP II: 1979 prophecy on video; "first major feature shot entirely on high-definition videotape". EP III (2005 interview) [KEY]: "In IV, people didn't know whether Vader was a robot or a monster, or if there was anybody in there. This way, when you see him walk into the spaceship in Episode IV, you're going to say, 'Oh my God, that's Anakin. The poor guy is still stuck in that suit.' So the tension and drama is completely reversed." Skywalker Ranch 1,700 → 6,500 acres.
- **p118-119** Designing the Galaxy: McQuarrie: Lucas "wanted to get what he could see in his mind onto the screen". CONCEPT ART: Vader "a tall, black, majestic figure with fluttering robes, possibly wearing an exotic helmet, like a Japanese warrior, with a black silk scarf across his face ... Vader first appeared when he jumped from one spaceship to another, so Lucas agreed he should wear breathing apparatus. 'George liked the mask that I did for Vader, with the big goggles and so he said, "That's great, that's fine," and we just left it at that.'" John Mollo: actor "wouldn't have to go around all day in the whole caboodle." Developing characters: Chewbacca had fangs; C-3PO from Metropolis; lightsaber-wielding hero resembles Lucas. EXPANDED CONCEPTS [KEY]: Dermot Power's "vampiric, samurai-like female character. 'I deliberately curved the Sith's lightsaber,' Power says. 'I wanted something exotic, almost Arabic.' Power's sketches were temporarily shelved when the veteran actor Christopher Lee signed on to play Count Dooku, but the art was later utilized for Asajj Ventress". Caption: Ventress debuted in Dark Horse "Schism". Production paintings; Storyboards (Benton Jew); Animatics (Dan Gregoire; ROTS); Costume design (Iain McCaig, Trisha Biggar; Theed "clothing of paradise"; Amidala: Pre-Raphaelite + art nouveau + Tibetan and Mongolian vestments). Natividad Coruscant monorail.
- **p120-121** Visual Effects: ILM "groundbreaking work for over 125 features"; Lucas reluctant "unless I had the technology available to really tell the kind of story I was interested in telling." Jurassic Park: "With CG at my disposal, I knew I could do whatever I wanted." Matte paintings (Cloud City). Motion-control: Dykstra, Dykstraflex. LIGHTSABER EVOLUTION: "Spinning wooden 'blades' ... coated with material that reflected the set's lights, then photographed through a half-silvered mirror ... enhanced by rotoscoping. In the second trilogy ... metal blades, and were illuminated by CG effects". CG: Kamino corridor. Sound design (Burtt; Matthew Wood). Musical score: Williams, "87-piece London Symphony Orchestra in March 1977"; "late-nineteenth-century, romantic orchestral scores".
- **p122-123** Model-Making: Steve Gawley with ILM since 1975, "more than 40 films"; Grant McCune. Stop-motion AT-ATs (Jon Berg, Tom St. Amand; Joe Johnston sketches). HAND PUPPETS: Stuart Freeborn sculpted Yoda's foam latex puppet; "brought to life by puppeteer Frank Oz, who also supplied Yoda's voice." Concept models: Michael Patrick Murnane, Zam Wesell maquette in Sculpey ("when you hand over a 3-D model, you don't have to explain much"). Plastic models: TIE interceptor model by Joe Johnston/Lary Tan, "about 61 centimeters (2 feet) across". Full-scale landspeeder (three-wheeled; Death Valley). Rod puppet: rancor by Phil Tippett, "61-centimeter (2 feet) tall ... controlled by three puppeteers." Set models (paper & cardboard Yavin hangar). MINIATURES: "Revenge of the Sith features the largest miniature ever built for a Star Wars film—the volcanic planet Mustafar"; methycel for lava; Nick D'Abo.
- **p124-125** Movie Posters: Struzan: "Photography is a direct reflection of reality. Art is interpretive, therefore it embodies more emotion and feeling." Style "A" by Tom Jung; Debbie Reynolds asked for a duplicate; Jung painted Thrawn trilogy covers. Kastel's Empire "A" evokes Gone with the Wind; Jedi Style "B" by Kazuhiko Sano. TITLE CHANGE [KEY]: "Lucas—believing a true Jedi would never seek revenge—changed the film's title just before its release. 'The title was always intended to be Return of the Jedi,' Lucas says. 'We'd had so many difficulties with people trying to report stuff to the media that we called the film Revenge of the Jedi to throw people off.'" Teaser posters: TPM teaser November 1998 ("spectral, ominous shadow"). AOTC teaser theme "forbidden love"; ROTS teaser fall 2004. COVERT OPERATIONS: Blue Harvest "Horror Beyond Imagination"; Kazanjian: ploy "worked out exceptionally well." Foreign posters: Witold Dybowski (Polish Jedi) "could only assume it was a death of huge proportions."
- **p126-127** Expanding the Universe (section opener): Lucas: "It certainly wasn't something anybody predicted, especially us. The first toys didn't come out until a year after the first film came out ... I enjoy toys, and I make the films to stimulate the imaginations of the audience". Art: Ewoks, Maul, ROTS video-game screenshot (Anakin vs Dooku), an uncaptioned game Sith figure.
- **p128-129** Merchandise: Lucas "negotiated away from 20th Century Fox the merchandising rights"; Wookiee mug idea: "wouldn't it be fun to have a Wookiee mug?" inspired by dog Indiana, "sort of a prototype for the Wookiee". Kenner "136 Star Wars action figures and sold more than 250 million units between 1978 and 1985"; Hasbro resumed 1995. Planet of the Hoojibs (1983 Buena Vista, from Marvel #55). LEGO 1998 deal, "the first property ever licensed by LEGO"; 36-cm Yoda; Ultimate Collector Series Yoda "1,078 pieces". Board games (Destroy Death Star Game 1977; Monopoly Saga Edition 2005). Chewbacca mug by Jim Rumpf, California Originals 1977–78. Master Replicas Jedi Training Remote. Revenge of the Spud: Darth Tater 2005.
- **p130-131** Continuing the Saga [KEY]: 1979 Lucas "after the success of Star Wars [Episode IV] I added another trilogy" and "story treatments on all nine" films, "but his more recent revelations contradict this. As guest of honour at the Star Wars: Celebration III convention in 2005, Lucas admitted to an audience that 'to be very honest with you, I never ever thought of anything that happened beyond Episode VI. It's the Darth Vader story.'" Announced 3-D animated Clone Wars and live-action series "between the events of Episode II ... and Episode III". Radio dramas (Brian Daley, NPR 1981, John Madden). HOLIDAY SPECIAL: Nov 17, 1978, CBS; animated cartoon introduced Boba Fett; "widely regarded as regrettable ... never aired again or officially released on video (although bootlegs of it do exist), so it has now assumed cult status." Ewok Adventures (1984/1985; "Lucasfilm-approved timelines place both stories before the events of Return of the Jedi"; Thomas G. Smith: walker toy "the Imperials had been there"; Emmy for effects; DVD 2005). Towani family; Teek (Niki Botelho). More Ewoks (Nelvana; Kneesaa). ANIMATED DROIDS: 13 episodes + The Great Heep; Vlix; Boonta Speeder Races; "Droids is set 15 years before A New Hope." Clone Wars 2003, Genndy Tartakovsky, Emmy; introduced Grievous before Ep III.
- **p132-133** Star Wars Publishing: Nov 1976 novelization "credited to the director but ghostwritten by Alan Dean Foster"; half-million sold by Feb 1977. "In 1978, Foster's Splinter of the Mind's Eye became the first official Star Wars 'expanded universe' novel". Favorite Vader (McQuarrie cover: "George looked at the helmet and said it never looked better!"). MARVEL COMICS: Roy Thomas; Chaykin, Leialoha; thin bipedal Jabba in #2: "Who knew there would be sequels or Special Editions?" Saga continues: Heir to the Empire (Bantam, 1991) "introduced Mara Jade and Grand Admiral Thrawn". Brian Daley Han Solo novels. Rinzler's Art of ROTS foreword quote. Stover's ROTS novelization "features new sequences that expand Anakin's fall to the dark side, inspired by a meeting with Lucas". Dark Horse: Mike Richardson 1986, SW comics since 1991; "over 100 collected editions"; Brian Ching Republic #67 (2004).
- **p134-135** Video Games: Hayden Christensen: "[my brother and I] used to lock ourselves in my bedroom and relay the controller back and forth until we became Jedi Knights ... I used to have dreams that I was in the Star Wars game." ESB (Parker Bros 1982): "hit each walker 48 times." X-Wing (1993–94). KOTOR (LucasArts/BioWare 2003): "set four millennia before Episode IV ... Korriban from the Tales of the Jedi comics". THE SITH LORDS (Obsidian 2004): "picks up five years into the civil war that has devastated the Jedi. It also introduces yet another nightmare-inducing Sith Lord, Darth Nihilus" [NB: KOTOR II is set five years AFTER KOTOR I's end; the "five years into the civil war" wording is loose — flag]. Battlefront (Pandemic 2004). Republic Commando ("One Touch Squad Control", 16 players online). Jump to Lightspeed (SWG expansion). ROTS game: "an amazing alternate ending if you defeat Obi-Wan on Mustafar."
- **p136-137** STAR WARS TIMELINE (real-world): Lucas at Grauman's: "I said, 'My God, what's going on here? It must be a premiere or something.' I looked at the marquee, and it was Star Wars." 1971 Lucasfilm incorporated; 1972 notes for first of four drafts; 1973 Aug 20 turns down higher salary in exchange for sequel, merchandising, publishing rights; 1975 ILM; Dykstraflex; Sprocket Systems; 1976 renamed "Star Wars"; Jan 15 fourth draft; Mar 22 Tunisia; Jul 16 Elstree; Dec novelization; 1977 Mar Williams/LSO; May 1 Northpoint test screening; May 25 opens at 32 theaters, 350 by mid-June; 1978 Apr 3 "seven Academy Awards"; Nov 17 Holiday Special. 1979 Computer Division; Sprocket → Skywalker Sound; "March 5: Episode V: The Empire Stikes [sic] Back begins principal photography in Finse, Norway" (alt crop verified); Sep 24 Elstree. 1980 May 21 ESB; two Academy Awards. 1981 Pixar Image Computer, EditDroid, SoundDroid; Go-Motion; Feb 9 syndicated strip; Sep 5 NPR radio drama. 1982 Games Division; Jan 11 Revenge of the Jedi photography at Pinewood "with a sandstorm scene that is ultimately cut"; June ANH on video. Caption: Guinness's 62nd birthday in Tunisia, April 2, 1976.
- **p138-139** Timeline cont.: 1983 Jan 27 title changed to Return of the Jedi; Feb 17–18 ESB radio drama (10 eps); May 25 ROTJ, one Academy Award. 1984 Mar 11 strip ends; Nov 25 Caravan of Courage (Emmy). 1985 Pixar Image Computer; Sep 7 Ewoks & Droids Adventure Hour; Nov 24 Battle for Endor (Emmy). 1986 Pixar sold "to Steve Jobs"; Feb 25 ROTJ video; Nov 1 Ewoks S2. 1987 Jan 9 Star Tours. 1994 Nov 1 first draft of Ep I. 1995 ILM tech award; Aug 29 THX trilogy video. 1996 Oct 19 ROTJ radio drama (six-episode). 1997 Jan 18 Special Edition premiere (Mann Village/Bruin); Jan 31 release. Chewbacca MTV Lifetime Achievement 1997. 1998 Jun 26–Sep 26 TPM filming at Leavesden. 1999 Apr 30–May 2 Celebration, Denver; May 19 TPM; "June 18: The Phantom Menace becomes the first major full-length motion picture to be publicly screened using digital electronic projectors." 2000 Dolby Digital Surround EX; Jun 26 AOTC filming Sydney; Sep 20 wrap at Elstree—"first major motion picture to be shot completely on digital". 2001 ILM awards; Oct 16 TPM DVD. 2002 May 16 AOTC; August Lucas begins Ep III script; Nov 12 AOTC DVD. 2003 Jun 30–Sep 17 Ep III photography Sydney. 2004 Sep 21 trilogy DVD. 2005 Mar 22 Clone Wars DVD; "May 19: The final Star Wars film in the six-part saga, Episode III: Revenge of the Sith, has its worldwide theatrical release." Caption: "the Star Wars timeline is all set to continue beyond the release of Episode III. Stay tuned!" Yoda caption: "When nine hundred years old you reach, look as good you will not. Hmm?"
