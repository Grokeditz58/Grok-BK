# 170 summary — Rinzler, *The Making of Star Wars* (2007)

Full typography-faithful notes on J. W. Rinzler’s making-of, built from Charles Lippincott’s 1975–1978 “Lost Interviews” plus production paperwork, with a smaller set of later interviews (Jones, Cushing, and some 2000s extras) flagged as such.

The book follows *Star Wars* from early 1973 treatments through the 25 May 1977 opening and the remainder of 1977. Draft identities are kept separate: Vader as a tall grim-looking general (May 1974) versus a seven-foot Sith knight (January 1975) versus half-man/half-machine (January 1976); third-draft “You were once my master” and a wounded-Ben escape versus fourth-draft Beddoes boards in which Kenobi traps Vader behind a blast door and is helped to the ship, versus the filmed death; Han as an alien Ureallian versus a young Corellian pirate in debt first to Oxus, then to Jabba (ship construction vs. a dropped spice shipment). Casting, Elstree/Shepperton/Tunisia production, the Jympson dismissal, the front-projection failure and bluescreen switch, the white-corridor opening, Jones’s 1 March 1977 session ($7,500; Welles contacted first; “narrow band of expression”; Aqua-Lung / iron-lung breathing, “a whole part of the plot that essentially got cut out”), the Northpoint preview and Metro dud, the requested PG, and the merchandising/sequel-rights deal are locked in chapter order.

Enhanced-only “George Lucas Expands His Universe” (Lucas speaking as C-3PO, 28 July 1977; Leia, 5 August; Han, 11 August) is first-thought worldbuilding, not official continuity: Vader still a Jedi who is trusted and who decimates the Order; Luke’s father “named Annikin”; no Death Star readouts for Vader; midi-chlorians already named in 1977; Han’s gypsy / Wookiee / Academy / coldppedas / Saberhing / “free-enterprise small businessman” biography; *Falcon* “of Corellian design, from Crell.” “Star Wars Progression” codes first appearances across nine draft stages.
