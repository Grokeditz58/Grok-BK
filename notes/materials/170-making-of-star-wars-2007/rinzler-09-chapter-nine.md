Logistically, the Obi-Wan/Vader fight is filmed first, three days beginning **27 May**, before their meeting on **1 June**. Stunt coordinator **Peter Diamond**, typography locked: “George said, ‘I’ve got these laser swords—I don’t want broadswords and I don’t want fencing. I want it somewhere in between.’ So I had to create a style that was unique.” He trains Prowse and Guinness; shoot goes slowly. “It’s a natural tendency when you are cutting at someone’s head to bring it down as hard as you can… David Prowse is such a heavy-handed man, every time they touched swords, the blades kept breaking.” EE video: printed daily of Ben’s duel with Vader, **“with Prowse speaking Vader’s lines”** (0:27). Captions: Lucas demonstrates to Guinness “how to surrender himself to the Force”; a special-effects rig stands in for the departed Jedi. EE video: 1 June 1976 hangar shootout, three cameras (1:29).

**17 May** Lippincott interviews Barry, still worrying about front projection: can’t build full size (fringing); “a perfect case of the cart before the horse.” **27 May**, second unit starts front-projection tests on Stage 8 (extra in the pirate-ship cockpit after principals). **Kurtz**: “We desperately needed the plates, but we weren’t getting them. And the shots that ILM *did* send were not right… long shots of the approach, and they were giving us short pieces… They kept saying that we’d have to cut away. Instead, we spliced them all together to make them long enough.” **Blalack**: “We delivered sixty plates; we thought they would work.” **Dykstra**: “When they reached the point… when they needed the process plates, we didn’t have ’em.” After tests: “it simply wasn’t working” (Blalack) — focus in the English rig, contrast, compositing. **Dykstra**, typography locked: timing an actor to a few seconds of pre-filmed footage against gray — “he’s only got 60 frames to get his line out with feeling… I didn’t foresee that problem… It was our fault that they didn’t use the front projection. It was another moment for which I felt very bad about things.”

19 May: more prison shootout. **Hamill** on stormtrooper armor: “you couldn’t sit down… sawhorses… You get panicky inside those helmets… sickly green… wax in your ears, because of the explosions… I only once freaked out and said, ‘Get me outta here!’ ” Nine days over; crew increasingly ill and mishap-prone (progress reports). **Lucas dismisses editor John Jympson** “about halfway through production.” **Lucas**: “Unfortunately, it didn’t work out… I don’t think he fully understood the movie and what I was trying to do. I shoot in a very peculiar way, in a documentary style, and it takes a lot of hard editing to make it work… I realized why directors are such horrible people, in a way, because you want things to be right, and people will just not listen to you and there is no time to be nice to people, no time to be delicate.” **Stears** on making explosive charges without a manufacturing license: “highly illegal and highly irregular… the authorities know we do all this here.” SCS COMP: 98; SCREEN TIME: 79M 18S.

**2 June**, after Guinness/Obi-Wan leaves to disengage the tractor beam: Hamill, Ford, Daniels, Mayhew, Baker plot the rescue. **Daniels** mumbles “rubbish” to Alec once rather than “screw up the tape”; Guinness “never batted an eyelid.” On “the Princess is going to be terminated”: “You can’t fool about with that line… What George instantly spotted was that I only existed when I moved. If I didn’t move, the voice could be coming from anywhere. So I very carefully had to think out how to move as I spoke, to speak the lines with my head, physically.” Suit still chafes; he’d take it off between shots, delaying redressing; “if I’d been allowed to do all my scenes at once, it wouldn’t have taken nearly as long.” Looks through the viewfinder and wears only what’s in frame; insert of the comlink takes about twenty attempts, sticky pad in the palm. Nearly all *Falcon* cockpit scenes shot back-to-back late May and mid-June. **Lucas**: “See-Threepio hasn’t been fixed, and neither has Artoo-Detoo. Artoo cost us at least a week just because his head wouldn’t work… I said I want those heads to work, to turn… and they never worked.” **Freeborn**: Chewbacca’s eyes separate from the hair inside the mask; “we haven’t got a 100 percent answer… and we never will.” Front projection abandoned for **bluescreen**; Dykstra, Edlund, and Blalack scheduled for Elstree. **Blalack**: VistaVision of actors against blue must be lit correctly or “a salvage job on top of our normal workload.” Caption: mid-May Stage 3 *half-Falcon* scenes; sandbags in the elevator shaft. EE video: command-office dailies, **Mayhew as Chewbacca speaking the Wookiee’s lines in English** (0:33).

## SHOOTING ON A CRANE STRING

**9–14 June**, back to Shepperton: throne room converted to the giant Rebel hangar in “maybe three or four days,” sets on forty-foot wheeled units (Barry). H Stage too small; real hangar unobtainable. Forced perspective on the back half of the runway — “*Gone with the Wind* tricks,” unpopular because they “limit the director.” Painted cutout starfighters; two real ships to be multiplied in post. X-wing canopy hydraulics close, then won’t open — they drill a hole so the actor can screwdriver himself out. Takeoff: “Get one of those gigantic cranes” outside “the biggest stage in Europe,” hole in the roof, wire lifts the ship.

## A FAREWELL TO GUINNESS

Guinness’s last day, **16 June**: first-unit retake in the pirate-ship hallway. When Alderaan blows, Obi-Wan reels and clutches his forehead — “an unpardonable cliché” in Guinness’s opinion. “I still go hot and cold when I think of that scene.” **Hamill**: “He was the strongest, most solid thing… A lot of times you couldn’t see it on the set, but when the film was developed… There was just an aura. I think he was playing a wizard, but he had magic all of the time.” Greek-restaurant night: dancing, pants rolled up; Guinness more thrilled at the *Horse’s Mouth* (1958) screenplay nomination than at winning an Oscar. **Ford** missed that loose night; Guinness “trying to find me a place to live on one day’s acquaintanceship.” **Lucas**: “after working with him I was staggered that he was such a creative and disciplined person.” EE video: Hamill interviewed at Elstree by Kurtz off screen (1:50). SCS COMP: 112; SCREEN TIME: 92M 30S.

More than fourteen days behind. **Kurtz**: “we knew that nothing was being accomplished in Los Angeles.” **Dykstra**: without George at ILM, “it’s really tough to get that cohesive spirit.” During all of principal photography ILM completes only **two shots** — the escape pod leaving the Rebel ship and sailing toward Tatooine. First: Edlund designs it; McCune, Jamie Shourt, volleyball net on the roof, high-speed camera; first take “perfect”; “for three or four months that was the only shot that the shop was able to put out.” Second, about a month later, **Dennis Muren** (Edlund away in London): pod mounted off-axis to tumble, sweep toward the planet as if gravity takes over, orange fill as if reflected off Tatooine, “one afternoon”; avoid crossing in front of the planet (bluescreen not solved). **Burtt** sends about twenty tapes to England every two weeks — explosions, jets, bears, “Here’s a Wookiee getting angry, here’s a Wookiee being lovable.” “I never got any response… I honestly think they were so busy they probably never had a chance to listen… I think Bunny Alsup heard them.” Johnston escape-pod concepts dated **11 May 1976**; McCune describes solenoids, claws, no. 44 flashbulbs, fish scales and mica dust, air jets.

## BLUESCREEN SLINGERS FROM THE FAR WEST

Lucas is hoarse and ill when Dykstra, Edlund, and Blalack arrive **23 June**, TWA Flight 760. **Barry**: “George is flying by the seat of his pants at all times.” Word reaches Spielberg’s *Close Encounters* set; **Bob Shepherd**: “George was getting wasted badly; I’m surprised he’s still alive.” **Lucas**: “The biggest change during filming was from front projection to bluescreen. We had shot the approach to the Death Star, but once we got the results, we realized it wasn’t going to work.” **Dykstra**: “then we really got rolling again… maybe George felt like I knew as much as Tommy Howard.” They convert VistaVision to Nikon lenses, test, check Kodak perforations, about a week. **Blalack**: all but “one or two of the TIE ships going toward the Death Star” plates shelved; “we started over.” Exciting to see the full-sized pirate ship and half-dressed stormtrooper extras “resting in the sun.” **Kurtz** with Stan Taylor from Technicolor: go over everything so they won’t hear “None of this stuff is any good” back in the US; then, “We have 360 shots, that makes one shot a day… are we going to finish on time or not?” Dykstra: “If things go right, yeah.” “We gave them the last chance at that time.” Fox’s Ray Gosnell monitors costs (Watts: “he knows his business”). **Lucas**: “The bluescreen cost another $100,000… Lighting the bluescreen really slowed us down—it would take forever, hours and hours.”

## MONSTER TENTACLE

Garbage masher, Stage 4, **21–22 June**; actors in “increasingly nasty water.” Dia Noga “less than it could have been.” **Lucas** had already tried a trash-compactor escape in *THX 1138* and “it failed miserably, so I had to cut it out.” Drafts: “a semi-transparent, fairly enormous monster with almost magical powers,” scaled down. “It’s a cross between a jellyfish and an octopus, a transparent muck-monster, which can take any shape.” **Stears**: “The Dia Noga is a bit of a sad story… You’ll never see the body, just the tentacles.” Early diode-inflation plan died when budget cuts combined the garbage room and Dia Noga cave; tank too small. **Lucas**: “I kept rejecting things to the point where all we had left was a tentacle.” Wet suits optional; Fisher thought mandatory: “I was going to look like Walter Brennan from the waist down.” Hamill/Ford ad-lib fight over the door serial: Hamill planned “2–––” (his own number); blocked away from the door, Ford says the line and starts doing *his* number; “Come on, say mine”; finally Ford reads Hamill’s number: “Happy now, you big baby?” Next: “disused hallway” (why no stormtroopers notice four wet Rebels). **Fisher**, 105 degrees: walking too fast; George: “Now, act more like a princess. Stand up straight… ‘Faster’ you can do. ‘More intense’ you can do… ‘Remember how you were in the scene with Peter Cushing?’ ” Kurtz: at one point he and George figured they were making “about $1.10 an hour.”

**Thursday, 24 June**: Harrison Ford’s last day. **Ford**, typography locked: “I get a concept of the character, but I don’t get a concept of the way the character will behave until I see how the other people are going to act in a scene and until I get to see what the set is like and the situation.” Rinzler: Han Solo is “not just… Ford’s own interpretation… and Lucas’s direction—Solo was also the right response to the performances of his fellow actors.” Caption: Lucas looks at Vader in his TIE fighter, Stage 8. **Monday, 28 June**: Ford flies to LA with Fisher (on a break); Mayhew’s last day, returns to hospital porter work. **Fisher** on lunch with Mayhew in “hairy earphones,” 7'2", “they’d serve us like we were regular people… I’d go out to get cigarettes and magazines in my entire outfit, and … nothing.” Progress reports first mention “blue backing” on X-wing cockpits. Dykstra, Edlund, Blalack return to ILM. SCS COMP: 173; SCREEN TIME: 103M 08S.

Gil Taylor increasingly ill; more electricians for bluescreen. Movies are not done in a jolly atmosphere of self-congratulatory lunches at the Polo Lounge… The potential problem… is it can become a committee movie.”

## THE WHITE CORRIDOR

Night walk with Barry through the Rebel ship — a redecorated *Falcon* main hold — and Lucas finds it wanting. **Lucas**, typography locked: originally “the little alleyway with the Princess and the robots. That was all we had. And I just realized I couldn’t shoot a battle, five pages of dialogue, and all these people running around, and have it all take place in one little hallway… ‘John, you have to build another big hallway next to this little hallway’—and that created a whole big ruckus with Fox… a lot of screaming and yelling. But ultimately, as the director, if you decide that it is vital to the film, it is vital to the film… I was very concerned that the opening, the first interior of the film, be spectacular and look opulent, and not just be a set re-dress… eventually John, who is a genius, did it.” **Barry**: “I think George wanted the set to look, at first sight, like you are in your conventional all-white interior *2001*-type spaceship—and then the door blows down and in comes Darth Vader—*black* against a white hallway!” Stears “a little ambitious and blew the walls off the set a couple of times” (Kurtz); still “much faster than any other powder man.” **Lucas** wanted hits to be “a big, huge flash,” not “a little squib.” Last principal scenes on Stage 9: scene 6, rebel officer’s neck broken — **Peter Geddis**; scene 11 “commander” — **Constantin De Goguel** (sometimes Constantine Gregory). EE video: end-of-principal dailies, July 1976, stormtroopers blast onto the rebel ship (1:12); **8 July 1976**, **Prowse as Vader chokes the rebel captain** (0:50). **Fisher**: “I got felled by a paralyzing ray, which I loved, because I knew I would have to do what my mother called ‘pratfalls.’ ”

**16 July**: “Completion of principal photography in U.K. today.” Hamill and Fisher travel to LA 17 July; Lucas also 17 July; Daniels and Baker finish; editing equipment and film arrive in the US 27 July. Remaining US scenes listed: mostly R2, banthas, landspeeder, sandcrawler. One more UK day: first official postproduction Friday — insert of Luke’s gloved hand switching off the targeting computer, and C-3PO in the Rebel corridor (stand-ins). Derek Ball records Shelagh Fraser (Beru) wild track. **23 July**, Brian West Arriflex: R2 for scene 2; cockpit hands; landspeeder with a Luke double against bluescreen. **Daniels**: his stand-in “a beautiful blond debutante, a girl small enough to get into my costume. She was really quite good.” 84-day shoot, **twenty days over**; 322,704 feet exposed, 226,717 printed, 219 magnetic sound rolls; average 1 minute 30 seconds of script a day. Days worked: Hamill 70, Daniels 54, Ford 42, Fisher 37; about 100 sets grouped into “only about 40” (Barry). Biggest overages: Taylor’s lighting and transportation — planned 10 electricians/day, actually 20, some days 40, bluescreen days **60** electricians changing carbons on about 70 white-flamed arcs (Kurtz: “That wasn’t Taylor’s fault”). Death Star hallways/hangar repainted “about seven shades of gray lighter.” 6 July scrap: Lucas changes to Leia’s hologram monologue, shot 16 July. **Taylor**: “I only wish I could have my time over again to have a slightly different relationship with George.” Other overages “nickel-and-dime”; pound devaluation a ~$450,000 windfall; UK overage down to ~$600,000. **Kurtz**: “60 to 70 percent worked out well.” **Lucas**: “you’re asking the impossible of people… you can’t blame or fault somebody for not being able to do the impossible—by definition, it’s impossible.” SCS COMP: 344; SCREEN TIME: 124M 02S.
