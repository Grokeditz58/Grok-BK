## Opening (p. 106)

Budget cuts change the story: Alderaan eliminated, its scenes moved to the Death Star — “the fortuitous effect of giving the bad guys a single location… a more quickly moving story.” **Lucas** on writer-director hours: “If a director works ten hours a day, then a writer-director ends up working twenty hours a day… It was literally seven days a week—every waking hour, I was thinking about my movie.” Notes: “Change Death Star name … Luke doesn’t know father Jedi; Ben tells Luke about father.”

**29 December 1975** conversation with Alan Dean Foster (preparing the novelization), also Lippincott and Dykstra, in Dykstra’s office. Lucas, typography locked in substance:

- Fairy tales: “I put this little thing on it: ‘A long, long time ago in a galaxy far, far away, an incredible adventure took place.’ Basically it’s a fairy tale now. *Star Wars* is built on top of many things that came before. This film is a compilation of all those dreams, using them as history to create a new dream.” Rinzler: Bruno Bettelheim, *The Uses of Enchantment* (officially 1976, but Lucas says he read it between drafts): “I read Bettelheim, and it all began to inform the story and the characters.” Fairy-tale Leia more prominent, especially at the beginning.
- Kiber Crystal: “I think I’m taking the Kiber Crystal out. I thought it really detracted from Luke and Vader; it made them too much like supermen, and it’s hard to root for supermen.”
- The Force: “I’m dealing with the Force a little more subtly now. It’s a force field that has a good side and a bad side, and every person has this force field around them; and when you die, your aura doesn’t die with you, it joins the rest of the life force. It’s a big idea—I could write a whole movie just about the Force of Others. Now it really comes down to that scene… where Ben tries to get Luke to swordfight with the chrome baseball when he’s blindfolded. He has to trust his feelings rather than his senses and his logic—that’s essentially what the Force of Others comes down to.” Trench: four runs, Luke fails once, then “at the last moment, he just pushes the computer aside. Ben, in the end, says, ‘Luke, trust your feelings.’ ”
- Darth Vader: “Vader runs off in the end, shaking his fist: ‘I’ll get you yet!’ In a one-to-one fight Vader could probably destroy Luke.”
- Luke’s father: “I’m going to have his father leave him his laser sword… Otherwise there is no way in the world to explain what happens in the cantina.”
- Uncle Owen: “His uncle isn’t a son of a bitch anymore; because he gets killed, I have to make him sympathetic, so you’ll hate the Empire.”
- Ben: “When this cranky old man pulls out his laser sword and cuts down three guys in a second—that’s when you realize the old man is a master.”
- Tuskens: the “electric windmill” cut for cost; “Now they just beat him up.”
- Sequels, typography locked: “I want to have Luke kiss the princess in the second book. The second book will be *Gone with the Wind in Outer Space.* She likes Luke, but Han is Clark Gable. Well, she may appear to get Luke, because in the end I want Han to leave. Han splits at the end of the second book and **we learn who Darth Vader is** … In the third book, I want the story to be just about the soap opera of the Skywalker family, which ends with the destruction of the Empire. Then someday I want to do the backstory of Kenobi as a young man—a story of the Jedi and how the Emperor eventually takes over and turns the whole thing from a Republic into an Empire, and tricks all the Jedi and kills them. The whole battle where Luke’s father gets killed. That would be impossible to do, but it’s great to dream about.” Record this 29 Dec 1975 plan as stated; do not collapse with later saga revelations or with the fourth-draft “Vader kills Luke’s father” backstory below.

Rinzler: the cantina-to-blast-off stretch still gives Lucas trouble; Solo “is searching for a way to define himself, and Lucas is never quite satisfied.” Fourth draft replaces Jabba with Imperial bureaucrat Montross, whom Solo outfoxes. Luke’s motivation changes: third draft, he seeks adventure himself; fourth, “less sure of himself,” actions more varied. **Lucas** on hero and destiny, typography locked in part: usually the hero decides on his own; “This time the hero avoids that position. But then I have the position thrust upon him—because it’s inevitable. I’m taking the existential view and putting a slight determinist slant on it… What I would call an ‘unpoetic state’ will eventually become a ‘poetic state,’ because an unpoetic state will not last.” After Leia’s message, “Luke immediately rejects it… he wants to go fight the Empire… but he doesn’t feel that he can take on the responsibility. As a result, destiny comes into play… There is a scene where his friend Biggs explains that you can’t avoid the issue forever… To not make a decision is a decision… essentially what happened in the United States with the Vietnam War.”

## Fourth-Draft Summary, January 1, 1976 (p. 108)

Title: *The Adventures of Luke Starkiller as Taken from the “Journal of the Whills” (Saga I) Star Wars.* FADE IN: “A long, long time ago in a galaxy far, far away an incredible adventure took place …” War drums; roll-up: civil wars; “a brave alliance of underground freedom fighters”; hidden fortress among “the billion suns”; crushing victory; “THE DEATH STAR. Powerful enough to destroy an entire planet…” “The awesome yellow planet of Tatooine emerges from a total eclipse.” C-3PO spots R2 receiving plans from a mysterious young female; astromech given a “mission.” Shootout omitted (perhaps budget); cut to Vader in the rebel ship’s hallway. Girl stunned; **“Vader no longer mentions the Bogan Force when later threatening the girl, who turns out to be Senator Leia.”**

Biggs and Luke: Luke’s fear of leaving to join the Alliance. Biggs: “Deak and Windy are going away, why don’t you just tell me you’re afraid to go… If you want things to change you’re going to have to make them change.” Death Star appears much earlier; Imperial Senators and generals “sit around a black conference room.” **Governor Moff Tarkin is now on the side of the Empire.** Motti: Death Star will destroy “a planet or an entire system …possibly even a sun… If we were to destroy every planet that is even suspected of being sympathetic to the Alliance…” Tarkin: “The senate would not support the emperor. A move like that would only aid the rebellion.”

Luke decides to go to the Academy; Owen unhappy. He finds Leia’s message for a “Ben Kenobi,” has dinner, then R2 runs away — “obliged to leave home,” unlike the third draft. Tusken attack: curved double-pointed “Gaderffii”; Luke’s laser rifle smashed; “The sinister raider… lets out a horrible shrieking laugh” — “a laugh that used to belong to Darth Vader (on the rebel ship).” Ben arrives; “the Jedi explains that he’s been chased across the galaxy by Vader” (rough-draft idea of the old Jedi hunted by the Sith). Ben’s cave condensed to one scene. **Luke doesn’t know his father was a Jedi; Owen told him he was a navigator. Ben gives him his father’s lightsaber**; no talk of Luke joining the mission to Alderaan. Ben: “I’m afraid I’m getting too old for this sort of thing.” Luke: he’ll let Ben take the droid, “I’ll somehow explain it to my uncle …boy, what a day.” Shorter torture: Vader threatens Leia on the Death Star. Sandcrawler attacked, Jawas killed; Ben says stormtroopers; Luke finds aunt and uncle murdered. Tarkin designates **Alderaan, instead of Organa Major**, as first planet. Funeral pyre; Luke wants to go with Ben and “learn the ways of the Force; I want to become a Jedi like my father.” Mos Eisley: they meet **Chewbacca and Han Solo in the cantina**. New Death Star scene: a stormtrooper reports Ben’s cantina handiwork to Vader, “who senses something afoot.” Luke and Ben sell the landspeeder; **Solo spars with Imperial bureaucrat Montross** (spaceport closed); Solo outsmarts him; jump to “hyper-space.”

Sequence change: no prison planet; they realize Alderaan is destroyed, then TIE buzz, **gunport battle** (Han and Luke blast, Ben directs), all but one TIE destroyed, they pursue, **caught in the Death Star’s tractor beam**. Conceal themselves; **Ben leaves to disengage the tractor beam (Kiber Crystal eliminated)**. Money again: they discover the princess is held; Luke promises she’ll pay Han a lot. Rescue; bowels of the Death Star (not Alderaan); “Dia Nogu”; Luke and Leia swing across a “treacherous abyss.” Garbage masher, Ben versus Vader, and escape “much as in the third draft.” Post-escape gunport is absent (moved earlier). No Yavin lifepods; pirate ship orbits, they enter the temple; landing unseen. Assault similar; fighters now called “X-wings”; Han’s ship, when it comes to the rescue, “a ‘pirate manta-ship.’ ” R2 damaged; final attack broken into hundreds of shots with specific angles and dialogue.

STAR WARS PROGRESSION (selected): “A long time ago …” as intro; Utapau becomes Tatooine; Leia sends R2 with plans to Ben; **“Darth Vader strangles rebel officer”**; Tarkin on the Empire’s side; Tagge and Motti; hologram for Kenobi; destiny pushes Luke; R2 runs away; **“Ben Kenobi no longer mechanical”**; **“Luke doesn’t know who his father is”**; Jawas attacked, Owen and Beru killed by stormtroopers; Alderaan destroyed offscreen; Jawa bonfire; **Han meets Ben and Luke in cantina**; “hyper-space”; tractor beam; Ben’s mission is the tractor beam; princess scheduled to be terminated; Luke and Leia swing the abyss; Wedge Antilles; X-wing; R2 damaged; **“Ben advises Luke to use the Force, so he doesn’t use targeting computer.”**

## Man-Machine Progression

Rinzler: identity of the man-machine character changes draft to draft — Akira (first), Montross (second), Ben Kenobi (third) — “but ultimately it is Darth Vader who comes to embody the script’s ambivalence toward technology or, more accurately, toward humanity. Although it is not explicitly stated, Vader made the jump to man-machine in Lucas’s mind while he was at work on this latest version.” Caption: Montross recast as a background pirate beside Jabba (Mollo sketch).

**Lucas**, typography locked — this is the fourth-draft backstory, do not collapse with later films or with the 29 Dec 1975 “we learn who Darth Vader is” sequel plan: “The thing about Vader wasn’t really developed until the fourth draft, when I was sorting out Vader’s real character and who he was. The backstory is about Ben and Luke’s father and Vader, when they are young Jedi Knights. **Vader kills Luke’s father**, then Ben and Vader have a confrontation, just like they have in *Star Wars*, and Ben almost kills Vader. As a matter of fact, he falls into a volcanic pit and gets fried and is one destroyed being. That’s why he has to wear the suit with a mask, because it’s a breathing mask. It’s like a walking iron lung. His face is all horrible inside. I was going to have a close-up of Vader where you could see the inside of his face, but then we said, ‘No, no, it would destroy the mystique of the whole thing.’ ” Then: “A machine is only as bad as the man that sits behind it… It’s an extension of mankind… Man’s relationship to machines is a theme that I have used in all my films, whether it’s a boy in a car or the robots in *THX.*”

## HE’S GONE TO LONDON TO MAKE A MOVIE

Early January 1976: Lucas to England with the fourth draft; house in Hampstead; late-March Tunisia start. EMI offices staffed (Watts’s corridor rundown: Kurtz, Bunny, Watts, Pat Carr, Bruce Sharman, Brian Gibbs and accounts, Ralph Leo Fox auditor, Graham Henderson set costs, Andrew Mitchell EMI MD). New heads: costume designer **John Mollo** and makeup **Stuart Freeborn**, hired the previous month, work starting in the new year. Freeborn was on *The Omen* making false dogs; Lucas popped in: “If you can make these dogs that all work, then have a go at making the animals for this picture.” Career from Korda 1936; wife Kay and son Graham. **Lucas** wanted him for the *2001* apes. Every creature here is a prototype. First three or four weeks Freeborn works alone on Chewbacca from McQuarrie’s design, then family and assistants.

Mollo: talks with Milena Canonero (*Barry Lyndon*, *A Clockwork Orange*) who was about to do a Fellini documentary; she recommended her *Barry Lyndon* assistant. **Lucas**: “I wanted somebody that really knew armor… military hardware, rather than somebody who knew how to design for the stage. I wanted designs that wouldn’t stand out.” Mollo had written military-fashion books with brother Andrew; *Charge of the Light Brigade* (three thousand costumes); *Napoleon*. After Christmas, Ron Beck; fashion showing. **Mollo**, typography locked: they Polaroided stock approximations; “For Darth Vader, we put on a black motorcycle suit, a Nazi helmet, a gas mask, and a monk’s cloak we found in the Middle Ages department.” Live show at Berman’s in January: “George would say, ‘I don’t like that; I do like this.’ We did very little drawing; it was more of a practical make-do amend, because there was already an established style.” Lucas and McQuarrie had worked costumes 24 July–29 August 1975. **Lucas**: “For the stormtroopers and Darth Vader, Ralph had a very strong influence. Essentially they are his designs. The other characters, such as the princess, Mollo actually did.” Paper dolls; books in the Elstree office; constant talk with Barry.

Schedule now twelve weeks instead of twenty-six or sixteen. **Barry**: “It really is quite a nightmare.” **Lucas**: Fox’s last-minute wait forced twice as many people on golden time, twenty-four hours; makeup people “six weeks short… The monsters were part of it, the sets were part of it, the special effects were part of it… the ramifications go all the way through the picture.” **Stears**: “That’s why we’ve burned the midnight oil.” **Norman Reynolds**: “grave doubts about getting it done in time.” Construction manager Bill Walsh “making an enormous contribution” (Barry). Chewbacca from “straight yak hair.” **Lucas**: “I would go in there every day and push the nose around… We were trying to do a combination of a monkey, a dog, and a cat. I really wanted it to be cat-like more than anything else.” **Freeborn**: “he had to look nice, though he could be very ferocious… fun making a monster that looked friendly and nice for a change.” EE video (Rinzler EE): Freeborn at work with Kay at Elstree (no audio; 1:16).

## FIRST OF THE FINAL CAST

Friday production meetings; Wednesday-evening screenings (*Forbidden Planet*, Leone’s *Once Upon a Time in the West*, Fellini’s *Satyricon*). **Watts**: Tunisia “like the landscape of the Leone film, which was indicative of the feel George wanted to inject into the space age… he is an instinctive director; he is communicating through film.” Lucas reviewing tests: some terrific in person fail on film; videotape ≠ film. **Anthony Daniels** signed as C-3PO on **5 December 1975**, no test needed. Second interview: Pat Carr said “cast” meaning plaster; Daniels hadn’t been told he had the part — “Lucas had cast me the first time—and my agent hadn’t wanted to tell me about it in case I hated the whole idea!” Early January: Guinness agrees, **£15,000 per week and 2 percent of net profits**. To *The Sunday Times*: “I feel there’s a new kind of filmmaker about. The days of the big cigar are over.” **Ford**: “It’s a real American story and it has a mythological quality… I think that Guinness thought this was a picture about America.” Ensemble must work around Guinness. Options still open: **Walken for Han**; Charley Martin Smith, Will Seltzer, and Hamill for Luke. **Ford**: “I think if George had chosen Walken, the princess would have been Jodie Foster.” Foster and Terri Nunn not yet eighteen — limited hours, a drawback to the compressed schedule.

## A CONVERSATION

**10 January** Lucas debriefs Kurtz on fourth-draft changes (logistics vs. new/unfilmed ideas). Among the beats: Luke/Biggs later, possibly half outside; Luke will take the droids only as far as Ben; Tuskens loot the car, no windmill; **crystal chamber cut**, maybe replaced by Vader/Leia/torture-robot; cantina then morning intercut of selling the speeder with Han prepping the ship, then gunfight with Imperial troops, takeoff “just in the nick of time”; hyper-space game and Force lesson; tractor-beamed to Death Star; at one point Lucas tries a beat where they go into the living area “and Darth Vader’s there. But Ben has mysteriously disappeared… ‘Nice try,’ and he marches them off”; later he walks it back — Vader won’t be with that group; Ben wipes out troops taking Artoo, opens the cell (no prison-garb change, no big control-room fight); Ben finds Leia because “he has the Force of Others… Maybe he just senses her presence”; Dia Noga with robots, Artoo under water with “little periscope,” skin diver; Ben vs. Vader in a hallway “just like they did before. Before they fought over the Kiber Crystal, but now the Kiber Crystal’s gone”; Ben “wounded, but not as bad” (once budgeted a hospital “smock”); no lifepods, cut to jungle and temple; Crystal Chamber and docking-bay control room out — “I’ve got to tell John Dykstra about that.” “Truth of the matter, I have much more script than I need…” Ivor Beddoes takes over principal-photography storyboards; Johnston continues VFX boards, “concentrating on Ben Kenobi battling Darth Vader and a group of storm-troopers.” EE audio (Rinzler EE): Lucas and Kurtz discuss the story as he nears a shooting script (Recorded **20 January 1976**; 1:39). Caption date of the audio (20 Jan) is later than the 10 Jan debrief in the running text — record both, do not collapse.

## GETTING DOWN TO SETS

Lucas and Barry “both sitting on the same stool in front of the drawing board” for three or four weeks. **Barry**: “What can you see from the doorway?” **Lucas**: learned forced perspective, false walls, “I had never worked on a set before.” UK full-scale pirate-ship exterior coordinated from ILM photos; extra X- and Y-fighter model casts sent to the full-size shops. Mollo Vader sketches: “an insignia and a lightsaber with a broadsword handle.” Captions on guns: Han Solo’s blaster; Christian’s technique of repurposing found objects; “Sterling Stormtrooper’s Gun”; Mauser-inspired Han blaster; Tusken gun with silencer and holster power-pack line. EE audio (Rinzler EE): Christian on designing Han’s blaster, “the prototype weapon for all those that followed” (Interview by Rinzler, **2012**; 1:24). **Watts** on the eight-hour time shift and Atlantic split. Barry into the junk business, inspired by ILM kit-bashing: £10,000 dinghy machine; comlink “part of a faucet”; lightsaber handle “a very old photographic flash unit”; Luke’s binoculars “an old Ronoflex.” Gaffi sticks from a weapons-hire company. “Rather than have your slick streamlined ray guns, we took actual World War II machine guns and cannibized one into another. George likes what he calls the ‘visceral’ quality that real weapons have.” First EMI mechanical tests on R2; blueprint 20 January 1976 (P. Childs).

## VOYAGE TO THE UNKNOWN / LASER SWORD / LANDSPEEDER / THE REBEL BASE

First FX tests **14 January**: “laser swords, R2-D2 model, and illuminated control boards.” Second **23 January**: “laser swords and Jawa ‘eyes.’ ” Between them, **20 January** meeting: Lucas, Kurtz, Gil Taylor (new to the production). EE video: 14 January tests for “Artoo’s head” (no audio; 0:22).

**LASER SWORD** — Lucas: three scenes: Luke turns it on; a “flash” in the cantina; “the final battle between Ben and the warlord… at least now it’s down to a very controlled setup.” Taylor asks if laser sword and laser gun are the same. Lucas: no; laser guns are real guns firing a blank “that makes a big flame”; sound later; optical bolts “where it is necessary.” EE video: Stears and others test early laser-sword effects, circa 23 January 1976 (no audio; 0:45). EE audio: Christian on the first lightsaber prop (Rinzler, 2012; 2:14).

**LANDSPEEDER** — hovercraft, “a foot or two off the ground.” Taylor recalls Hitchcock’s *Number 17* (1930s; he was a clapper loader). Kurtz: Wescam from a helicopter. Lucas: mirrors/shiny plates in front of the wheels to mask them; closed cockpit; shoot from outside through glass; change the key light quickly to create speed (same idea for fighters). The thing that makes war-movie dogfights real: “the key lights change all the time… I want lots of flashing, strobing, and moving light, so the excitement continues from the exterior to the interior.” EE audio: Lucas, Kurtz, Taylor on shooting the landspeeder from a moving vehicle (20 January 1976; 2:22).

**THE REBEL BASE** — source lighting in the hangar; “slightly filtered to give it a romantic quality”; hot and dark spots; fluorescents; floor floodlights; “the scene can be much darker than it is in Ralph’s painting.” Kurtz: extras moved, camera locked, seven or eight VistaVision matte shots. Taylor doubts even Shepperton is big enough; Lucas: airplane hangar, length but not height; double-expose people, matte the rest. Day-for-night undecided.

## THE CONTRACT, PART II / Challenge I-A

Late 1975, “serious negotiations” (Rigrod). Nine-page deal memo must become a 40-to-60-page production-distribution contract. Fox, by waiting, has bargained itself into a corner: *Graffiti* now over $100 million, top-ten all-time; Lucas would normally command more pay; Fox antagonized the production. **Lucas**: “The longer it took, the tougher we got… We were willing to take it from them… I had written four scripts—they had only paid me for one… We left the deal memo intact. I left the money, I left the points… But we did say, ‘You want to negotiate the contract now? We’ll negotiate the *whole* contract.’ ” **Pollock**: they did not reopen George’s money (Fox expected $250–300k to direct instead of $85k, more points); “George didn’t need the money anymore, so we went after… control—control over the making of the picture and control over the exploitation of all the ancillary rights.” **Lucas**: “they were shocked because nobody had ever done that before.”

**1 February 1976** formal budget: below-the-line $6,568,932; total projected **$8,228,228**. Lucas $72,700 to direct, $50,000 to write; living costs $8,210 reimbursed; out-of-pocket covered by early 1976. Budget still has leftover draft items: Kiber Crystal £300; two Sith Lords’ costumes £500 each; forty Aquillian rangers £6,000; Montross pirate outfit £150; address list still has Bill Bailey as Montross, Jay Benedict as Deak Starkiller “only as an extra… as an in-joke, from the second draft.” Pollock on contingencies (overbudget takeover, recut). **Jeff Berg**: open area of sequels, television, publishing, merchandising, soundtrack — “the life of *Star Wars* would exist beyond the making of the first theatrical motion picture.” **Jake Bloom**: Fox’s historic merchandising vs. “We weren’t interested in a buck—we were interested in preserving whatever rights we could… if the picture was successful we would be selling out.”

**Challenge I-A** (landspeeder): first October 1975 build seated four, “enormous.” Tunisia roads narrower; they take a three-wheel “Bomb Bug,” shorten and squeeze it, “a little bit too small.” **Lucas**: “He wouldn’t really be able to afford the big job.” Clay sculptures; English car company built it. Dilley/Stears carousel, sixty-foot diameter, for hovering close-ups. Long-shot hover still a conundrum (helicopter hang; Stears underground monorail). Roger Christian brought carpenter Bill Harmon (Monty Python stories for Lucas, a fan); Harmon made wooden R2s from marine ply he had at home, “as he had no budget for materials,” and mock-up landspeeders from wheelbarrows.

## THE THRILLING THREE / Uncanny Cans / PT1145 / ROBOT ARMOR

Late January: final leads. After Guinness, **Harrison Ford as Han Solo, Mark Hamill as Luke Starkiller, Carrie Fisher as Princess Leia Organa.** **Lucas**, typography locked: “Harrison’s test was very good… he had a quality. The Han Solo part wasn’t written for him, but I’d had him in my mind when I was writing it—even though I’d decided at one point that I wasn’t going to use anybody from the past… I had a black actor [Glynn Turman] who was one of the finalists and more heroic, and I had an older actor [Walken], who was the more maniacal of the group; Harrison was the funnier, goofier one—but he could also play mean. When I went younger with Mark, I decided I would also go younger with Han… he really worked well with Carrie and Mark—the combination was very good.” EE audio (Rinzler EE): Lippincott asks Lucas why he chose Ford as Han (Recorded **24 March 1977**; 0:39) — after release; do not collapse with 1975–76 Lost Interviews. Turman recommended by Roos after *Cooley High* (1975). **Ford**: “I’ve always had the feeling that I was hired for the job because I did the right thing for that character… I think George cast the relationship between Han and Luke… he picked the kid automatically as soon as he picked me.” Hamill: “If you read the original description of Luke, it’s almost exactly the way Mark is” (Lucas); finalist was older, more collegiate, “more like Kurt in *American Graffiti*”; Mark “more idealistic, naïve, and hopeful; a little more Disney-esque.” Agent slipped the news mid grocery-list lunch. **Lucas** on Leia: another girl (Terri Nunn) “very pretty and petite, but she also had an edge… Whereas Carrie is a very warm person; she’s a fun-loving, goofy kid who can also play a very hard, sophisticated, tough leader… if she played a tough person, somehow underneath it you knew that she really had a warm heart.” Fisher ran outside: she’d seen *Graffiti* three times.

Salaries: Hamill $1,000/week, Fisher $850, Ford $750. **Peter Cushing** as Governor Tarkin, £2,000 per day. **Lucas**: Tarkin “kept evolving into a bigger and bigger part… after we’d started designing the costumes, and I saw what Darth Vader looked like, I felt I really needed a human villain, too, because you can’t see Darth Vader’s face… Peter Cushing was my first choice… a really good villain.” Cushing to *Starlog* (later): “When you get to be my age and you’re still wanted…” UK Equity: waiver via the Film Producers Association — three hundred local jobs (Kurtz).

**Uncanny Cans:** UK art department listed (Christian, Dilley, Barry, Bill Welch, Reynolds); five draftsmen; twelve weeks for dozens of sets, two robots, weapons, armor. **Barry**: design should be “only a subliminal contribution.” Pirate-ship interior: November 1975 sketch still matches Cantwell’s long corridor; after the redesign, maquettes, 17 February 1976 undercarriage blueprints, circular corridor. “George wanted it to look like a spaceship from *2001* that had aged two hundred years.” Removable panels for the training remote; ramp must match the already-built exterior. Death Star: garbage room 23 February; cells corridor 1 March; conference room 12 March. Corridors built concentrically; McQuarrie “navy-blue Death Star” drawings; “you have to always build it on the curve.”

**PT1145:** ILM first model-explosion attempts look bad; “one errant Y-wing even slid down a wire, detonated, and put a hole in John Dykstra’s leg.” Possible added Yavin jungle plates. **Ben Burtt** still without a quota; handles computer graphics for set screens for about three months; Dan O’Bannon later takes over; Blalack and Burtt find **Larry Cuba** (Cal Arts; contract February, work March, 1 June deadline). Death Star plans for the debriefing; Cuba wanted one continuous shot into the trench; technical linkages “not a trivial task.” Johnston designed trench modules: shallow/wide/low detail at entry, deeper/narrower/higher near the exhaust port. Cuba’s PT1145 “basic digital computer” plus Mitchell; two minutes a frame, ~2,000 frames, three days for ninety seconds. Burtt sound: Lucas “did not want anything in the picture to sound electronic, except maybe the robot language.” Organic recordings: Marineland dolphins, seals, sea lions; walrus **Petulia** — “A lot of her sounds became part of the Wookiee… a combination of Petulia and a young cinnamon bear by the name of Pooh.” Pacific Airmotive jet tests (747s down to helicopter/turbine); Mojave gunnery range; stereo gun/target miking with a collector’s weapons.

**ROBOT ARMOR** — **Mollo**, typography locked: “he wanted the Imperial people to look efficient, totalitarian, fascist; and the rebels, the goodies, to look like something out of a Western or the US Marines… ‘I don’t want anyone to notice the costumes. They’ve got to look familiar, but *not* familiar at the same time.’ ” Books on SF, WWII, Vietnam, Japanese armor; electronics shops, “I’m going to be sticking this on a robot.” **Vader’s one outfit budgeted at £500 ($1,173).** **Mollo**: “Vader was fixed in the McQuarrie painting… The costumiers made the basic costume out of leather, while we in the studio made the masque, the armor, the belt, and the funny chest box with lights on it. To me it looks like a Nazi helmet and pieces of trench armor that they wore in World War I. So it wouldn’t be too hot and uncomfortable for the actor, we made it in pieces.” EE audio: Mollo on turning sketches into costumes (Interview by Arnold, 1979; 1:04). Cross-ref notes 169. C-3PO’s single outfit **£7,750 ($18,189)**. Path: Lucas → McQuarrie → Stears/Barry, Liz Moore; back to Lucas **11 February 1976**. Nine faces; Lucas “found two coins for the eyes”; didn’t quite like the made-up mouth. Daniels plaster-cast: “one of the most disgusting experiences in my life”; straws up the nose; first cast warped (“twisted spine”); second cast with Liz Moore; Lucas “fiddling around with my elbow or my knee or my shoulder.” First road trains to Tunisia **16 February 1976** (Roadrunner Productions press release; 24,000 cu ft).

## DEBONAIR DIALOGUE

First quarter 1976, before dawn, Lucas revises the fourth draft into the shooting script; most changes in the first two-thirds. Unhappy with dialogue, he hires Gloria and Willard Huyck “at the very last minute” for “snappy one-liners.” Huyck met Lucas at USC (Lucas lent $80 to get a short out of the lab). Shared “small-town… Middle-class WASP” background. **Lucas** still frustrated, typography locked: “It’s very hard to write about nothing. *Graffiti*, I wrote in three weeks. This one took me three years… I had a lot of vague concepts, but I didn’t really know where to go with it, and I’ve never fully resolved it. It’s very hard stumbling across the desert, picking up rocks, not knowing what I’m looking for, and knowing the rock that I’ve got is not the rock I’m looking for… I’m still not very happy with the script. I never have been.”

## HIGH NOON

Two weeks before shooting, last road trains leave for Tunisia — without the still-unready landspeeder and robots. First train late January, second February; five days, ferry/road/ferry, twelve trucks total; first construction crew already there six weeks. Four Land Rovers, mobile kitchen, nested location sets (sandcrawler track units in three sections). Three weeks before cameras, the picture almost collapses: negotiations drag into early March. Kurtz “deal breakers”: (1) who pays $45,000 in legal fees; (2) still-unsigned agreements affecting chain of title; (3) the production-distribution contract details. **Lucas**: “I said, ‘I’m not going to start shooting this picture until all these outstanding points are agreed upon.’ Because once I’d started the picture, they would’ve had me.” Late February: Kurtz to LA, meets Ladd; wants Pollock and Berg to get verbal commitment from Ladd and Dennis Stanfill on the $45,000. Rigrod’s notes: if unresolved, Kurtz and Lucas were ready to “fly back”—abandon the picture. American Security Interest and British “Charge” in peril without signed agreements. Two weeks of high-gear talks; **seven days before Lucas was either going to shut down or board for Tunisia, “Fox blinked.”** Compromises; not exact final-contract language, but Lucas’s main demands; Pollock signs key papers; Fox pays legal fees; Lucas signs directing and writing services, giving Fox chain of title. **Lucas**: “It wasn’t until a week before we started shooting. Gary went over and got the agreement, brought it back, and I signed it.” Final caption: Mollo sketch, Solo costume directions: “T-shirt, long sleeves, black jeans, yellow stripe into boot.”
