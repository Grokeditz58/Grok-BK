## REPORT NOS. 15–17: WEDNESDAY, APRIL 7–FRIDAY, APRIL 9, 1976

CALL: 08.30; EMI STUDIOS, BOREHAMWOOD. STAGE 7: INT. LARS’ KITCHEN; INT. ANCHORHEAD POWER STATION; 28 [BERU FILLS PITCHER WITH BLUE MILK]; A32 [OWEN LOOKING FOR LUKE, TALKS WITH BERU]; 9 [ANCHORHEAD: BIGGS/LUKE REUNION]. STAGE 1: INT. LARS’ HOMESTEAD (GARAGE); 27 [C-3PO TAKES OIL BATH AND LUKE DISCOVERS LEIA’s MESSAGE]; A29 [LUKE FINDS C-3PO HIDING IN GARAGE]. STAGE 3: INT. DOCKING BAY 94; AA53 PART [JABBA AND HAN SOLO].

**Harrison Ford** arrived in England **1 April**, before the location group returned. First contact: costume fitting. **Ford**: George had suggested a costume; it included the shirt “but instead of the small collar, it had a huge Peter Pan shawl-type collar. I said, ‘No, no, no. That’s wrong. Can’t wear that.’ So they took it off. Later on the set, George never missed the collar. He had a concept of the costume, but it was loose.” Easy rapport with Lucas; leftover apprehension with Kurtz from *Graffiti* — **Ford**: “I heard that Gary had prepped certain members of the English production group with information that I might be very difficult to deal with… most of Paul LeMat’s antics were pinned on me.” **Kurtz** tells Stears to fix Artoo. **Lucas**, after Tunisia: “I went back to England and got a lot of sleep… I was hoping things would go better. But they didn’t.”

Studio day: 8:30 start, tea ~10, lunch 1:15–2:15, tea 4; wrap hard stop **5:30 PM** unless a morning overtime vote. Even mid-scene. Night stage moves become daytime moves — “huge amounts of lost time,” wearing on Lucas. Friday, Day Seventeen: Docking Bay 94 and the *Millennium Falcon* exterior, built in near-record time from the ILM miniature (including mistakes — a too-thick styrene edge, “much to the amusement of Joe Johnston”). **Barry**: “That sort of preconceived idea is very dangerous, because you find yourself building things that don’t fit in any stage.” Only half the *Falcon* fits on Stage 3; too large to move; other sets built around it. *Sight and Sound*’s Philip Strick: “a spectacular pirate spaceship, brooding like a huge concrete manta-ray.”

First day for **Peter Mayhew** as Chewbacca; contract signed **27 February 1976**. Path: newspaper story on big feet → Minoton in *Sinbad and the Eye of the Tiger* (1977); a *Sinbad* makeup man suggested him. “Four or five months after playing a Minoton, I was playing a Wookiee.” Kept his day job as deputy head porter at a London hospital. **Hamill**: “He’s just the most gentle… this big, gangling sweet guy. He was so shy at first.”

Scene 27, oil bath: Daniels lowered into oil and colored water (he’d had it heated — “still winter in England”); tape fails, “my leg began to float away”; ear mic picks up “very outrageous gossip.”

Strategic lunch: Guinness, having mulled Obi-Wan’s death between Tunisia and the studio, “was ready to walk off the picture.” **Lucas**, typography locked: “He was terribly upset… he said, ‘I’m not doing this.’ He didn’t like the idea of being a ghost. That’s the part he really didn’t like, the idea of giving yourself up willingly to join the Force… I had to explain why I was doing it… in the last half of the movie he didn’t have anything to do, it wasn’t dramatic to have him standing around, and I wanted his character to have an impact. Once I explained it was much better for the movie, he looked at it and said, ‘You’re right. This is much better.’ ” “The idea of having Ben go on afterward as part of the Force was a thematic idea that was in the earliest scripts… It was really a Castaneda *Tales of Power* thing.” Afterward Guinness tells Hamill “it would be far more effective if he sacrificed himself.” EE video: Kurtz interviews Guinness at Elstree about the character and acting with “robots” (0:51). Caption: **Declan Mulholland** (Jabba the Hutt) on Stage 3 with Ford and Mayhew; Paul Blake (the “Alien”) fitted with a mask. SCS COMP: 33; SCREEN TIME: 30M 13S.

Tuesday: interior cantina — “the set piece that had been in Lucas’s mind since 1973,” meant as a surprise. **Freeborn**: “George didn’t really want to go way-out on the people, other than the creatures… Everything’s pretty normal up to that point… we weren’t to introduce odd things like Mr. Spock *before* that scene.” Freeborn, Kay, son, six assistants: life casts, rubber and foam; “ugly humans” sketched on photos, okayed by Lucas. **Mollo**: a complete chart with Lucas — peasants, Martians, space pilots, pirates, tied to Freeborn’s heads; “you truss them all up on the day.” Forty-two extras. **Lucas**: “I wanted a round bar… a lot of little alcoves… a big shaft of light coming down the center.” **Barry**: cubicles “where you could have a secret discussion.” In-jokes: five Western Costume spacesuits (*Destination Moon*; *Lost in Space*); a “mini Han Solo” at the bar dressed like Ford.

**20 April**: Ford’s first dialogue with Guinness. **Ford**: “I’ve always been impressed by Guinness, man. It just scared the shit out of me… he could have been any one of a thousand monsters. But he turned out to be an honest, simple, direct kind of guy… prepared and ready.” **Lucas**: Guinness’s “very mischievous sense of humor… making me explain things that didn’t have to be explained… *I’m not going to let you get away with it that easy.*” **Guinness** on Lucas: “His eye is a very pure eye… like a litmus paper… I don’t think I’ve ever come across anyone so immersed in film. I have an idea that he goes to bed in it—wrapped up, you know, in the actual material.” Same alcove: Ford and **Paul Blake** (friend of Daniels). Later in the script Jabba calls him “Greedo”; “during the cantina scene he is simply called an ‘Alien.’ ” Restricted booth, stiff movements. Freeborn ill, unfinished work: mask “not very expressive,” hands hard to maneuver, trouble grasping the blaster. **Lucas**: cantina moved up a week, he “kept adding monsters”; Freeborn hospitalized a few weeks before; “The ones we did have were the background monsters, which weren’t meant to be key monsters.” Day players Angela Staines and Christine Hewett as Han’s “space girls”; others as “contract artistes”; Ted Burnett the bartender. **Hamill**: when Harrison arrives, “new perspectives… Threepio and I were split up a lot and he felt a little left out.” **Mayhew**: “You first see Chewbacca in the cantina background talking to Obi-Wan… Bang, bang, bang, the whole scene worked.” EE video: mid-April daily, Ben swinging into action (Lucas’s reflection in protective glass) (0:18). EE video: **21 April 1976** daily, Ford turning the tables on Greedo (Blake speaks off camera; Lucas says “Cut”); ejected shell from Christian’s blank-firing blaster hits the floor (1:19).

## ENTER THE PRINCESS

**19 April** Fisher arrives; **20 April** first costume fitting at Berman’s. She’d worried about hair and wardrobe. **Fisher**: one sketch had “a little Peter Pan leotard,” rejected; “at least thirty hairdos tried on me. And they didn’t like it when I got to London.” Pat McDermott’s idea — George: “That’s okay.” White dress, “put dirt all over it”; from day one, a gun with charges, practice shooting. **Mollo**: slit up the side so she could move; special boots; “Jean Harlow was the type, so we looked through a few *Vogue*s and came up with that slightly different belt.” Same day: new script pages dated **19 April** circulated, copy to Fox. **Ladd**: “That’s when they decided about the death of Obi-Wan.” (Decision was made in Tunisia, ch. 7; these are the blue pages — record both, do not collapse chronology.)

Last of the name changes: **Luke Starkiller becomes Luke Skywalker.** **Lucas**, typography locked: “That I did because I felt a lot of people were confusing him with someone like Charles Manson. It had very unpleasant connotations.” *Skywalker* was the general’s name in the treatment; “for a long while Lucas didn’t think it was strong enough.” No reshoots: the last name isn’t spoken until the prison cell — “I’m Luke Skywalker … I’ve come to rescue you!” SCS COMP: 42; SCREEN TIME: 39M 32S.

## Musical Names

**McQuarrie**: “George kind of swapped names around… ‘I just don’t want to think up new ones.’ ” Lucas on final choices, typography locked:

- **Han Solo:** “It could have been from some Solo [paper] cups.”
- **Obi-Wan “Ben” Kenobi:** “I picked *Ben* because it was a very easy name; *Kenobi* was a combination of a lot of words that I put together. The name came out of thin air.”
- **Leia Organa:** “I just picked that name. But there was a planet Organa Major in the film for a long time. And she ended up with the name of the planet because she was originally from there, though afterward her planet’s name was changed to Alderaan.”
- **Darth Vader:** “That’s just another one of those things that came out of thin air. It sort of appeared in my head one day. I had lots of Darth this and Darth that, and Dark Lord of the Sith. The early name was actually *Dark Water.* Then I added lots of last names, Vaders and Wilsons and Smiths, and I just came up with the combination of *Darth* and *Vader.*”
- **Chewbacca:** “I came up with a whole bunch of Wookiee words, just changing words around, and I liked *Chewbacca* the best.” (Rinzler: *Wookiee* from *THX 1138*, Terry McGovern wild track: “I think I just ran over a Wookiee.”)
- **R2-D2:** late night on *THX 1138*, looking for “Reel 2, Dialogue 2,” someone yelled “R2D2”; Lucas and Walter Murch “both loved that name,” notebook.
- **C-3PO:** “Once I had *R2-D2*, I had to do something sort of like it, so I just made up another one.”
- **Moff Tarkin:** “just a name that was made up out of nowhere.”
- **Jawa and Tusken Raider:** “I looked around until I found a name that fit them… I knew I wanted the Jawas to be very small and very shrouded… little eyes that bugged out, like in the forest when you have all those little eyes.”

## REPORT NOS. 25–26: FRIDAY, APRIL 23–MONDAY, APRIL 26, 1976

STAGE 7: INT. BEN KENOBI’s CAVE, SC: 42 [OBI-WAN VIEWS LEIA’s MESSAGE, ETC.]. STAGE 2: INT. DEATH STAR, SCS: 86 [KENOBI SNEAKS AROUND]; 111 [VADER FOLLOWS KENOBI].

Ben’s home: one day first unit, one second unit, later pickups (lens problem). **Barry**: “Ben Kenobi—what does he do in his spare time?” Pared-down dwelling: rug, lightsaber trunk, mementos. Dialogue is “one of the two or three main expository scenes”: Force explained, mission revealed, lightsaber unveiled (glowed when viewed through the camera). **Hamill**: “I think Ben Kenobi is like the father I always wanted… when I find out that Guinness knew my father… it was easy for me because I couldn’t help but be in awe.” Guinness to *The Sunday Times*, **2 May**: “I tried to make him uncomplicated… to be simple carries a lot of weight. The laser sword… rather like a Japanese sword with a row of laser buttons. But I must confess I’m pretty much lost as to what is required of me… I simply trust the director.” Improvisation: script “You must do what you feel” becomes “You must do what you feel is right, of course.” Lightsaber: spinning wooden sword, partly reflective, photographed through a half-silvered mirror — “illuminated from within.” Daniels sits yoga-legged, warns he could “chop off fingers without knowing.”

5:30 stop again. **Lucas** wanted to finish leftover shots and move at night; they wouldn’t. Contract “quarters”: fifteen minutes, up to four, if mid-shot. He sets “a very elaborate, long dolly shot in the Obi-Wan scene, and I took the four quarters—but the crew was very angry with me. I was a bearded kid, around thirty years old. I was just this crazy American who was doing this really dopey movie.” Friday walk-through of Monday’s Death Star corridor; panels added; **Darth Vader, played by David Prowse**, “quickly completed his scene.” **Prowse**, typography locked: “George told me he was making a space fantasy and asked me to consider playing either Chewbacca or Darth Vader… I turned down Chewbacca at once. I know that people remember villains longer than heroes. At the time I didn’t know I’d be wearing a mask. And throughout production I thought Vader’s voice would be mine.” (He had played large parts in *A Clockwork Orange*; owned weight-lifting gyms.)

Jympson problem: Lucas finally sees assemblies — questionable take selection, trouble with match-cuts. He is hesitant to criticize (not enough prep, little guidance). To a crew member at the time: “Jympson is in an impossible position, cutting material that I haven’t even talked to him about. It’s totally unfair for me to judge him now.” The editor becomes “very impatient,” complains loudly. **Barry** (a couple of weeks later): “the editor was very unsympathetic to George.” Lucas “fairly touchy about people seeing rushes” (Barry): low-budget films were private; he doesn’t want people “losing confidence.” EE video: Prowse being dressed as Vader (no audio; 1:30). EE video dated **25 June 1976** (later than this report block): gonk-droid gag, Jack Purvis, doesn’t make the final cut; colored slugs where film was lifted (0:45). SCS COMP: 45; SCREEN TIME: 44M 29S.

## Death Star Control Room / Death Star Conference Room

**Lucas**: “most of the Death Star really is just one set.” **Barry**: panels made by an outside company in plastic, reused, “quite expensive.” Cushing with Prowse in control room and conference room. Larry Cuba graphic originally for monitoring the heroes on a big screen. First idea: *Things to Come* (1936) — “huge, silent, sophisticated,” just a screen; Barry added podiums; a right-hand wall cast quickly from other-stage panels. Diagonal-feeling door actually “sliding down horizontally.” George’s “Tinkertoy”: maybe six units, endless assemblies; sometimes they’d “change the ‘continual corridor’ while they stood and waited.”

Conference room: **Lucas** had “a very vague idea” — “Design a round room around a round table.” Chairs with a slit in the back; two taller to accent the sitter. “He decided he wanted to keep the round motif… Intellectually, it’s all wrong, because… they’re the enemy; the interiors should really be very angular. But it works.” **Cushing** often shot waist-up: English size 12 shoes, boots far too small, “absolute agony”; “whenever possible, could you please shoot me from the waist up? These boots are *killing me.*” EE audio: Barry on designing sets, other departments, Lucas’s involvement (Interview by Lippincott, **17 May 1976**; 1:33).

## ATTACK OF THE GIANT SETS

Now four days behind. **Watts** to Lippincott, **10 May 1976**: “literally only one day ahead on the set construction.” *THX* and *Graffiti* ~40 people; *The Star Wars* **950** paid employees, two large studios. **Lucas**: “It was really like riding a freight train at about 120 miles per hour and having the guys trying to build the track in front of you as you go.” Messages degrade down the chain; frustrating for him and Barry. Watts: changes should come through the production office — “now happening.” **Barry**, 17 May: “You can dictate the letter, but it’s going to come out in somebody else’s handwriting. It’s driving me potty… Ironically, the more you do get involved, the more it ends up like a Stanley Kubrick film, taking fourteen months instead of eight weeks. And the sets all are terrific, but nobody notices that the actors are very dull.” Night walks after rushes, “nothing’s a horrid surprise.” Lucas: two to four cameras, decide shots on the set. Crew and Fox expect wide establishing shots of expensive sets; he often starts close, “newsreel” / “shot on location” (Barry). **Ladd**: negative reaction from England — “We spent all this money on these big, elaborate sets and he’s only shooting a piece of it!” **Watts**: every crew member is there “to help George tell a story… I wish I had a piece of the action…” Caption: prison floor of off-the-shelf plastic forklift pallets; Hamill first speaks “I’m Luke Skywalker …” SCS COMP: 65; SCREEN TIME: 60M 01S.

## CONFLICT

**Kurtz**: Barry and Taylor “didn’t get along very well… didn’t talk much, unless I forced them to.” Barry didn’t leave enough room for lighting; Taylor created problems for Barry. Death Star walls: Barry wanted “navy-blue steel,” too dark in the conference room, changed to lighter gray; metal light panels distorted under “thousands of photo floods.” **Stears**: Barry’s artistic sets “don’t work for us… Gil’s had awful problems.”

Ladd visits late April/early May to see dailies, primarily to placate Fox (*Star Wars* “over $10 million—how are we ever going to get our money back?” — Hellman) and shore up his own base after *The Blue Bird* (released 6 April), later “turkey of the decade.” Shown a rough assembly without Lucas. **Ladd**, typography locked: “my real reaction was: *utter and complete panic.* I didn’t sleep that night. But the next day I spoke to George… ‘This is *not* what I want and this is *not* what it’s going to look like.’ ” End-battle page-count fear (three-hour picture); Lucas says it’s timed, whole movie “would not go over something like two-ten.”

After Ladd leaves, another Fox executive end-runs in London, *Lucky Lady* hangover. **Lucas**: “They said it all looked fuzzy… Peter Beale told Gil, without telling me, to stop using the gauze… a day later I noticed a difference in dailies… Then I found out a few minutes later that the studio had told him not to do it.” Already unhappy that Taylor hadn’t given him documentary-style lighting; he’d been working more with the operator on framing. Feels “betrayed by both his DP and the studio.” Five days over; R2 still unreliable; costs escalating.
