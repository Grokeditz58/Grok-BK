## Opening (p. 14)

*American Graffiti* released 1 August 1973 after Lucas arranged extra screenings to push Universal into booking it. Pays debts; mortgage on a San Anselmo house; January 1974, a second building, an 1869 Victorian, remodeled as offices, carriage house as editing rooms (later Park Way). **Lucas** on the first writing year: “When I was writing *Star Wars*, for the first year, there was an infinite number of distractions… *Graffiti* was a huge hit, plus I was restoring my office at the same time. Building a screening room kept me going for nine, ten months.” No hired writer (had not worked on *THX* or *Graffiti*). Daily routine at Medway, “a little tower,” desk built from three doors, typography locked: “I grew up in a middle-class Midwest-style American town with the corresponding work ethic… I sit at my desk for eight hours a day no matter what happens, even if I don’t write anything. It’s a terrible way to live… I can’t get out of my chair until five o’clock or five thirty or whenever the news comes on.” Calendar quotas, five pages a day, “I’ve always got about one page done by four o’clock.” Notebook, bed, shower, driving. Videotapes TV dogfights (*The Bridges at Toko-Ri*, later captions *Tora! Tora! Tora!*, *The Dam Busters*) onto 16mm, edited “according to my story of *Star Wars*” for ship movement. Original vision, typography locked: “one of the key visions I had of the film when I started was of a dogfight in outer space with spaceships—two ships flying through space shooting each other… In *Star Trek* it was always one ship sitting here and another ship sitting there, and they shot these little lasers and one of them disappeared.”

## CHANGING BUREAUCRATS

10 January 1974: Lucasfilm loans out “George Lucas” as director to “The Star Wars Corporation.” Winter 1973–74, often alone (Marcia in LA, then Tucson, cutting Scorsese’s *Alice Doesn’t Live Here Anymore*); Kurtz still on *Apocalypse Now*. **Lucas** on reading, typography locked: “It’s not really research so much as mythology and fantasy are taking over my life. I read everything from *John Carter of Mars* to *The Golden Bough*… I’m trying to make a classic genre picture, a classic space opera—and there are certain concepts that have been developed by writers, primarily Edgar Rice Burroughs, that are traditional, and you keep those traditional aspects about the project.” **Martin Scorsese**: Lucas “had all these books with him, like Isaac Asimov’s *Guide to the Bible*… he wanted to tap into the collective unconscious of fairy tales.” Screened Hawks’s *Air Force* (1943) and Curtiz’s *Robin Hood* (1938). EE audio (Rinzler EE): Lucas on feelings post–*American Graffiti* (Interview by Arnold, 1979; 1:38). Cross-ref notes 169.

Legal-pad notes transferring *Apocalypse Now* into the fantasy, typography locked: “Theme: Aquilae is a small independent country like North Vietnam threatened by a neighbor or provincial rebellion, instigated by gangsters aided by empire. Fight to get rightful planet back. Half of system has been lost to gangsters … The empire is like America ten years from now, after gangsters assassinated the Emperor and were elevated to power in a rigged election … We are at a turning point: fascism or revolution.” **Lucas** on structure vs. Godard/cinema verité taste: “The biggest thing was just finding a story that was interesting but not a gimmick.” “I like Godard films… In film school I tended away from storytelling… Then I forced my way back into storytelling… This is one of the reasons why with *Star Wars* I want to attempt a storytelling film.”

Character notes, typography locked (ellipsis joins as in the book): “Notes on new beginning … for three main characters—the general, the princess, the boy (Starkiller)—make development chart … Han very old (150 years)…Establish impossibility of attacking Death Star … Should threat be bigger, more sinister?…A conflict between freedom and conformity … Tell at least two stories: Starkiller becomes a man (not good enough); Valorum wakes up (morally speaking)…Valorum like a Green Beret who realizes wrong of Empire … Make Owen Lars a geologist or something … thundersaber …” First appearance of the Vader name in this book’s note-lists, typography locked: “Kane Highsinger/Jedi friend; Leia Aquilae/princess; General Vader/Imperial Commander; Han Solo/friend …”—and, “for the first time,” “Seethreepio” and “Artwo Deetwo,” both listed as “workmen.” Later: “two workmen as robots? One dwarf/one *Metropolis* type.” **Lucas**: “The law of robotics is obviously where the robots came from… Isaac Asimov invented those robots.” Still later: “Make film more point-of-view of robots.”

## INTERREGNUM

Rough draft completed May 1974; first draft July 1974, “stories and dialogue are identical. Only the names have been changed.” Many later elements present “but as before, nothing is in its final form yet.” **Lucas** on structure: first half an escape (princess and general to a neutral planet); he decides “it would be much better to have it be a rescue movie instead of an escape movie.” Leia is captured about halfway through; rescue on Yavin and then the space fortress becomes the prime mover.

Common to both drafts: “the force of others,” which Rinzler flags as “an extension of the idea of ‘will’ in the earlier *Journal of the Whills*.” Lucas first wrote a related concept in a **deleted *THX 1138* scene**, typography locked:

> THX: “…there must be something independent; a force, reality.”
> SRT: “You mean like OMM. [the state-sanctioned deity]”
> THX: “Not like OMM as we know him, but the reality behind the illusion of OMM.”

**Lucas**, typography locked: “The ‘Force of others’ is what all basic religions are based on, especially the Eastern religions… which is, essentially, that there is a force, God, whatever you want to call it.” Rinzler: at this point “only the good guys seem to be aware of it.” Parenthetical: Laurent Bouzereau, *Star Wars: The Annotated Screenplays*, points out the phrase as a variation on “May the Lord be with you and your spirit” / *Dominus vobiscum et cum spiritu tuo* — **Bouzereau via Rinzler, not a Lucas line; do not collapse into Lucas’s own theology.** EE audio (Rinzler EE): Lucas on myths for children and how *Star Wars* was designed to fill that gap (Interview by Arnold, 1979; 1:35). Cross-ref notes 169.

## The *Star Wars* Rough-Draft Summary, May 1974 (p. 19)

Bound copy: Flash Gordon–like mock-up cover; comic-book illustrations; “Han Solo resembles a swamp creature,” “Captain Starkiller” (Annikin) a Buck Rogers type. Opens on Utapau; five moons; roll-up, typography locked in part: until the GREAT REBELLION, the JEDI BENDU were “the most feared warriors in the universe,” “personal bodyguards of the EMPEROR,” architects of the IMPERIAL SPACE FORCE; now hunted to near extinction by “a ferocious and sinister rival warrior sect, THE KNIGHTS OF SITH.”

Annikin Starkiller, “a tall, heavy-set boy of eighteen,” with father Kane (a Jedi) and ten-year-old brother Deak on Utapau (Kessil system). A “large, sinister SITH warrior in black robes and a face mask” with a “long lazersword” cuts Deak down; Annikin holds off “the seven-foot-tall Sith Knight” until Kane “cuts in half the face-masked warrior.” **This seven-foot Sith is not named Vader in this draft — do not collapse with the general.** They bury Deak and go to Aquilae.

Alderaan is “capital of the new Empire.” Governor Hoedaack appointed First Lord of the Aquilaean System. Clieg Whitsun; Bail Antilles arrested. **Governor Hoedaack talks with “Darth Vader, a tall, grim-looking general,”** about conquering Aquilae; Vader thinks it will be difficult because General Skywalker leads the defenses. On Aquilae, Count Sandage counsels surrender. **General Luke Skywalker, “a large man in his sixties,”** argues for resistance; Grande Mouff Tarkin “wears the long, black robes of the Aquilaean religion.” King Kayos adjourns: **“May the force of others be with you all.”** Princess Leia, “about fourteen years old, possessing a soft beauty and iron will,” goes to Yuell to study. King’s sons Biggs and Windy.

Kane asks Skywalker to take Annikin as “Padawan Learner”; reveals “There is nothing left but my head and right arm. I’ve lost too much, Luke. I’m dying.” A giant asteroid or moon detaches from the Anchorhead system — “as big as our third moon.” Palace of Lite: King and Queen watch “two giant twin suns set in the green sky.” Kane has gone to the spaceport of Gordon “to meet an old friend, **Han Solo, the Ureallian**.” King leaves for Amsel; Whitsun reports “A giant space fortress.” Annikin retrieves Leia from the Academy (a handmaiden remains as decoy). Attack on Aquilae; “Look at the size of that thing!” Devil Six. Construction robots ARTWO DETWO (R2D2), “a short (three-foot) claw-armed tri-ped,” and See Threepio (C3PO), “a tall, gleaming android of human proportions,” blown into freight canisters; Threepio: “This is madness … I’m still not accustomed to space travel.” Artwo can talk: “You’re a mindless, useless philosopher.” Lifepod ejected. King and convoy destroyed; Senate ends the war; remaining ships destroyed as they retreat; last to die is Mace, the pilot leader. **Vader orders the invasion.**

Robots trek Jundland, “ ‘No-Man’s Land,’ where the rugged desert mesas meet the foreboding dune sea,” argue, split; R2D2 finds Annikin and the princess in a landspeeder; they also pick up C3PO. Hidden fortress canyon; Leia’s father is dead, mother and two brothers alive. Queen sends Skywalker to Ophuchi with “the distillation of Aquilae’s greatest scientific minds” (what the Empire wants) and Leia. Party includes Annikin, Skywalker, robots, Captain Whitsun, princes Biggs and Windy, Leia. Skywalker cuts Count Sandage in two; fortress self-destructs. Invasion army on the dune sea. **In the palace, General Vader meets with Prince Valorum, a Black Knight of the Sith, whose goal is to track Skywalker.** Romance Annikin/Leia. Cantina on the outskirts of Gordon: General cuts an arm off one creature, a rodent-creature in two, a multi-eyed creature “from chin to groin.” In an alley, Skywalker and Whitsun meet **Han Solo, “a huge green-skinned monster with no nose and large gills.”**

Slum: Kane Starkiller; droids play “chess”; they discuss the space fortress, or the “death star.” To flee they need an extra energy pack; Kane rips one from his mechanical chest. **“May the force of others be with you,” he says just before dying.** Princes hidden in microcases. Trap: freighter captain is Valorum; princess gassed; they later overpower guards, hijack a royal starship, smash through the hangar, “hunter-destroyer spaceships,” asteroid belt, Forbidden Systems; Cliegg accidentally killed. Yavin, jungle, “eerie fog-laden purgatory.” Yourellian trappers and five Wookees — “huge gray and furry beasts”; Chewbacca “the largest of the Wookees.” Owen Lars, “an aged and scruffy-looking anthropologist,” wife Beru. **Han can speak Wookee**, confers with Chewbacca; they assault the royal outpost. Leia taken back to Aquilae; R2D2 projects the “death star fortress.” Skywalker teaches Wookees to fly. **Within the death star, Vader interrogates Princess Leia.** Annikin undercover; R2D2 “punches his claw arm into the computer socket”; Annikin disguises as a stormtrooper, is trapped and gassed. **Vader tells Valorum to kill him.** Skywalker leads nine starships painted in “colorful Wookee designs.” Valorum has a change of heart (“the Jedi are nobler than the bureaucrats”), helps Annikin rescue the princess; trash-compactor chute; aerial attack knocks out power. **Vader wants to abandon ship, but Governor Hoedaack won’t depart.** Lifepods; the couple kisses; the death star explodes. Throne room, Palace of Lite: Queen Leia; **Han presents Chewbacca and a delegation of Wookees with a treaty, gifts, and a medal of honor**; Valorum stands next to Han; robots designated class A-4, to serve Annikin Starkiller, “the new Lord Protector of Aquilae.”

STAR WARS PROGRESSION (selected; do not collapse elements that are still separate in this draft): roll-up; Emperor; Knights of the Sith; **“Seven-foot-tall Sith Knight”** (the Utapau warrior, not Vader); **“Darth Vader (a humanoid general)”**; Darklighter and Biggs as names; Senate; Grande Mouff Tarkin; **The Force (of Others)**; “May the Force (of Others) be with you” (King Kayos); Princess Leia; twin suns; Jedi in training; half-man/half-machine (Kane Starkiller); **“Han Solo (an alien Ureallian, friend of Kane Starkiller)”**; space fortress attacks Aquilae; R2D2 and C3PO; robots on the dune sea; stormtroopers; death star as alternate name; prison break; gunport dogfight; **“Chewbacca, a huge, furry Wookee, with whom Han Solo can communicate (they meet on the jungle planet Yavin)”**; Owen Lars an anthropologist; Artwo projects Death Star; **“Darth Vader puts Leia in prison on the Death Star”**; Artwo plugs in; Annikin disguises himself; Annikin and Valorum rescue Leia into a garbage chute; award ceremony.

## The *Star Wars* First-Draft Summary, July 1974 (p. 23)

Story and dialogue “exactly the same”; names changed (Lucas had treated rough-draft names as placeholders). List, typography locked as given: Ogana (Utapau); Dai Nogas (Jedi Bendu); Legion of Lettow (Knights of Sith); Justin Valor (Annikin Starkiller); Akira (father, Kane); Binks (brother, Deak); Townowi (Aquilae); Granicus (Alderaan); Son Hhut (Dashhat); Dodonna (Valorum); Jawas (Wookees); Hu Tho (Owen Lars). Also: Whitsun → Oxus; princes Biggs and Windy → Oeta and Puck; Princess Leia → Princess Zara; R2D2 and C3PO → A-2 and C-3; Padawan Learner → Juwo Learner. A note, typography locked: “Starkiller (Mifune)—a cross between *Yojimbo* and *Seven Samurai*…” — Rinzler: “It’s possible that Lucas contemplated changing the general’s name from Skywalker to Starkiller”; Kane does become Akira. STAR WARS PROGRESSION: Dai Noga as a name (for Jedi); a Hhut character (“Son Hhat,” ruler of the galactic kingdom); Jawas (“huge gray and furry beasts” — still the Wookee role, not the later diminutive traders); Dodonna as a name (a Sith Knight).

## A BEWILDERING JOURNEY

Only four copies of the first draft. Someone stamped Ladd’s copy “EYES ONLY—DO NOT COPY UNDER ANY CIRCUMSTANCES.” **Ladd**: “The first draft was a long time coming… We had a number of conversations about it, and seemed to be in sync about certain things.” Readers list: (1) Matthew Robbins and Hal Barwood; (2) Bill and Gloria Huyck; (3) John Milius; (4) Haskell Wexler; (5) Francis Ford Coppola; (6) Phil Kaufman. **Lucas**: “I run around with a crowd of writers… I usually help them on their editing and they help me on my scriptwriting.” Park Way (the converted Victorian) as a post-Zoetrope haven. **Lucy Wilson**, first hire, 1974, office on the sunporch: Barwood and Robbins writing *The Bingo Long Traveling All-Stars & Motor Kings*; Michael Ritchie on *Smile*; “George had invited these filmmakers basically to share his house with the same goal: that it would be a film community.” **Barwood** on lunches in San Anselmo and mutual encouragement when projects “stall out.” **Willard Huyck**: “When people read *Star Wars* originally, they didn’t have a clue really… it was really a universe that nobody could understand from the scripts.” That “nobody” included Ladd. August 1974: *Graffiti* had five Oscar nominations and a Golden Globe. **Jeff Berg**: “Fox had a very good deal, a hot new name in Hollywood for very little money… several times Laddie read the screenplay and said he just wasn’t sure what George was trying to say.” **Lucas**: “After *Graffiti* became a big hit, they couldn’t refuse it… Just in terms of politics and the political intrigue of Hollywood.” Fox goes to a second draft — “Lucas’s preference, because the movie he wanted to write into existence was proving elusive.”

## THE STUDIO STUTTER

**Lucas** on rewriting: “when you write, sometimes you rationalize away particular problems. You say, *I’ll deal with that later.* … now the next step is even more painful, because I have to confront the problems in a more serious way.” Fox delays the production-distribution contract (usually two or three months after the initial agreement). **Lucas**: “I got mad about a year after I had started working on it… they wouldn’t give me a contract. They were saying, ‘Well, he didn’t really finish the script, either’—but writing a contract is different from writing a script.” **Tom Pollock**: “It was not until late 1974 that we even got a piece of paper out of Fox because they are so fucking slow.” Meetings with William Immerman and Jeff Berg, 23 August 1974; Pollock letter 4 September with amendments to the Memorandum Agreement, typography locked: Paragraph 1, “The Star Wars Corporation will own … all sequel rights [to] the screenplay ‘The Star Wars.’ ” Paragraph 5, “merchandising rights and commercial tie-ups”; 5.B, “Either party may license merchandising rights … subject to the other party’s bettering the licensing deal.” 5.D, “SWC shall have the sole and exclusive right to use … the name ‘The Star Wars’ in connection with wholesale or retail outlets for the sale of merchandising items.” Pollock follow-up 17 December: “It is very important to me and to George Lucas that these matters be settled at once.” Immerman 27 December: can “proceed with preparation of a formal contract.” Correspondence then peters out; “nothing actually agreed upon.” **Warren Hellman**: “there was a lack of respect for George… part of the negotiations was just to see how much they could push George around because they felt like they could.”

## THE DARING DEVELOPMENT OF HEROES

**Ladd** on the twelve-week delivery in the deal memo: calls, delays, “I always kept thinking to myself, *Well, next week it will be here*…” Marcia cutting *Taxi Driver*. Second-draft notes, typography locked: “Sith knights look like Linda Blair in *Exorcist* [1973],” “as the bad guys are rechristened the Sith.” “Vader—do something evil in prison to Deak,” “as General Vader becomes a Dark Lord of the Sith, and Deak reappears as a brother, this time to Luke—the name of a boy, not a general, who replaces Annikin/Justin as the story’s main character.” “Luke reluctantly accepts the burden (artist, not a warrior, fear); establish Luke as a good pilot … farm boy: fulfills the legend of the son of the sons; pulls the sword from the stone. All he wants in life is to become a starpilot.” Leia back as Luke’s cousin, secondary: “Leia: tomboy, bright, tough, really soft and afraid; loves Luke but not admitting it.” **Han**, typography locked: “Make Han in bar like Bogart—freelance tough guy for hire.” Unused notes: “Jabba in prison cell”; Leia/Luke “seven stages/crucial scenes,” she crowned queen at the end; **“Han Solo a Wookiee? Wookiees talk to plants and animals.”** (contemplated; not the draft as written — record, do not collapse). Films noted: Ford’s *They Were Expendable* (1945), *Ben-Hur* (1959).

Chewbacca crystallization (Rinzler narrative, from Lucas’s habit of working anywhere): Alaskan malamute **Indiana** in the passenger seat, often mistaken from behind for a person; “Lucas slowly combined Indiana, the Wookiees of Yavin, and the name *Chewbacca* into Han Solo’s copilot: a great big, intelligent, friendly-but-fierce dog-creature sitting by his side.”

**Lucas** on writing, typography locked: “You go crazy writing. You get psychotic… You can just get so convoluted in what you’re thinking about that you get depressed, unbearably depressed… It always seems bad when you’re doing it.” Second draft finished **28 January 1975**, “still far from a shooting script”; archive copy, red cover, Frazetta-like illustration.

## A STARTLING SPACE FORTRESS

Major structural break between first and second drafts (Rinzler): the first draft was “far too long for one movie,” so the second begins “in the middle of the first—at the moment the robots escape.” In the first they flee the Death Star; in the second, now on the side of the good guys, they flee a besieged rebel starship. Note: “Whole film must be told from robots’ point of view,” a further development of *Hidden Fortress* peasants. He contemplated two movies (end film one with the Death Star escape; film two from the jungle-planet crash to the fortress attack) but instead “combined and juxtaposed many of the more exciting scenes into a shorter, tighter single film.” Structural note: “time lock” — “the empire has a terrible new weapon, a fortress station so powerful it can destroy a planet, possibly even a sun. It must be stopped before it can be put to use.” The fortress becomes “a horrific planet-destroying weapon,” “a character as well as an all-important structural element.”

Other *Hidden Fortress* elements jettisoned: the general is no longer major, “literally sleeping through some scenes”; the princess “has disappeared completely”; Justin/Annikin, now Luke, takes center stage. Rinzler gloss, typography on the split: “the character of the young Jedi Annikin/Justin has been split in two: **Han Solo takes on his womanizing ways and cavalier attitude, while Luke embodies his serious side, his metaphysical side.**” Both as bildungsroman figures. “Luke’s journey is told in part through his relationship with the Force, which in the second draft is more thoroughly analyzed. **It now has a dark side, which the bad guys can use**—thus enhancing their power to almost equal that of the good guys,” with a Hitchcock comparison (Rinzler, not Lucas).

## *Adventures of the Starkiller, Episode I: The Star Wars*, Second-Draft Summary, January 28, 1975 (p. 27)

Opens with a prophecy and a new roll-up, typography locked: ““… And in the time of greatest despair there shall come a savior, and he shall be known as: THE SON OF THE SUNS.”—*Journal of the Whills*, 3:127” The REPUBLIC GALACTICA is dead; trader barons; FIRST GALACTIC EMPIRE. Until “the tragic Holy rebellion of 06’,” the JEDI BENDU OF ASHLA were the most powerful warriors; “generations of Jedi Bendu knights learned the ways of the mysterious FORCE OF OTHERS, and acted as the guardians of peace and justice in the REPUBLIC.” Hunted by “THE BLACK KNIGHTS OF THE SITH.” Civil wars; seventy small solar systems unite; “Under the command of a mighty Jedi warrior known as THE STARKILLER, the REBEL ALLIANCE has won a crushing victory…” One more such defeat and Imperial control of the Outlands could be lost.

Rebel space fighter overwhelmed by four giant Imperial Star Destroyers; destroys one. ARTOO DETOO (R2-D2) and SEE THREEPIO (C-3PO) in the passageway; they see only the legs of their captain, **Deak Starkiller**. R2-D2 now “communicates through beeps and whistles, which only another robot can understand.” Ten stormtroopers “wearing ominous armored spacesuits” drop onto the craft. An Aquillian Ranger with a “long, deadly laser sword” and explosive “chrome ping-pong sized balls” appears, destroys a Star Destroyer, falls in battle. Deak sends R2 robots to Utapau to find his brother Luke at Owen Lars’s; C-3PO goes with R2-D2 in a lifepod.

Then, typography locked: “The remaining stormtroopers bow low toward the doorway. An awesome, seven-foot BLACK KNIGHT OF THE SITH makes his way into the blinding light of the cockpit area. This is LORD DARTH VADER, right hand to the MASTER OF THE SITH. His sinister face is partially obscured by his flowing black robes and grotesque breath mask, which are in sharp contrast to the fascist white armored suits of the Imperial stormtroopers … Taking a deep breath, he raises his arms and every object that isn’t bolted down is picked up by an invisible force and hurled at the young Jedi … Slowly Deak is forced to his knees as all his energy is drained from his being. Finally, he collapses in a heap. The sinister knight lets out a horrible, shrieking laugh …” **Vader takes Deak prisoner, “assumes that he is the son of the suns (Starkiller) and that they have altered the prophecy.”** (Vader in this draft is the seven-foot Sith; the earlier “humanoid general” role has been transformed — record the change, do not flatten the two drafts into one figure.)

Utapau: robots argue and separate; dwarf-like Jawas, sandcrawler, escape after a prisoners’ break; a slimy mechanic points them to Owen Lars and also reports them to a “gray Tusken.” Moisture ranch of Owen and Beru Lars; Luke’s young twin brothers Biggs and Windy; sixteen-year-old cousin Leia, daughter of Owen and Beru. They seek “Angel Blue,” code name for Luke. Luke Starkiller interrupts “laser sword training” and introduces himself as “the Skywalker.” R2-D2 projects a hologram from Deak: take the **Kiber Crystal** (“a plot device similar to the ring in J.R.R. Tolkien’s trilogy” — Rinzler gloss) to their father, “The Starkiller,” on Ogana Major. Owen: their planet is under siege by the Imperial Legions of Alderaan; Deak is probably imprisoned on Alderaan; Owen has trained Luke, “not yet a Jedi,” but a good pilot. Luke is divided (archaeology studies) but accepts; visits his mother’s grave as Utapau’s twin suns set. Talk with the twins, typography locked in substance: in another time, “a holy man called the Skywalker became aware of a powerful energy field which he believed influenced the destiny of all living creatures…” Biggs: “The ‘FORCE OF OTHERS’!” The Skywalker “was able to know the force, and it communicated with him.” “As you know, the ‘FORCE OF OTHERS’ has two halves: Ashla, the good, and Bogan, the paraforce or evil part.” He taught only his twelve children, who became the Jedi Bendu of the Ashla. After assassinations and rigged elections the Great Senate was “secretly controlled by the Power and Transport guilds”; Jedi who tried to purge it were denounced; “The Senate secretly instigated race wars, and aided anti-government terrorists… a totally controlled and oppressive police state was welcomed… The Empire was born.” “During one of his lessons a young PADAWAN-JEDI, a boy named Darklighter, came to know the evil half of the force, and fell victim to the spell of the dreaded Bogan. He ran away from his instructor and taught the evil ways of the Bogan Force to a clan of Sith pirates… They became the personal bodyguards of the Emperor.” “With every Jedi death, contact with the Ashla grows weaker, and the force of the Bogan grows more powerful.” Their father is a Jedi called “The Starkiller”; tomorrow Luke will join him.

Luke takes the “diamond-like” Kiber Crystal; loads the robots; a Tusken agent follows; Mos Eisley. Descriptions, typography locked: “Luke is standing next to HAN SOLO, a young Corellian pirate only a few years older than himself. He is a burly-bearded but ruggedly handsome boy dressed in a gaudy array of flamboyant apparel … Han turns to his companion, CHEWBACCA, an eight foot tall, savage-looking creature resembling a huge gray bushbaby-monkey with fierce ‘baboon’-like fangs… He is a two-hundred-year-old ‘WOOKIEE,’ and a sight to behold.” Cantina fight: “Solo ineffectively trying to defend Luke,” then Luke lights his “laser sword” and dispatches the bad guys. Solo will take them to Ogana Major “if Luke’s father will pay him a million credits on arrival”; Han is in debt to Oxus, “who is of ‘the grossest dimensions.’” No ship of his own: he “fakes a reactor overload on a pirate ship he serves on, causing the captain and other crew members—including one Jabba the Hutt—to flee in terror. Han then steals the ship,” keeping Chewbacca and science officer **Montross Holdaack** (“almost entirely robotic, with only his head and right arm organic”). Jump to “hyper-skip.”

Ogana Major “has mysteriously vanished—destroyed in an enormous attack.” They go to Alderaan to rescue Deak. City “suspended in a sea of cirrus methane”; Luke and Han steal stormtrooper uniforms, Chewbacca as prisoner; rescue the dying Deak; “vicious Dai Noga creatures” in the bowels of the city; trash compactor while fleeing two Sith Knights; four fin-winged TIE fighters; hyper-skip. Kiber Crystal keeps Deak alive on the way to the rebel stronghold on the fourth moon of Yavin; they are pursued by a giant space station. Lifepods to the Massassi Outpost; three Aquillian rangers, one of them Bail Antilles. **General Dodana** debriefs the Death Star attack. Grande Mouff Tarkin, “a thin, bird-like commander of the outland Kesselian Dragoons.” Behind him, **THE STARKILLER**, “a wizened old man with long silver hair,” apparently asleep, then “crystal-clear eyes open”; “an incalculable number of years,” long silver beard, penetrating gray-blue eyes; “An aura of power radiates from the ancient Jedi that almost knocks Tarkin over.” The Crystal revitalizes Luke’s father, who intends to train him as a Jedi. Exhaust-port plan; the station destroyed Ogana Major. **Han receives eight million, “but refuses to join the rebels.”**

Luke flies with C-3PO, R2-D2, and Antilles; Kiber Crystal in his pocket; attack on “the dual poles of the fortress.” **“Darth Vader leads the defense of the Death Star, ordering the men to their ships, but he soon senses the presence of the Kiber Crystal.”** They nearly trap Luke’s ship “when Han, Chewbacca, and Montross come to the rescue. **Vader crashes into Han’s ship, kamikaze-like, killing himself** and disabling the pirate craft.” Antilles and C-3PO fire the exhaust-port shots that destroy the Death Star (not Luke alone). ## LOOKING BACK

**Lucas**, typography locked: “While I was writing the rough draft, they ended up in this cantina about halfway through; they walked in and there were all these monsters… When I wrote the second draft about the only thing that remained was the cantina scene and the dogfight.” Swashbuckler caption context: Flynn in *Captain Blood* and *The Adventures of Robin Hood* (with Rathbone); Granger in *Scaramouche*. **Ladd**: “In came another script and it had a lot of different scenes and a lot of character changes.” The serial idea — “*Episode I*” in the title, and an end roll-up promising more — “must have added to Ladd’s confusion” (Rinzler). **Coppola**: “My recollection was that George had a whole *Star Wars* script that I thought was fine, and then he chucked it and started again with these two robots lost in the wilderness. I remember thinking that the first script he showed me was really good and I was sort of curious as to why he was, you know, dumping that.” **Hal Barwood**: “It started off in horrible shape… It did have Artoo and it did have Threepio, but it was very hard for us to wrap our heads around the idea of a golden robot and this little beer can… But George never gave up and he worked and worked and worked.”
