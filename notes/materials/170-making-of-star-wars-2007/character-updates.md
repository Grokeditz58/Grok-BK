### Fourth-draft Beddoes boards: Kenobi traps Vader behind a blast door and is helped to the ship

### Diamond: “I’ve got these laser swords—I don’t want broadswords and I don’t want fencing”

**Speaker:** Peter Diamond, reporting Lucas’s brief; Diamond on Prowse.
**Source:** The Obi-Wan/Vader fight is filmed first, three days beginning 27 May 1976, before their meeting on 1 June. “George said, ‘I’ve got these laser swords—I don’t want broadswords and I don’t want fencing. I want it somewhere in between.’ So I had to create a style that was unique.” “David Prowse is such a heavy-handed man, every time they touched swords, the blades kept breaking.” EE caption: printed daily of Ben’s duel with Vader, “with Prowse speaking Vader’s lines.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler p. 194–198; Rinzler EE, Chapter Nine video caption.
**Notes:** `notes/materials/170-making-of-star-wars-2007/rinzler-09-chapter-nine.md`

### White corridor: “black against a white hallway”

**Speaker:** John Barry; George Lucas; EE production-report heading.
**Source:** Barry: “I think George wanted the set to look, at first sight, like you are in your conventional all-white interior *2001*-type spaceship—and then the door blows down and in comes Darth Vader—*black* against a white hallway!” Lucas insisted on a new hallway rather than a *Falcon* hold re-dress: “I was very concerned that the opening, the first interior of the film, be spectacular and look opulent.” Production-report slug A3: “THE AWESOME, SEVEN-FOOT-TALL DARK LORD OF THE SITH MAKES HIS WAY INTO THE BLINDING LIGHT OF THE MAIN PASSAGEWAY.” EE video, 8 July 1976: “Prowse as Vader chokes the rebel captain.” Scene 6 neck-break: Peter Geddis. — typography-faithful, Rinzler p. 211–214; Rinzler EE, Chapter Nine video caption.
**Notes:** `notes/materials/170-making-of-star-wars-2007/rinzler-09-chapter-nine.md`

### Johnston: Vader’s TIE is “the general’s ship, the command car”

**Speaker:** Joe Johnston, reporting Lucas’s brief.
**Source:** “This was another quickie design, based on a two-by-two-inch sketch.” George: “Make it look like a faster, newer TIE model. It’s the general’s ship, the command car.” A dozen sketches; Steve Gawley builds it. — typography-faithful, Rinzler p. 227.
**Notes:** `notes/materials/170-making-of-star-wars-2007/rinzler-10-chapter-ten.md`

### Coppola screening: first Death Star scene moved later; Vader-and-aide search dropped

**Speaker:** Paul Hirsch, on Francis Ford Coppola’s reaction.
**Source:** “Francis thought that there was too much explanation in the beginning… So we moved the first scene inside the Death Star to much later in the film.” Originally Vader, Tarkin, and officers in the second reel, right after C-3PO reunites with R2 in the sandcrawler; after Coppola, the Death Star debate is placed after Ben’s cave, fourth reel. “once you’d followed Luke and picked up Ben, you knew how *bad* the bad guys were.” “We dropped some scenes that didn’t serve much purpose, like the scene with Vader and one of his aides walking through the Death Star halls, where he was saying something about searching for the robots—we knew all that stuff anyway.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler p. 258.
**Notes:** `notes/materials/170-making-of-star-wars-2007/rinzler-11-chapter-eleven.md`

### Jones: Orson Welles contacted first; “Do you want a day’s work?”; $7,500

**Speaker:** James Earl Jones; George Lucas. Rinzler Introduction flags Jones as one of the few speakers not interviewed until years later.
**Source:** 1 March 1977, Goldwyn Studios, one day, voice only. Jones chosen for Darth Vader dialogue, $7,500. Jones: “George Lucas always wanted a voice in the bass register. I understand that George did contact Orson Welles to read for the voice of Darth Vader before he contacted me. I was out of work and he said, ‘Do you want a day’s work?’ ” Lucas: “He was the best actor that I could possibly find. He has a deep, commanding voice.” Deal memo: 25 February 1977, Lucy Kroll Agency to James Nelson; Alsup to Jacob Bloom 29 August 1977. — typography-faithful, Rinzler p. 264, 266.
**Notes:** `notes/materials/170-making-of-star-wars-2007/rinzler-11-chapter-eleven.md`

### Jones: “a very narrow band of expression” / “never learned the beauties and subtleties of human expression”

**Speaker:** James Earl Jones; Rinzler’s bridging gloss flagged.
**Source:** Jones watches the scenes, studies “his character’s lack of emotion.” Masked, no lip-sync; finishes in about two and a half hours. Jones: “Vader is a man who never learned the beauties and subtleties of human expression. So we figured out the key to my work was to keep it on a very narrow band of expression—that was the secret.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler p. 264.
**Notes:** `notes/materials/170-making-of-star-wars-2007/rinzler-11-chapter-eleven.md`

### Worldizing, Aqua-Lung breathing, and the cut “iron lung” plot

**Speaker:** Sam Shaw on “worldizing”; George Lucas on Burtt’s breathing work.
**Source:** Technique originated with Walter Murch on *American Graffiti*: play the looped lines back through speakers in a regular room “so it has a natural presence… and then re-recording it.” Lucas: “He did about eighteen different kinds, through Aqua-Lungs and through tubes, trying to find the one that had the right sort of mechanical sound. And then we had to decide whether it would be totally rhythmical like an iron lung—that’s the idea, which was a whole part of the plot that essentially got cut out.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler p. 264.
**Notes:** `notes/materials/170-making-of-star-wars-2007/rinzler-11-chapter-eleven.md`

### Williams: “Vader’s theme is a lot of bassoons and muted trombones… the bad side of the Force”

**Speaker:** John Williams.
**Source:** “I think of Ben’s theme as also being the theme of the Jedi Knights, the Old Republic that Ben remembers. It also overlaps into the area of being the theme for the Force, the good Force that Ben represents… Vader’s theme is a lot of bassoons and muted trombones and low things, since he is the bad side of the Force.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler p. 268.
**Notes:** `notes/materials/170-making-of-star-wars-2007/rinzler-11-chapter-eleven.md`

### Lippincott: a five-year-old cries when Vader chokes the rebel; Fox asks for PG

**Speaker:** Charles Lippincott; *Daily Variety* as quoted by Rinzler.
**Source:** “I had a kid about five years old sitting in front of me. And when Darth Vader appears on the bridge of the Princess’s ship at the beginning and grabs the guy and chokes him, the kid began crying, and just really broke down. So I said to George, ‘This film is a PG.’ ” MPAA vote “split evenly between G and PG”; Fox “asked for a stricter rating.” Board men “absolutely bored… a couple of them fell asleep, and they all voted it a G”; a mother found it “a little too intense for kids” and was dismissed; they go back and “the board reneged and gave it a PG because we wanted that.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler p. 289–290.
**Notes:** `notes/materials/170-making-of-star-wars-2007/rinzler-12-chapter-twelve.md`

### Press racism charge: no African American actors except the “black”-robed villain, voiced by a prominent Black actor

**Speaker:** Rinzler paraphrasing a 1977 press accusation (flag as such; not a Lucas line).
**Source:** “it didn’t have any African American actors in it, with the exception of the ‘black’-robed villain Darth Vader, who, either conveniently or paradoxically, was voiced by a prominent black actor.” Record the charge; do not collapse it into a production fact or a rebuttal Lucas does not give here. — typography-faithful paraphrase, Rinzler p. 296–297.
**Notes:** `notes/materials/170-making-of-star-wars-2007/rinzler-12-chapter-twelve.md`

### Titelman, Lucas-as-Leia (5 August 1977): Vader “was still one of the Jedi”; Luke’s father is “named Annikin”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, speaking as Princess Leia to Carol Titelman, 5 August 1977. Rinzler’s Important Note: first thoughts, later modified, “*not* part of any official continuity.”
**Source:** Jedi rally to the Senate, “betrayed and eventually killed by Darth Vader.” “When the Jedi tried to restore order, Darth Vader was still one of the Jedi. What he would do is catch the Jedi off guard and, using his knowledge of the Force, he would kill the Jedi without them realizing what was happening. They trusted him and they didn’t realize he was the murderer who was decimating their ranks. At the height of the Jedi, there were several hundred thousand… they were eventually massacred by one of the special elite forces led by Darth Vader. Eventually only a few, including Ben and Luke’s father, were left. Luke’s father is named Annikin.” Do not collapse this trusted-Jedi-murderer account, or the name Annikin, with later official Anakin/Vader identity. — typography-faithful, Rinzler EE, “George Lucas Expands His Universe.”
**Notes:** `notes/materials/170-making-of-star-wars-2007/rinzler-13-deluxe-bonus.md`

### Titelman, Lucas-as-C-3PO (28 July 1977): no Death Star readouts for Vader; “the Emperor’s emissary”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, speaking as C-3PO to Carol Titelman, 28 July 1977. Same Important Note: not official continuity.
**Source:** “The Death Star had readouts about everyone on board, except Darth Vader. Darth Vader is really attached to the Emperor himself, and he was not really a part of the Death Star personnel or any of that system. Lord Vader worked directly for the Emperor and was the Emperor’s emissary. I’m sure people have seen the Emperor. There are no images of him [in Episode IV], but he’s there.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler EE, “George Lucas Expands His Universe.”
**Notes:** `notes/materials/170-making-of-star-wars-2007/rinzler-13-deluxe-bonus.md`

### Titelman, 1977: midi-chlorians; “Obviously Darth Vader knows the Force”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, in the same post-release Titelman sessions (reorganized by subject). Same Important Note: first thoughts, later modified, not official continuity.
**Source:** “It is said that certain creatures are born with a higher awareness of the Force than humans. Their brains are different; they have more midi-chlorians in their cells.” “Luke is on the road to knowing the Force, but it will be another twenty years before he actually begins to cope with it on a real level. He’s still an amateur. As far as Luke knows, Ben is the last of the Jedi Knights. But there could be more. Obviously Darth Vader knows the Force, but as far as we know at this point, Luke is one of the last surviving apprentices of the Force.” Anyone who studied and worked hard could learn it, “on your own.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler EE, “George Lucas Expands His Universe.”
**Notes:** `notes/materials/170-making-of-star-wars-2007/rinzler-13-deluxe-bonus.md`

### Rinzler gloss (flag): Luke “has to rid himself of his surrogate fathers—Uncle Owen, Darth Vader, and even Obi-Wan”

**Speaker:** J. W. Rinzler, 2007 commentary — not a Lucas line. Do not collapse into Lucas.
**Source:** “Psychologically speaking, Luke has to rid himself of his surrogate fathers—Uncle Owen, Darth Vader, and even Obi-Wan—before he can be truly independent.” Rinzler is here grouping Vader with Owen and Obi-Wan as a surrogate father in a reading of the 1977 film; record as Rinzler’s gloss, separate from the 1977 Titelman “Luke’s father is named Annikin” and from later official identity. — typography-faithful, Rinzler p. 305.

### Titelman, Lucas-as-Han (11 August 1977): space gypsies, Wookiees from about seven to twelve, Space Academy, booted for hustling

### Titelman, Lucas-as-Han: “a free-enterprise small businessman”; “slightly self-destructive”; “a girlfriend in every port”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, speaking as Han Solo to Carol Titelman, 11 August 1977. Same Important Note: not official continuity.
**Source:** Fell into smuggling at about twenty because of Empire tariffs and monopolistic trade companies. “Han could be described as a smuggler, but in reality he’s a free-enterprise small businessman who is trading between systems without benefit of a proper license from the trade unions.” Gambles heavily; “I think he enjoys this: He’s slightly self-destructive and enjoys being on the brink of disaster… worth ten billion credits one day and the next time you meet him, he’s in debt up to his ears.” Empire destroyed a bigger, slower ship than the *Falcon*; he dropped a whole shipment. “He doesn’t really think of it as a political thing so much as somebody who is getting in his way, although he frequently works with, and for, bounty hunters, gangsters, Rebels, and the Empire if the price is right. He has a girlfriend in every port.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler EE, “George Lucas Expands His Universe.”
