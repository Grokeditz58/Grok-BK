## Opening (p. 32)

The second draft does not move Fox to a financial or legal commitment; no official budget yet. **Andy Rigrod**: “for a couple of years after the initial negotiations, there were no formal agreements… the budget, which started at about $3 million.” **Lucas**: after the second script “Gary really wanted to get started… I had already taken a year and a half to write.” Budget stall: no one knew what anything would look like. Solution: hire artists for production illustrations and models. McQuarrie hired November 1974; first painting started late January, finished **31 January 1975**.

**Hal Barwood** meets McQuarrie at Boeing (SST concept paintings, circa 1968); circa 1973, Barwood/Robbins *Star Dancing* paintings; Lucas tags along at lunch: “You know, I’m gonna make a science-fiction movie someday. Maybe we’ll get together.” **McQuarrie**: Lucas talked about “an intergalactic war picture”; “I thought, ‘Gee, that sounds ambitious.’… I never expected to see him again.” Years later, weekly salary. Paintings “a substitute for arm waving and verbal descriptions, and to start budget talks.” First meeting: treatment illustrations, comic-book landspeeder refs, a lemur drawing for Chewbacca, plus Lucas’s own sketches. Caption: at that November 1974 meeting Lucas drew a TIE fighter, X-wing, and Death Star — “perhaps the first conceptual record of these designs.”

## A DREAMING EYE

McQuarrie bio (born 1929; Art Center 1954–56; Korea; Boeing; from 1968, CBS News Apollo coverage). His own bio answer to *Graffiti*’s “Where were you in ’62?”: “dropped out; read books; walked on beach; Zen; not sure what I want to do.” On the job: “I felt, *oh yes*—this is what I was meant to do.” **Bunny Alsup** (Kurtz’s assistant): McQuarrie “very quiet… they were all happily working, trying to tune in to the same finite idea.” **Lucas** on the process: list of about ten scenes; iterative roughs; “Ralph also adds in an enormous amount of his own detail… I really trusted him.” **McQuarrie**: paint “the ideal look… don’t worry about how things are going to get done… I finished four key moments… So as much as I designed this, George really designed it, too.” Rinzler: often counted as four, actually **five** moments from the second draft.

Vader design, typography locked — **McQuarrie**: “For Darth Vader, George just said he would like to have a very tall, dark fluttering figure that had a spooky feeling like it came in on the wind… He mentioned the look of Arab costumes, all tied up in silk and rags. He liked the idea of Vader having a big hat, like a fisherman’s hat, a big long metal thing that came down. George wanted him to have some sort of mask, because they were supposed to leap down from this big ship to a smaller ship that the rebels were in. The space suits began as being necessary for their survival in space, but the suits became part of their character.” Early sketches rejected then revised (“I kind of like that hat, but maybe you should fool around with it a little bit more”). From November 1974, *Metropolis* (with Alfred Abel) as a guide for first R2-D2 / C-3PO sketches; one tiny figure recalls *The Day the Earth Stood Still*.

## LAUNCH OF THE SPACESHIPS

**Colin J. Cantwell** hired around the same time (McQuarrie says days or weeks earlier; accounting: both paid from November 1974). Barwood recommended him. **Lucas** saw his San Diego planetarium space show. “Class of *2001*” (Trumbull / Kubrick; also *The Andromeda Strain*). Models for budget and for miniature effects. Lucas signs off; McQuarrie incorporates approved ships. Third artist: **Alex Tavoularis**, mid-February 1975, storyboards of the opening sequence (Dean Tavoularis’s brother; Coppola circle). Fox will not pay for any of them; the $15,000 development money is short of Cantwell alone at $20,000. Lucas pays from *Graffiti* profits. **Warren Hellman**: “basic apathy toward the project within Fox… The studio hoped a lot of times that it would just go away.” **Lucas**: *Graffiti* got made by “persistent, dogmatic attitude… *Star Wars* was a little more dubious …” **Kurtz**: “Each step we dragged Fox along.” First approved concept model perhaps Cantwell’s Y-wing. **Cantwell**: “he wanted to do something very different from *2001*… as immersive and as new as *2001* but… this action saga with a comicbook nobility.”

## From Script to Concept Art

McQuarrie, about twenty-one production paintings, mostly gouache or gouache and acrylic. Five key illustrations in a day or two each, late January to early March 1975. Book note locked as printed: “(Note: All tides in quotes are from McQuarrie’s notes.)” [sic: *titles*]. Dated paintings:

- **31 January 1975:** “Artoo and Threepio leave the pod in the desert.” **McQuarrie**: *Silent Running* robots were “square boxes with legs,” so “if they’re square, I’ll make mine round… like a garbage can with a dome on top”; three legs, “like a person on crutches”; Oregon-coast cliff plus sand dunes. **Lucas**: showed *Metropolis* and *Silent Running*; “I knew I wanted one via *Metropolis;* it’s in the script… one to be a stubby little robot and… a kind of human robot. One is a public relations guy and one is just a standard robot robot.” EE audio (Rinzler EE): McQuarrie on working with Lucas on the first illustrations (Interview by Arnold, 1979; 2:47). Cross-ref notes 169.
- **14–15 February 1975:** “Laser duel”: Jedi Deak Starkiller fights Darth Vader on the rebel ship, as in the second draft. Early color study and pencil study. Caption: McQuarrie’s early Vader sketches; in one, “Vader brandishes what looks like an épée from a swashbuckling film.”
- **20 February 1975:** “Imperial City, Alderaan—city floats in gray clouds.” Usually not counted among the first (reasons later).
- **22 February 1975:** “Battle for Death Star (fighters dive on sphere).” First illustration of the Death Star, from Lucas’s description (inspired by a John Berkey illustration). Still small enough for individual windows; “just a floating weapon/space station—the big hole on the bottom being where the cannon would emerge.” **Cantwell**: George wanted the Y-wing “like a World War II TBF Torpedo Bomber” with three crew.
- **6 March 1975:** “Cantina.” Second draft: Han Solo tries to outdraw an alien, Luke and robots watching. McQuarrie first thought a primitive place with torn banners; Lucas had him add a mechanical device like the Aquillian ranger’s “chrome ping-pong sized balls” — “little seekers… an automatic police force that could carry out a death sentence.”
- Thumbnails also for a painting “hitherto unknown to this period”: Annikin Starkiller attacked on Yavin by a ravenous insect-like creature — “the only completed illustration known to exist for the rough draft.” Rinzler: possibly Lucas was toying with reinserting the scene.

## CALLING ALL CREW

April 1975: overlapping production push. On or a few days after **5 April 1975**, Kurtz to London with all of McQuarrie’s paintings, interviewing department heads. **Kurtz** on England: cost; North Africa closer; “slightly bizarre architecture that still looked real”; English cameraman. US would cost almost twice as much in some below-the-line areas. Late March 1975, before flying over, Lucas alters the second draft: **he changes Luke into a girl.** **Lucas**, typography locked: “The original treatment was about a princess and an old man… and then I wrote her out for a while, and the second draft didn’t really have any girls in it at all. I was very disturbed about that. I didn’t want to make a movie without any women in it. So I struggled with that, and at one point Luke was a girl. I just changed the main character from a guy to a girl.”

One-week England trip; interview cinematographers (David Watkin, Gil Taylor, Anthony Richmond, Dick Bush, Peter Suschitzky) and art directors (Michael Stringer, Brian Eatwell); younger enthusiasm vs. veteran experience. Kurtz surveys studios, sound-stage sizes, post facilities. Fox hires consultant production designer **Elliot Scott** for two weeks; he reads the revised second draft; recommends **John Stears** (mechanical effects; Bond; explosives license). **Stears**: feasibility study; “long talks about the feel of the movie, and what’s physically, technically, and financially possible within the budget.” **Lucas**: Stears already on before Lucas arrived; stayed because of reputation. **Kurtz** still cannot pin a budget: “I came up with a $15 million and a $6 million and $10 million budget… And it was totally arbitrary. You have to design the sets before you know how much things are going to cost.”

## The Girl Without a Name / Circa April 1, 1975: Luke as girl overlooking Mos Eisley

Second group of McQuarrie paintings, late March–early April; some are “the only visual remnants of Lucas’s transformation of Luke into a girl.” **McQuarrie** on the “fantastic five” painting: “Han Solo is in the blue cape… almost like a one-sheet artwork, depicting the five main characters. And that was when Luke was a girl. George liked this Chewbacca, but I suppose he thought it could be a little more weird, and he decided to take off the flak jacket.” Dated/captioned:

- **1 April 1975:** “The fantastic five painting (Luke is a girl at this time)”.
- Circa 1 April 1975: Han Solo and Luke-as-girl; another logo-poster concept with “a bearded Han Solo with light-saber, the Death Star, and the girl-with-no-name.”
- Circa 1 April 1975: “Luke as girl overlooking Mos Eisley.” **McQuarrie**: cliff first view of Mos Eisley, intended as a matte, two suns, floating car, “you didn’t actually see the ground. Luke was a girl here, and she’s carrying that big long rifle which George wanted.” **Lucas** on the landspeeder: comic books, SF novels, *National Geographic*, “a door handle that looks like a spaceship.” Cantwell built a concept model.
- **28 March 1975:** “Imperial troopers in Death Star corridor.” Cantwell pirate-ship prototype in the background. “As described in the second draft, Chewbacca is carrying the wounded Deak on his shoulder. A bearded Han Solo wears a blue cape and holds a lightsaber, with Luke standing behind him; **at this point in the creative process, storm-troopers could also use lightsabers.**”
- Circa **31 March 1975:** pirate ship in Alderaan docking bay (cloud city/prison).
- **3 April 1975:** Temple on Yavin / “Rebel space base (fighters on fourth moon, outside temple hideout).” **McQuarrie**: “Aztec-like ruin… one of these seven wonders of the galaxy, a pile of giant disks.”

## AN OLD MAN ON THE SIDE OF THE ROAD

Between second and third drafts: a **six-page story synopsis dated 1 May 1975** (primer for Fox executives), then Lucas types a four-page single-spaced outline. About a month after making Luke a girl, he changes her back into Luke “while at the same time resuscitating the princess.” **Lucas**, typography locked: “It was at that moment… that I came up with the idea that Luke and the princess are twins. I simply divided the character in two.” Rinzler: already germinating (Biggs/Windy as twin-like princes, then actual twins; twin suns); mythological twins. **Lucas**: “The princess is everything Luke wants to be… She is socially conscious, whereas he is thrown into things; intellectually, she is a strong leader, and he is just a kid.” In the abbreviated synopses she eclipses Deak, who nearly disappears, surviving only as an unnamed “Captain.” An unnamed older Jedi appears for the first time — “quite literally, the wizard on the side of the road”; payment in food, recalling *Seven Samurai* rice.

Outline: the old man’s powers “like those of ‘Don Juan’” — Carlos Castaneda, *The Teachings of Don Juan* (1968) and sequels, including *Tales of Power* (1975). Rinzler: Castaneda’s Don Juan Matus, shaman/shape-shift/awareness beyond death, “seems to have been key in transforming Lucas’s concept of the Jedi from a samurai warrior into a more mystical warrior.” **Lucas**: “I spent about a year reading lots of fairy tales—and that’s when it starts to move away from Kurosawa and toward Joe Campbell… About the time I was doing the third draft I read *The Hero with a Thousand Faces*, and I started to realize I was following those rules unconsciously. So I said, *I’ll make it fit more into that classic mold.*” Rinzler précis of the monomyth (call, trials, surpass mentor, apotheosis). Structural changes: Kiber Crystal moves from the moisture farm to Alderaan, where the old man has to *search* for it; the princess, not Deak, is rescued; Death Star attack supplemented “with a duel between Luke and Darth Vader, who fight on its surface.”

## Story Synopsis and Typed Outline Summary, May 1, 1975 (p. 47)

Title: *The Adventures of Luke Starkiller* (episode one) “The Star Wars.” Executive intro, typography locked in part: “In the grand tradition of Edgar Rice Burroughs’s *John Carter of Mars*, and Alex Raymond’s *Flash Gordon*… an engaging human drama set in a fantasy world that paralyzes the imagination.” Roll-up still kills the REPUBLIC GALACTICA; Jedi Knights hunted by “the DARK LORDS OF THE SITH.” Two Star Destroyers pursue a tiny silver spacecraft above **Organa**; robots; stormtroopers; a rebel with a “laser sword,” another with a laser pistol. **Darth Vader enters.** Robots to the surface, captured by “vile little Jawa Metal Trappers,” sold to a moisture farmer, meet “Luke Starkiller, who dreams of someday becoming a mighty Jedi warrior and thereby learning the mystical ways of the ‘force of others.’ ” A rebel princess has been captured and taken to “the dreaded cloud city of Alderaan.” Luke “runs away from home with the two robots to rescue the princess.” Genre pitch: “Heroic Quest”; ending, outnumbered rebels attack the “Death Star.”

Supplemental four undated note-pages: lifepod spared (no life-forms); robots argue and split; “Threepio gets picked up and Artoo gets chased/shot by Jawas”; bought by Owen Lars and his nephew at Anchorhead. Dinner: Owen won’t let him go to the Academy — “they argue and Owen strikes the boy, who is protected by Beru.” Message from the “Captain”: princess prisoner on Alderaan; signal device in R2 to locate her. Luke kisses sleeping Beru, leaves with the robots. “On his way to the spaceport, he passes a ‘poor old man.’ … the old man talks about his adventures as a Jedi. Luke is ‘in awe’ and wants to become his apprentice; the old man agrees and will train him ‘for food.’ … the old man gives Luke a lesson about the ‘force of others …Old man can do magic, read minds, talk to things like Don Juan.’ ”

Cantina: old man talks to Chewbacca; “Bugs molest Luke, start fight. Old man cuts them down.” Chewbacca leads them to Han, “who is a captain.” Negotiated fee to Alderaan, following R2’s signal. Hide in the ship as TIEs escort it in. “Darth Vader and other Sith Knights sense the presence of a Jedi, but they find only the robots.” Old man looks for the Kiber Crystal; the others rescue the princess: typography locked — “She’s a tough babe; doesn’t appreciate their help—a trap? **Han punches her in the face and Chewbacca carries her out.**” Dia Noga; old man gets the crystal; Sith Knights “become ill,” confront and wound him; rescued. **Vader “lets them go,”** but sends TIEs that quickly give up. Old man says they’re being followed; princess still goes to her “hidden fortress.” Crash-land on Yavin; loyal troops. **Han receives his money and leaves, “which saddens Luke.”** Control War Room spots “something very big”; old man knows it’s the Death Star; destroy it by putting a “bomb in its exhaust system.” Skeptics; **“Luke lands on the Death Star with the bomb—‘runs into Vader; sword fight—Han to the rescue. Artoo shot … End scene—all come together, cheer. Make Luke a Jedi knight.’ ”**

STAR WARS PROGRESSION: robots sold to Owen by Jawas; Owen a farmer, antagonistic toward Luke; old man/Jedi on the road teaches “the mystical ways of the Force of Others”; **Han Solo captain of his ship**; old man on his own mission (Alderaan, Kiber Crystal); heroes rescue the princess from her detention cell (Alderaan); **“Princess at odds with Han, attracted to Luke”**; Luke blows up Death Star (with bomb).

## VALIANT VAN NUYS

Lucas decides to create his own special-effects house (cost and control), originally hoped for Northern California. Due diligence: Howard Anderson, Linwood Dunn, Jim Danforth, Phil Taylor; Trumbull more interested in directing after *Silent Running*. April 1975 meeting with **John Dykstra** (Trumbull’s Future General). **Dykstra**: Lucas “outlined the concept… primarily with regard to what the special effects would be… More than anything else, he wanted to get fluidity of motion… the illusion of actually photographing spaceships from a camera platform in space.” Lucas’s condensed aerial-battle short: 20–25 hours of videotape → 4–5 hours → about an hour transferred to 16mm → cut to about eight minutes; shown to Dykstra. Verbal commitment two or three weeks later. Motion-control camera: **Lucas** — “John had wanted to build one… this seemed like the perfect opportunity.” Late May 1975: empty warehouse at **6842 Valjean Avenue, Van Nuys**, $2,300/month; Dykstra persuades Lucas they need to be near Hollywood labs; Lucas “reluctantly agreed.” **Richard Edlund** (via Richard Alexander, from Bob Abel’s): “director of photography for the special effects.” Rumors: ~350 shots in two years vs. *2001*’s ~205 shots with about three years (eighteen months almost exclusively on shots). “Everybody said, ‘It just *can’t be done* in the time allotted.’ ” Fox had independently estimated ~$7 million (Van Der Veer and others, given 350 shots and storyboards); Lucas/Dykstra said $2.3 million; **Fox cut it to $1.5 million.** *2001*: 205 shots cost $6.5 million of $10.5 million in 1967 dollars. When the half million was needed to start, Fox “delayed for about four or five weeks. So I had to put up the cash” (Lucas). Early estimate caption: four seconds of the opening, two weeks and $15,017.

## A NEW LEGION

Name, typography locked — **Lucas**: “It just popped into my head… We were sitting in an industrial park and using light to create magic. That’s what they were going to do.” **Industrial Light & Magic** is born. First hires, June 1975: Grant McCune starts “day one… June 1 of ’75”; Jerry Greenwood; Richard Alexander; Bob Shepherd (production manager); Al Miller; Don Trumbull (Doug’s father) on camera-system blueprints; Bill and Jamie Shourt; Edlund, camera department. July: Robbie Blalack (optical), Adam Beckett (animation/rotoscope). Edlund had loved *THX 1138*; TV work (*Outer Limits*, *Twilight Zone*, *Star Trek* — “I teleported hundreds of guys”; played Thing on *The Addams Family*). Warehouse laid out: two optical printers, rotoscope, two stages, model/machine/wood shops; upstairs animation, editorial, art, screening room. **Cantwell** on the TIE, typography locked: “The TIE has different technology… just this ball in the middle and the two solar fins. But especially you can’t tell how Darth Vader gets in; it’s something alien.” **Dykstra** wanted a nondescript location, 24-foot bays, 12,000 square feet on two stages; landlord Jim Hanna loose about modifications. After walls (~six weeks): models, camera system, optical compositing started together. Dykstra: “I sat at a little desk and figured out how to spend $1.28 million.” After the first three weeks in June, Lucas had already paid **$87,921**. **Edlund**: “providential… We all got together, in a highly unorthodox way, in this little warehouse out in Van Nuys.”

## TRAVELING THE WORLD

Spring 1975, simultaneous with ILM: Kurtz flies with Coppola to the Florida Everglades (jungle planet vs. Vietnam for a revived *Apocalypse Now*); then Olympic National Forest, three weeks, rejected (restrictions); Toronto IMAX (tab maybe half a million, but theater conversion and opticals “just beyond us”); Calgary to talk with Anthony Masters (*Buffalo Bill and the Indians*). Lucas and Kurtz to Mexico City, July 4 weekend (second trip; Guaymas the previous spring). Huycks on *Lucky Lady* (Fox), “strangely tangential” throughout. Lucas talks with DP **Geoffrey Unsworth** and production designer **John Barry**. Barry: draftsman on *Cleopatra*; a week on Kubrick’s unmade *Napoleon*; *Kelly’s Heroes*; *A Clockwork Orange*; suggested by Elliot Scott. **Barry**: “I have just enough experience to be able to cope with the problems, and just enough inexperience so I should take it on… anybody with more experience would have just said, ‘I don’t think I can do it in that amount of time.’ ” Needs “seven months minimum”; principal photography tentatively February 1976. **Lucas** wants someone “really down to earth and solid… without costing me ten times.” Unsworth: “very flexible”; Lucas wanted “a very diffused look, similar to… *Murder on the Orient Express* [1974]. It had that fairy-tale quality about it.” Both say okay. Set decorator **Roger Christian** (also from *Lucky Lady*, had fallen ill) is the third hire from that crew. Barry later flies to San Francisco to talk a **$1 million** sets budget.

## THE *JAWS* CONNECTION

No Fox-lot office. **Charles Lippincott** (hired for marketing and merchandising): Lucas “believed politically… that you don’t want to be on the same lot as the studio… Fox did give me a hard time… But they couldn’t do anything, ’cause George was at Universal.” Occasional use of Kurtz’s Universal bungalow (426); Spielberg nearby, back from Martha’s Vineyard, in *Jaws* post. Lucas already leaning toward a classical, romantic soundtrack; Spielberg suggests the *Jaws* composer. **Lucas**: “Steven said, ‘I worked with this guy and he’s great!’ ” Spring 1975 meeting with **John Williams**. **Williams**: “Steven Spielberg introduced me to George… and told me about *Star Wars* and George Lucas, and how much he admired him.” Williams reads the script but won’t compose for more than a year (*Family Plot*, *Black Sunday*, a violin concerto).

Sound: Murch preapproved in the deal memo but occupied (*Julia*, committed to *The Black Stallion*). **Lucas**: they asked Ken Miura at USC, “Who’s the next Walter Murch?” — **Ben Burtt.** McQuarrie finishes first-round paintings by May; **11 June 1975** completes *The Star Wars* logo (Lucas wanted something like *American Graffiti* stationery); first logo sketches show Han Solo, then Luke in starpilot costume “posing like Han Solo in the Fantastic Five illustration.” McQuarrie freelance 27 June–24 July. **Burtt**: collecting sound since about seven; met Kurtz, saw paintings; they wanted a Wookiee voice before shooting. **Kurtz**, typography locked: “We’ve got this big sidekick, like a giant teddy bear, but he can be ferocious if he’s coming to the rescue; the rest of the time he’s like a big dog.” Not English; animal but understandable emotion. Burtt sees the script after a month, meets Lucas in August; they want a whole library. **Lucas** briefs spaceships, laser swords, zoo animals, dialects, “weird zaps and cracks.” Burtt: Zoetrope sent a Nagra; “George thought that bear sounds might be the right direction to take for the Wookiee”; he also broke down the script (laser guns, swords, landspeeder, ships, sets) into a notebook. Records Pooh the bear; ARP (Coppola’s), Moog, Eventide Harmonizer, Urei dip filter; libraries (Occidental College birds) plus rented trained animals in the quiet desert.

## THE ARRIVAL OF GENERAL BEN KENOBI

Third draft developed from the May 1 synopsis and notes, written in short stints at Medway and on the road. **Bunny Alsup** now at Park Way, typing pages: “Science fiction was foreign to me… Spelling the names was also a challenge, because he was inconsistent in how he spelled them.” Notes: “Robots and rebel troops listen as the weird sounds of stormtroopers are heard on the top of the ship … Sith rips rebel’s arm off at end of battle … only seven Sith—one in each sector … small ships avoid deflector shields/tractor beams.” “New scenes: Luke sees space battle.” Robbins and Barwood thought Luke needed to appear earlier, intercut with the robots; **Lucas** also lists notes from Coppola, Spielberg, “all of my friends.”

Leia returns, replacing Deak, reintroduced at the outset (as in the treatment) rather than halfway through on Alderaan. The old man is named **General Ben Kenobi** — mentor role originally filled by General Skywalker in the rough and first drafts. “Perhaps due to Lucas’s appreciation of Campbell… Luke is more of a loner; he is stripped of his brothers and cousins—and now his father is a dead Jedi.” Late July–early August, McQuarrie costume designs, “a first look at General Ben Kenobi,” sleeveless shirt recalling Kurosawa samurai. Notes: “princess send Luke to Old Ben”; idea that “didn’t quite make it into the third draft: **‘combine Han and Biggs.’**” Force stages in the notes, typography locked: “1) Luke learns about Force (in desert); 2) Luke tries to experience Force (in ship); 3) Luke becomes a man of the Force (Alderaan); 4) Ben explains Luke’s experience (in ship); 5) Luke with crystal struggles with Force (on Yavin).”

**Lucas**, typography locked: “I have come to the conclusion that there is a force larger than the individual. It is controlled by the individuals, and it controls them. All I’m saying is that the pure soul is connected to a larger energy field that you would begin to understand if you went all the way back and saw yourself in your purest sense.” Another note: “Learn about the Force—all around us in every living creature.” Defined in similar terms by Kenobi in the third draft. As the journey links more to the Force, the Kiber Crystal fades; Rinzler: a Hitchcock “MacGuffin,” now with a rival in the Death Star plans — “little space for two MacGuffins.” The crystal remains in the aerial attack. Abandoned surface climax, typography locked — **Lucas**: “Between the second and third drafts, Luke stopped on the surface on the Death Star. He and Artoo had to go and take the bomb by hand, open up the little hatch, and drop the bomb in it—then they had fifteen seconds or something to get off the surface before the thing blew up—but as they were going back to the ship, Darth Vader arrived. So Luke and Vader had a big swordfight. Luke finally overcame Vader and then jumped in the ship and took off. But in trying to intercut the dogfight with the old-fashioned swordfight, I realized that the film would have just stopped dead. It was too risky.” On construction vs. plot: “Neither *Graffiti* nor *THX* had plots. I’m not really interested in plots… it’s a plotted movie, and I find plots boring because they’re so mechanical… I’m much more into the scenes and the nuances of what’s going on.”

## Third-Draft Summary, August 1975 (p. 60)

Title: *The Star Wars: From the Adventures of Luke Starkiller*. Roll-up: REPUBLIC GALACTICA dead; Jedi hunted by “the sinister agents of the Emperor: the DARK LORDS OF THE SITH.” Rebel armies from hidden fortresses in the Great Rift have won a crushing victory. “To crush the rebellion once and for all, the Emperor has sent one of his most ferocious Dark Lords to find the secret rebel strongholds and destroy them …” One Star Destroyer pursues the rebel ship above Utapau; immediately a pitched interior battle, stormtroopers blasting through the front door. On the planet, Luke — checking a mechanical rig like a “Christmas tree” — sees lights in the sky, goes to the power station: Fixer, Camie, Deak, and Windy; meets old pal **Biggs**, just back from the Academy.

Princess Leia Organa, typography locked: “A beautiful young girl about sixteen years old huddles in a small alcove… The fear in her eyes slowly gives way to anger… She steps from her hiding place and blasts two troopers with her laser pistol, but she is greatly outnumbered and quickly captured.” **Brought to Darth Vader, who tells her the information she intercepted will soon be recovered.** Robots escape, eventually bought by Owen Lars, Luke’s uncle. **“Meanwhile, Vader tortures Leia in the Alderaan prison; he wants to know where he can find the stolen information—and the rebel base.”** Dinner: Owen has used Luke’s education money for the robots; Luke wants to be a “startrooper” and “fight like his father, who was a Jedi.” Triggers a 15-inch 3-D hologram of Leia: deliver the R-2 to “the authorities on Organa Major”; “I am lost. The Sith Lord will surely take me to Alderaan.” Luke leaves a video-slate: typography locked in part — he’s taken the speeder, the “droids,” and “my father’s lightsaber… I’ll miss you, Aunt Beru. I love you both …” Heads out to find “an old friend of Luke’s father: ‘General Kenobi was my father’s commander, and he is probably the greatest of all the Jedi knights … or at least my father thought so.’ ” Tusken Raiders tie him up, dangling and rotating; **BEN KENOBI** appears, “a shabby old desert rat of a man… at least seventy,” “scraggly white beard.” Luke: “You’re General Kenobi?!? The Jedi Knight? The commander of the White Legions?” Introduces himself as “the son of Annikin Starkiller,” knows Ben’s *Diary of the Clone Wars* by heart. Ben is hesitant; “he is old and, as he illustrates by breaking open his arm, now partly mechanical… he has little of the Force left in him.”

Ben on the Force, typography locked: “Let’s just say the Force is something a Jedi Warrior deals with. It is an energy field in oneself, a power that controls one’s acts, yet obeys one’s commands. It is nothing, yet it makes marvels appear before your very eyes. All living things generate this Force field, even you … It surrounds you and radiates from you. A Jedi can feel it flowing from him … (patting his stomach) …from here! …When a creature dies, the force it generated remains. The Force is all around us. It can be collected and transmitted through the use of a Kiber Crystal. It’s the only way to amplify the power of the Force within you.” Crystal taken at the **Battle of Condawn** — “during which Luke’s father was killed. **One of Ben’s disciples took the crystal and became a Sith Lord.** The few crystals that remain are in the possession of the Sith Lords on Alderaan.” Do not collapse this into a later “Vader killed Luke’s father” line the summary does not speak; record both this battle-death and the duel lines below. Across the galaxy, in a crystal chamber on Alderaan, the Sith feel Ben has joined. **Vader:** “Something old has been awakened. The Force has suddenly grown stronger. We must travel future paths with caution.”

## THE RETURN OF *APOCALYPSE NOW*

**Lucas**, typography locked: he had worked on *Star Wars* about two years and was starting preproduction; Coppola, after *The Godfather II* (1974), said he would finance movies himself — “come and do this movie.” “I had a choice: I had put four years into *Apocalypse Now*, and two years into *Star Wars*—but something inside said, ‘Do *Star Wars* and then try to do *Apocalypse Now.*’ … he said, ‘We really have to do it right now; I’ve got the money’… So I said, ‘Why don’t you do it then?’ So he went off and did it.” Part of the decision was *Graffiti* fan mail: “I seemed to have struck a chord with kids; I had found something they were missing.” After the 1960s, “the drugs were really getting bad, kids were dying, and there was nothing left to protest. But *Graffiti* just said, *Get into your car and go chase some girls. That’s all you have to do.*” *THX* pessimistic; *Graffiti* “said essentially that we were all very good. *Apocalypse Now* was very much like *THX*, and *Star Wars* was very much like *American Graffiti*, so I thought it’d be more beneficial for kids.” Ten- and twelve-year-olds “didn’t have the fantasy life that we’d had… *Kojak* and *The Six Million Dollar Man*, but they didn’t have any real vision of all the incredible and crazy and wonderful things that we had when we were young—pirate movies and Westerns and all that.” Coppola’s sons, eleven and eight, “went crazy” at a space film; “They’re the ones who I talked to about the story. I know what they like.”
