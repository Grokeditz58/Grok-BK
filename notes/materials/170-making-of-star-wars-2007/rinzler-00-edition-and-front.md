## Foreword by Peter Jackson (vi–vii)

Jackson, sixteen in New Zealand, waited until December 1977 for the local release; soundtrack and stills first. “Luke really was us… just a kid like us who was swept away in this adventure.” “I know what George means by ‘documentary camera,’ because the film had a truthfulness to it…” Lived-in / used worlds later fed *The Lord of the Rings* and Weta (“almost like a Skywalker South”). Meeting Lucas: “a slightly mysterious figure, almost a mythic figure… I wasn’t expecting him to be as laid-back.” “George throws away the toolbox and invents a new toolbox before he even starts making his film.” Photo caption: Jackson and Lucas at the Letterman Digital Arts Center in 2006. Signed Wellington, New Zealand.

## Introduction (viii)

Rinzler’s thesis, typography locked: “Because the original story of the original film has never been told—because the original words have remained hidden, almost unknown and forgotten.” Steve Sansweet tip → Jo Donaldson / Robyn Stanley → four boxes of Charles Lippincott’s unfinished making-of (more than fifty interviews, 1975–1978, many over sixty transcribed pages). “The interviews were still fresh, sometimes quite candid, and—above all—more accurate than much of what has been written about the film since.” Sixteen interviews, including Lucas, Kurtz, Ford, Hamill, Fisher, Daniels, and department heads, “were completed a year or more *before* the film was released on May 25, 1977.” John Barry “absent from nearly every account… due to his untimely death in 1979.” Rinzler had shadowed Lucas on *Revenge of the Sith* (2002–2005) via Rick McCallum; also “working on another book with George Lucas” 2005–2007; visit to Park Way; bound drafts including “the hard-to-find May 1, 1975, story synopsis and typed outline.” **Quote policy, typography locked — flag as Rinzler’s rule, and date every quote:** “nearly all of the quotes in this book are from the Lost Interviews recorded at that time. So, if someone is complaining about their career or explaining how little they know about special effects, these comments should be read as circa 1975 to 1978 (as should all dollar amounts)… (The few exceptions are quotes from those—such as James Earl Jones and Peter Cushing—who were not interviewed until quite a few years later.)”

## The Lost Interviews (ix)

That which we remember is, more often than not, that which we would like to have been; or that which we hope to be. Thus our memory and our identity are ever at odds; our history ever a tall tale told by inattentive idealists.” Chronological list (unless noted, Lippincott).
