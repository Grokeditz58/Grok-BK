## Opening (p. 138)

Last days in London: Alan Ladd, newly Fox senior VP of worldwide production, arrives for “final points.” ILM is behind. **Lucas**: “They hadn’t done any special effects… ILM was supposed to have all the plates for the front projection with the spaceships done by the time we started… they only had about three of them.” First lead in: **Mark Hamill** (former McDonald’s short-order cook, Baskin-Robbins scooper, AP copyboy). Never been to England or Africa; jet-lagged costume fitting, then EMI. Sets unpainted; “*Millennium Falcon* was just wood.” Screening-room intro: “Oh, this is Luke Starkiller.” Crew “blasé.” He gets lost walking back, IRA-nervous hotel staff call police, paddy wagon, finally finds the hotel.

## LAST PLANE TO TUNISIA

Ladd and Kurtz finalize costs; wardrobe about £90,000 ($220,000). **Mollo**: “really quite a comfortable budget… materials… so cheap.” Exception: stormtroopers **£40,000 ($93,000)**, still not ready a week before location. “Stormtroopers were the nightmare costume”: plaster body cast, sculpted armor, “George changed all the kneecaps,” vacuum-form plastic; “we had something like *four days before shooting*” to figure how it goes on or off “in about five minutes.” Then George wants some in “combat order” — “Lots of stuff on the back.” Mollo: Boy Scout backpack rack, plastic seed boxes, drainpipe, laboratory pipe, sprayed black; rank from a motorcyclist chest protector, one painted orange, one black. C-3PO: “specially engineered wheels and rings”; Daniels “only put on the complete costume once, just before we flew to Tunisia.” Last-minute gear misses the $5,000 road train; they charter a Lockheed Hercules C-130 at **$22,000** round trip, two days before cameras. Cocktail party, Hotel Africa, Maghreb room, **17 March**, 6:30 PM. Hamill: Tuesday arrival, Thursday off, Friday Chinese lunch with George and Gary; **Saturday, 20 March**, Heathrow, “STAR WARS stickers,” “I’m going to Africa!”

## THE FIRST NIGHT

Chartered BA London–Djerba; layover, then car/truck to Nefta. Daniels had been in *Rosencrantz and Guildenstern Are Dead* at the Criterion; doesn’t sleep — German tourists at 4 AM, call at 6. Hamill: huge one-story hotel complex, golf, discos; next day “drove forever in the rain to the salt flats.” Tozeur “tiny” (Watts). Berber women “one eye”; night driving “a terrible hassle.” Zeffirelli’s *Jesus of Nazareth* (1977) TV miniseries has used up local technicians, plaster, rental cars, hotel space; big hotel closed for remodeling; “double and triple up… fourth-rate hotels.” About a hundred from England, twenty-five Tunisians; French the common language. Night of **Sunday, 21 March**: cramped sleep before a production “almost three years in the works, but still wasn’t ready for prime time.”

## REPORT NO. 1: MONDAY, MARCH 22, 1976

CALL: 06.30; SALT FLATS AT NEFTA; SETS: EXT. LARS’ HOMESTEAD; SCENE NUMBERS: 26 PART [PURCHASE OF C-3PO AND R2-D2]; 29 PART [LUKE AND GIANT TWIN SUNS]; B29 [LUKE AND C-3PO RUSH OUT OF HOMESTEAD TO LOOK FOR R2-D2]. Rinzler note: not every scene listed; “Part”/“PT” means shot over several days.

Sandcrawler partial (bottom only). Cast: Hamill, Daniels, Baker in R2, **Phil Brown** as Owen — Lucas notes: “good Owen-type, a scruffy American.” **Brown**: “Uncle Owen was a straightforward curmudgeon—which I am anyway.” Twelve local children as Jawas. **Daniels** on the suit, typography locked in substance: never sat in the “ANTHONY DANIELS” chair; leaning board at about seventy degrees; nylon leotards, “very nasty little sailing shoes”; “a rather large crotch, which I think George liked to define as ‘space eroticism’ ”; thick rubber corset; front/back pieces with opposite armholes; sheet-steel sculpted hands so heavy “I could hold my hand up for about twenty seconds.” Two hours to put on; bolted at neck and waist, “no way on earth that I could get out”; “precision-engineered, and if I got in the way, I got very badly cut up.” Ten paces from the tent, “it hurt so much that I couldn’t make it to the set about three hundred yards away… I just felt I wasn’t going to make it through the film.” Couldn’t sleep in Nefta either. **Hamill** enjoys day one: “a modicum of continuity… I really got to know Threepio.” Daniels learns “binary loadlifters” don’t matter “because my lips weren’t moving.”

Robot failures: batteries die; kick-down leg; head turn; scene where Owen picks the red robot and its head is supposed to blow — “the radio-controlled robot had all the controls *in* the head, so we couldn’t put the exploding head on it.” **Kurtz** considered postponing two weeks but hotter weather “would have been a real disaster.” Solution: fiberglass backup on piano wire, exploding head, Les Dilley repaints it red. **Baker**: robots “careening over the desert… just crashed into me, tipped me over.” **Hamill** on Lucas: “always calm… four robots in one shot… If two of them hit the mark, it’s a print… we moved really fast.” **Barry**: “When Artoo falls off the ramp… he’s prepared to ride the punches.” Twin-suns shot failed (weather). Daniels: “covered in scars and scratches… That was the last time I ever wore the costume all day.” UNIT DISMISSED: 19.20; SCS COMPLETED TODAY: 1; SCREEN TIME TAKEN TODAY: 2M 43S.

## REPORT NOS. 2–4: TUESDAY, MARCH 23–THURSDAY, MARCH 25, 1976

CALL: 06.30; SALT FLATS AND SAND DUNES AT NEFTA; SETS: EXT. LARS’ HOMESTEAD; EXT. EDGE OF DUNE SEA; EXT. DESERT WASTELAND; EXT. SANDCRAWLER; SCS: 26; 15 [C-3PO AND BLEACHED BONES]; 14 [C-3PO AND R2-D2 ARGUE ABOUT WHICH WAY TO GO]; B32 [STORMTROOPERS FIND EVIDENCE OF DROIDS]; C42 [LUKE DISCOVERS DEAD UNCLE OWEN AND AUNT BERU]; 3 PART [LUKE IN WASTELAND WITH DROID THAT MALFUNCTIONS].

Day two: sunset failed again; “torrents” of rain; trucks stuck in mud. Day three morning: auditor Ralph Leo leaves with first rushes for Jympson in England. Scene 15: Daniels “barely move… just as I got ’round the corner, I fell over… arm sticking in the sand at ninety degrees… if nobody stood in my immediate range of vision, I was totally alone… In the evenings I would seek out the biggest group of people making the most noise to relocate myself as a human being.” Afternoon: Sir Alec and Lady Guinness arrive in Tozeur; wrap 5:57 PM, “bad light.” Day four: steak-and-kidney pie from the mobile kitchen; Hamill and Kurtz bond over Carl Barks Donald Duck comics. **Watts**: “We worked like stink, twenty hours every day… a very good shakedown period.” He hires a persistent local office boy. To make up time, two units: second unit “Jawa activity shots with robots in front of sandcrawler”; first unit B32, stormtroopers played by “six local men,” 8,500 dinars ($6.50) for the day. **Lucas**: “It was purely a case of Fox not putting up the money until it was too late… every day we would lose an hour or so due to those robots… that six-week delay cost an enormous amount of money. Ultimately, we had to scrap whole days… ‘Okay, we’ll try to pick that up when we get back to the United States.’ ” A truck carrying robots catches fire; two homestead robots damaged. **Mollo** on stormtrooper assembly: black leotard, vacuum-form pieces, “ordinary domestic rubber gloves, with a bit of latex shoved on the front; the boots were ordinary spring-sided black boots painted white with shoe dye. Strange to say, it all worked.” SCS COMP: 5; TIME TAKEN: 6M 46S; DAILY STATUS: 3/4 DAY OVER.

## REPORT NOS. 5–7: FRIDAY, MARCH 26–SUNDAY, MARCH 28, 1976

CANYON IN TOZEUR; SETS: EXT. ROCK CANYON; SCS: 37 [LUKE AND C-3PO FIND THE FUGITIVE R2-D2]; 38 PART (SOME OF THIS TO BE SHOT IN USA) [LUKE ATTACKED BY TUSKEN RAIDER]; 39 PART [ARRIVAL OF BEN KENOBI IN CANYON]; 40 [LUKE FINDS INJURED C-3PO].

Landspeeder problems also argue for US pickups. Banthas (elephants) had always been scheduled later; **Barry**: circus elephants “not a practical idea.” Day Five, Luke finds errant R2: **Hamill** — “George is Luke… I always felt that way.” George downplays the restraining-bolt line: “It’s not a big deal… ‘Noo, I don’t think he’s going to try anything.’ ” Hamill does it small, thinking he’ll prove George wrong; “Cut. Print it. Perfect.” After that, “I often felt like I was playing George; I even went so far as to do his little beard gestures.” Lucas’s nickname *The Kid* until the beard. **Daniels**: “See-Threepio is a kind of English butler, a cross between Laurel and Hardy with his friend… he loves being around Luke because that’s his purpose—to look after people… I kept acting the whole thing out, facially… even though no one could see me.” Caption: when Luke finds dead aunt and uncle, Hamill wants to fall sobbing; Lucas prefers “a more neutral performance, so that audiences would be able to project their feelings onto him” — Eisenstein/Kuleshov montage (Rinzler). Luke’s poncho “just a straight blanket with some edging”; hat abandoned; five Luke outfits, £2,020 ($4,700). Moisture collectors “made out of airplane junk” (Barry). EE video: late March, Stears radio-controls a droid, Hamill watches the skies (no audio; 0:46).

Friday wraps early, heavy rain. Saturday storm. **Watts**: “it is pissing rain and there’s a wind like you wouldn’t believe.” Rest day; salt flats so slippery they need locals’ Wellington boots; “The top had gone off the homestead and that was already halfway to Algeria somewhere.” **Barry**: “It blew three miles in the night, it just rolled and rolled and rolled.” **Lucas**: “The sandcrawler had been completely blown apart! … The army trucks that came to pull the stuff out of the mud got stuck in the mud.” Sunday: Guinness first appears in costume. **Bunny Alsup**: before camera “he lay down in the sand in his brand-new clean costume and dirtified himself… one of his goals on the film was for everything to be used and dirty.” Hamill and Daniels had dinner with Sir Alec and Lady Guinness twice. Hamill on Guinness: like going before the principal, then loosens up; “May I suggest something?” — “Stop and think of what you’re saying. Are you *talking* about going or are you *going* there?” **Guinness**: “a great feeling of strange space that stretches on for hundreds of miles; it’s genuine, real and gritty—it’s not some made-up world.” EE video: Guinness clowns with Baker, then films finding damaged C-3PO (torso only, pants “off”) (no audio; 0:42). SCS COMP: 7; TIME TAKEN: 11M 0S.

## REPORT NOS. 8–10: MONDAY, MARCH 29–WEDNESDAY, MARCH 31, 1976

TOZEUR; ROCK CANYON; SCS: 38 [TUSKEN ATTACKS LUKE]; 47 [VIEW OF MOS EISLEY, “SCUM AND VILLAINY”]; 35 [TUSKEN RAIDERS ESPY LUKE IN LANDSPEEDER]; 17 [JAWAS NEUTRALIZE R2-D2]; 18 [JAWAS CARRY R2-D2 TO SANDCRAWLER]; B42 [LUKE AND BEN DISCOVER DEAD JAWAS]; E42 [JAWA BONFIRE].

Complex two-unit day. Stunt supervisor **Peter Diamond** plays the Tusken; rehearses without costume, then can’t see in the headgear — “Hamill dodged the blind stuntman’s violent blows, his look of fear was real.” Climb to the cliff for a view later replaced by a Mos Eisley matte; tea urn, mules, porters; Daniels put into metal just in time for tea, stuck in it through the break. End of day: twin-suns (scene 29) finally shot on VistaVision, clouds gone. Tuesday (Day Nine): Jawas — Jack Purvis; Kurtz’s two children; an English truck driver’s son; Mahjoub from the Tunis hotel; local children 8–10. **Mollo**: “supposed to look like little rats, sort of grimy and filthy.” George’s first prototype “too theatrical”; pulled back to “a black stocking mask and these eye-bulbs… a little brown cloak with a Russian Cossack hood, and a scarf.” Daniels drops a Jawa cadaver into a bonfire, coordinating with the landspeeder on its carousel. **Daniels** on Baker: “he didn’t read the script from beginning to end, so I was totally on my own… Often it was very lonely doing scenes with him, for he wasn’t there.” On Hamill: jokes about upstaging; “one of the things that is a delight is the way Luke talks to See-Threepio. The same with Alec Guinness, though he must have had a very difficult time… to suddenly start acting with robot scrap heaps.” **Baker**: “I found him to not be an easy guy to get close to… we were not close friends.” Daniels overhears Taylor vs. Kurtz as light fades (fragmented in the book): “Who’s meant … be lighting this … movie,…or me, because … you’re … it, I’ll … off!” Radio-control pattern: **Stears** — mornings perfect, by two o’clock interference; maybe mica under the sand; Arabic stations. **Hamill**: “the robots would go bananas, bumping into each other, falling down, breaking.”

Script problem found and solved on location, typography locked — **Lucas**: Ben has nothing to do after the Death Star escape; the station is too easy to escape; “the film needed more drama. The answer to both structural problems was to kill off the Jedi Knight. At one point C-3PO and Chewbacca were also candidates for death, but Ben was the most logical choice—as it also enabled Lucas to make an important point about death and its relation to the Force. Kenobi wouldn’t just die, he would disappear, joining the Force with his consciousness intact.” “I started writing the revised fourth draft while we were in London doing preproduction. I continued it when I was in Tunisia and I didn’t finish it till we were back in England. I had been toying with the idea of killing Obi-wan but I made the decision while we were in the desert… I had this climactic scene that had no climax about two-thirds of the way through the film… no real threat in the Death Star. The villains were like tenpins… As I originally wrote it, Ben Kenobi and Vader have a swordfight, and Ben hits a switch and the door slams closed and they all run away, and Vader is left standing there with egg on his face… It totally diminished any impact the Death Star had.” Bunny Alsup’s IBM typewriter “fell off the plane” in Tunisia and broke; she types changes on a French manual typewriter (keys in different places) while sick with dysentery from fish.

Sandcrawler £54,850 ($128,000); NASA-style treads on the Tunisia–Algeria border, plus Tunisian military trucks, prompt Algerian official concern — “a massive military mobilization.” **Lucas**: “They had to come and inspect the sandcrawler to verify that it was not a secret weapon.” (It had already been blown apart and moved in pieces.) **Lucas** tells Guinness, typography locked: “I took Alec aside and told him I was going to kill him off halfway through the picture… ‘You mean I get killed but I don’t have a death scene?’ he said. But he kept it under control.” EE video: printed daily of scene 47, above Mos Eisley, circa end of March (0:22). SCS COMP: 15; SCREEN TIME: 15M 48S.

(Lock odd capitalization in the report heads as printed.)

Morning of 1 April: several hours to Matmata, Sidi Driss Hotel as homestead. **Shelagh Fraser** (Aunt Beru) joins; Lucas notes: “a little British, but okay.” Courtyard dressed with storage tanks, toys, aircraft-galley kitchen units, drainpipes, special fabric; homestead courtyard £6,200 ($14,500). One day, then Djerba. Day Twelve: first “Old West” Mos Eisley — 12 Jawa children, 4 Tuskens, 6 stormtroopers, 4 starpilots, 3 robots, 16 farmers. **Barry** spotted low domes from the ferry road; added premade domes, “strange aerials” to mask electrical poles, a crashed spaceship around the one tree, sandcrawler track-roller as street dressing. “We never wasted a thing!” Locals “a bit amazed”; guard on location; Kurtz: “Those crazy Americans!” Day Thirteen: C-3PO suit coming undone — “my trousers have completely given up… bits of me would fall apart… a greebly would fall off.” Prop man Maxie looks after him (Arabic mixed with French). Landspeeder prototypes dress the cantina exterior. Then the mosque location.

**4 April**, last Tunisian day: **Garrick Hagon** (Biggs), **Anthony Forrest** (Fixer), **Koo Stark** (Camie) arrive for Anchorhead with Hamill. Scene 8: a local woman shakes her fist at speeding Luke. **Stark**: remote R2 fails to stop and “wandered onto the set of *Jesus of Nazareth*!” They speed through the day’s work. Daniels and Hamill dinner: “this peculiar world.” Cast-and-crew party paid by Lucas and Kurtz. Only cameraman Jeff Glover and assistant Mike Shackleton stay for aerial landspeeder plates and R2 pickups. **Watts**: “We got out of Tunisia on schedule… We were underscheduled in preparation time.” **Lucas** does not appear at the party, typography locked: “By the end of the location shoot, I’d only got about two-thirds of what I’d set out to get… I was very depressed about the whole thing. I was so depressed I couldn’t even go to the wrap party… I was seriously, seriously depressed at that point. Because nothing had gone right… I was compromising right and left just to get semi-things done. I was desperately unhappy.” EE audio: Christian describes lunch on the road to Djerba with a tired and dejected Lucas (Rinzler, 2012; 1:39). EE video: Ajim, Djerba, extra lowered into a droid as foreground dressing, early April (no audio; 1:20). Caption: 2 April 1976, Hamill, Kurtz, and Lucas toast Guinness’s birthday in Djerba; a “crashed” spaceship masks a tree. SCS COMP: 28; SCREEN TIME: 24M 39S.
