## Opening (p. 2)

Fall 1968, *The Rain People*: Mona Skager, typography locked — Lucas “would write and I would type it up”; in a motel room “he started talking about holograms, spaceships, and the wave of the future… he had a vision of doing some kind of science-fiction film.” Lucas already writing feature *THX 1138* while contemplating “a more fantastic, cinematic variation on a theme.”

**Lucas**, typography locked: “I had thought about doing what became *Star Wars* long before *THX 1138*. I’ve always been intrigued with Flash Gordon. It was one of my favorite serials and comic books, along with Tommy Tomorrow and those kinds of things. I really loved adventures in outer space, and I wanted to do something in that genre, which is where *THX* partially came from. THX really is Buck Rogers in the twentieth century, rather than Buck Rogers in the future.”

EE audio captions (Rinzler EE, Chapter One): Lucas on Flash Gordon serials “sparked his desire to make the Star Wars saga” (Interview by Arnold, 1979); Lucas on deciding to go to USC (Interview by Alan Arnold, 1979). Cross-ref notes 169. Captions only; the audio files were not separately transcribed.

## Black Thursday — and Beyond!

American Zoetrope, Folsom Street, 1969; *THX 1138* its first film. **Lucas**: Coppola “had to force me to write my first script… ‘You’re never going to be a good director unless you learn how to write.’” First *THX* script “terrible”; a hired writer “worse”; rewrite with Walter Murch. 19 November 1970 Warner screening; budget $777,777.77; studio cut five minutes; released 11 March 1971. Warner withdrew Zoetrope funding and called a $300,000 development loan (*Apocalypse Now*, *The Conversation*, *The Black Stallion*). Lucas “happy with *THX 1138*” personally; “unemployed and penniless” commercially — “it led to many positives—and, eventually, to *Star Wars.*”

## Night of the Director / Future *THX*

Coppola challenges Lucas to a “happier” film → *American Graffiti*. **Lucas**: “I don’t want to write this; I can’t stand writing” (italics as in the book). Huycks unavailable. May 1971 Europe / Cannes Directors’ Fortnight for *THX*; stop in New York during *The Godfather*. **Lucas** tries to buy Flash Gordon from King Features: “They said they wanted Federico Fellini to direct it, and they wanted 80 percent of the gross, so I said forget it.” Crystallization, typography locked: “I realized that Flash Gordon wasn’t the movie I wanted to do; if I had done it, I would’ve had to have Ming the Merciless in it—and I didn’t want to have Ming the Merciless. I decided at that point to do something more original… I wanted to take ancient mythological motifs and update them—I wanted to have something totally free and fun, the way I remembered space fantasy.”

Picker at Cannes: $5,000 to develop *Graffiti*, $5,000 on delivery, $15,000 if made — same day as Lucas’s birthday and Sofia Coppola’s birth. Huycks decline (their own movie). **Lucas** to Picker on a second idea: “I’ve been toying with this idea of a space-opera fantasy film in the vein of Flash Gordon… And he said, ‘Great, we’ll make a deal for that, too.’ And that was really the birth of *Star Wars.* It was only a notion up to then—at that point, it became an obligation [laughs]!”

*Apocalypse Now* already turned down by UA; “Dr. Strangelove in Vietnam,” 16mm documentary style, with John Milius. Meets Gary Kurtz via Techniscope / *THX* reel; Kurtz later line-produces *Graffiti* after *Apocalypse* stalls. UA two-picture agreement 28 December 1971 (*Graffiti* and “a second picture”). UA had registered the title “The Star Wars” with the MPAA on **3 August 1971**, but the agreement does not name it. Lucasfilm incorporated that year; lawyers Tom Pollock, Jake Bloom, Andy Rigrod; agent Jeff Berg. UA passes on the *Graffiti* script; Coppola, after *The Godfather*, comes in as producer for Universal. Inducement letter 6 April 1972: *Graffiti* budget not to exceed $775,000, plus two future pictures with priority. Huycks come on for dialogue (Steve/Laurie). Principal photography 26 June–4 August, twenty-nine days (cf. Arnold/Lucas “twenty-eight days” in notes 169 — record, do not collapse). **Harrison Ford** (as Bob Falfa) on Kurtz: “He was the guy who told me no more drinking beer on the streets, and then no more drinking beer in the trailer, and then no more drinking beer.”

## Vietnam Wars in Space

Kurtz scouts Philippines/Hong Kong Nov–Dec 1972 for *Apocalypse Now*; Columbia drops it after Lucas turns in *Graffiti*. Universal pessimistic, considers TV. **Lucas**, typography locked: “I was in debt… I started to work on *Star Wars* rather than continue with *Apocalypse Now.*” Carry-over, typography locked: “A lot of my interest in *Apocalypse Now* was carried over into *Star Wars*… I would essentially deal with some of the same interesting concepts that I was going to use and convert them into space fantasy, so you’d have essentially a large technological empire going after a small group of freedom fighters or human beings.” Also speed/hot cars: Fiat Bianchina → THX’s Lola T70 → Milner’s yellow deuce in *Graffiti*.

## The Force of the Will (Journal of the Whills, p. 8)

Early 1973 name lists: “Emperor Ford Xerxes XII,” then Xenos, Thorpe, Roland, Monroe, Lars, Kane, Hayden, Crispin, Leila, Zena, Owen, Mace, Wan, Star, Bail, Biggs, Bligh, Cain, Clegg, Fleet, Valorum. Combinations: Alexander Xerxes XII “Emperor of Decarte”; Owen Lars “Imperial General”; **Han Solo is “leader of the Hubble people”** (Rinzler gloss: probably Edwin Hubble); Mace Windy a “Jedi-Bendu”; C.2. Thorpe a space pilot; Lord Annakin Starkiller “King of Bebers”; Luke Skywalker “Prince of Bebers.” Locales: Yoshiro and Aquilae desert; Brunhuld then Alderaan “city planets, capital of the ‘Border System’”; Yavin a jungle planet “whose natives are eight-foot-tall Wookies” (spelling locked); Ophuchi; Norton II ice; etc.

Handwritten two-page *Journal of the Whills, [Part] I*: “the story of Mace Windy, a revered Jedi-Bendu of Ophuchi, as related to us by C. J. Thorpe, padawaan learner to the famed Jedi.” Chuiee Two Thorpe of Kissel; father **“Han Dardell Thorpe, chief pilot of the renown galactic cruiser *Tarnack*”**; Intersystems Academy; “Jedi-Templer”; Windy “Warlord to the Chairman of the Alliance of Independent Systems,” thought “even more powerful than the Imperial leader of the Galactic Empire,” replaced by comrades’ fear. Part II trails off on a fusion-portables shipment to Yavin and a summons to “the desolate second planet of Yoshiro.”

**Lucas** on the title, typography locked: “*Journal of the Whills* came from the fact that you ‘will’ things to happen.” Rinzler: like a Disney storybook device, “whatever story followed came from a book.” Recycled later: “the Jedi; the phonetics of *Chuiee;* a great pilot named Han; an academy; and the galactic Empire.” This is **not** yet a statement of the Force as later defined — do not collapse with later Force theology.

## The Hidden Plot / May 1973 treatment (p. 9)

Kurosawa: Yoshiro / Toshiro Mifune; *Seven Samurai* master-disciple and fast cuts at USC; **Lucas**: “*Hidden Fortress* was an influence on *Star Wars* right from the very beginning… I had some scenes—the cantina scene and the space battle scene—but I couldn’t think of a basic plot. Originally, the film was a good concept in search of a story.” Two bureaucrats/peasants: “like having two clowns—it goes back to Shakespeare, which is probably where Kurosawa got it.” General Skywalker’s head-scratch from Mifune; can’t-check-the-steed beat from *Hidden Fortress*. *The Searchers* saloon → cantina. “lost” boys of *Peter Pan* become “jungle rebels with just a hint of the Vietcong” (Rinzler gloss).

Treatment, started 17 April 1973, submitted 20 May; fourteen typed pages, title “The Star Wars.” Opens DEEP SPACE, planet Aquilae, fighters vs “gargantuan space fortress,” thirty-third century, civil wars. A rebel princess flees; guarded by general **(LUKE SKYWALKER)**; two bickering bureaucrats from the fortress; disguises; wastelands toward spaceport “Gordon,” then Ophuchi; ruined temple; ten rebel boys; cantina; “lazer sword” (spelling locked) — “One of the bullies lies double, slashed from chin to groin.” Giant furry alien on Yavin; cantankerous farmer “married to an Alien”; prison on Alderaan (here “capital of the Empire”); award ceremony; bureaucrats realize they have been “adventuring with demigods.” **No Han Solo and no Darth Vader in this treatment.** STAR WARS PROGRESSION list at the end of the summary (fighters vs fortress, rebels, princess, Skywalker as a general, bickering bureaucrats, landspeeder, temple, cantina, lazer sword, spy, Yavin/Wookees, furry alien, farmer, Alderaan as Imperial capital, prison rescue, award ceremony).

## A Clockwork Studio / Adventures of Fox (to p. 13)

Berg to UA “an epic space fantasy”; 7 May 1973, fourteen-page treatment plus ten attached images (NASA, tanks, fantasy illustrations) and a $20,000 development ask. UA passes 29 May, money the main reason (Pollock: they had said $3 million). Contractual submit to Universal; Lucas unhappy after a five-minute *Graffiti* recut. Ten-day option; Tannen asks more time; Lucasfilm treats silence as no. Wasserman “We don’t make science-fiction movies” is a circulated story; Pollock: “Ned was in a waffling state because he was in a waffling state about *American Graffiti*.” Berg: Universal “never screw up” procedurally; they didn’t believe in the idea or in Lucas at $3 million. **Lucas**: “UA was very cool… they thought of it as a big, expensive movie and they didn’t understand it. Universal was the same way… I think Disney would have accepted this movie if Walt Disney were still alive.”

Fox: Berg with Alan Ladd Jr.; Ladd screens *Graffiti* at 9 a.m. on the Fox lot — “it absolutely bowled me over.” **Ladd**: “George said he had this idea about *Star Wars*, so I said, let’s make the deal… I felt that *Graffiti* was further from what he was really into than *Star Wars.*” *Planet of the Apes* sequels as a possible Fox appetite (Kurtz). Pollock, 13 July 1973 letter: “a very lowball deal” — $15,000 development, $50,000 to write, $100,000 to direct, $3 million budget, 40% net to Lucasfilm / 60% Fox; Kurtz $50,000 to produce; Marcia Lucas and Verna Fields preapproved editors, Walter Murch postproduction; “shall not have the right to assign an executive producer or exercise any other production controls other than standard location auditors.” **Lucas**: “I’m very, very adamant about my creative work… I was very anti-corporation… So I fought for many years to make sure no one could tell me what to do.” Memo Agreement signed retroactively 20 August 1973, “just nineteen days after *Graffiti* was released… but based on the *pre-release* July terms.” **Lucas**: “I actually needed the money in May because I was so far in debt… In September, by the time they sent me a check, *Graffiti* was already a big hit… If I’d just waited until *Graffiti* came out, I could have made the deal much better.” Turnaround at any board meeting; Universal still had priority if it elected *Radioland Murders*. Warren Hellman: Fox “going broke,” board “moronic conversation” on budgets. $10,000 on signing, $10,000 on first-draft delivery, $30,000 on start of photography — “only an agreement to go to the next step.” Joe Viskocil at a USC *Graffiti* screening: Lucas names “*Star Wars*” as his next project — “bells started ringing.”
