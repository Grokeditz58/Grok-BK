## 160 — Shields, *George Lucas* (Chelsea House, 2002)

Unused staff/cast lines, typography as locked in `shields-01.md` / `shields-02.md` / `shields-03.md`:

- Irwin Allen, after an early screening, p. 9: “There’s no stars, there’s no love story, what are they clapping at?” Friends’ screening: Spielberg and De Palma “said nothing. Marcia burst into tears” (narrative, not a Marcia quotation).
- Francis Ford Coppola, p. 36, on Lucas’s aloofness on *Finian’s Rainbow*: “You gotta learn how to do this” — interacting with the crew.
- Steven Spielberg on Lucas’s cutting, p. 39: “George makes his visuals come to life with montage. That makes him unique in our generation, since most of us do it instead with composition and camera placement.”
- *Star Wars* shoot, p. 61. An unnamed technician says Lucas knew what he wanted “but ‘the problem was that none of us knew what that was.’” Mark Hamill: “I have a sneaking suspicion that if there were a way to make movies without actors, George would do it.” Harrison Ford: “You can type this [expletive] George, but you sure can’t say it.” Shields prints “[expletive]”; do not silently restore a swear word from another book.
- Michael McAllister (ILM), p. 75, on *Willow*: if it had been someone else’s movie, Lucasfilm would never have signed on, “But this was for the boss and the boss gets to work however he wants.”
- John Lasseter (Shields’s spelling “Lassetter,” corrected in the notes): “You’d kill yourself on effects, but no one remembered the films.”
- Ben Burtt’s *Film Sound Today* remarks are paraphrased (rust, dents, real motors, plus a sound-source list) without a lockable first-person sentence; no Burtt quotation is entered. Shields’s p. 67 Kershner paragraph is Shields’s own wording and is already in a 160 character entry as Shields, not as Kershner; it is not re-entered here as a Kershner quote.

## 162 — Jim Smith, *George Lucas* (Virgin Film, 2003)

British typography (single quotes, spaced ellipses) as locked in the 162 notes. Several of these lines already sat inside Lucas/Smith-headed entries; they are re-entered here under the actual speaker.

- Alan Ladd Jr., p. 54 (scan-verified): “I’m interested, I don’t understand this, but I loved *American Graffiti* and whatever you do is OK with me . . .”
- Brian De Palma’s sarcasm about “the farts of others” is given as the reason “the force of others” “lose[s] the second half of its name” (pp. 55–56).
- Lawrence Kasdan, p. 101, on when Lucas knew Luke and Leia were siblings: Kasdan himself learned it when handed the *Jedi* outline. On Lucas: “I don’t know when he knew that ... I don’t know if that was [always] part of the scheme. It’s about family, this saga.”
- Father reveal, pp. 102–103 (Smith’s production account): no script version, including the shooting draft, contains “I am your father.” The shooting draft had “Obi-Wan is your father.” Kershner and Lucas told Hamill the real line in secret. Smith estimates as few as five people (Lucas, Kasdan, Hamill, Jones, and Kershner) knew before release. Kershner: “the perfect crime.” **Do not collapse** Smith’s shooting-draft line “Obi-Wan is your father” with Jones (166), who reports that Prowse on set delivered “Obi-Wan killed your father.”
- Kershner, formerly Lucas’s USC teacher, is told: “Remember, nothing will work, so you’ve got to improvise” and “Don’t be intimidated by what they tell you can’t be done.” On Hoth, Kershner: “consider it as . . . shooting a documentary.”
- Kasdan, brought back for *Jedi*, calls it “a favour.” He suggested Luke becoming “the Emperor’s new enforcer,” Luke dying, or Luke using the Death Star on the Imperial capital; “all of these were rejected by Lucas who felt very strongly that the ending as planned was the core of the film.”
- Spielberg, 2002, after offering to direct *Jedi* and being refused: “Star Wars is George’s baby … This is George’s . . . fingerprints.”
- Richard Marquand “always strenuously denied” that Lucas directed *Jedi* in all but name.
- Ford, Introduction: “repeatedly quoted” telling Lucas, “You can write this shit, but you can’t say it.” This is not the same typography as Shields’s “[expletive]” / “type this” wording; record both, do not collapse.
- Ford, at the *American Graffiti* wrap-party reel: “This is a fucking hit!”
- Coppola, VO in *filmmaker* (1968): “The system will fall by its own weight. It can’t fail to!”

## 163 — Rau and Rau, *George Lucas: Creator of Star Wars* (Franklin Watts, 1999)

Quotations are the Raus’ report, typography locked from the printed pages as already noted.

- Spielberg, p. 25: “George Lucas was always the star of the student film festivals then.”
- Coppola, on meeting Lucas on *Finian’s Rainbow*: “We started a conversation and a friendship.”
- Ralph McQuarrie: “He wanted people to look and say, ‘Gee, that looks great, just like something on the screen.’”
- Hamill, on the *Star Wars* cattle call: “There were guys literally everywhere, in age from sixteen to thirty-five.” Ford: “George wanted and hired strong personalities.” Peter Mayhew: “My first impression was that he [Lucas] was an average sort of guy,” “He didn’t look like the image of a Hollywood director.” Ford again: “Very little time was wasted. George didn’t have an authoritarian attitude like many directors. . . . He was different. . . . He encouraged our contributions.”
- John Williams: “Why don’t you let me write our own classical music?”
- Kasdan: “I liked George, and he was always under pressure—they were always starting movies before they had scripts.” “George understood that you could integrate the effects into the story so that they formed part of the story in a way people had never really tried before.”
- Indy, compared with Han: Lucas imagined Indy “rebellious, but with some morals, a lot like Han Solo.” Spielberg envisioned someone “a little more sleazy”; they compromised on someone “like Harrison Ford.”

## 165 — Norwich, *I Am George Lucas* (Scholastic, 2013)

- Spielberg, p. 35: “George Lucas was always the star of the student festivals then,” — Rau 1999 had “student film festivals.” Record both wordings; do not silently harmonize.
- Attribution slip, p. 45: “I was very grateful to have someone of my own generation around to discuss what I was trying to do as opposed to what I was able to do,” Francis later recalled. “I very quickly became aware of his superior intelligence.” In Rau 1999 the first sentence was a Lucas quote; Norwich gives the whole block to Coppola and adds the second sentence. “like two halves of a whole” remains correctly George’s. Do not collapse the two attributions.
- **Redeems-his-father attribution conflict.** Norwich, pp. 107–108, reports a January 2012 *New York Times* interview: “The movies were the story of Anakin Skywalker and Luke Skywalker, and when Luke saves the galaxy and redeems his father, that’s where that story ends.” Jones (166), p. 438, n.11, cites the same wording to Geoff Boucher, *Los Angeles Times,* May 7, 2008. Do not collapse without checking the originals.
- Not entered as staff quotations: the p. 80 Kershner “clash” is Norwich’s wording (and prints “directing reigns”); James Earl Jones is named as Vader’s voice, not quoted; Chewbacca’s voice “used a mix of bears, walruses, dogs, and lions” is Norwich on Burtt and the sound engineers.
