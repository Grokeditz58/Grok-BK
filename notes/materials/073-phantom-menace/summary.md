# The Phantom Menace (novelization): summary of strongest findings

Source: Terry Brooks, *Star Wars: Episode I The Phantom Menace* (Del Rey 1999; ebook v3.1, eISBN 978-0-307-79567-0). This ebook also bundles James Luceno's *Darth Maul: End Game* (2012), Del Rey era intros and excerpts, and the novels timeline. Hand-read in full on 2026-11-03 CST. Citations are "TPM ch. N", "End Game", or "[excerpt title]". Detailed notes are in pm-01 to pm-07.

1. **Maul is narrator-rated as Qui-Gon's superior in skill and strength, and as holding off both Jedi at his absolute peak.**
   > "he was younger, quicker, and stronger than Qui-Gon, and he was gaining ground rapidly." (TPM ch. 15, Tatooine)
   > "He was skilled, Anakin saw—more skilled, perhaps, than the men he faced." (TPM ch. 21)
   > "The Sith Lord he battled with Obi-Wan was more than his equal in weapons training, and he had the advantage of being younger and stronger. Qui-Gon was nearing sixty" (TPM ch. 22)
   > "Together, they were able to hold their own against the Sith Lord, but their efforts at attack ... were woefully inadequate." / "Darth Maul was a warrior in his prime, never to be any better, his powers at their apex." (TPM ch. 22)
   > Sidious: "He had been more than the equal of the Jedi Knights he had faced and should have been able to defeat them easily. It was bad luck and chance that had led to his death" (TPM ch. 24)
   - Qui-Gon's credentials raise the bar: "one of the most able swordsmen in the Jedi order". His Master considered him "one of the best the Master had taught in his more than four hundred years in the order" (TPM ch. 22).
   - Counterpoint from Maul's side (End Game): Qui-Gon was a "worthy opponent", and Maul had not faced such a fight "since he had fought and been defeated by his own Master". Maul also pre-scouted the generator complex and *planned* the laser-gate separation, which undercuts the "bad luck" excuse.
2. **The Obi-Wan vs Maul duel has a novel-only telekinetic exchange, and Maul is "the stronger".**
   > "Darth Maul began to use his command of the Force to fling heavy objects at Obi-Wan ... Obi-Wan responded in kind, and the air was filled with deadly missiles." / "for a time it was fought evenly. But Darth Maul was the stronger of the two" (TPM ch. 23)
   - Obi-Wan severs half of Maul's saberstaff (TPM ch. 23). Maul's wounds over the fight are noted ("burns and slashes").
3. **Obi-Wan's comeback: a Force leap and saber summon, which the Council treats as proof of his Knighthood.**
   > "Imbued with the power of the Force, he cleared the rim easily, somersaulting behind the Sith Lord in a single smooth, powerful motion. Even as he landed, he was drawing Qui-Gon Jinn's fallen lightsaber to his outstretched hand" (TPM ch. 23)
   > "Only a Jedi Knight fully in tune with the Force could have saved himself against such an adversary." (TPM ch. 24)
   - The cut is a chest slash in Brooks. End Game retells it as "bisecting him at the hips" and makes Maul's own gloating the cause ("sabotaged himself by drawing out his moment of victory").
4. **Anakin: a precognitive torpedo kill, and a connection to the Force rated above Yoda's.**
   > "he could see what no one else could ... responding to a voice that spoke to him alone, that whispered always of the future ... faster than thought" (TPM ch. 23). Torpedoes through "a broad vent" destroy the Droid Control Ship.
   > "That extraordinarily high midi-chlorian count gave the boy a connection to the Force that even Jedi Masters on the order of Yoda might never achieve." (TPM ch. 24)
   - The Council test: Anakin names images before they are shown (TPM ch. 17). There is also the novel-only reverse-thrust kill on a pursuing fighter (TPM ch. 23) and the podrace instincts (TPM ch. 10–12).
   - Bundled *Rogue Planet* excerpt: at 12 he levitates himself and Obi-Wan through a shield port. "Obi-Wan Kenobi had never felt so close to such a powerful connection with the Force, not in Qui-Gon, nor Mace Windu. Not even in Yoda." This is a few lines after "not ... able to levitate ... more than a few centimeters."
5. **Maul's mobility and durability.**
   - On Tatooine (TPM ch. 15), Maul falls about 20 m, "landed in a crouch, and rose instantly to his feet."
   - End Game adds that he caught a STAP mid-Force-jump, tore through "a score of mounted Gungans", moved "faster than human eyes could follow", crushed a droid head, and fought on despite a leg wound from Togorian pirates.
   - "I have waited a long time for this. I have trained for it endlessly." (TPM ch. 20)
6. **Tech and battle data.**
   - The Gungan fambaa shield stopped tank cannons, and its stated limit is that it "would not deflect small, slow-moving droids" (TPM ch. 21).
   - The Federation battleship's shields repel fighter attacks from outside; Anakin gets past them by flying into the open hangar (TPM ch. 23).
   - The midichlorian lore in full: "Without the midi-chlorians, life could not exist" (TPM ch. 17).
7. **Notable non-TPM extras in the bundle.**
   - *Shatterpoint* intro: Mace created Vaapad and sees shatterpoints. These are covered in 061.
   - *Death Star*: Lambda shuttle stats. *Rogue Squadron*: X-wing shield mechanics.
   - *Vector Prime*: Jaina's Force-piloting "three moves ahead". *Betrayal*: Jacen redirects blaster bolts against about 20 troopers.
   - The timeline lists the TPM-era reading order: Saboteur*, Cloak of Deception, Shadow Hunter, **TPM**, Rogue Planet, Outbound Flight, The Approaching Storm.
