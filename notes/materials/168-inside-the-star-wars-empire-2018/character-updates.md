### Empty seven-foot Vader suit without James Earl Jones’s voice

**Speaker:** Bill Kimberlin (ILM staff memoir; describing a model-shop mannequin, not a production line).
**Source:** “One Saturday… I saw an outside telephone repair guy had brought his kids with him… ‘Would you guys like to see Darth Vader?’ Their dad said it was OK, so I took them over to the model shop, which had a fully costumed seven-foot-tall Darth Vader on a hidden mannequin. Also, R2-D2 and C-3PO were there. This was long before any commercial mock-ups of these characters were out in the real world… They were impressed, but I could tell that they were also slightly disappointed… Without the lighting and music, without James Earl Jones’s thundering Darth Vader voice, it was just an empty suit.” — typography-faithful quote, Kimberlin, “Mrs. Hickman.”

### “Of course we can” / absolute certainty (X-wing blow-up)

**Speaker:** George Lucas, as reported by Bill Kimberlin; Kimberlin’s “absolute certainty” gloss flagged as Kimberlin.

### SB19 “wow” shot: sixty-three elements, two seconds, “Great”

**Speaker:** George Lucas (“wow” / “Great”), as reported by Bill Kimberlin; production facts are Kimberlin’s.
**Source:** Space-battle shot #19, “pictured on the cover of this book. This was the shot that George had told Ken that he wanted to be a ‘wow’ shot. Ken had set up four camera crews shooting the battling spaceships for three months just to get this shot. It contained sixty-three separate elements… It would take about ten hours when it came time for the final printing… It took almost a year to get that shot from start to finish… Oh, and look quickly, because it’s only two seconds long.” Temporary composite shown to Lucas: “I ran it for him and he said, ‘Great.’ That was it. His most frequent expression of approval.” Art director locked as printed “Joe Johnson.” Caption: “*Jedi* Space Battle shot #19 (cover photo) was also used for this poster.” — typography-faithful quotes, Kimberlin, “What’s It Like?”; Kimberlin, photo insert 2.

### Ackbar window: “When it does come up, we will figure out an answer” / “making it up as we went along”

**Speaker:** George Lucas (the window line), as reported by Bill Kimberlin; “making it up as we went along” is Kimberlin’s conclusion, not a Lucas quote. Do not collapse with later “always planned” claims or with Kaminski’s thesis.

### Work print of the original *Star Wars*: “Look to your left!” / “worse than amateurish”

**Speaker:** George Lucas (off-camera direction), as heard by Bill Kimberlin on a work-print reel; the “awful, worse than amateurish” and “didn’t expect much” judgments are Kimberlin’s (the latter flagged as rumor).
**Source:** “a reel of picture and a reel of sound from one sequence in the first film. It comprised some of the opening scenes inside a spaceship with Princess Leia trying to escape some stormtroopers… This was not the finished movie—it was the final edit but the music, sound mix, visual effects, color grading, etc., were all not present… Watching it was an eye-opener. Without the final polish it looked awful, worse than amateurish. The prop guns made fizzing and popping sounds when fired, with fake-looking sparks spitting out the front of them. The actors were yelling and it was hard to hear what they were saying, and on top of that you could hear George from somewhere behind the camera yelling, ‘Look to your left! Look to your left!’” “The original *Star Wars* crew was no exception, and I could see why it was rumored that they didn’t expect much from it.” — typography-faithful quote, Kimberlin, “Hollywood.”

### “There is only one filmmaker at this company, and that is George Lucas”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, as reported by Bill Kimberlin (“had stated many times”).
**Source:** Reasons for selling the computer division that became Pixar: “Money was the reason for the decision to sell what would become Pixar to Jobs—that and the fact that George had stated many times, ‘There is only one filmmaker at this company, and that is George Lucas.’ He didn’t want things going out, essentially under his name, that could bomb and tarnish Lucasfilm’s reputation… But the money was the biggest factor.” After Pixar’s later success, typography locked: “I heard George comment on his having sold it. ‘The only thing I regret,’ he said, ‘is having let this successful model out into the world where others could copy it.’” Jobs bought it “for $10 million, $5 million in cash and $5 million in other guarantees, with Lucas retaining the use of certain software.” Feature-animation pitch: next day the president of Lucasfilm said, “Don’t ever mention this idea to George again.” — typography-faithful quotes, Kimberlin, “Pixar.”
**Notes:** `notes/168-inside-the-star-wars-empire-2018/kimberlin-03-screamer-through-defining.md`

### Marcia Lucas a “full partner”; $50 million buyback; *Raiders* “no emotional resolution”

**Speaker:** Bill Kimberlin (divorce/inventory/$50 million and “full partner”); Marcia Lucas (*Raiders* line), as reported by Kimberlin; Spielberg shot the added scene. Do not collapse with Jones 166.
**Source:** “After the successful release of *Jedi,* George and Marcia announced that they were divorcing… Marcia was a full partner in building the film empire that was now to be split. The sad thing was that her contributions were no longer going to be felt. It is not easy to find someone who is not only capable of pointing out flaws in dramatic film structure, but also able to tell you how to fix them.” Spielberg friends’ screening of *Raiders*: “At the end of the screening, Marcia said, ‘There is no emotional resolution to the story. You need a scene where we find out what happens to Indy and the girl.’ She pinpointed the problem and offered a solution. Priceless advice. Steven shot an additional scene, released the movie, and the rest is history.” “they split it right down the middle and George bought back the half of the company he didn’t already own for $50 million.” Later Disney sale “$4.05 billion, not including the Ranch.” Earlier, typography locked, Marcia was “every bit a full partner,” had cut *Taxi Driver*, and fashioned an emotional scene from almost-scrap Ewok footage. — typography-faithful quotes, Kimberlin, “Pixar”; also “Mrs. Hickman.”

### Spaz and Dippé “banned” from any *Star Wars* films; CG on a dropped *Jedi* shot

**Speaker:** George Lucas (“Why are people like this working for my company?”), as reported by Bill Kimberlin; the lifetime *Star Wars* ban and the later restoration credit are Kimberlin’s report.
**Source:** After an unauthorized Timothy Leary talk (~1990, during *T2*), Steve “Spaz” Williams and Mark Dippé wander into George’s Ranch office. “When George learned of this, he initially wanted the office offenders fired… ‘Why are people like this working for my company?’ he reportedly asked. When it was explained to him that there were certain ‘key’ employees that ILM could not do without, he relented. But they were famously ‘banned’ from the Ranch.” “Spaz and Mark Dippé survived, but they could never go back to the Ranch or work on any *Star War*s films, *ever*.” (XHTML italicization of “*Star War*s” locked.) Later: “Spaz was back in business and would go on to do CG work on a scene in the restoration of *Return of the Jedi* that George had had to drop in the original version because it looked so fake.” Spaz’s earlier dino-run test “convinced Spielberg to do all of *Jurassic Park* in computer-generated images.” — typography-faithful quotes, Kimberlin, “*The Mask*”; Kimberlin, “*Jurassic Park*.”

### Special Edition: “This *is* the original version”; a “comedy version of *Star Wars*”; “It’s a distraction”

**Speaker:** George Lucas (“This *is* the original version”; the comedy-*Star Wars* remark; comments in the Building C screening), as reported by Bill Kimberlin. “It’s a distraction” is Lucasfilm executives, as reported by Kimberlin. Tom Christopher and Rick McCallum (“Who the hell is this guy?”) as reported by Kimberlin / Christopher. Kimberlin’s van Gogh / Kafka / Harper Lee “who should decide” gloss is Kimberlin, not Lucas. Do not collapse the “original version” claim with later fan or critic accounts of the Special Edition.
**Source:** “When it came time to restore the original *Star Wars* and rerelease it (1997), George called a few people together in the Building C theater and we screened his personal print.” “As the film rolled in theater C, George made comments on the scenes he wanted to fix or, in some cases, add. From his perspective, these changes were all based on what he wanted to do in the first place but had either run out of time, technology, or money on the first go-round.” (“From his perspective” flagged as Kimberlin.) “After George pointed out all of the shots he wanted fixed, he said something I have wondered about ever since. He said that he was going to do a comedy version of *Star Wars.* It’s been years since that day and it hasn’t happened yet, but remember, you heard it here first!” “None of the Lucasfilm executives wanted George fooling around with his old movies. ‘It’s a distraction,’ they said. ‘We are not in the restoration business.’” ~1996: “The ancillary money stream from toy deals that George had so famously bargained for with the original *Star Wars* had dwindled.” Tom Christopher, hired “to handle all the film and lab details,” could not find the original wipe negatives in Los Angeles: “Nobody asked me. They were in my ILM editorial warehouse.” Protection masters “did it on the cheap.” “Tom wanted to know whether they should order a duplicate negative of the original version. It could easily be done, he said, and then there would always be an original version of this historic movie. George said, ‘This *is* the original version,’ meaning that what everyone else had described as a restoration, he considered the final version of the movie.” “The original *Star Wars* was rereleased in 1997 as the ‘Special Edition’ and it skyrocketed to the number one position at the box office for three weeks, grossing over $138 million. This surprised a lot of people, even George I suspect. *Empire* and *Jedi* soon followed, all of them together grossing more than $250 million. These unwanted rereleases helped build a cash pile that could fund the new *Star Wars* films that would ultimately generate billions of dollars in new revenue.” — typography-faithful quotes, Kimberlin, “The Distraction.”
