# 168 summary — Kimberlin, *Inside the Star Wars Empire* (Lyons Press, 2018)

Staff memoir by ILM visual-effects editor / department director Bill Kimberlin (hired May 1982 on *Revenge of the Jedi*; twenty years; last day a Friday in 2002). Not a Lucas biography and not a history of ILM (Foreword). All statements are as reported by Kimberlin. Flag his glosses. Staff, crew, and cast lines get the same literal-quote treatment as Lucas lines. Do not invent printed page numbers; cite by chapter title. The whole book was hand-read: front matter, twenty-eight vignettes in three text blocks, two photo inserts, and About the Author.

## Priority Lucas / saga / ILM lines

- **Hire and Ranch.** May 1982, *Revenge of the Jedi* space-battle hire. Spielberg, typography locked “Stephen Spielberg,” on Indy 2 as “a big Busby Berkeley–type dance number.” *American Graffiti* $750,000 / $50 million, “made George and Marcia independently wealthy.” Flash Gordon rights unobtainable, so Lucas wrote his own. Toy rights / own-the-movies / distribution fee. ILM name as Kimberlin etymology (“light industrial” + “magic”). Ranch ~5,000 acres / ≥$100 million; “Lucasland”; Lake Ewok / “Butthead Lake.” Kerner Company Optical Research Lab / *Blue Harvest*. Stained-glass artist “would eventually break up George’s marriage and run off with his wife, Marcia” — Kimberlin’s report; do not collapse with Jones 166.

- **Marcia Lucas.** “Every bit a full partner”; cut *Taxi Driver*; fashioned an emotional scene from almost-scrap Ewok footage. After *Jedi*, divorce: inventory even “one splicer…”; “they split it right down the middle and George bought back the half of the company he didn’t already own for $50 million.” Later Disney sale “$4.05 billion, not including the Ranch.” *Raiders* friends’ screening: “There is no emotional resolution to the story. You need a scene where we find out what happens to Indy and the girl.” Spielberg shot an additional scene. “I’d much rather hear what Marcia Lucas had to say” than a preview-company score. “When you get a big jolt of money, it’s very easy to be in awe of it and lose touch with reality.” Marcia “often said that George was ‘centered.’” Do not collapse these with Jones 166.

- **Empty Vader suit.** A fully costumed seven-foot Darth Vader on a hidden mannequin in the model shop, plus R2-D2 and C-3PO, shown to a telephone repairman’s kids. “Without the lighting and music, without James Earl Jones’s thundering Darth Vader voice, it was just an empty suit.” — Kimberlin, “Mrs. Hickman.”

- **Title change.** “Originally, *Return of the Jedi* was called *Revenge of the Jedi*. George had decided to change the name of the movie reportedly because some little kid wrote and reminded him that Jedis don’t seek revenge. Whether they do or not, I have no idea.” Do not collapse with Kazanjian / Cocks / Lucas “always Return” / Pollock / Kaminski. “I still like the sentiment in ‘Do. Or do not. There is no try,’ because that comes directly out of George’s personality.”

- **Line-up room / SB19 / Ackbar.** “Take this X-wing and blow him up”; Kimberlin: “I don’t think we can do that.” Lucas: “Of course we can,” “with such force that I instantly glimpsed what had got him where he was. Absolute certainty.” SB19: sixty-three elements, four crews three months, ~10 hours compositing, two seconds on screen, the “wow” shot; Lucas: “Great.” VistaVision Ackbar plates: “One of the supervisors, probably Richard Edlund, asked George what the window Akbar was looking out of was made of. George said, ‘Well, that hasn’t come up. When it does come up, we will figure out an answer.’” Kimberlin: “We were all sitting here participating in a story that George was making up as we went along.” Flag as Kimberlin; do not collapse with later “always planned” claims.

- **Work print of the original *Star Wars*.** Opening Leia-escaping-stormtroopers sequence without music, mix, or effects: “awful, worse than amateurish.” “you could hear George from somewhere behind the camera yelling, ‘Look to your left! Look to your left!’” Rumor the original crew “didn’t expect much”; *Graffiti* pronounced “unreleasable.” “If you gave George Lucas two months and a hundred pounds of film, he could deliver you a movie… Steven Spielberg… was all thumbs.” Even on *The Phantom Menace* he wanted classic-movie clips “to scrape them for ideas.” Favorite bake-off reel: *Empire*. Muren “nine Oscars… more Oscars than anyone alive.”

- **Digital actors / “long and shaggy.”** Lucas: “You can’t replace actors. We’ve created duplicates, clones, but they can’t act. They’re a computer, for God’s sake.” Kimberlin: “The original screenplay versions of *Star Wars* were so long and shaggy that they had to be made into three films.” Flag as Kimberlin; do not collapse with six/nine statements or with Kaminski. *Jedi* lunch: Arthur Penn “‘A’ director… ‘A’ salary”; later Lucas “quoted as saying, ‘I am at a point financially where no one can tell me I am too old or too un-cool to make movies.’” After *Empire*: “We all noticed that George got new tires for his Camaro.” *E.T.* surpasses *Star Wars*: “Good, now maybe they will leave me alone.” Tim handshake, George “almost leaped backwards.” “You want decisions? Bring the lights down and roll the film, I’ll give you decisions.” Jane Bay / Olivier: “I’m talking to Chris right now. Tell him I’ll call back.” “At my rate, those are the most expensive T-shirts you will ever own.” “Interactive is games. How hard is that to figure out?”

- **The Players / Pixar / *The Mask*.** Ron Howard on *Graffiti* direction: “faster,” “slower,” or “that was great.” Ron: “Just tell us what you want. We’re actors, we can do it.” “George, the supreme commander and benevolent dictator” — Kimberlin. Oracle Unix DB, 1982; “Egg Company (George naming things again).” Lucas “turned down Vilmos Zsigmond as the cinematographer of one of his *Star Wars* movies because ‘the guy wants a million bucks.’” “George named the Ewoks in *Star Wars* after these early Marin County Miwoks” — Kimberlin etymology, not a Lucas quote here. Feature-animation pitch: next day, “Don’t ever mention this idea to George again.” Sale reasons: money, plus George “had stated many times, ‘There is only one filmmaker at this company, and that is George Lucas.’” Jobs bought Pixar for $10 million ($5M cash + $5M guarantees). After Pixar’s success, Kimberlin heard George: “The only thing I regret… is having let this successful model out into the world where others could copy it.” Jobs’s Porsche plate NEXT, “contrary to what others have written, Jobs’s car did indeed have a license plate.” Spaz Williams’s dino run “convinced Spielberg to do all of *Jurassic Park* in computer-generated images.” After the Leary-dinner office incident (~1990, during *T2*): “Why are people like this working for my company?” he reportedly asked; relented as “key” employees; “Spaz and Mark Dippé survived, but they could never go back to the Ranch or work on any *Star War*s films, *ever*.” Spaz later did CG “on a scene in the restoration of *Return of the Jedi* that George had had to drop in the original version because it looked so fake.” “This is a business, not a fan club.” After *The Phantom Menace*, Lucas “sign[ed] things for his employees for hours.” “George used to say, ‘There are a lot of pencils and typewriters in the world but not a lot of novels. Why? Because it takes guts to write a book.’”

- **The Distraction (1997 Special Edition).** Building C screening of Lucas’s personal print. “From his perspective, these changes were all based on what he wanted to do in the first place but had either run out of time, technology, or money on the first go-round.” Lucas “said that he was going to do a comedy version of *Star Wars.*” Executives: “It’s a distraction… We are not in the restoration business.” ~1996 ancillary toy and video money had “dwindled”; “If George would commit to make three more *Star Wars* sequels, there was no limit to the money that could be made.” Tom Christopher finds Fox negative and Midwest salt-mine separation masters; wipe negatives were in Kimberlin’s ILM editorial warehouse. Protection masters “on the cheap” (dry, not wet). Tom asks whether to order a duplicate negative of the original version. Lucas: “This *is* the original version,” “meaning that what everyone else had described as a restoration, he considered the final version of the movie.” Kimberlin’s van Gogh / Kafka / Harper Lee gloss is Kimberlin, not Lucas. Special Edition #1 for three weeks, “grossing over $138 million”; *Empire* and *Jedi* followed, “all of them together grossing more than $250 million,” funding the prequels.

- **John Knoll / Nilo Rodis / late company.** Knoll “is the only person I ever heard George Lucas label a genius.” Nilo Rodis “flunk[ed] his original job interview with George Lucas by saying he didn’t read, watch, or like science fiction. George hired him anyway because of his talent.” Door story: “There should be a door there” / “Just because I happen to mention something, that doesn’t mean everyone should stop everything and do it!” / the door is gone. After Kimberlin left: “George hired a union-busting law firm and got the union thrown out.” Record as Kimberlin’s report. ~1999 Presidio / Letterman Digital Arts Center; “admitting that the Ranch was not where all the new young talent wanted” to work. Lucas after donating “hundreds of millions” to USC: “If you want a discipline to be taken seriously, you need a serious building.” 2002: golden C-3PO twenty-year statue; receptionist on day one: “Day one.” “Like George Lucas, I felt that now no one could tell that I was too old or uncool to make movies.”
