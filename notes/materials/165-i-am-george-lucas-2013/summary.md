# 165 summary — Grace Norwich, *I Am George Lucas* (Scholastic, 2013)

Unauthorized Scholastic “I AM” series juvenile biography, first printing August 2013. 127 numbered pages plus unnumbered front and rear matter (132 PDF page-image leaves). Secondary source. Full visual hand-read of PDF `sXtEkBLa`. The catalog-preferred epub `JS1Q1LAS` is the same dirty Calibre/IA conversion and was **not** quoted. Juvenile cbr `4KkgkRpD` was not fetched.

The Introduction (pp. 4–8) and the rear-cover blurb are first-person Norwich-as-Lucas. Do **not** treat “I’ve been called the force behind the Force.” or “I am George Lucas.” as certified Lucas interview lines. Body chapters are third-person. Attributed “he said” / “George said” / “As he later explained” quotations are Norwich’s report of Lucas, typography locked from the printed pages, not independently checked transcripts.

## Priority hits (Vader / Anakin / the Force / Sith / Chosen One / power / canon)

- **Introduction, pp. 4 and 8 — Norwich-as-Lucas, not a certified interview.** Opening: "I've been called the force behind the Force." The same introduction quotes the film's "May the Force be with you" and the crawl "a long time ago in a galaxy far, far away. . . ." and closes, p. 8: "And the Force was with me all along. I am George Lucas." Index entry: "Force, the (concept), 4, 8, 65-66, 105."
- **Ch. 6, pp. 65–66 — Force definition (authors + screenplay) and the kids'-film / morality quotation (attributed Lucas).** Crops: `quotes/q01-p65-force-definition.jpg` and `quotes/q02-p66-kids-film-morality.jpg`. Authors, p. 65: "As George wrote and rewrote, the notion of the "Force" came into sharper focus. In the screenplay, the character of Obi-Wan Kenobi defines it as "an energy field created by all living things. It surrounds us and penetrates us; it binds the galaxy together."" Semicolon, not "and binds." Authors, still p. 65 into p. 66: "The Force can be used for good or evil. This tension was central to what George was trying to create with *Star Wars*." Then, typography locked, attributed: "As he later explained, "I wanted to make a kids' film that would strengthen contemporary mythology and introduce a basic kind of morality. Nobody's saying the very basic things. They're dealing in the abstract. Everybody's forgetting to tell the kids, 'Hey, this is right and this is wrong.'"" Earlier in the chapter (pp. 61–62), authors' wording, not a first-person quote: "he was interested in creating a modern myth that dealt with the forces of good and evil. These two ideas were the beginning of a movie called *Star Wars*." Research list includes "world religions, from Buddhism to Christianity," and "the American anthropologist Joseph Campbell, who wrote extensively about mythology." 10 Things #6 later names *The Hero with a Thousand Faces*.
- **Ch. 7, p. 76 — authors, "Dark Side" in quotes, title case:** "part two of the original *Star Wars* trilogy would give the upper hand to the "Dark Side" in the epic battle between good and evil." Yoda sidebar, p. 78, is a production-history simplification (puppet "enhanced with the use of computer animation" — flag). Film Notes p. 79 mention "a startling discovery by Luke about Darth Vader's true identity" without spoiling "I am your father."
- **Ch. 9, p. 93 Film Notes and p. 95 — authors.** Film Notes: "Luke returns to destroy the Death Star, where he engages in a final battle between good and evil with Darth Vader. The force of good prevails, and the rebels go on to destroy the Death Star and defeat the evil Empire once and for all." Lowercase "force." P. 95: "*Return of the Jedi* concluded the second trilogy of the *Star Wars* epic." (Authors treating the original trilogy as the second trilogy.) Special Edition: the original *Star Wars* had been "about forty percent of what I wanted it to be," he had said (Rau 1999 had "about sixty percent" — record both as their books' wording).
- **Ch. 10, p. 100 — attributed "George said," the book's main Anakin/Vader statement.** Crop: `quotes/q03-p100-sweet-kid-dark-path.jpg`. "The first trilogy is primarily about how Anakin Skywalker, father of Luke Skywalker, became Darth Vader. "He was a sweet kid, helpful, just like most people imagine themselves to be," George said. "He eventually realizes he's going down the dark path, but he thinks it's justifiable. The idea is to see how a **democracy** becomes a **dictatorship**, and how a good person goes bad—and still, in the end, thinks he's doing the right thing."" Bold vocabulary words and em dash as printed. The next paragraph calls this "the backstory to the second trilogy that fans already knew so well (it's often referred to as the *Star Wars* "prequel trilogy")" — here "second trilogy" most naturally means the original trilogy, but p. 99 uses "the second trilogy" for the prequels being made. Flag the inconsistent first/second/prequel terminology; do not silently harmonize it.
- **Ch. 11, pp. 107–108 — attributed Lucas, January 2012 *New York Times*, as reported by Norwich.** Crops: `quotes/q04-p107-retiring-after-jedi.jpg` and `quotes/q05-p108-redeems-his-father.jpg`. "I'm retiring." Then: "I get asked all the time, 'What happens after *Return of the Jedi?*' and there really is no answer for that," he said. "The movies were the story of Anakin Skywalker and Luke Skywalker, and when Luke saves the galaxy and redeems his father, that's where that story ends." Norwich immediately adds, with italic *is*: "Despite all of George's denials, it seems there *is* still a *Star Wars* story to tell." 2012 Disney acquisition "for over $4 billion"; the book's 2013 claim is that "new *Star Wars* films will be made" and "George will have to wait a little longer to retire, as he will serve as the creative consultant for the movies."
- **No midichlorians. No "Chosen One." "Sith" appears only in the title *Revenge of the Sith*.** No canon-hierarchy discussion. The Glossary (pp. 120–121) defines only the book's bold vocabulary (including Democracy and Dictatorship). The Index (pp. 125–127) has "Force, the (concept), 4, 8, 65-66, 105"; "Darth Vader (*Star Wars* character), 67, 79, 93, 100"; "Anakin Skywalker (*Star Wars* character), 100, 101, 104"; "morality in films, 66"; "Star Wars trilogy, 1st, 97, 99, 100, 102-103"; "Star Wars trilogy, 2nd (original), 13, 68, 73, 75, 76, 83, 91, 95, 99, 100, 102, 118."

## Factual slips and claims in the book (do not repeat as fact; record as the book's text)

- Unauthorized first-person Introduction and rear cover are Norwich-as-Lucas, not a certified Lucas interview.
- p. 21: the comics-shed quotation is given to Wendy, not George (Rau 1999, p. 12, gave the same words to George).
- p. 45: the block "I was very grateful to have someone of my own generation around to discuss what I was trying to do as opposed to what I was able to do" is here given entirely to Coppola, with a second sentence, "I very quickly became aware of his superior intelligence." In Rau 1999 the first sentence was a Lucas quote.
- Time Line / introduction: *Star Wars* "to date, making $775 million" and "winning seven Academy Awards"; ch. 6 p. 73 says "ten Academy Awards nominations and earning $775 million worldwide." Record both. The $775 million figure is the book's 2013 "to date" claim.
- *American Graffiti*: Universal "only $600,000 and twenty-eight days" (p. 54; Rau said $750,000), then "cost a total of $777,000" (p. 58). Opened "August 1, 1973" (record as the book's date). Cut "about four and a half minutes" (Rau said five; Smith 2.5).
- p. 70 typo as printed: "openings scenes" on Tatooine. p. 71 typo: "technical whizzes who knew who how to operate computer-controlled cameras." p. 80 typo: "directing reigns" (should be "reins"). p. 99 typo: "millions of fan who had seen the original." p. 54: "There's lot of times" (missing "a"). p. 110: ILM "one of most successful of its kind" (missing "the").
- p. 70: "computer-generated special effects" for much of the 1977 film (anachronism/simplification — mostly optical/model work). p. 78: Yoda's scenes "enhanced with the use of computer animation" (ESB Yoda was a puppet). p. 92: Endor actors "later added in with computers" (optical compositing). Flag all three as the book's wording.
- p. 84: "In the Bible, the covenant is a lost chest that contains the stone tablets with the Ten Commandments on them." (The Ark is the chest; the covenant is the agreement.)
- *Raiders* opened "June 14, 1981" (Time Line and p. 88; common U.S. date is June 12). Record as the book's date.
- Time Line: *Attack of the Clones* "May 19, 2002"; body p. 105 says "May 16, 2002." Internal inconsistency; the common U.S. date is May 16.
- First/second/prequel terminology is inconsistent: Time Line / p. 73 treat 1977–83 as "the *Star Wars* trilogy"; p. 95 says ROTJ "concluded the second trilogy of the *Star Wars* epic"; p. 97 "start in on the first trilogy"; p. 99 "the second trilogy was pretty much a guaranteed success" (here apparently the prequels); p. 100 "backstory to the second trilogy" (here apparently the original trilogy) / "prequel trilogy." The Index lists "Star Wars trilogy, 1st" and "Star Wars trilogy, 2nd (original)."
- p. 93 Film Notes: "Episode VI of the series opens with the building of a new Death Star by Darth Vader and the Galactic Empire." (Vader "building" is a simplification.)
- Ch. 6 / Time Line: 1978 Marin / Skywalker Ranch property; ch. 6 also says *Graffiti* proceeds bought a Victorian in Marin as Lucasfilm HQ; ch. 11 says Skywalker Ranch was bought in 1978 "with proceeds from *American Graffiti*" and that Lucasfilm was "founded in 1971."
- 10 Things #10: "George has three children, all of whom are adopted." (Amanda, Katie, Jett; Mellody Hobson / Everest are not in this August 2013 first printing.) Ch. 11 also says "raising three children of his own."
- 10 MORE #4: "*Star Wars* was originally imagined as one movie, but George realized the story was too big, so he divided it into three parts." (The original trilogy only, in this book.)
- 10 MORE #10: "*Star Wars: Episode I – The Phantom Menace* has earned more than $1 billion worldwide, making it the most successful of all the *Star Wars* films." The book's 2013 "to date" claim (the same book cites the 1977 film at $775 million).
- Time Line 1987 "LucasArts Learning" vs. body p. 111, "1991 founded Lucas Learning."
- The unnamed "established Hollywood cinematographer" who encouraged USC (p. 33) is not identified; other bios often name Haskell Wexler — do not add the name.

Birthdate is correct here (May 14, 1944). USC graduation is 1966 (correct). The post-crash "go straight" is spelled correctly (unlike the 2015 Rise comic's STRAIT). "Chewbacca the Wookiee" and "R2-D2" are spelled correctly in the Film Notes.
