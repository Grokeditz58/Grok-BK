## Introduction (pp. 4–9) — first-person Norwich-as-Lucas

This is the author’s literary persona, **not** a certified Lucas interview. Do not promote these lines as Lucas’s own words.

- **p. 4, typography locked:** "I've been called the force behind the Force." "In 1977, my movie *Star Wars*, with its immortal line, "May the Force be with you," opened in a few dozen movie theaters around the country." The film is "set "a long time ago in a galaxy far, far away. . . ."" (spaced ellipsis as printed).
- p. 5: full-page drawing of a young bearded Lucas at a camera.
- p. 6: Hamburger Hamlet / May 25, 1977 / Chinese Theatre opening (same anecdote family as Rau 1999). "much needed" is unhyphenated.
- p. 7: fans "paying to see *Star Wars* hundreds of times!"; first weekend "about $1.5 million"; opened "wide" "just under $7 million in its first weekend"; "In the end, *Star Wars* would earn $775 million worldwide, making it one of" — completed p. 8 — "the highest-grossing films of all time." The $775 million figure is the book's "to date" claim; it also appears on the Time Line.
- **p. 8 close, typography locked:** "And the Force was with me all along. I am George Lucas."
- p. 9: full-page portrait of older Lucas.

## People You Will Meet (pp. 10–11)

George Lucas; George Lucas, Sr.; Wendy Lucas; Steven Spielberg; Harrison Ford; Mark Hamill ("The TV actor who played Luke Skywalker"); Carrie Fisher; Marcia Griffin (married 1969); Francis Ford Coppola ("mentor").

## Time Line (pp. 12–13)

May 14, 1944 birth (correct); June 12, 1962 crash; 1966 USC (correct); 1969 marries Marcia; 1971 *THX 1138*; 1973 *Graffiti*; May 25, 1977 *Star Wars* "winning seven Academy Awards and, to date, making $775 million"; 1978 Marin County / Skywalker Ranch property; May 21, 1980 *Empire* + Skywalker Sound; June 14, 1981 *Raiders* (possible slip; U.S. release is commonly June 12, 1981 — record as the book's date; repeated p. 88); May 25, 1983 *Return of the Jedi* "completing the *Star Wars* trilogy"; 1987 LucasArts Learning; May 19, 1999 *The Phantom Menace*; May 19, 2002 *Attack of the Clones* (body p. 105 later says May 16, 2002 — internal inconsistency; actual U.S. release was May 16); May 19, 2005 *Revenge of the Sith*; 2012 Disney acquires Lucasfilm Ltd. for $4 billion.

## Ch. 1: Modesto Beginnings (pp. 14–21)

Third person from here on. Quiet Modesto versus Los Angeles; Depression; George Walton Lucas, Sr. "was not the type of man who let outside forces get in his way" (lowercase "forces," not the Force). Stationery-store partnership; married Dorothy Bomberger 1933 (he twenty, she eighteen). First child Ann, then Katherine two years later, then George May 14, 1944 (five-pound, fourteen-ounce; "father's dark eyes and pointy ears"); housekeeper Till; Wendy born three years after George; pals John Plummer and George Frankenstein; backyard zoo/rides.

- **Lucas, p. 20, typography locked, "he once said in an interview":** "I liked all the normal kinds of adventure books, *Kidnapped*, *Treasure Island*, *Huck Finn* sorts of things," he once said in an interview. "I loved *Swiss Family Robinson* and that whole period of South Sea adventure movies. I liked Westerns." Television in 1954, revolving stand; *Gunsmoke*, *Maverick*, *Flash Gordon Conquers the Universe*.
- Sidebar p. 21, "Now That's a Comic Book Collection!": Batman, Superman, Scrooge McDuck; George and Wendy combined allowances, "more than five hundred in all!"
- **Attribution slip vs. Rau 1999, p. 21:** the shed quotation is given to Wendy, not George: "We had so many that eventually my dad built a big shed in the back, and there was one room strictly devoted to comics, floor to ceiling," Wendy recalled.
- Close: teenage search for "real action and excitement—with near tragic results."

## Ch. 2: A Need for Speed (pp. 22–29)

p. 22: full-page illustration of a boy watching television (Flash Gordon-like figure). C student at Thomas Downey High School; okay in art and music.

- **pp. 23–24, typography locked:** "One of the big problems I had . . . was that I always wanted to learn something other than what I was being taught," he said. Father expected good grades; Elvis, Chuck Berry, Hershey bars, Coca-Colas. Awkward line p. 24: "Rock music and junk food may not help earn straight As in high school, but as bad habits go, they're not exactly life threatening." "I've always been very into building things," he said, recalling chess sets carved from wood.
- **p. 25:** "My teenage years . . . were completely devoted to cars," he explained. "That was the most important thing in my life from about the ages of fourteen to twenty." Cruising; Fiat Bianchina from his father; "it had a sewing machine motor," so he souped it up to seventy mph; earlier roof-bashing accident, cut off the roof, installed a roll bar; "greaser" look, Vaseline in long hair; raced around the state as a "daring speed demon."
- June 12, 1962: three term papers plus finals due; Wendy almost came to the library but stayed home; a Chevy Impala plowed into the Fiat "a short distance from the thirteen-acre walnut farm," "flip five times before slamming into a walnut tree."
- **p. 29, typography locked; "straight" spelled correctly** (unlike the 2015 Rise comic's STRAIT): "I realized that I'd been living my life so close to the edge for so long," he said. "That's when I decided to go straight, to be a better student, to try to do something with myself." Heartbeat was quiet; coughing up blood; no recollection of the accident when he woke.

## Ch. 3: Time to Buckle Down (pp. 30–39)

p. 30: full-page classroom illustration. Near-death "didn't change the mediocre grades"; Modesto Junior College instead of a four-year school. "With the same devotion he'd once saved for cars and racing, George now applied to his classwork at Modesto." Vocabulary **studious**. Graduated MJC June 9, 1964, "nearly two years to the day from his horrific car crash," associate arts degree. Photography, 35-mm camera, bedroom darkroom, a friend's 8-millimeter movie camera. John Plummer suggested USC; at the same time George "was helping build a race car for an established Hollywood cinematographer, who encouraged him to consider USC's film school" (unnamed here; other bios often name Haskell Wexler — do not add the name). "The decision didn't sit well with George Sr." Film school "in the 1960s… was a new field of academic study."

- **p. 34, typography locked, here a direct quote** (Rau 1999 had this as authors' wording): George said to his father, "I'll never be back, and as a matter of fact, I'll be a millionaire before I'm thirty."
- **USC:** "a time when I really blossomed. When I went in there, I didn't know anything. The school helped me focus on film, and I loved doing that. They helped me become what I am today." "I made lots of movies while in school while everybody else was running around saying, 'Oh, I wish I could make a movie. I wish they'd give me some film.'"
- **Spielberg:** "George Lucas was always the star of the student festivals then," (Rau had "student film festivals").
- Sidebar pp. 36–37, "The Film School Generation": Coppola (UCLA; *The Godfather*, *Apocalypse Now*, *The Outsiders*), De Palma (Columbia; *Carrie*, *Scarface*, *The Untouchables*), Scorsese (NYU; *Taxi Driver*, *Goodfellas*, *Gangs of New York*), Spielberg (Cal State Long Beach; *Jaws*, *Jurassic Park*, *Saving Private Ryan*).
- Close p. 39: would George shine outside student festivals?

## Ch. 4: Life in the Real World (pp. 40–49)

p. 40: full-page graduation-caps illustration (unnumbered chapter art). Graduated USC 1966, BA in cinema. Not crazy about Hollywood; "too many limits on creativity." Assistant on U.S. government educational and propaganda films for the military. Nearly drafted; a physical revealed diabetes; kept him out of Vietnam. Met Marcia Griffin, assistant editor, also born in Modesto; dates; official couple. Scholarship to direct a short about the making of a Hollywood-produced Western (*McKenna's Gold* is not named).

- **p. 44, typography locked:** "I didn't really have much feeling about [Hollywood]," he said. "I thought I'd see for myself and know why I disliked it, rather than just saying it out of hand."
- Warner Bros. scholarship 1967 (Rau said 1968); assignment was to learn from Coppola directing *Finian's Rainbow*.
- **Attribution slip, p. 45:** "I was very grateful to have someone of my own generation around to discuss what I was trying to do as opposed to what I was able to do," Francis later recalled. "I very quickly became aware of his superior intelligence." In Rau 1999 the first sentence was a Lucas quote; here Norwich gives the whole block to Coppola and adds the second sentence. "like two halves of a whole," as George once put it — correctly George's.
- American Zoetrope 1969, same year George and Marcia "tie the knot"; "zoetrope means 'movement of life,'" (etymological simplification). *THX 1138* from the USC student short; Warner accepted five of seven pitched projects including *THX*. Paid only $15,000 to write a script; ten-week shoot.
- **p. 47, typography locked:** "I realized that I might never have the chance again to make this totally off-the-wall movie, without any real supervision," he said. (Rau: "might never get the chance again.") Studio thought it too strange; demanded money back; threatened to terminate every Zoetrope deal; Warner gave in but cut about four minutes; mixed reviews 1971; Zoetrope setback forced George and Francis to go separate ways.
- Film Notes p. 48, *THX 1138* (1971): Co-writer and Director; Duvall as THX, Pleasence as SEN, McOmie as LUH.

## Ch. 5: Creating an American Classic (pp. 50–59)

p. 50: chapter-art theater / *THX 1138* poster. Soul-searching after the *THX* failure; first feature still one of his favorites.

- **p. 51, typography locked:** worried his reputation was now that of "a cold, weird director, a science-fiction sort of guy who carried a calculator. And I'm not like that at all. So, I thought, maybe I'll do something exactly the opposite." *American Graffiti* as the opposite; "It all happened to me, but I sort of glamorized it," he said in an interview. "It had music all the way through it," he explained. "Not just the score but actual songs from the period, and that is something that nobody had done before."
- **p. 54, typography locked, grammar as printed:** "My first six years in the business were hopeless," he recalls. "There's lot of times when you sit and you say, 'Why am I doing this? I'll never make it. It's just not going to happen. I should really go out and get a real job, and try to survive.'" (Missing "a" in "There's lot of times" — record as printed.)
- Universal: only $600,000 and twenty-eight days (Rau said $750,000); songs cut from eighty to about forty-five; fresh faces: Ron Howard, Richard Dreyfuss, Harrison Ford. Town backed out; night shooting; early-to-bed George struggled. Preview mixed; Coppola offered to buy it if Universal refused; Universal came around but cut "about four and a half minutes" (Rau said five; Smith 2.5).
- **p. 58, typography locked:** "I was really angry, and I remain angry to this day," he said. That's when he vowed never to give up control of a movie again. Opened August 1, 1973 (record as the book's date); cost a total of $777,000 (inconsistent with the earlier $600,000 figure — flag both) and would eventually earn the studio $115 million; five Oscar nominations including Best Director and Best Original Screenplay. Profit share more than $7 million; twenty-nine-year-old "had more than lived up to his word" about being a millionaire before thirty.
- Film Notes p. 57: Curt/Dreyfuss, Steve/Howard, John/Le Mat, Terry/Charles Martin Smith, Bob Falfa/Ford.
