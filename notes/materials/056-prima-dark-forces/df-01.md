# *Dark Forces Official Player's Guide* (1995): part 1, front matter, Strategy Briefing and the 14-mission shoot-through

**Source:** *Dark Forces Official Player's Guide*, written by **Jeff Hoff**. Infotainment World, Inc., San Mateo CA, 1995. ISBN 1-57280-022-4; cover price $18.95 USA / $26.95 Canada. Series Editor Peter Spear; Senior Editor Willem Knibbe; design by Van Winkle Design Group; Publisher Ron Resnick.
**Copy read:** MEGA `QC1G0ByK` (`Kileko's Files/Informational Books/Prima Guides/Star_Wars_Dark_Forces_Official_Guide.pdf`). It is a 259-page image-only scan made in 2011 with Acrobat 10.1.1. It has no text layer, so I rendered every page at 150 dpi, ran OCR on all of them, read the OCR text from start to finish, and checked layout pages, art and maps against the page images. The first OCR pass skipped some bold text on gray Jedi Tip boxes, so I ran a second, thresholded OCR pass over all 259 pages and read every line it recovered (see the last section). The source file is not committed.
**Not a Prima book:** the MEGA folder is called "Prima Guides", but this guide was published by **Infotainment World** (its imprint page says "INFOTAINMENT WORLD™ is a trademark of international Data Group"). The guide also says: "The computer games and characters covered in this book ... are property of their respective companies, Infotainment World has no affiliation with these companies and the information in this book is not officially sanctioned by those companies." (PDF p. 4)
**Page numbering:** printed page = PDF page − 6 up to the color insert. The 16-page color map insert ("Mission Maps", PDF pp. 51–66) is unnumbered. After the insert, printed page = PDF page − 22. For example, Chapter 3 begins on p. 201, which is PDF p. 223. Citations below give the PDF page.

## Front matter (PDF pp. 1–6)
- **Covers:** the cover is the game's box art, a stormtrooper firing over a blaster muzzle, captioned "OFFICIAL PLAYER'S GUIDE … BY JEFF HOFF". PDF p. 3 repeats it as a half-title.
- **Dedication (PDF p. 4):** "To Jill, Jenna, and Julia, the three J's who bring such easy joy and smiles into my life."
- **Acknowledgments (PDF p. 4):**
  - "Dan Connors exceeded expectations on every request for information and assistance."
  - "Brett Tosti permitted access to his awesome team of testers (particularly Reed Derleth) when he shouldn't have."
  - "Matt Tateishi and Chris Ross prepared the level maps so someone could actually read them."
- **Legal (PDF p. 4):** "Dark Forces game © 1994 Lucasfilm Ltd. and LucasArts Entertainment Company." The page also gives the iMUSE U.S. patent number.
- **Contents (PDF p. 5):** 1 Strategy Briefing (p. 1); 2 The Missions (p. 13, covering the 14 missions from Secret Base on p. 15 to The Arc Hammer on p. 181); 3 The Making of Dark Forces (p. 201); 4 The Dark Forces Script and Conceptual Art (p. 213).
- **"How to Use This Book" (PDF p. 6):**
  - The walkthroughs are called a "shoot-through" for all 14 missions, and the text "assumes you're playing the game at its hardest difficulty level".
  - It describes the script chapter as "the mission-by-mission story of Dark Forces in screenplay format as written by Justin Chin, lead artist and story writer".
  - It closes: "It’s been said too many times, so I won't wish that the Force be with you. Just keep your powder dry and crouch a lot. And may the gods be with you when you meet Boba Fett. Bon chance."
- **Chapter openers:** Chapter 1 (PDF p. 7) has a pencil concept sketch of Kyle Katarn. Chapter 2 (PDF p. 19) has a pencil sketch of Jan Ors in a flight jacket.

## Chapter 1, Strategy Briefing (PDF pp. 8–18)
- **Cheat codes (PDF pp. 8–9):** every code starts with LA ("for LucasArts").
  - LAIMLAME: "Type in this puppy and you are invulnerable. You are also a lame excuse for a Rebel warrior, hence the code name."
  - POSTAL: "You're mailing it in if you use this code a lot."
  - The pondering toggle (its code is lost in the OCR): "What the heck is pondering? I don't really know, but if you type in this code, it freezes all defenders in their tracks".
  - There is also a warp code for each of the 14 missions: SECBASE, TALAY, SEWERS, TESTBASE, GROMAS, DTENTION, RAMSHED, ROBOTICS, NARSHADA, JABSHIP, IMPCITY, FUELSTAT, EXECUTOR, ARC.
- **Weapon table (PDF p. 11):** it gives each weapon's official name, its nickname and the mission where it first appears:
  - Modified Bryar Pistol (Blaster Pistol) and Stormtrooper Laser Rifle (Blaster Rifle): Mission I.
  - Thermal Detonators: Mission III.
  - Imperial Repeater Gun ("Auto Gun"): Mission IV.
  - Jeron Fusion Cutter: Mission V.
  - I.M. Mine ("Claymores") and Packered Mortar Gun: Mission VI.
  - Stouker Concussion Rifle: Mission IX.
  - Assault Cannon, with the nicknames "Dark Trooper Weapon" and "Plasma Cannon": Mission XI.
- **Balancing history (PDF p. 12):** "In earlier versions of the game, the auto gun and fusion cutter were significantly more powerful than they are now and were the clear-cut favorites among the designers and testers. But these weapons were tamed in the final version."
- **Weapon notes:**
  - Blaster rifle (PDF p. 12): "When the auto gun and fusion cutter were emasculated in the final version, the blaster rifle instantly became more glamorous and desirable."
  - Concussion rifle (PDF p. 13): "Bossks carry these weapons".
  - Mortar gun (PDF p. 13): "It's mostly “smoke,” as one tester puts it."
- **Enemy ladder, toughest to weakest (PDF pp. 14–17):**
  - Boba Fett: "This bounty hunter is Dark" … "Forces’ most formidable" … "foe." The OCR interleaves two columns here; the sentence is whole on the page image.
  - Phase Three dark trooper: "This is your final Dark Forces confrontation, and it's a tough one."
  - Phase Two dark trooper: "doesn't reflect your weapon rounds. But that is little consolation" given "its increased speed, ability to fly, protective armor, and sheer mass. And let's not forget its assault cannon, which is a vast improvement on the Phase One sword."
  - Phase One dark trooper: "bullets reflect off their shield and kill you before they kill them."
  - Ree-Yees: "perhaps your deadliest enemy".
  - Bossks: "These guys wield a concussion rifle like Jimi Hendrix wielded a guitar." "One tester puts it baldly: “They're a pain in the ass.”"
  - After them come Kell dragons, ceiling turrets, Gamorreans, stormtroopers and officers, dianogas, droids and mousebots.
- **The species naming:** the guide uses "Bossk" and "Ree-Yees" as the names of whole enemy *types* ("a dead one gives up 100 power cells"), not of the single named characters. This matches the game's own enemy labels.
- **Code keys (PDF p. 18):** the guide includes screenshots of the large code letters because "often when you're working the code lock, the larger images of the code letters don't appear to be the same."

## Chapter 2, the mission shoot-through (PDF pp. 20–222)
Each mission opens with a briefing box (location, objectives, weapons) and a short first-person monologue by Kyle. The step-by-step captions follow, then black-and-white level maps. The 16-page color "Mission Maps" insert (PDF pp. 51–66) says the blueprints were "created by Matt Tateishi, Ingar Shu, Jim Current, and Brett Tosti as they were designing the levels".

**Kyle's mission monologues (in-universe flavor text written for the guide):**
- **I, Secret Base, Danuta (PDF p. 21):** "Somewhere inside is the only thing that can save the Rebel Alliance from total annihilation." "Kyle be nimble, Kyle be quick..."
- **II, Talay: TAK Base (PDF p. 31):** "Is there anything in the cosmos more beautiful than the purple night sky of Talay? There won't be any more stargazing for the massacred inhabitants of this TAK Base." "The Empire's new weapon is clearly far beyond the experimental stage."
- **III, Anoat City (PDF p. 43):** "What kind of scum builds doomsday weapons for the Empire? I remember hearing about Moff Rebus during my training days. Everyone said he was quirky, and some said he was a genius."
- **IV, Research Facility, Fest (PDF p. 71):** "Fest could be a wild goose chase — but do I have any choice?"
- **V, Gromas Mines (PDF p. 85):** "The most we can give the beast is a sharp pain in the backside..."
- **VI, Detention Center (PDF p. 99):** "We owe this to Crix. Without his work, we wouldn't have known about the dark trooper project until it was too late to make any difference."
- **VII, Ramsees Hed, Cal-Seti (PDF p. 115):** "The Empire loves to station them and Bossks on these isolated outposts." The objective box reads "…OF A SMUGGLER SHIP SO IT CAN BE FOLLOWED TO THE DARK TROOPER ROBOTICS FACTORY."
- **VIII, Robotics Facility, Anteevy (PDF p. 127):** "If I destroy this place, General Mohc is going to be plenty testy."
- **IX, Nar Shaddaa (PDF p. 139):** "Everyone's an alien in this place, and everyone's got something to hide. Me, I've got something to find."
- **X, Jabba's Ship (PDF p. 151):** "Jabba the Hutt, the Fat One, the Slimy One, returns. This is not good. He's taken my gear, all my weapons, the nava card — and Jan."
- **XI, Imperial City (ISO building, Coruscant, PDF p. 165):** "Infiltrating the Imperial City? I should have my head examined."
- **XII, Fuel Station Ergo (PDF p. 183):** "Whoa, Kyle. One mission at a time, one mission at a time."
- **XIII, The Executor (PDF p. 193):** "A cloaking device would be nice for this jaunt."
- **XIV, The Arc Hammer (PDF p. 203):** "It’s just me against a waiting army that knows exactly where I am and what I want to do. Is this fun or what?"

**Notable story beats and trivia in the captions:**
- **Mission I (PDF p. 27):** "When you take them, a Kyle voice-over confirms your success. Time to make your escape — the princess is waiting for your booty."
- **Mission II (PDF p. 40):** the prize is a dark trooper weapon prototype: "Your courage and stealth has landed you a prototype dark trooper weapon. And your reward? A trip to the subterranean city of Anoat. Sewers anyone?"
- **Mission III, Moff Rebus (PDF p. 67):** "Beyond them you find your basic chunky dark trooper weapon designer. He looks like some grizzled gun runner but doesn't put up a fight. (Go ahead and shoot him to get it out of your system — nothing happens.)"
- **Mission IV (PDF pp. 80–82):** "That's the metal phrik, the object of your desire." "Evidence points to stark moons in the Gromas system."
- **Mission V, the first dark trooper (PDF p. 93):** "Holy phrik! This burnt-looking thing rising out of some basement is a dark trooper. With sword in hand and destruction hard-wired into every rivet, he’s coming after you."
- **Mission VI (PDF pp. 109–110):** the compactor puzzle had to be made easier: "The timing is tricky but not nearly as tricky as in early versions of the game. The designers have smoothed the challenge from impossible to just plain tough." Madine is freed: "Crix is waiting for you in the first cell. Go give him a hug and your mission is complete."
- **Mission VII (PDF p. 121):** "On the right is the lone attempt in Dark Forces at bathroom humor. Open the white latrine door and you find a cross section of Imperial lackeys taking care of kidney business."
- **Mission VIII (PDF p. 135):** "You have just put a major thumbtack in General Mohc's dark trooper balloon."
- **Mission XI (PDF p. 168):** "This large yellow door adorned with the scowling mug of the Emperor himself conceals an extra life and a revive." At the end of the mission (PDF p. 176), "there’s only that leatherneck mercenary Boba Fett looking smug and ready to wipe you off the face of the galaxy." Afterwards "Jan decides to return. You have every right to be upset by her absence, and she gives you a lame excuse."
- **Mission XIII (PDF p. 200):** "“Here we go,” Kyle says. A man of few words, our Kyle."
- **Mission XIV (PDF p. 204):** in a Jedi Tip about a hard jump, the author admits: "Game testers say they make it about 30 percent of the time" … "(I'm at about 10 percent)". The OCR misreads "(I'm" as "(Vm"; I checked the page image.
- **Mohc (PDF pp. 217–218):** "This is the evil General Mohc in his best Phase Three dark trooper costume." "Congratulations! General Mohc is now a pile of heavy metal. You've saved the Rebel Alliance — for the moment."
- **Designer note (PDF p. 218):** the last officer outside the escape shuttle "was put here not so much as a threat but as ‘a reference point to show you how big the ship is."

**Canon and lore points to note:**
- The guide calls the Executor a Super Star Destroyer and uses it as the stepping-stone to the Arc Hammer: "When you're snooping around Imperial Super Star Destroyers, it helps to be invisible." (PDF p. 193)
- The underworld link: in the script (part 2) Madine reports that Mohc "has solicited the help of an underworld kingpin, unknown to us at this time" (PDF p. 248). Later Jabba calls Kyle "the one that has been a thorn in Mohc's side" (PDF p. 251). The guide never names Jabba as the kingpin outright.
- The dark trooper progression runs through three phases: Phase One (sword, reflective shield), Phase Two (flight, assault cannon) and Phase Three (Mohc's own suit).

## Jedi Tips recovered by the second OCR pass
The first pass missed some gray sidebar boxes. These are the most notable ones the second pass recovered. I read each one on the page image, and the quotes follow the printed page rather than the OCR, which garbles a few letters in the bold sidebar type.
- **Boba Fett (PDF p. 177):** "One could write a book about the different ways to defeat the mighty Boba Fett (the most difficult opponent to kill in the game)". The guide's advice is to "lure him into the areas" beyond the ship and "play hide and seek. [Although he is better at it than you, tending to find you before you find him.)" Its best trick is the secret room above the pad: "shoot at Boba Fett through the window with your plasma cannon. Use the rockets (secondary mode) to freeze him, then switch to plasma shells".
- **Bossks (PDF p. 141):** "Bossks brandish concussion rifles that spit a deadly, fiery-blue explosion." It "takes a Bossk a second to aim and fire the" rifle, "a maneuver that looks like a hairy rock-and-roll star getting ready to play his guitar".
- **Mission XIV (PDF p. 204):** "There are quite a few stormtroopers on the other side of the door and just one of you on this side." The fix is to open the door and, while backing up, "fire a mortar shell or throw a thermal detonator into the room."
- Most of the other recovered lines are tactical repeats, such as rifle-versus-pistol trade-offs (PDF p. 22), Kell dragon punching (PDF p. 152) and turret tactics on the Arc Hammer (PDF p. 209). They add no story content.
