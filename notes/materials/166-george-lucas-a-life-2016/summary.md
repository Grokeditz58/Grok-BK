# 166 summary — Brian Jay Jones, *George Lucas: A Life* (Little, Brown, 2016)


## What this book is

A 2016 unauthorized adult biography from birth (1944, Modesto) through mid-2016: USC, *THX 1138*, *American Graffiti*, the original trilogy, the ranch and companies, *Willow* / Indy / *Young Indy*, the Special Editions and prequels, *Clone Wars*, *Red Tails*, the 2012 Disney sale, and *The Force Awakens*. Jones is a synthesizer of published sources (Pollock, Kline, Rinzler’s Making-of volumes, Hearn, Silberman, Clarke, Charlie Rose, Kurtz interviews, and many others) plus a short list of on-the-record interviews. The 1,517 endnotes were all read; priority-line sources are tabulated in `jones-13-back-matter.md`.

## Priority Lucas lines (Force / Vader / saga ending)

- **Pp. 19–20.** Lucas on sudden questions — “Wait a second—What is the world? What are we? What am I? How do I function in this, and what’s going on here?” Jones, not Lucas, then writes that they were questions he would, “with the creation of the Force in *Star Wars,* attempt to answer in his films.” Religion later “a melding of Methodist and Buddhist.” (“It’s Marin County… We’re *all* Buddhists up here.”) Notes: ch. 1 n.49, 51 Pollock; n.50, 52 Arnold; n.53 Cagle, *Time,* April 29, 2002.

- **P. 74.** Roman Kroitor in Lipsett’s *21-87*: humans “become aware of some kind of force… behind this apparent mask which we see in front of us, and they call it God.” Lowercase “force” in Kroitor’s quotation. Lucas later calls his own Force “an echo of that phrase in *21-87*.” Notes: ch. 3 n.15, 17 Silberman, “Life after Darth,” *Wired,* May 2005.

- **Pp. 113–114.** “this issue of leaving a safe environment and going into the unknown” runs from *THX* through *American Graffiti* into *Star Wars.* Note: ch. 4 n.108, *THX 1138* commentary.

- **May 1974 draft, pp. 179–181.** Vader a minor “tall, grim-looking general.” First use of “May the Force of Others be with you.” Force of Others divided into Ashla and Bogan; Kiber Crystal as a MacGuffin. Later the crystal is dropped and the Force is made “more ethereal,” rather “than to have it solidified in a thing like a crystal.” Notes: ch. 6 n.44 *MOSW* 19–22; n.150 *COGL* 98.

- **1975, still separate characters:** “Vader kills Luke’s father, then Ben and Vader have a confrontation, just like they have in *Star Wars,* and Ben almost kills Vader.” Jones: Lucas later asserted this had been his plan all along, but in 1975 “he still clearly intended for Vader and Luke’s father to be separate characters.” Dutch *vader* / high-school classmate Gary Vader is Jones’s coincidence comment, not a planned-reveal claim. Note: ch. 6 n.152 *MOSW* 111.

- **P. 268.** In Lucas’s own *Empire* draft, as Luke battled Vader, he inserted *“I am your father”* and pulled that page from copies. No superscript on the paragraph. Published typo: “And here of his handwritten script.”

- **1983, p. ~327.** First trilogy “melodramatic, showing the politics that put the Empire in place”; final three “moral choices, and the wisdom needed to distinguish right from wrong.” Note: ch. 10 n.25 Clarke, *Time,* May 23, 1983. Spring 1988, to Champlin: notes enough not for just three trilogies but for *four.* No superscript on the Champlin sentence.

- **Special Edition / HAN SHOT FIRST.** Jones cites the May 15, 1976 shooting script as proving Greedo never fired (Mayhew posted the pages in 2015). Lucas’s later rationale is John Wayne / “you don’t shoot people [first].” Notes: ch. 11 n.27–31.

- **Midi-chlorians, pp. 403–404.** Jones’s gloss: “drained the Force of its mystery and awe.” (Kurtz’s response is under staff, below.)

- **P. 438 (n.11).** “The movies were the story of Anakin Skywalker and Luke Skywalker, and when Luke saves the galaxy and redeems his father, that’s where that story ends.” Jones: Geoff Boucher, *Los Angeles Times,* May 7, 2008. **Same wording in notes 165 was attributed by Norwich to a January 2012 *New York Times* interview. Do not collapse without checking the originals.** Later, to make the company “attractive,” he “admitted now he might have a few ideas for Episodes VII, VIII, and IX in a drawer.”

- **2011 Blu-ray.** Vader given an anguished “Noooooooo!” as he throws the Emperor. Note: ch. 13 n.53 (fan-reaction source).

- **“*Star Wars* is finished.”** Ch. 12 n.60 Silberman, “Life after Darth.”

- **Disney sale / *The Force Awakens*.** $4.05 billion, October 30, 2012. Compromise: Disney must use his treatments; he will not hand them over until the deal is done. Disney then “had disregarded the story treatments.” Charlie Rose, Christmas Day 2015: “They decided they didn’t want to use those stories, they decided they were gonna go do their own thing.” Watching *TFA* is “like attending the wedding of your grown child after you’ve been divorced.” “They wanted to do a retro movie. I don’t like that.” “I will go my way, and I’ll let them go their way.” Jones: “George Lucas had given up control. But that didn’t mean he was ever going to be happy about it.” Close of the book, same Rose interview: “I *tried,*” he said, laughing.

## Priority staff / crew / cast lines

- **Gary Kurtz** (on-the-record for Jones, and in earlier interviews Jones cites). On the Force in the mid-1970s: “The concept of the Force was an important one in the story, and the difficulty is trying to create a religious spiritual concept that works in a very simple way without heavy exposition.” (ch. 6 n.74, *Empire of Dreams*). On the later nine-part-all-along claim: “That’s not true.… there wasn’t enough material to do other movies.” (ch. 9 n.38). Bittersweet *Jedi* ending: Han dies mid-film, Leia becomes queen, “Luke riding off into the sunset, metaphorically, on his own.” Lucas “wouldn’t even consider it.” Ford, sardonically: “George didn’t think there was any future in dead Han toys.” Kurtz: “They make three times as much on toys as they do on films. It’s natural to make decisions that protect the toy business, but that’s not the best thing for making quality films.” (ch. 9 n.76–80). Special Edition: “probably a wrong philosophy.… It’s not the way it was in the first place. The way it was in the first place was the way we released it.” Midi-chlorians: “the destruction of the spiritual center of the force, turning [it] into DNA and blood.” Also: “George has a very clear idea of what he wants. And whether you agree with that or not, he goes about getting that.” (ch. 11 n.72, Plume / IGN, November 11, 2002).

- **Marcia Lucas** (photo-insert caption, typography locked): “If the audience doesn’t cheer when Han Solo comes in at the last second in the *Millennium Falcon* to help Luke when he’s being chased by Vader, the picture doesn’t work.” She won the editing Oscar; “Lucas, to this day, has yet to win an Oscar.”

- **Dave Prowse.** Cast dubs him “Darth Farmer” for his West Country accent. On the *Empire* gantry he delivered “Obi-Wan killed your father” and learned the real line in the theater.

- **James Earl Jones.** Asks to stay out of the *Star Wars* credits, “insisting that he was ‘just special effects.’” Records “No. *I* am your father.” “As Jones recorded Vader’s dialogue in the winter of 1980, he remained convinced that Vader was lying.” (Jones here is James Earl Jones, not the biographer.) Ben Burtt supplied the signature breathing by “recording himself huffing into a scuba mouthpiece.”

- **Brian De Palma,** Parkhouse friends screening: “What’s all the Force shit? Where’s the blood when they shoot people?” Then helps rewrite the crawl.

- **Lawrence Kasdan,** *Jedi* story conference. Lucas teases “Now I am Vader.… Now I will go and kill the fleet and I will rule the universe.” Kasdan: “That’s what I think should happen.” Lucas: “No, no, no.… Come on, this is for kids.”

- **Howard Kazanjian** on *Jedi*: “You can look at *Jedi* and see the Vietnam War there. You can see the Ewok guerrillas hiding in the jungles, taking on this improper force of mechanized bullies—and winning.” Vader’s funeral pyre was a pickup after Duwayne Dunham and Kazanjian flagged an unclear fate. (ch. 9 n.129 *MOROTJ* 259; n.142 Champlin.)

- **Ralph McQuarrie.** Jones, not a direct McQuarrie quotation, calls the Vader helmet a “happy accident”: a spacesuit for boarding a spacecraft that Lucas “loved… so much it became the villain’s regular uniform.” No superscript on that sentence.

- **Kathleen Kennedy / Bob Iger / Harrison Ford.** Treatments “had a lot of potential” (Iger, public). Kennedy on *TFA*: “he really liked it,” which Jones calls tepid; “he can’t do that unless he’s running everything.” Ford returns “largely on promises of a meaningful story arc that would close the book on Han Solo once and for all.”
