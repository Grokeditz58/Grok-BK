# Jones, *George Lucas: A Life* (2016): notes — ch. 11 “A Digital Universe,” 1994–1999 (pp. 382–407)

Typography locked from the publisher XHTML. Citations: Jones p. N; Jones Notes, ch. 11 n. Y.

## Starting Episode I; family CEO; “digital back lot” (pp. 382–390)

**Tuesday, Nov. 1, 1994:** after breakfast and school drop-off (Amanda 13, nursing a cold; Katie 6), he returns to Parkhouse and sits down to write the next *Star Wars* script. 2015: he wants his epitaph to read “I was a great dad.” On-camera before a word is written: “I’m all set. Now all I need is an idea.” Fifty, jeans and plaid, beard mostly white, pompadour. Still analog: longhand on lined notebook paper, as in 1972. Plan: write all three at once — “I’ll take a year to write them, a year to prepare them, and a year to shoot them. And I’ll shoot them all at once.” Not planning to direct; will “decide after [he got] the films prepared.” Directing keeps him away from the children. “I didn’t want to go back and write one of these movies unless I had the technology available to really tell the kind of story I was interested in telling.… So I waited until I had the technological means to do that.” (n.2)

Coppola’s “single mother” label worn with pride. *Working Mother* Family Champion 1994; ranch child care, domestic-partner insurance. “I guess I qualify as a working mother.” “I rule at the will of the people who work for me.” Norby resigned 1992; Gordon Radley president/COO nearly a decade by staying out of the way. No IPO; sole stockholder. “This little country… about 2,000 citizens.” Ranch amenities. Land now nearly 5,000 acres, still bought in the accountant’s name; Big Rock offices approved, ILM still stuck in San Rafael. ILM’s *Forrest Gump* (Hanks into Kennedy footage, digital feather). Spielberg founds DreamWorks; Lucas: “We have an agreement not to overtly hurt each other.” Still the misunderstood little guy: company is “a little mud hut outside the castle.” “Acquire the means of production.” Carrie Fisher: “Skywalker [Ranch] is where George gets to make up the rules.”

McCallum scouting, billed to **JAK Productions** (Jett, Amanda, Katie). Doug Chiang as art director (McQuarrie’s old role). Designs start as he writes. Vague briefs: “This character is cowardly and insecure.” Jar Jar Binks, “a character Lucas had high hopes for,” nearly eighteen months of design.

**First rough draft Jan. 13, 1995, titled *The Beginning.*** Never entirely finished; he will revise during filming and post, as on *Young Indy.* Animatics by David Dozoretz and Ben Burtt to pre-visualize the whole film — “the first time I was able to use computerized animatics.” First sequence assembled: **the podrace, “the high-tech drag race Lucas envisioned as young Anakin Skywalker’s ticket to freedom.”** Jones: fast cars as escape, from the car in *THX* to Milner’s deuce coupe to the *Falcon.* “It was little surprise that Lucas had included it in the script from the very beginning.”

Aug. 1995 THX/digitally remastered trilogy on video: 28 million cassettes, >$100 million profit. Hasbro/Kenner “The Power of the Force”; more adults than children at FAO Schwarz. “*Star Wars* Summit” at the ranch for licensees: quality control, especially publishing — “some characters—such as Luke Skywalker, who’d been given a love interest in a fiery smuggler named Mara Jade—were living lives far beyond the ones he had written for them.” April 1995 second summit: hints he may direct the first prequel. Predicts he “would never go above the $50 million range.” “That wouldn’t even be close. When all was said and done, Episode I would cost nearly $115 million. And almost all of it would come out of Lucas’s own pocket.”

Leavesden Aerodrome (ex-Rolls-Royce, *GoldenEye*) reserved 2.5 years, not sentimental Elstree (now a shell). “Digital back lot”: partial practical sets + blue/greenscreen. By Dec., ~60 sets, 800,000 sq ft.

## Special Editions and “Han shot first” (pp. 390–394) — KEY typography locked

One more digital test “before unleashing its full power on Episode I”: a movie “so terrible… ‘Oh God, that’s so awful, I can’t watch this.’” *Star Wars.* “The original *Star Wars* was a joke, technically.” “There were things I had to compromise on that weren’t the way I really wanted them to be.”

Plotted 1993 with McCallum, Muren, ILM. Still unhappy with the cantina (blames tightfisted Fox). Hopes to restore Han–Jabba at Docking Bay 94 with a digital Jabba. Muren suggests cleaning dogfights and explosions; Lucas then proposes new digital footage in *Empire* and *Jedi* too, in time for the 1997 twentieth anniversary. 1994: negatives deteriorated, *Star Wars* “grubby with dirt, deeply scratched, its colors faded.” ILM spends more than a year restoring, sometimes cutting new effects into the original negative. ~$5 million for the restoration and “a little more than four and a half minutes of new or reworked material.”

*Star Wars: The Special Edition* Jan. 1997 (*Empire* Feb., *Jedi* March); $35 million in four days. Fans furious at digital clutter (extra stormtroopers, droids, dewbacks, Jawas in Mos Eisley). Jabba scene “added a bit more backstory… but ultimately left the two characters unchanged.”

**Greedo / “HAN SHOT FIRST,” pp. 392–394 (n.27–32).** “At Lucas’s request, ILM had altered the cantina confrontation between Han Solo and the bounty hunter Greedo—which had ended with Han gunning the hapless Greedo down—to instead show Greedo squeezing off a shot first, thus turning Han’s previously aggressive blast into what Lucas saw as simply self-defense.” Rallying cry on T-shirts, stickers, the Internet. Lucas first: Greedo had *always* shot first, muddy close-ups caused “confusion.” “I put a little wider shot in there that made it clear that Greedo is the one who shot first, but everyone wanted to think that Han shot first, because they wanted to think that he actually just gunned him down.”

**Jones, typography locked:** “And yet this was more of Lucas’s retroactive continuity—for his own shooting script for *Star Wars,* dated May 15, 1976, clearly never gave Greedo a chance to squeeze off a shot,” then quotes:

“Suddenly the slimy alien disappears in a blinding flash of light. Han pulls his smoking gun from beneath the table as the other patrons look on in bemused amazement.

*HAN: … but it will take a lot more than the likes of you to finish me off…*

Han gets up and starts out of the cantina, flipping the bartender some coins as he leaves.

*HAN: Sorry for the mess.*” (n.28)

Lucas “determined to stick to his long-running narrative that he’d had all of the details of the entire *Star Wars* saga planned out from the start.” “Han Solo was going to marry Leia, and you look back and say, ‘Should he be a cold-blooded killer?’ Because I was thinking mythologically—should he be a cowboy, should he be John Wayne? And I said, ‘Yeah, he should be John Wayne.’ And when you’re John Wayne, you don’t shoot people [first]—you let them have the first shot. It’s a mythological reality that we hope our society pays attention to.” (n.29) “Another good try, but fans weren’t buying it, and never would.” 2004: “The special edition, that’s the one I wanted out there.… This is the movie I wanted it to be, and I’m sorry you saw half a completed film and fell in love with it.” (n.30) **Kurtz:** the changes were “probably a wrong philosophy.… It’s not the way it was in the first place. The way it was in the first place was the way we released it.” Jones wonders how Lucas might have reacted to Kurosawa digitally altering *The Hidden Fortress.* He later wears a “HAN SHOT FIRST” T-shirt while directing *Revenge of the Sith.*

The SEs proved he “could actually pull off the things that [he] wanted to pull off.” He announces he will direct all three prequels. Had asked Ron Howard, Spielberg, and Zemeckis; each said, “George, *you* should do it.” Howard: “too daunting.” **Coppola:** “I think *Star Wars,* it’s a pity, because George Lucas was a very experimental crazy guy, and he got lost in this big production and never got out of it.” Marcia: like focusing on a pea at the bottom of an inverted Lucasfilm pyramid. Lucas: “We were going to be attempting new things… I needed to be there at all times.” “It took a long time for me to adjust to *Star Wars.…* I finally did, and I’m going back to it. *Star Wars* is my destiny.” (n.38)

## Casting, shoot, complete control, release, midi-chlorians (pp. 394–407) — KEY Force

Casting director Robin Gurland, two years, still no script. Samuel L. Jackson public appeal on *TFI Friday,* Dec. 1996; “You can make me a stormtrooper.” Lands **Mace Windu, “a name Lucas salvaged from the opening line of his 1973 first draft of *Journal of the Whills.*”** Liam Neeson as Qui-Gon Jinn (Guinness-like gravitas); first meeting they swap family stories. Michael Jackson wants to play Jar Jar in full makeup, not CGI; Lucas gently refuses. Ahmed Best: “ultimately Michael Jackson would have been bigger than the movie.” Best stand-in with eye headpiece and gill costume; voice as “a kind of West Indian patois,” loping stride digitized later. “George told me that we were really going out on a limb.”

Principal photography June 26, 1997, Leavesden; script finished “practically the last minute”; Lucas’s set name tag reads YODA. “As soon as Liam Neeson walked on the set, dressed as a Jedi, I said to myself, ‘I guess I’m back.’” Mostly bluescreen. New, younger, lighter Yoda puppet (Oz: the original was “really fuckin’ heavy”). New R2s; skeletal Threepio a rod puppet, Daniels still the voice. Still bad with actors; “I’m not like some directors who will sit for days and analyze what is going on.” “Sometimes I say cut… sometimes I forget.” Digital widescreen not ready; a few HD-tape test scenes. Costs climb; “the most expensive independent film in history,” all his money, as with *Empire.* “I was gambling everything again.”

Same *Young Indy* crew. McCallum “never says no” — “that was part of the problem.” **Unlike the first trilogy, when Lucas “had often been subjected to pushback from Gary Kurtz, Irvin Kershner, Lawrence Kasdan, and Marcia Lucas… the prequels would be entirely Lucas’s vision, without compromise. His control was complete.”** Tunisia storm destroys sets: “as if the storm had hidden away for twenty years.” Wraps Sept. 30; 2,500 shots; Muren asked for 2,200 effects in 18 months: “Well, just think about it,” and he walks out. Editing still his favorite; “the script is just a rough sketch… the filming is just gathering the materials—but the editing is how I create the final draft.” Pickups include Anakin saying good-bye to Padmé’s handmaiden. “He only directed half of the movie during principal photography. The rest he directed afterward, here at ILM.” (Knoll)

Licensing: Hasbro and Galoob stock >$225 million; Lego’s first license (by 2012 “the world’s most valuable toy company”); Happy Meals, Jar Jar Pez, billion-dollar Pepsi cans. **Fox pays $80 million for distribution without seeing a frame, and must transfer all remaining original-*Star Wars* rights back to Lucasfilm** (already held *Empire* and *Jedi*). “And like that, Lucas owned all of *Star Wars* outright. The circle, as Vader had said, was now complete.”

Total control of promotion; starwars.com dribbles. Sept. 1998 official title not *Shadows of the Empire* but ***Star Wars: Episode I—The Phantom Menace.*** Trailer Nov. 1998, 2:10, twenty-six cities; fans buy *Meet Joe Black*/*Waterboy* tickets just for the trailer; 10 million downloads; March 1999 trailer crashes the site (340/sec). “George Lucas must be a superior form of life!”

He hugs ILM’s Chrissie England: “I’m really proud of you” — “words he had never once spoken to Marcia.” Manages expectations: “For every person who loves Episode I, there will be two or three who hate it.” Spielberg: “Your jaw will hang open for a week.” **Opens May 19, 1999, $28.5 million day, $132.4 million week, $926 million worldwide.** Critics cool (Ansen: “a disappointment. A big one”; Ebert: “a visionary breakthrough”). Fans later concede too much trade/taxes, “endless scenes of computer-generated talking frogs fighting computer-generated robots.” McGregor: “disappointing” and “flat.”

**Jar Jar:** *Rolling Stone* “JAR JAR SUPERSTAR”; fans find him annoying. Racism charges; Joe Morgenstern (*WSJ*): “a Rastafarian Stepin Fetchit on platform hoofs, crossed annoyingly with Butterfly McQueen.” Spokesperson Lynne Hale: “absurd.” Stings Ahmed Best (African American): “when your work is criticized negatively, you feel a hit.”

**Midi-chlorians, pp. 403–404 (n.72–73) — typography locked, priority Force.** “Worse, Lucas had made a tweak to the *Star Wars* mythos that many would consider as unforgivable as Greedo shooting first: a biological explanation for the Force. To explain Anakin’s unique status as ‘the Chosen One’ who would bring balance to the Force, Lucas had introduced the concept of midi-chlorians, intelligent microscopic forms that exist symbiotically in the cells of living beings and help tune them in to the power of the Force. It was an ill-advised explanation that drained the Force of its mystery and awe, making it a mere biological attribute like blue eyes. Gary Kurtz, whose own spiritual views had helped shape the concept of the Force two decades earlier, called the idea ‘the destruction of the spiritual center of the force, turning [it] into DNA and blood.’ Kurtz was also quick to note, however, that ‘George has a very clear idea of what he wants. And whether you agree with that or not, he goes about getting that.’”

Internet backlash (unlike muted Ewok grumbling in 1983). Lucas chalks it up to “one person typing out their opinion,” then rises to the bait: fans who nitpick midi-chlorians or “Anakin building C-3PO” are “nitpicky.” “I’m sorry if they don’t like it. They should go back and see *The Matrix* or something.” Eventually abandons the Internet. Hasbro ($650 million for all three prequels) stock down 25 percent on unsold toys; Lucasfilm privately admits the movie “did not live up to expectations” and was over-marketed (Qui-Gon necktie).

Presidio / Letterman digital arts complex: after a decade of trying to put ILM on the ranch, he changes plans — ILM, THX, LucasArts, Lucasfilm offices in plain sight at the Golden Gate, near the old Zoetrope warehouse. June 14, 1999, Presidio Trust approves. “create an organization to make the kind of movies [he] wanted to make.” “But first, he had more *Star Wars* to attend to.”
