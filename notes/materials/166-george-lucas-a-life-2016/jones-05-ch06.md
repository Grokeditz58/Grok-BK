# Jones, *George Lucas: A Life* (2016): notes — ch. 6 “Bleeding on the Page,” 1973–1976 (pp. 167–214)

Typography locked from the publisher XHTML. Citations: Jones p. N; endnotes as Jones Notes, ch. 6 n. Y. PART II EMPIRE 1973–1983 opens p. 165.

## Writing as a job; *Apocalypse* ceded; lists and “The Journal of the Whills” (pp. 167–172)

Lucas to *Filmmakers Newsletter,* 1974 (n.1): “I don’t have a natural talent for writing.… When I sit down, I bleed on the page, and it’s just awful.” Nine-to-five at the wooden desk, five pages a day on a wall calendar, 1941 Wurlitzer reward. “You go crazy writing.” (n.3)

Jones: *Star Wars* was “partly Coppola’s fault.” Late 1972 Lucas still thinks *Apocalypse Now* is next; Columbia “pretty much got a deal” until Coppola refuses to shrink his points, so Lucas’s share would shrink. Lucas cedes it “at least for the moment.” Jones’s counterfactual: if Coppola had negotiated, Lucas’s next film “might very well have been *Apocalypse Now,* and *Star Wars* would have continued to be a contractual add-on… or perhaps not at all.” Lucas is in debt; *Graffiti* is unreleased. “I’ll start developing *Star Wars.*” (n.8)

Feb. 1973, Mill Valley: “It’s your basic superhero in outer space.… I realized that what I really wanted to do was a contemporary action fantasy.” (n.9) Lists: Emperor Ford Xerxes XII, Owen, Mace, Biggs, Valorum. Luke Skywalker is there “from the very start,” but as “Prince of Bebers”; Han Solo is “leader of the Hubble people.” Alderaan, Yavin, Herald Square, Toshiro Mifune. Then a short fragment, “The Journal of the Whills”: a storybook falling open (Disney device). “This is the story of Mace Windy, a revered Jedi-bendu of Opuchi, as related to us by C. J. Thorpe, padawan learner to the famed Jedi.” Trails off mid-sentence; a nonstarter.

Key vision: “a dogfight in space with spaceships.” He tapes *The Bridges at Toko-Ri* and *Tora! Tora! Tora!*, compiles twenty-plus hours, cuts an eight-minute 16 mm reel “to see if the movement would generate excitement.” (n.12–13) Jones: “the wet concrete he would pour into the mold for the cornerstone of his own film empire.”

**April 17 treatment, *The Star Wars,* fourteen handwritten pages (pp. 171–172).** Flash Gordon + *Hidden Fortress*: princess escorted by a wise general; two bumbling bureaucrats. Luke is an *aged general* guarding a young princess on Aquilae; two escaped bureaucrats; spaceport; “lazer sword”; he recruits and trains ten boys; stolen ship; dogfight; asteroid field; crash on Yavin among giant furry aliens; princess captured; assault on Imperial prison; awards ceremony where the princess is revealed “as her true goddess self.” (n.14) Lucas still finds it “vague.” Bound in black leather with gold embossing. Pitch to Berg: “a space opera in the tradition of Flash Gordon and Buck Rogers… James Bond and *2001* combined… But it’s not camp.” (n.16)

## Studio passes; Ladd and Fox; merchandising and sequels (pp. 172–177, 184–187)

UA’s Chasman (ten pages of NASA/comic illustrations included) wires no after three weeks. Universal still owns him from the *Graffiti* deal; Tanen baffled, “a very tough time understanding [it]” (n.26); Lucas: “a $6 million idea, which I’ll make for $3 million.” (n.19) Universal passes end of June.

Alan Ladd Jr. at Fox: Berg smuggles a *Graffiti* print; Ladd “absolutely bowled over.” Lucas talks to Ladd in film language (*The Seahawk,* *Captain Blood,* Flash Gordon), not photos. Ladd waits out Universal. Mid-July memo: $50,000 to write, $100,000 to direct, $15,000 development, $3 million budget, **$10,000 on signing** — “That was why I made the deal.” (n.27) Kurtz as producer, Fields and Marcia editing, Murch post; no assigned executive producer. “I fought for many years to make sure no one could tell me what to do.” (n.29) Ladd: “It was a gamble, and I was betting on Lucas.” Lucas: “[Ladd] invested in me, he did not invest in the movie.” (n.32–33)

Aug. 1, 1973: *Graffiti* hits; Lucas is hot. He does not reopen the fee (Berg thought $500,000 was possible). “I’ll do it for the deal memo, but we haven’t talked about things like merchandising rights, [and] sequel rights.” (n.35) Wants it produced by Lucasfilm (still irked that Zoetrope costs were billed against *THX*). Signs the memo late August; it is only a memo, with an escape clause after the screenplay. Sets up The Star Wars Corporation; Lucasfilm “loans” him as director. Deadline for first draft: Oct. 31, 1973 — he will miss it.

**August 23, 1974 letter (pp. 184–187).** Still no formal contract. “I don’t want more money… I do want the rights to make these sequels.” (n.54) Huyck: “You’re lucky to get ten million dollars… Let’s just make the first one.” Also merchandising / “sole and exclusive right” to use the name *Star Wars.* “When I was writing, I had visions of R2-D2 mugs and little windup robots, but I thought that would be the end of it.” (n.60) Fox “giving away merchandising rights, just for the publicity.” “I simply said, ‘I’m gonna be able to make T-shirts… and I’m gonna be able to sell this movie even though the studio won’t.’” (n.62) Milius recalls Lucas: “I’m going to make five times as much money as Francis on these science fiction toys. And I won’t have to make *The Godfather.*” (n.63) Fox trades merchandising and sequels because they got *Graffiti*’s director cheaply. Gareth Wigan, decades later: “George was enormously far-sighted, and the studio wasn’t… George *did* know the world was changing. I mean, *he changed it.*” (n.66)

Coppola offers him *Apocalypse* again, now self-financed; Lucas declines — “I had sort of fallen in love with” the *Star Wars* draft (n.57). “I thought, ‘we all know what a terrible mess we have made of the world’… what we really need is something more positive.” (n.58) To *Film Quarterly,* 1977 (n.59): “dreams and fantasies, getting children to believe there is more to life than garbage and killing… a whole generation has grown up without fairy tales.”

## Reading, Vietnam, May 1974 rough draft (pp. 177–181) — KEY for Force/Vader

Distractions: Supersnipe comic shop partnership (Edward Summer); Parkhouse renovation “kept me going for nine, ten months.” (n.38) Marcia lead-edits *Alice Doesn’t Live Here Anymore*; later *Taxi Driver* and *New York, New York.* Lucas shuttles to Tucson, reading, “keeping his eye on Scorsese.”

Reading: Burroughs *A Princess of Mars*; Doc Smith Lensman; Harry Harrison (*Stainless Steel Rat,* *Bill, the Galactic Hero* — frustrated farm boy); Asimov *Guide to the Bible* and Frazer *The Golden Bough* (Scorsese remembered him reading them); Kirby *New Gods* (Orion, son of Darkseid). **Lucas, p. 179 (n.41):** “I researched kids’ movies and how they work and how myths work… I found that myth always took place over the hill, in some exotic, far-off land. For the Greeks, it was Ulysses going off into the unknown. For America it was out West.… The last place left ‘over the hill’ is space.”

**Vietnam, p. 179 (n.42–43):** “I figured that I couldn’t make [*Apocalypse*] because it was about the Vietnam War, so I would essentially deal with some of the same interesting concepts… and convert them into space fantasy so you’d have a large technological empire going after a small group of freedom fighters.” In notes for the next draft, Aquilae is “a small independent country like North Vietnam threatened by a neighbor or provincial rebellion,” the Empire “like America ten years from now… allowing the crime rate to rise to the point where a ‘total control’ police state was welcomed by the people.” Jones: “This was Lucas coyly hiding his liberal politics in plain sight.” An associate: “Most people have no realization that part of it [*Star Wars*] is about a Vietnam situation.”

**May 1974 rough draft (eight months late), 191 scenes, 33,000 words, p. 180 (n.44).** Young Annikin Starkiller trains as a Jedi Bendu under seventy-year-old general Luke Skywalker. Two droids (one short and squat, the other a gleaming “*Metropolis* style” robot). Han Solo is “a huge green-skinned monster with no nose and large gills.” Fourteen-year-old Princess Leia. “Lazerswords,” Wookiees. **Darth Vader is a “tall, grim-looking general”—and a relatively minor character.** First appearance of the farewell: “May the Force of Others be with you.” Still holding cantina, asteroid chase, prison rescue, awards ceremony. Unsure what the Empire is searching for; too many characters/locations/backstories. Ladd asks for a second draft.

## Names, Chewie, R2-D2, Indiana Smith, friends, merchandising already covered (pp. 181–187)

Number-two pencils, blue-and-green-lined notebook paper, hunched cursive, pocket notebook. **Chewbacca:** Marcia driving with Alaskan malamute Indiana in the passenger seat, “nearly as big as a person,” looking like her copilot. **R2-D2:** Walter Murch, editing *Graffiti,* asks for “R2 D2” (Reel 2, Dialogue 2); Lucas scribbles it down. “As I was writing, I would say the names to myself, and if I had a hard time dealing with a name phonetically, I would change it.” (n.46) July 1974 he briefly changes almost every name: R2-D2 → A-2, Jedi Bendu → Dai Nogas, Annikin → Justin Valor, Leia → Zara, Wookiees → Jawas.

Stuck, he daydreams other serials, especially *Don Winslow of the Navy.* “I began thinking it’d be a good idea to have an archaeologist in a 1930s-style serial.… That’s how I came up with the idea of Indiana Smith.” (n.48) Name from the malamute; later slightly changed. Marcia brings TV dinners or crustless tuna sandwiches; “I’m real hard, but I only tell him what he already knows.” (n.49) Huycks, Milius, Coppola (few changes: “I thought it was terrific”). Temporary thaw with Coppola (*Film Quarterly* “stinky kid” interview, n.51). Spielberg warehouse: Lucas stuck in the *Jaws* shark’s mouth. Jones notes *Jaws* will be the highest-grossing film “at least until Lucas surpassed it with *Star Wars* two years later.”

## McQuarrie, Force of Others / Ashla / Bogan / Kiber Crystal, second-draft plot (pp. 187–190) — KEY

Colin Cantwell (miniatures for *2001,* *Andromeda Strain*): “comic book nobility”; kitbashes X-wing and TIE so “you had to instantly know the bad guys from the good guys… by how [a ship] looks and feels.” (n.69) Ralph McQuarrie (via Barwood/Robbins): “used universe” aesthetic. “I just describe what I want and then he does it.” **Vader’s design “a happy accident” (p. 188):** McQuarrie “had given the ‘grim-looking general’ a spacesuit he thought the character would need to board a spacecraft—but Lucas loved the look so much it became the villain’s regular uniform.” “When words could not convey my ideas, I could always point to one of Ralph’s fabulous illustrations and say, ‘Do it like this.’” (n.71) First painting, Jan. 2, 1975: primitive R2 and 3PO on a desert planet, bound with the new script ** *Adventures of the Starkiller, Ep. I: The Star Wars.***

**Force, pp. 189–190 (n.74).** In this draft Lucas “more carefully fleshed out the concept of the Force—still called the *Force of Others*… dividing it neatly into a good side called Ashla and a bad side called Bogan.” It “could be intensified through the possession of a mystical Kiber Crystal—Lucas’s first, but by no means last, great MacGuffin.” Kurtz: “The concept of the Force was an important one… the difficulty is trying to create a religious spiritual concept that works in a very simple way without heavy exposition.” Droids become more central (*Hidden Fortress* peasants); Coppola annoyed he “chucked” a script Coppola thought was fine and “was interested in the two servants.” Leia downgraded to Luke’s cousin, almost written out. Focus on “the Starkiller” and his sons.

Plot of this draft: Luke gets a hologram from brother Deak asking him to bring the Kiber Crystal to their wounded father, the Starkiller. Han is now a “burly-bearded but ruggedly handsome boy”—“pretty much Coppola as a starpilot”—Chewie “a huge gray bushbaby monkey.” Rescue Deak from Cloud City, Death Star pursuit, Yavin, crystal revives the Starkiller; Luke leads the assault but is *not* the one who fires the fatal shot; roll-up promises “the perilous search for THE PRINCESS OF ONDOS.” (n.76) Bones falling into place (droids, hiring Han/Chewie, garbage chute, Death Star), still too much backstory. Ladd confused; Coppola asks why he is “dumping” the first-draft elements.

## ILM, *Jaws,* third draft, Kenobi from Castaneda, casting, contract, fourth draft / Vader-father (pp. 190–214) — KEY

Fox stalling on budget. Kurtz books Elstree seventeen weeks from March 1976 (Pinewood required house staff). Private guesses $2.5–3.5 million; Kurtz later ~$13 million; they submit $10 million. Fox no longer has in-house effects. Danforth and Trumbull say no (Trumbull to *Close Encounters*). Lucas: “It’s really become binary—either you do it yourself, or you don’t get a say.” (n.79) John Dykstra, 27, from *Silent Running.* Lucas does not want “men in black suits with models on sticks.” Motion control (Berkeley PDP-11 precedent): move the camera, not the model; “documentary-style special effects.” “I don’t care, kid, just do it.” Warehouse at 6842 Valjean, Van Nuys. Young non-union crew (Edlund, Muren, Johnston, Kuran). **Industrial Light and Magic:** Lucas later says the name “popped into his head.” Industrial park + Dykstraflex “Light”; “Forget the Industrial and the Light—this is going to have to be Magic. Otherwise we’re doomed, making a movie nobody wants.” (n.94–95) Seeded with *Graffiti* money (~$88,000 in the first three weeks). Ron Howard: “How many people think the solution to gaining quality control… is to start their own special-effects company?”

June 20, 1975: *Jaws* opens wide, TV ads, merchandising — “The modern blockbuster was born.”

Script: no leading female, so Luke briefly becomes a girl (one McQuarrie painting), then male again. **May 1975 six-page synopsis (n.98):** a mystical old man “lifted straight out of” Carlos Castaneda’s *The Teachings of Don Juan.* “Old man can do magic, read minds, talk to things like *Don Juan.*” August third draft: General Ben Kenobi. Luke more firmly the hero; Leia (not Deak) captured. Kiber Crystal still in, but stolen Death Star plans becoming more interesting. “I find plots boring, because they’re so mechanical.” (n.99) “Each story was a totally different story about totally different characters before I finally landed on *the* story.” (n.100)

Friends tell him to make *Apocalypse* or *Taxi Driver.* Marcia begs Brian De Palma: “George thinks he has no talent.” (n.102) Joint *Carrie*/*Star Wars* casting with Roos at Zoetrope/Goldwyn. “Brian did all the talking, because George didn’t talk then.” (Fisher, n.104) Love triangle as intergalactic *Casablanca*; play it straight, not camp. Looks first (Hamill, n.106). Passes on Travolta, Nolte, Tommy Lee Jones. Marcia insecure about Leia auditions; Lucas: first vow “never to date an actress.” Passes Irving, Foster, Purl, Cindy Williams (he wanted “a young Cindy Williams”). Briefly the “perfect princess”: Terri Nunn, just turned fourteen. Han evolved: green-skinned alien → burly Coppola type → cynical pirate, “part James Dean, part Humphrey Bogart.” Briefly considers an African American (Glynn Turman); Kurtz: “He didn’t want *Guess Who’s Coming to Dinner,*” given a possible Han–Leia romance (n.110). Also Pacino, Kurt Russell; almost settles on Christopher Walken. Roos plants carpenter Ford hanging a door. Dec. 30: Hamill (“Who talks like this?”), Fisher, Ford chemistry. Two trios: Walken/Seltzer/Nunn (“more serious, more realistic”) vs Ford/Hamill/Fisher (“more fun, more goofy”). Nunn is a minor; Seltzer “too intellectual”; Hamill “idealistic, naïve, and hopeful.” Fisher: “I got [the part] with the proviso that I went to a fat farm, and that I lose ten pounds.” (n.120) Coppola and Ladd uneasy about the unknown leads.

Guinness lunch; diary: “a small, neat-faced young man with a black beard… not much sense of humour. But I liked him.” “Fairy-tale rubbish but *could* be interesting perhaps.” (n.124–127) John Barry and Roger Christian from *Lucky Lady* Mexico sets: “wanted it all [looking] real and used.” Christian: “spaceships should be things you see in garages with oil dripping.” (n.129) Ben Burtt as “the next Walter Murch”; Chewie from four bears, a lion, a seal, and a walrus; “Collect weird, strange sounds.” (n.132) John Williams via Spielberg on Martha’s Vineyard: “a very sort of Max Steiner–type, old-fashioned, romantic movie score.” (n.134) Lucas: “He’s right up there with Buddy Holly and the Drifters.” (n.136)

London: Prowse given choice of Vader or Chewie, “chose the villain”; Mayhew as Chewie. Threepio voice planned as “a Bronx used-car dealer”; Daniels almost doesn’t audition, locked on by McQuarrie’s painting. Still no official Fox green light; Lucas and Kurtz spend nearly $1 million of their own. *Lucky Lady* overruns spook Fox; production halted pending a December board meeting. Lucas shops it back to UA and Universal; both pass. Universal internal memo: “how much faith we have in Mr. Lucas’s ability to pull it all off.” Lucas, *Rolling Stone* 1980: “I hold grudges.” (n.143) Cost-cutting actually improves the script: Cloud City removed, Leia imprisoned on the Death Star. *Space: 1999* Eagles resemble Solo’s still-unnamed pirate ship; redesign as a flying hot rod / “Porkburger”; named *Millennium Falcon* only in March 1976.

Dec. 13, 1975 board (Princess Grace present): Ladd, “I’m a believer in this.” Green light at $8.3 million, final figure $8,228,228. Lucas: Fox asking for a $15 million movie on half the budget. Formal contract ~Feb. 1976, two weeks before cameras: 40 percent of gross; sequels, TV, publishing, merchandising. Pollock: “one of the most brilliantly written movie contracts I have ever read… of a filmmaker getting what they want.” (n.148)

**Fourth draft, Jan. 1, 1976, titled *The Adventures of Luke Starkiller, as Taken from the Journal of the Whills, Saga I: Star Wars.* KEY typography-locked, pp. 213–214 (n.150–154).** Kiber Crystal removed, making the Force “more ethereal… than to have it solidified in a thing like a crystal.” To Alan Dean Foster: “The Force was ‘a big idea.’… ‘[Luke] has to trust his feelings rather than his senses and his logic—that’s essentially what the Force of Others comes down to.’”

**Jones, p. 213 (n.152), typography locked — do not flatten this:** “There is no indication that Lucas ever intended for Darth Vader to be Anakin Skywalker, or that he would be the father of Luke and Leia, twins separated at birth. While Lucas would, over the course of three decades, perpetuate a kind of retroactive continuity by asserting that this had been his plan all along, in 1975 he still clearly intended for Vader and Luke’s father to be separate characters. Vader’s backstory, he explained, was ‘about Ben and Luke’s father and Vader, when they are young Jedi knights. Vader kills Luke’s father, then Ben and Vader have a confrontation, just like they have in *Star Wars,* and Ben almost kills Vader.’ As for the name ‘Vader,’ Lucas has made much of the linguistic coincidence that *vader* is the Dutch word for ‘father’—but it was also a name he’d likely heard nearly daily at Downey High School, where he had a schoolmate one grade ahead, an all-conference athlete named Gary Vader.”

Lucas still unhappy with the script: “I had a lot of vague concepts… I’ve never fully resolved it.… I kept simplifying it… but I’m still not very happy with the script. I never have been.” (n.153) “In the end, I really didn’t think we were going to make any money at all on *Star Wars.*” (n.154)
