# Jones, *George Lucas: A Life* (2016): notes — ch. 12 “Cynical Optimism,” 1999–2005 (pp. 408–435)

Typography locked from the publisher XHTML. Citations: Jones p. N; Jones Notes, ch. 12 n. Y.

## Episode II writing, all-digital, Sydney (pp. 408–416)

By May 1999 he has already been writing Episode II nearly a year. Harder than the others: “largely a love story, driven by character and dialogue, neither of which was Lucas’s strong suit.” Fourteen or fifteen drafts before he lets his assistant type. **Title page, March 13, 2000, typography locked:** at the center he has scrawled

EPISODE II

JAR JAR’S GREAT ADVENTURE

“The title was a joke,” his way of showing he has taken *Phantom Menace* criticism to heart. Binks relegated to a minor supporting role; “any larger plans Lucas had had for the loping Gungan were shelved.” He always defends the character; at a 2015 Disney Expo he suggests Binks was inspired in part by Goofy’s look and mannerisms: “I know that you will look at them differently now.… I love Goofy and I love Jar Jar.” “Fans delighted in theorizing that Lucas’s abandoned storyline would have revealed the bumbling comic relief to be a cold-blooded Sith Lord.” Lucas offers “only coy silence.” (n.1)

Done with film: Episode II entirely digital. About six minutes of *TPM* digital (lightsaber duels) convinced him it can be clearer than film, needing softer lighting. Sony/Panavision special cameras. Industry hand-wringing; would they even be called *films*? Lucas: “It will be called *cinema.*” Production base moves from London to new Fox Studios, Sydney (first major film there); extended lease to keep sets for pickups; a few sequences still at Elstree.

Pre-production again without a finished script. Chiang at the Main House: Lucas stamps OK or **FABULOUSO [*sic*]** (only four drawings get FABULOUSO in eighteen months). Casting from Nov. 1999 for characters still barely described. Seventy sets, more than *TPM*. “George had always told us that Episode II was going to be much smaller… but it didn’t turn out that way.” By April 2000, two months from cameras, he brings in Jonathan Hales (*Young Indy*); Lucas talks, Hales scribbles. Meanwhile Lucas races the Toyota Pro/Celebrity Race in Long Beach. Hales delivers June 23 as Lucas leaves for Sydney; no one has a complete script. Lucas: “Some of this is brilliant, some of it is not so brilliant.… We’ll talk about what’s not brilliant.”

Principal photography Monday, June 26, 2000: McDiarmid as Palpatine on Stage 6, only a podium and elevated pod, rest bluescreen for the Senate. Script “just arrived at the last minute.” First real R2 appearance; McGregor: “Everyone goes a bit silly.” Daniels in the gold suit for the first time since *Jedi,* dingy/nearly gray; shuffles up: “Hello, I am See-Threepio. And you are?” Lucas: “I am… astonished.… Astonished that you can still get into that suit!”

Digital HD: dailies same day, no bad negatives; no reloads; cheaper, more shots. He wonders if he can skip practical sets entirely; John Knoll talks him off that ledge (shadows/reflections). DP David Tattersall: “George is the only one who seems to like [bluescreen].… There’s no artistry in lighting bluescreen.” High-def “forced filmmakers to up their game” (blemishes show). McGregor: “a bit like playing chess.” Hayden Christensen (year-long search, momentary look at DiCaprio): “Usually we were just in a sea of blue.” Christopher Lee, 78, hacks at a CG Yoda who won’t be added for six months.

**Digital Yoda, pp. 413–414, 416–417 — typography locked.** “A dream come true”; he’d been frustrated by Yoda’s lack of mobility since *Empire.* No longer limited to Oz’s arm or being carried on Hamill’s back. Oz still the voice, no longer the performer; Yoda entirely in the computer in post, free to move. Lucas warmer with actors than “faster” / “more intense.” Protective of Natalie Portman — “the young queen who would eventually give birth to the twins Luke and Leia” — first year at Harvard, falls asleep in fittings. Christensen as Anakin/Vader: McCallum, and Lucas agrees, “There is something about him that makes you think, ‘Yeah, this guy could lose it.’”

Coppola visits early July, dinner; a set photo still shows Lucas as padawan, hands in pockets, Coppola at center. Late August Italy (Palace of Caserta: lights hung from helium balloons); Seville, Lucas mobbed for autographs. Mid-September Tunisia: *TPM* sets still maintained as tourist sites; farther out, the 1976 Lars homestead, buried props unearthed, dining room occupied but unaltered. “It was a very nostalgic experience.” Wraps Sept. 20 at Elstree (first time back since *Last Crusade*), McGregor on a flying assassin droid; sixty days, a day and a half early. “My heart is actually in the editing room.”

## Yoda duel, “cynical optimist,” *AOTC* release, Mustafar setup (pp. 416–423)

“I never thought I’d do the *Star Wars* prequels, because there was no real way I could get Yoda to fight. But once you had digital, there was no end to what you could do.” He tells Knoll he “held back” effects on *TPM* “because I wasn’t sure you guys [at ILM] could do it” — “a scary thing to hear” after 2,000-plus shots. Rob Coleman studies Oz frame by frame. Once Lucas decides on a high-energy duel — script direction only **“YODA ATTACKS!”** — they must not make him look silly. “We set ourselves an impossible task and then just hoped we could accomplish it.” Result: physical yet graceful Yoda vs Count Dooku, one of the film’s most talked-about sequences.

Coruscant sky chase: Anakin in “essentially a flying hot rod with an exposed engine in the front, much like Lucas’s old Fiat Bianchina. It was even yellow—yet another in a long line of yellow Lucas race cars.” Almost entirely CG, from Burtt/Dozoretz animatics. Digital stormtrooper battalions (original trilogy limited by costumes). Actors moved between scenes, backgrounds swapped; no film-transfer generation loss.

First full cut Feb. 2001, nearly three hours; pickups all bluescreen at Ealing, no sets. “Star Wars had become a truly digital universe.”

**2001 UK census:** just over 390,000 write “Jedi” as religion — fourth-largest in England and Wales. Census office headline: “390,000 JEDI THERE ARE.” **Lucas to *Time,* the chapter title (n.21):** “I’m a cynical optimist.… I’m a cynic who has hope for the human race.”

Aug. 2001 title *Attack of the Clones* announced; groans. Official line: it “harken[ed] back to the sense of pure fun” of the pulps. *EW:* “When you’re managing a multibillion dollar toy franchise, maybe you don’t have time to come up with cool titles.” Oct.: *TPM* first SW DVD, deleted scenes with finished effects, $45 million first week. Merchandising cut nearly two-thirds after Episode I “over-licensed, over-shipped… over-saturated”; no neckties, no fast food, no official soda (Pepsi had circulated nearly 8 billion cans). Hidden trailer unlocked with a *TPM* DVD; full trailer in front of *Harry Potter.* Love-story emphasis leaves some fans cold; webmasters invited to the ranch for 20 minutes of questions. “Lucas had turned his own fans into cynical optimists.”

ILM theater: always second-row center; full-sized R2 and a VFX-Oscar totem. No win since *Gump* (lost *TPM* to *The Matrix*). He keeps editing from that seat. Final effects shot signed April 8, 2002. Days later he asks the art department to start Episode III with no script, “only” able to describe **“the volcano world where Anakin Skywalker would be cut down and Darth Vader would rise from the ashes.”** He had “loved the concept drawings of Vader’s castle on a sea of lava that Ralph McQuarrie had done… during pre-production on *The Empire Strikes Back.* He wanted to see *that* in the next film—or at least something like it.” McCallum: “We never truly know what George is doing.”

April 10 final cut; Stag Theater preview from a DVD, digital projector. Most theaters still film; he will spur conversion as he did with THX/*Jedi.* In 2002 only ~30 theaters in the world are digital; by 2014, 92 percent of 40,045 U.S. screens. “Lucas had led the charge, clenching a DVD of *Attack of the Clones* in his upraised fist.”

**Opens May 16, 2002, 3,161 theaters; $110.2 million in four days, $649 million worldwide** — short of *TPM,* fourth for the year. A. O. Scott: audiences will line up “like weary Brezhnev-era Muscovites.” Some kinder (*Rolling Stone* notes a dark side “Lucas hasn’t flaunted since… *Empire*”). Loses VFX Oscar to *Two Towers.* Ebert: they “talk and talk and talk”; Travers: dialogue that induces “projectile vomiting.” Ansen, “Attack of the Groans”: better than *TPM,* “not great.” Lucas claims clunky dialogue, over-the-top battles, and the love scenes the *Times* called “the most embarrassing romantic avowals in recent screen history” were all intentional: “It is presented very honestly… really played to the hilt.… this film is even more of a melodrama than the others.” Patrick Goldstein (*Times*): “SECLUSION HAS LEFT LUCAS OUT OF TOUCH”; “No one seems to deliver bad news.… Lucas’s best work was made with strong collaborators.” Lucas: “That’s one of the problems of a democracy. You get these individual voices that are very loud, and very dysfunctional.… And if you cater to those voices, you end up with a very dysfunctional society.” Jones’s gloss: “Create. Oversee. *Control everything.*”

## Writing *Sith,* Mustafar, Spielberg, shoot, DVD ghosts, release, “finished” (pp. 423–435) — KEY Vader/Anakin

June 2002 attic art department already designing Episode III with no script. McCallum: “building backward, designing a twenty-five-floor skyscraper without foundations.” “Do you know what it’s like to budget a film without a script?” Lucas: “I’ve painted myself into a corner. I have to get from there to here, and I have to connect these two things [the original trilogy and the prequels] in a very precise way.… Writing is a lot of puzzle-solving.” Vacations in Canada with the kids; jots ideas for III and a working-title *Indiana Jones IV.* Nov. 1 he tells McCallum and Coleman he’ll start “when reality hits.” McCallum: “You said you’ve *been* writing.” Lucas: “I’ve been *thinking* about it.” Three weeks later, leaving a design review: “I just have to figure out a movie to go with it.” Mid-December, four days a week at the desk. Reviews with Bocquet a list of sets and characters “including a ten-year-old Han Solo, whom Lucas envisioned working with Yoda to track down the villainous General Grievous.” “This is all subject to change.”

**Fifty-five-page rough draft, Jan. 31, 2003, already titled *Revenge of the Sith,* “a nod to his abandoned title for *Return of the Jedi.*”** Only McCallum gets the completed draft; designers get oral descriptions so they won’t “lock something down—because everything is going to change.” April 10, first full draft; second starts immediately; Sydney in two months.

Feb. 8, 2003, groundbreaking for the Presidio Digital Arts Center with Mayor Willie Brown and Nancy Pelosi ($300 million, aim 2005). Agreement hadn’t been signed until Aug. 2001. **Micheline Chau** (on her way to replacing Radley as president/COO) tells him he can’t visit the site unless invited: “George and I had a conversation about freedom and letting go.” He is invited only three times in a year and a half — long enough to review twenty window designs and more than a hundred fabric swatches.

May 1: he throws the script on the art-department table, looks tired, says he’s “bringing in a guest director so I don’t have to do this anymore.… The director’s coming in on Thursday.” Panic (Ryan Church wonders if he must go back to Hollywood). **May 8: the guest is Spielberg** — not to direct the film, but to edit animatics on a laptop “while you’re just sitting by the pool.” Spielberg works mostly the climactic Anakin/Obi-Wan duel on Mustafar: “I want these guys pouring, dripping sweat. Their hair at some point should be smoking.” Lucas: “We have very, very similar sensibilities.”

Arrives Sydney June 15 with a still-revising third draft and the children. **Family cameos:** Lucas’s screen debut as blue-faced Baron Papanoida at the opera, 15-year-old Katie as his daughter; Amanda 22 huddling with senators; 10-year-old Jett reprises Jedi youngling Zett Jukassa, “would even get to die heroically on-screen.” Fourth and final draft June 26; filming June 30, Stage 2. Seventy-one sets; budget $113 million vs $120 million for *AOTC.* Digital “video village”: every angle on plasma, selects shots, transmits immediately to Burtt at the ranch. McCallum: “Working with George is a George medium.” Lucas: “I’ve been accused of being a micromanager, but… I’m really responsible for every single detail on the picture.” July 2, Daniels in gold: Lucas calls out, “***Star Wars* has arrived!**” Mayhew as Chewie, audible gasps. McGregor “quite choked up” in a final scene with R2. Still mutters “Oh, I just want to get through it,” but better than 1977. Attributes mellowing to the kids (“the key to a nervous breakdown”). Wraps Sept. 17, five days early; “the most fun film I’ve ever worked on.” Unsentimental at the good-byes. To Knoll: “I’ve been working on it for eighteen months, and I have exactly eighteen months to go.”

Hovers at ILM over lunch (sandwich and soda). Invites Coppola, Dec., to watch digital directing (move characters, raise an arm, insert a blink). Coppola: “Are you changing the script much, or is it holding up pretty well?” “It’s holding up.” “You’re doing what you like to do most. Having fun.” Pickups at Shepperton, late Aug. 2004 — first time back since the 1977 throne-room ceremony. Last shot of Daniels as Threepio. “It’s only been twenty-five years.” “And we’re still not getting it right.” “Thank you for everything.”

**Sept. 2004 original-trilogy DVD — typography locked, Force ghosts / Emperor.** He can’t keep his hands off: “inserting several new tweaks to the films he’d already modified.” During Episode III he records McDiarmid reading the Emperor’s *Empire* dialogue “—which had been made several years before McDiarmid had even been cast in the role—and dubbed his voice over the original dialogue, thus ensuring consistency with the prequels.” “Curiously, he also digitally removed actor Sebastian Shaw from the final scene in *Jedi*—where the redeemed Anakin materializes to join Yoda and Ben Kenobi at Luke’s side—and replaced him with Hayden Christensen. (He had been only half-joking when he suggested to ILM that they insert Liam Neeson’s Qui-Gon Jinn into the scene as well.)” HAN SHOT FIRST rumbles back; DVDs still do more than $100 million on day one.

Nov. 2003: *Star Wars: Clone Wars* micro-series (Tartakovsky, 25 episodes, 3–12 minutes) to “bridge the gap” between II and III; Lucas gives a story line and ground rules, then largely leaves him alone. Pleased enough to let Tartakovsky introduce General Grievous in March 2005, two months before the film.

Fans line up early (“Nerdy Gras”). May 16 London premiere at the UCI Empire, end of a six-film marathon. Lucas worried it is too downbeat; first SW PG-13. “It’s not a happy movie by any stretch of the imagination. It’s a tragedy.… It will probably be the least successful of the *Star Wars* movies—but I know that.” **Wrong: $50 million single-day record, $108 million opening weekend, $848 million worldwide.** A. O. Scott: best of the prequels, even better than the original *Star Wars.* *Variety:* fans will be “inspired enough to believe again.” Ansen: compares favorably with *Empire,* “wide-eyed, childlike approach to storytelling… has remained the same.… he’s stayed true to his vision, and that vision has changed the cultural landscape irrevocably.” Still, bad dialogue, no chemistry, too much digital showing off; everyone loves Yoda.

June: ribbon-cutting at the Letterman Digital Arts Center, named for the 1898 army hospital (recycled remains in the DNA of the buildings). 23 acres, four buildings, 850,000 sq ft, $350 million, all Lucasfilm. ILM, THX, LucasArts, GLEF, Lucasfilm HQ, ~2,500 employees. Bronze Yoda at the entrance; public peek in Building B lobby (Vader, Boba Fett; a carbonite block of tongue-lolling Jar Jar, “proving once and for all that Lucas wasn’t above poking a little fun at himself”). Chau: they spent “a lot of years hidden away”; no IPO, “only ever be one stockholder.”

June 9, 2005, AFI Life Achievement Award (after Ford 2000, Spielberg 1995). Spielberg: “You have done more for the collective consciousness of this planet than you will ever know.” Lucas: expected “a room full of stormtroopers and Princess Leias”; bows to Spielberg as “my partner, my pal, my inspiration, [and] my challenger” (same phrase Jones used earlier for the first Spielberg meeting). Thanks Coppola: “he took me from not being able to write a word to being the king of wooden dialogue” — Ford laughs hardest. Biggest thanks to the children; Amanda and Katie weep. “I love cinema.… thank you all for going to the movies, especially *mine!*”

ROTS finishes as 2005’s highest-grossing U.S. film, third of the six SW films. “Happy and relieved.” Wants “a very different kind of filmmaking.” Esoteric art films, maybe another Indy. **Close, p. 435 (n.60):** “Of one thing he was certain: there would be no more *Star Wars.* ‘*Star Wars* is finished,’ Lucas said. ‘I do have a lot of other movies that I want to make.… I don’t have to answer to anybody.… That’s what I’ve earned: to be able to do what I want to do.’”
