## Prologue: Out of Control, March 1976 (pp. 3–6)

Tunisia, first day of *Star Wars* filming, morning of March 22, 1976. R2-D2’s batteries already dead; remote-control robots malfunctioning, signals scrambled by Arabic radio. Mark Hamill: “The robots would go bananas, bumping into each other, falling down, breaking.” “It took hours to get them set up again.” (n.1)

- **Lucas, p. 4, typography locked (n.2):** “I was very depressed about the whole thing.”
- **Lucas, p. 4 (n.3):** “It was purely a case of Fox not putting up the money until it was too late. Every day we would lose an hour or so due to those robots, and we wouldn’t have lost that time if we’d had another six weeks to finish them and test them and have them working before we started.”
- Anthony Daniels miserable in the C-3PO costume; falls over and cannot get up.
- **Lucas, p. 5 (n.5), typography to re-check in XHTML before sealing** (possible quotation-mark irregularity around the last sentence): “It was very, very difficult getting things to work. The truth is that the robots didn’t work at all. Threepio works very painfully.… I couldn’t get Artoo to go more than a few feet without running into something.… Everything was a prototype… like, ‘Gee, we’re going to build this—we have no money, but have to try to make this work. But nothing really worked.”
- **Lucas, p. 5 (n.6):** “They tell people what to do without reason. Sooner or later, they decided they know more about making movies than directors. Studio heads. You can’t fight them because they’ve got the money.” Jones’s close: “If *Star Wars* worked out, one thing would have to change for sure: *he’d* control the money.”
- Rain in Tunisia’s Nefta Valley for the first time in seven years; Tunisian army; a set blown “halfway to Algeria” (crew member, n.7). Eighty-four-day shoot, twenty days over schedule.
- **Lucas, p. 6 (n.8):** “It kept getting cut down because of all the drama, and I didn’t think it’d turned out very well.” “I was seriously, seriously depressed at that point, because nothing had gone right. Everything was screwed up. I was desperately unhappy.” Skipped his own end-of-Tunisia party. Jones closes: a little more than a year before release, “the *Star Wars* project was a mess, and the movie was going to be terrible. Lucas was certain of it.”

## Ch. 1: Scrawny Little Devil, 1944–1962 (pp. 9–37)

- Lucas on ancestry, p. 9 (n.1): “some criminal or somebody who got thrown out of England or France”; “My family came from nowhere. Nobody knows where we originally came from.” P. 9–10 (n.2): “I’m always sort of living for tomorrow, for better or for worse. It’s just a personality quirk.” P. 10 (n.3): “It’s great not to have been born a prince. I appreciate that. I truly believe in this country, that you can do anything if you apply yourself.”
- George Sr. as “a very old-fashioned kind of guy… kind of a classic small-town businessman who you’d see in a movie” (p. 10, n.4). Walton Lucas died of diabetes 1928 (condition “leapfrog[s] one generation” to George Jr.). Modesto founded 1870; motto WATER, WEALTH, CONTENTMENT, HEALTH. George Sr. and Dorothy Bomberger married August 3, 1933 (he twenty, she eighteen). L. M. Morris Company; 10 percent of the business in 1937; full sale January 26, 1949; Morris dies seven days later.
- Birth: 5:30 a.m. Sunday, May 14, 1944, Mother’s Day; five pounds, fourteen ounces; earlier name Jeffrey abandoned for George Walton Lucas Jr. Prominent/floppy ear taped by George Sr. “Scrawny little devil,” said George Sr. (p. 15, n.26). Housekeeper Mildred Shelley (“Till”). Wendy born three years later. Dorothy often ill.
- Toys, trains, “environments,” backyard roller coaster with John Plummer. **Lucas, p. 18 (n.41–42):** “When I was very young, I loved make-believe. But it was the kind of make-believe that used all the technological toys I could come by, like model airplanes and cars. I suppose that an extension of that interest led to what later occupied my mind, the *Star Wars* stories.” Still, “there wasn’t much as a kid that inspired me in what I did as an adult.” Or so he would always claim. Childhood not romanticized: “I was very much aware that growing up wasn’t pleasant, it was just… frightening.” (p. 18, n.43) Mentors/big-brother figures; sister Ann’s fiancé killed in Korea. “normal, tough, repressed childhood filled with fear and trepidation all over the place… But generally I enjoyed it. It was good.” (pp. 18–19, n.45)
- Ambivalence about Modesto (“California” / “south of San Francisco” / finally “Modesto”). “Modesto was really Norman Rockwell, *Boys’ Life* magazine… raking leaves on Saturday afternoons and having bonfires. Just very classic Americana.” (p. 19, n.47)
- **KEY, pp. 19–20 — God, religion, and Jones’s Force gloss.** At twelve or thirteen he rebelled against Sunday school (n.48). At six, a “very profound” mystical experience: “It centered around God,” wondering “‘What is God?’ But more than that, ‘What is reality? What is this?’ It’s as if you reach a point and suddenly you say, ‘Wait a second—What is the world? What are we? What am I? How do I function in this, and what’s going on here?’” (n.49) **Jones (not a Lucas quote):** “They were questions Lucas would struggle with, explore, and, with the creation of the Force in *Star Wars,* attempt to answer in his films.” Then **Lucas, p. 20 (n.50):** “I have strong feelings about God and the nature of life, but I’m not devoted to one particular faith.” Raised Methodist; intrigued by Till’s German Lutheran church and ritual. “He would eventually describe his religion as a melding of Methodist and Buddhist. (‘It’s Marin County,’ he said in 2002… ‘We’re *all* Buddhists up here.’)” (n.53)
- School: bored; “One of the big problems I had, more than anything else, was that I always wanted to learn something other than what was being taught… I was *bored.*” (p. 20, n.56) Adventure books; Landmark books addiction; “I wasn’t that much of a reader” yet comics voraciously. Shed for comics built by father (Jones attributes the collection/shed to George and Wendy pooling allowances — not the Wendy-vs-George attribution fight as a first-person shed quote). Prefers SF comics (Tommy Tomorrow, EC *Weird Science*) and especially Scrooge McDuck / Carl Barks; Jones: “there would be a bit of Uncle Scrooge’s continent-hopping adventuring in Indiana Jones’s DNA.” Norman Rockwell covers.
- Television 1954 (age ten); serials, especially Flash Gordon: “Thinking back on what I really enjoyed as a kid, it was those serials, that bizarre way of looking at things. I don’t think I ever grew out of it.” (p. 25, n.77) John Ford westerns on TV before he knew who Ford was. Rarely went to theaters (“to chase girls”). Loved Disneyland (family at opening day July 1955); Jones links Rocket to the Moon to later *Star Wars* Disney ride. *Daily Bugle* / *Weekly Bugle* with Melvin Cellini, 1955.
- Cars from about 14–20 (p. 30, n.96). Fiat Bianchina from father, souped up; Jones explicitly compares it to Han Solo’s *Millennium Falcon* (p. 31). Faros car club mascot/bait. Cruising Tenth. “I’ll never be back… And as a matter of fact, I’m going to be a millionaire before I’m thirty!” (p. 36, n.126) — typography locked as a direct exchange with George Sr. Also, recalled in 1997: “There are two things I know for sure. One is that I will end up doing something with cars, and two, that I will never be president of a company.” (p. 36, n.127)
- Crash, June 12, 1962, chapter ends mid-wreck at p. 37: Frank Ferreira’s Chevy Impala T-bones the Fiat on Sylvan Road; Bianchina wraps around a walnut tree. (Aftermath in ch. 2.)

## Ch. 2: Geeks and Nerds, 1962–1966 (pp. 38–67)

- Racing belt snapped; thrown clear; front-page *Modesto Bee* photo; hospital; “Mom, did I do something wrong?” Teachers give Ds so he can graduate.
- **Lucas, pp. 39–40 (n.5–6), typography locked:** “I realized more than anything else what a thin thread we hang on in life, and I really wanted to make something out of my life.” Jones links this to the age-six existential questions. “I was in an accident that, in theory, no one could survive. So it was like, ‘Well, I’m here, and every day now is an extra day. I’ve been given an extra day so I’ve got to make the most of it. And then the next day I began with *two* extra days.’… You can’t help in that situation but get into a mindset like that.… You’ve been given this gift and every single day is a gift. And I wanted to make the most of it.” “like almost starting a new life.” Racing days over (n.7–8). **Note:** this book does **not** use the “go straight” wording found in Rau 1999 and Norwich 2013.
- MJC, associate in arts June 9, 1964; anthropology; father blocks Art Center; almost San Francisco State; Plummer steers him to USC entrance exam; Haskell Wexler friendship in the pits — Wexler encouraged him but Lucas “got into USC on his own merits” (Jones rejects a strings-pulled version).
- USC cinema, fall 1964 as a junior. “the geeks and nerds of our era” (p. 46, n.32). “When I finally discovered film, I really fell madly in love with it, ate it and slept it 24 hours a day.” (p. 46, n.33) Dirty Dozen / “USC Mafia”: Huyck, Kleiser, Milius, Murch, Deschanel, Robbins. Clean Cut Cinema Club. “these little star troopers” drawings in the Portola Drive house (Kleiser, p. 56, n.61) — early doodles, Jones’s wording is Kleiser’s “star troopers.”
- Student films: *Look at LIFE* (animation assignment, Vorkapich influence); *Freiheit*; *Herbie* (with Paul Golding); senior *1:42.08* (cinéma vérité at Willow Springs; title from a lap time). “Whenever I broke the rules, I made a good film, so there wasn’t much the faculty could do about it.” (p. 62, n.79)
- **Kurosawa, pp. 58–59 (n.70–71) — design/mythology influence Jones ties to *Star Wars*:** At Milius’s encouragement, Lucas was “really blown away” by *The Seven Samurai.* Loved horizontal wipes, rat-a-tat editing, “the dusty, slightly worn look of his sets and costumes. Everything in a Kurosawa movie looked as if it had been used, repaired, then used again—a design aesthetic that Lucas would bring to *Star Wars.*” Also: Kurosawa “plunk[ed] audiences down in the middle of medieval or nineteenth-century Japan without the benefit of backstory. Give audiences a bit of time with the mythology, thought Kurosawa, and the foreign would feel familiar—another conceit Lucas would bring to *Star Wars.*” (The “thought Kurosawa” clause is Jones’s gloss.)
- Vorkapich *Life and Death of 9413: A Hollywood Extra*: number stamped on the forehead; “a device Lucas connected with strongly enough to borrow later for *THX 1138.*” (p. 53, Jones)
- Graduation August 6, 1966, BA in cinema (correct date). Planned avant-garde + documentary cameraman. Draft; opposed to Vietnam; failed physical, diabetes, 4-F, Orinase. Work with Saul Bass on *Grand Prix* titles and *Why Man Creates.* Autumn party: tells Matthew Robbins he wanted to “make a movie about someone escaping from the police… from an all-pervasive Big Brother, eye-in-the-sky” (Robbins, p. 66, n.93). Robbins and Murch write a two-page *Breakout* treatment, early October; trapdoor-in-the-desert ending (the happy ending denied in *Freiheit*). USIA grip job at the close.
