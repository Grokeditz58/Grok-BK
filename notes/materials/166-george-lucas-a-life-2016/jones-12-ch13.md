# Jones, *George Lucas: A Life* (2016): notes — ch. 13 “Letting Go,” 2005–2016 (pp. 436–472)

Typography locked from the publisher XHTML. Citations: Jones p. N; Jones Notes, ch. 13 n. Y. End of the narrative.

## Hobson; “that’s where that story ends”; widgets and TV (pp. 436–443) — KEY ending-of-saga line

Sixty-one, concentrating on being a father: “Children are the key to life, the key to joy, the key to happiness.” Lonely as a single parent; “I’d rather be married.” *60 Minutes* 2005: “What love life?” Coppola: he wants “Queen Noor or Grace Kelly.” Meets Mellody Hobson, 37, president of Ariel Investments, at a fund-raiser; quietly dating by early 2006. Huffington thought he was “a ladies man.” Seen at a 2006 White House reception (Spielberg a Kennedy Center honoree) and the 2007 Oscars (Oprah gapes). Hold hands constantly. Geffen: “I’ve never seen him this happy.” Hobson to Winfrey: no preconceived idea of a partner. Lucas: “I’m a 60s, West Coast, liberal, radical, artsy, dyed in the wool 99 percenter… And she’s an East Coast, Princeton grad, Wall Street fund manager.… we realized we have *everything* in common.” Jones glibly ties it to Anakin in *AOTC*: “Unconditional love is essential to a Jedi’s life.”

**KEY, p. 438 (n.11) — typography locked, the saga’s ending as he then defined it.** Would there be VII–IX? He throws cold water: “I get asked all the time, ‘What happens after *Return of the Jedi*?’ and there really is no answer for that. The movies were the story of Anakin Skywalker and Luke Skywalker, and when Luke saves the galaxy and redeems his father, that’s where that story ends.” Prequels alone $2.4 billion. Lucasfilm will be “a widget-driven” company: books, games, music, effects; except maybe another Indy, no more movies. “I have no intention of running a film company.… I’m trying to get back to that place [where] the company functions without me and *Star Wars.*” Virtually no debt. ILM now one shop among many; *ROTS* is the first SW film for which ILM is not Oscar-nominated. LucasArts “a mess” (Jim Ward): missed consoles, too rooted in SW; staff slashed. He does not want to be Pixar — “I’m not depending on these people to take the company into a megahit reality” — but the comparison “probably stung”; he founds Lucasfilm Animation (Big Rock + a sandcrawler-shaped building in Singapore).

TV cheaper than features: “for the same $200 million… fifty two-hour movies.” Prescient 1994 *WSJ* interview: home shopping (Amazon before it existed) and “party-line” multiplayer games. Two SW series announced: live-action *Star Wars: Underworld* (“*Deadwood* in space,” between III and IV) “languish[es] in development hell, and never [is] produced”; CG *Clone Wars* follow-up to Tartakovsky, who thinks going back to the well is “the easiest thing to do.” “It’s not a matter of trying to prove anything to anybody. I don’t have to.” Still promises college-style aesthetic films; Chau has “heard it all before” and is thinking “what life is like after *Star Wars,* and after George.”

Merch surges: Episode III goods >$3 billion; Hasbro +15%. Fans buy anything, including McQuarrie-concept droids and a stormtrooper whose removable helmet reveals Lucas’s face (also an X-wing pilot, “Jorg Sacul”—Lucas backward). Comics/novels officially the Expanded Universe; Lucasfilm hires staff to catalog it, “though it was Lucas, and Lucas alone, who would ultimately decide what was considered to be ‘canon.’” Jones: “in the grand scheme… *Star Wars* no longer belonged to Lucasfilm, or even to George Lucas; it belonged to everybody.”

30th anniversary 2007: Rose Parade grand marshal; USPS stamps and 400 R2 mailboxes; Celebration IV 35,000, Celebration Europe 30,000; Paris Reunion II. May 25 Goldwyn Theater: he jokes about tinkering again (“It’s a *JOKE,* people!”). Cooperates with *Robot Chicken* (lends his own voice) and *Family Guy* — a mood shift Jones traces to Hobson.

## *Crystal Skull*; *Clone Wars*; Blu-ray “Noooooooo!”; Iger breakfast (pp. 443–454)

Indy IV, twenty years, many writers, some friction. 1992 after the 1950s *Young Indy* episode: atomic age, MacGuffin “aliens. For God’s sake, it can’t miss.” Ford and Spielberg veto (Spielberg “had my fill of extraterrestrials”). Jeb Stuart draft May 1994, *Indiana Jones and the Saucermen from Mars* (Roswell). Jeff Boam next; almost pre-production 1996, then *Independence Day* kills Spielberg’s nerve. 2000 AFI Ford tribute revives it; Lucas’s only requirement: “I won’t do it without aliens.” Crystal skull from an unproduced *Young Indy* episode in Guatemala, then “what if it was an alien skull?” Frank Darabont, *Indiana Jones and the City of the Gods,* May 2003: Spielberg “ecstatic… the best script he’d read since *Raiders*”; Lucas “threw ice water on the whole thing.” Jeff Nathanson *Atomic Ants* also rejected. David Koepp, July 2006 *Destroyer of Worlds;* Lucas still wants flying saucers. Title fight: Lucas wants *Kingdom of the Crystal Skulls,* Spielberg a single Skull, “just get the word *kingdom* in there somewhere.” Spielberg wants as few digital effects as possible: “I want to do the real deal.” Eighty days NM/CT/Hawaii/LA. Spielberg: “I never liked the MacGuffin.… I will always defer to George as the storyteller of the Indy series.” Edits manually on film; Lucas: “a sentimental sort of guy.” Lucas to *Time:* “it’s basically *Phantom Menace* we’re making.” Opens May 22, 2008, mixed-to-lukewarm reviews, $151.1 million in five days. “I’m doing all my selling for two more weeks. Then I’m sold out.”

Esoteric films wait again. Coppola’s $5 million *Youth Without Youth* sinks; Lucas has “little patience with that kind of martyred artist mentality. ‘If you’re making a work of art or a film and nobody sees it, I don’t see where it does anybody any good.’” Back to “a sandbox I love to play in”: *The Clone Wars* with Dave Filoni (and eventually Katie Lucas). He finances $750k–$1 million an episode, shops only after 22 are made, no notes: “You either put it on the air or you don’t.” Most pass; a theatrical *Clone Wars* movie (Aug. 2008) gets Time Warner/Cartoon Network on board. The movie is a dud ($65 million off $8 million, but a steep second-week drop); *Times:* “The Empire Goes Slack.” Some don’t like rooting for Anakin, “knowing he would soon become Darth Vader.” Designs deliberately *Thunderbirds*-like. Series premiere Oct. 3, 2008: 4 million viewers, channel record; five more seasons. “Star Wars truly was always on now, and on everywhere.”

LucasArts back in the SW universe (*Battlefront,* *Lego Star Wars*). ILM on *Iron Man, Harry Potter, Pirates,* and Cameron’s *Avatar* (2010, then the most successful film of all time). Lucas to *The New Yorker:* “Creating a universe is daunting.… I did it with *Star Wars,* and now he’s trying to challenge that.” Ranch inn with rooms named for Ford, McCay, Gershwin; USC-red fire engines. Obama via Hobson: “a hero in the making.” *Daily Show* 2010: Stewart teases *TPM*; Lucas on fans’ love and anger: “It’s a work of fiction. It’s a metaphor. It’s not real.… And therefore you can either like it or not like it. Whatever.”

**2011 Blu-ray, p. 452 (n.53) — typography locked.** More Han-Greedo fiddling; Ewok blinks at last. “But the latest heresy, fans thought, was giving Vader an anguished ‘Noooooooo!’ as he lifted the Emperor off Luke and threw him over the railing to his death at the end of *Return of the Jedi.*” Social media: “George Lucas hates *Star Wars* fans and happens to find himself in the best position to hurt them.” Saga Blu-ray still sells nearly 3 million, $258 million.

May 2011, Disney World, Star Tours reopening. Breakfast at the closed Hollywood Brown Derby with Bob Iger: *Would you consider selling Lucasfilm to Disney?* He’s 67, restructuring so the company needs him less, but still has *Clone Wars* S4 and the live-action series and movie ideas. Qualified no: “I’m not ready to pursue that now. But when I am, I’d love to talk.” Iger, having bought Pixar and Marvel, will wait.

## *Red Tails*; Kennedy; the sale; wedding; museum; *TFA* (pp. 454–472) — KEY sale and treatments

Another trilogy in mind since late 1980s (George Hall / Tuskegee Airmen): “Like *Tucker,* it’s a story too good to be true.” Three parts: training in segregated Alabama; WWII dogfights (the place to start: “I see the movie less as a race picture than as an aerial action adventure”); return to civilian racism. Insists on a black writer and black director. After prequel delays: John Ridley script 2007, Anthony Hemingway (*The Wire*) directs — after the interview Hemingway says “May the Force be with you,” wants to “jump off the fifteenth floor,” then gets the job in the car and cries. *Red Tails* shoot April 2009, digital, hour-plus ILM dogfights, greenscreen cockpits. Lucas does re-shoots when Hemingway leaves for *Treme;* Aaron McGruder (*Boondocks,* who had mocked Jar Jar and Lucas) writes new dialogue; Jar Jar “never even came up.”

Hollywood won’t distribute: “It’s because it’s an all-black movie.… *Nooo, we don’t know how to market a movie like this.*” Fox distributes but pays none of the costs; Lucas puts up $58 million production + $35 million distribution/prints. “I’m making it for black teenagers.… They have a right to have it kind of Hollywoodized and aggrandized and made corny and wonderful just like anybody else does.” Brutal reviews; *Mother Jones* “vapid filmmaking.” He worries failure will scare studios off major black films; two years later *12 Years a Slave* (Ridley’s Oscar) wins Best Picture. Bitter: “My girlfriend is black, and I’ve learned a lot about racism, including the fact that it hasn’t gone away, especially in American business.” To the *Times:* “I’m retiring.… I’m moving away from the business, from the company, from all this kind of stuff.”

Lunch in New York with Kathleen Kennedy (in town for *Lincoln*). He wants her to co-chair, then head the company. She never blinks. June 1 she steps in. “I’ve spent my life building Lucasfilm—and as I shift my focus… I wanted to make sure it was in the hands of someone equipped to carry my vision into the future.” To make it “attractive” and function without him: *Star Wars.* **“While Lucas had insisted the story he had set out to tell was finished with *Return of the Jedi,* he admitted now he might have a few ideas for Episodes VII, VIII, and IX in a drawer in his office. ‘So I said [to Kennedy], “Well, let’s just do these movies.”’”** Michael Arndt on VII from Lucas’s “brief synopsis”; Kasdan to consult. Aug.: Celebration VI panel (no hint), then lunch with Hamill and Fisher in Orlando to persuade them back. Hamill shocked. Both agree; Ford agrees after “considerable discussion,” “largely on promises of a meaningful story arc that would close the book on Han Solo once and for all.”

Then he surprises Kennedy with selling to Disney — at first “down the road.” A year after the Brown Derby breakfast he is back with Iger. He wants to hand the company over *and* keep complete control of *Star Wars* (his obituary will start “*Star Wars* creator…”). Iger offers Marvel/Pixar-style limited autonomy but Disney, not Lucasfilm, would have final say. “For the deal to succeed, it was Lucas, not Disney, who would have to pay the highest price: he was going to have to relinquish control.” Compromise: Disney must take his story treatments as the basis for future films. He refuses to hand the treatments over until the deal is done: “Buying my stories is part of what the deal is.… I could’ve said, ‘Fine, well, I’ll just sell the company to somebody else.’” Iger signs an agreement limiting who at Disney may read them. Publicly Iger says they “had a lot of potential.” “It wouldn’t be the last time Lucas would squabble with Disney over his *Star Wars* story treatments.” Partnership “brought his own life full circle” (Disneyland opening day 1955; Star Tours).

**Oct. 30, 2012, cameras rolling: sale signed, $4.05 billion — half cash, half nearly 40 million Disney shares.** All of it to the sole stockholder. He becomes one of Disney’s biggest shareholders (2%); only the Steve Jobs Trust, which sold “my company” Pixar, owns more. Iger: “He was saying goodbye.” Statement: “It is now time for me to pass *Star Wars* on to a new generation of filmmakers.” Disney promises another SW movie by Christmas 2015. Kennedy puts VII into pre-production, J. J. Abrams to direct, script entirely to Kasdan. Lucas still sits in early story sessions “trying to explain the rules of the universe he’d created.” Then the company moves on without him.

Jan. 2013 proposal to Hobson; a surrogate already due in August. **Saturday, June 22, wedding at Skywalker Ranch;** Bill Bradley walks her down; Jett best man; Amanda and Katie bridesmaids; Bill Moyers officiates. Two hundred guests: Ford, Spielberg, Oprah, Katzenberg, De Niro, Jackson, Ron Howard, Quincy Jones; Van Morrison and Janelle Monáe. Chicago reception: Prince fronts a 22-piece band. Still jeans and Taco Bell/Sizzler; they go to regular movie theaters, not a closed screening room. **Aug. 9, 2013: Everest Hobson Lucas, his first biological child,** at sixty-nine.

By 2015 *Forbes* #94, ~$5 billion. Embarrassed: “most of the money I’ve made is in defense of trying to keep creative control of my movies.” 2010 Giving Pledge; majority to education. USC $175 million (2006), three chairs (2014), $10 million diversity fund with Hobson (2015); $25 million UChicago Lab Schools arts hall, required to be named for Gordon Parks; $500,000 to the Norman Rockwell Museum. Owns 50+ Rockwells; 2010 Smithsonian exhibit with Spielberg. Wants a Museum of Narrative Art (art, cels, comics, posters, props, most of his SW collection). Presidio Trust rejects the Crissy Field commissary, Feb. 2014. Hobson calls Rahm Emanuel; lakefront lot, $1/year rent; Ma Yansong design compared to an amoeba or Jabba. Friends of the Parks sues; May 2016 Hobson warns they may leave Chicago; late June he is “done fighting.” As of July 2016 (the book’s present tense) no decision among SF/Oakland island, or Garcetti’s LA (maybe “just a bit too close to Hollywood”).

Jan. 2015: *Strange Magic,* animated musical *Midsummer Night’s Dream* “for his daughters,” ELO title, “*American Graffiti* with fairies”; tanks in two weeks. He barely cares; dotes on Everest.

***The Force Awakens,* pp. 468–469 (n.92–97) — typography locked.** Title Nov. 2014; he is not excited. Private viewing with Kennedy in early December; she tells reporters “he really liked it” — “a tepid response.” His clarification: “I think the fans are going to love it. It’s very much the kind of movie they’ve been looking for.” “But it clearly wasn’t the kind of movie George Lucas had been looking for.” **Disney “had disregarded the story treatments he had given them in 2012”** and used Kasdan/Arndt/Abrams, “but none by Lucas.” To Charlie Rose, Christmas Day 2015: “They decided they didn’t want to use those stories, they decided they were gonna go do their own thing.” Apart from the 2012 sessions he wasn’t involved. Kennedy: “he can’t do that unless he’s running everything.” He compares tearing himself away to divorce, watching *TFA* “like attending the wedding of your grown child after you’ve been divorced. I gotta go to the wedding. My ex will be there, my new wife will be there…” Resents that Disney let Abrams make a movie “for the fans.” “They wanted to do a retro movie. I don’t like that.” To him that’s like studio bullying. Prequels, whatever people thought, were “faithful to his own vision.” “If I get in there, I’m just going to cause trouble.… I don’t have the control to do that anymore.… And so I said, ‘Okay, I will go my way, and I’ll let them go their way.’” “George Lucas had given up control. But that didn’t mean he was ever going to be happy about it.”

Coda, pp. 469–472: 72, retired, still at Park Way; still owns Skywalker Ranch (now 6,100 acres) and Skywalker Sound, for a fee to Disney or anyone. Jeans and flannel; off social media since *TPM* venom; not a recluse. Still hopes to build the museum and will put his hands on every detail. *TFA* past $2 billion; VIII slated 2017 (unnamed as of mid-2016), IX 2019; Disney announces *Rogue One,* plus Han Solo and Boba Fett movies. “The *Star Wars* universe, born of Lucas’s sweat and frustration and scrawled onto his remarkably low-tech notepads in pencil, seemed poised to exist in perpetuity.” Indy 5: Spielberg will bring Ford *and* Lucas as EP. “I would never make an Indiana Jones film without George Lucas. That’d be insane.” Take that, *Force Awakens.* Still hopes for small personal films; Coppola and Spielberg are “still waiting, George!” Legacy: wants to be remembered first as a great dad; then “one of the pioneers of digital cinema”; with a twinkle, “the maker of some of those esoteric twentieth-century science fiction movies.” Jones’s close: stationery-store son who said no to the family business and built an empire; Peter Jackson: “the Thomas Edison of the modern film industry.” Charlie Rose, Dec. 2015, first line of the obituary: **“I *tried,*” he said, laughing.**
