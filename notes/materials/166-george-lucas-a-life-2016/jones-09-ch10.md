# Jones, *George Lucas: A Life* (2016): notes — ch. 10 “Empty Flash,” 1983–1994 (pp. 325–381)

Typography locked from the publisher XHTML. Citations: Jones p. N; Jones Notes, ch. 10 n. Y. PART III RETURN 1983–2016 opens p. 323.

## Divorce, retirement, merchandising, games, THX (pp. 325–332)

Late June 1983, Elstree, *Temple of Doom.* A month after announcing the divorce, “a several-year tailspin.” (n.2) Success “a very devastating experience.” Second-unit on Club Obi Wan mass fistfight; looks miserable. Spielberg later: *Temple* “too dark, too subterranean, and much too horrific… There’s not an ounce of my own personal feeling in *Temple of Doom.*” (n.3) **Lucas’s on-screen metaphor, p. 326:** Mola Ram ripping out a still-beating heart “as literal a metaphor for Lucas’s own heartbreak as he would ever put on-screen.” “I was going through a divorce… and I was in a really bad mood.” Frank Marshall: “there’s a lot of darkness being worked out.” Principal photography wraps Aug. 26, 1983; first time in more than a decade Lucasfilm has no movies in production. He retires, as promised.

Divorce: Marcia entitled to half under California law; he is “not inclined to settle graciously.” Marcia: “He was very bitter and vindictive.” (n.6) Company + unfinished ranch worth $50–100 million; he will not give her “a single square inch of Skywalker Ranch” nor perpetual support. Pays her off with nearly all cash on hand, “about $50 million,” and keeps the ranch. Chips away at mutual friends so she is not invited to parties; “he wants to blackball me too.” (n.7) **Jones, n.8:** her Oscar will be acknowledged among SW’s seven, “but her contributions to Lucas’s films and to the company would be all but erased from most Lucasfilm-sanctioned histories.” Reconciliation with bankrupt Coppola: “Francis had lost everything financially. George had lost everything emotionally.”

*Jedi* merchandising (fifty-plus licensees, cream rinse to Kenner) funds the ranch. Officially he is only “pok[ing] my head into some doors.” ILM on *Trek III,* *Starman,* and *Temple.* Computer games: Peter Langston; Atari $1 million to “see what you can make.” No SW or Indy in the first games (stand-alone). Fractal tech on Atari 800; *Ballblazer* and *Rescue on Fractalus!* (working title *Rebel Rescue*). Lucas plays a demo, asks why there’s nothing to shoot at; when told no FIRE button: “Was that a game design, or a moral choice?” “Principle, uh huh.… It’s not going to work.” FIRE button added. Atari collapses on *E.T.*; Lucasfilm keeps the million, deals with Epyx, 1986. Mantra: “Stay small. Be the best. Don’t lose any money.” Hal Barwood: like a rich uncle who “paid the tuition… but never knew what your major was.”

THX: Tom Holman’s crossover at Sprocket C Building (“Tom Holman Crossover” / “Experiment” → THX, “a serendipitous nod” to *THX 1138*). *Temple* mixed for THX; other theaters would sound terrible. March 1, 1983 Theater Alignment Program; nearly a hundred theaters at first, later more than four thousand “THX Certified Cinemas”; the metallic logo and “deep note.” “Sound is half the experience in seeing a film.” (n.19) Now he controls even how they sound in the theater, and gets paid for it.

## Pixar, dating, SW future notes, ranch politics, *Temple* PG-13 (pp. 332–338) — KEY future-trilogy lines

Catmull’s group more interested in making movies with the Pixar computer than in Lucas’s digital editing system. EditDroid and SoundDroid ready to show by 1984. They plan a SIGGRAPH 1984 short.

Lucas on “fun and sun,” dance and guitar, racing again. Dec. 1983 starts dating Linda Ronstadt (introduced Oct. at Concord Pavilion). Odd pairing; five years; neither discusses it publicly. Drugstore clerk thinks he is her houseboy.

**KEY, p. 333 (n.25) — typography locked, future trilogies.** Reporters ask about more *Star Wars.* “In 1983 Lucas would say only that he was reading books on mythology and scrawling vague notes with themes and ideas for the next two trilogies. The first trilogy, he explained, would be melodramatic, showing the politics that put the Empire in place. The final three, he continued, would be all about ‘moral choices, and the wisdom needed to distinguish right from wrong.’ More than that, however, he couldn’t, or wouldn’t, say.”

Retirement means he is *more* involved day-to-day. Greber steps aside; Lucas is the boss without a formal title, lone shareholder. “Family businesses didn’t need stockholders.” Buys Grady Ranch and Big Rock Ranch; neighbors accuse him of “an industrial beachhead”; fight lasts nearly a decade; ILM stays at Kerner.

*Temple of Doom* (May 23, 1984): MPAA leans R for graphic violence. Lucas: “too gross to be PG… wasn’t quite gross enough to be an R.” MPAA relents to PG, then creates PG-13. $9 million opening day; $333 million off ~$29 million. Reviews: “dark,” *Post* “mean spirited and corrupt at its core.” Lucas: “we went darker than any of us really wanted to go” but wanted a different movie from *Raiders.* Thirty years later: “I like *Temple of Doom*… is it fun to think back about that stuff emotionally for us?… Nooooo.”

## SIGGRAPH, TV Ewoks, Norby, layoffs, selling Pixar (pp. 335–346)

1984 yearbook: Pixar computer to move to ILM; ILM doesn’t want it. After Genesis Effect, the group’s only other credit is the *Jedi* briefing hologram of Endor’s force field around the Death Star — “hardly the earth-shattering stuff Catmull knew they were capable of.” “We kept waiting for George to come around and ask us to be in the movies… but he never came.” (Smith, n.34) NAB 1984: EditDroid (uses a *Jedi* snippet; attendees just want to see *Star Wars*); SoundDroid used on *Amadeus.*

SIGGRAPH Minneapolis, *The Adventures of Andre and Wally B.*, two minutes, Lasseter designs. Lucas is there only because Ronstadt is touring *What’s New.* Audience roars. **Lucas “didn’t like it.”** “He couldn’t make the leap from the crudeness of it then to what it could be.… He took it literally for what it was, and assumed that’s all we could do.” (Smith, n.37) Wants them on the editing system, not cartoons. “The only person inside Lucasfilm who had any business making movies was George Lucas.”

Television: still smarting from the Holiday Special, he will “handpick his creative team.” *The Ewok Adventure* (ABC, Nov. 25, 1984): producer Tom Smith (ex-ILM manager); writer is his nanny Bob Carrau, “no experience,” Lucas plans to dictate the story; director John Korty, who “couldn’t figure out if I was doing George a favor.” Lucas memos for “more conflict… more fights… more explosions.” Sequel *The Battle for Endor* (Nov. 24, 1985), Ken and Jim Wheat; budget line for the “George Factor”; Joe Johnston rubber stamps GREAT / CBB / 86. Lucas: “I want this to be all about death.” Parents’ discretion; a little girl’s family killed by Marauders. Brief third film considered, then shelved. ABC Saturday cartoons *Droids* and *Ewoks* (Nelvana, same as the Holiday Special Boba Fett cartoon). Stewart Copeland on *Droids:* “On his desk he had rows of toys, and that was what the music was for. ‘This is the product, here are the toys.’” *Ewoks* two seasons, *Droids* one, beaten by *The Smurfs.*

Feb. 1985: Doug Norby (Harvard, ex-Itel CFO) as president; “Do what you have to do, and I’m just going to stay out of it.” Lucas scolds staff as uninvited guests who eat his food. ILM opened for anyone, ~$25 million flat rate. Kenner glut; 1985 SW toy sales $35 million, down from $135 million. Cash out in about five months. Marcia (now married to Rodrigues, new daughter) agrees to spread the divorce settlement over ten years; he pays it off in five. Norby broaches more SW films; Lucas “exhausted.” Layoffs; staff think The Dougs are running over him; Bob Doris: “I’m sure The Dougs weren’t doing anything more or less than George wanted them to do.” “Queen’s rules”: do not approach George, do not chat him up, keep to work if he starts a conversation. “This company has gotten too diversified.… We’re going to concentrate on movies.” Stained-glass knight in *Young Sherlock Holmes* — first fully CG character, credited to ILM, done on Pixar from a Lasseter design.

EditDroid/SoundDroid spun into The Droid Works with Convergence. Graphics group: “We can’t afford to fund this stuff.” Asking price $35 million including the team (medical imaging talks with Siemens and Philips go nowhere). Alan Kay introduces **Steve Jobs** (then fighting Apple’s board). Jobs sees the GUI as “apocalyptic,” offers $10–15 million, waits. Nov. 1985, after Philips/GM collapses, fire-sale: Jobs capitalizes Pixar with $10 million — $5 million to buy exclusive rights (Lucasfilm retains use), $5 million to run it; Catmull president, Smith VP. “Misreported that Jobs purchased Pixar from Lucasfilm outright”; purchased by Jobs *and* the forty employees. Signed Feb. 1986 in San Francisco, halfway. Jones: “the one that got away” later a $7 billion company, but “a shortsightedness born of Lucas’s own kind of intense focus.” “Once that [Pixar computer] was developed, then we didn’t need a company that manufactured computer hardware.… So we sold that off.” (n.55) “Entertainment is good ideas, not technology.… I’m not that enamored with new technology; I just acknowledge its existence.” (n.56)

## Ranch opens, helping friends, SDI suit, *Howard,* Disney, “more SW?” (pp. 346–360)

Summer 1985: first phase open. “Like a big home, a big fraternity.” No soundstages (guerrilla-location hangover). “Lucasfilm is not a production company… service organizations.” Main House 50,000 sq ft, library under the stained-glass dome (Rodrigues’s windows), office only from the upper balcony; Barks, Raymond, later Rockwells. Many furnishings selected by Marcia “in her last unhappy years… serving as Skywalker’s decorator.” Carriage House, Stable, Gate House, Brook House; Tech Building as a brick winery → Skywalker Sound. He never lives there (Parkhouse). Elaborate backstory: 1869 sea captain, later additions in period styles. “It’s my biggest movie.… I’ve always been a frustrated architect.” Man-made lake, 2,000 Oregon trees; “a special effect worthy of ILM,” private world-building like Disneyland. “I prefer making movies.”

1985 mostly rescuing friends, often uncredited: Wexler’s *Latino*; Murch fired from *Return to Oz,* Lucas flies to London, “Big Brother’s arm.” Critics “didn’t come down nearly as hard as they would have if my name had been on it.” Coppola/Schrader *Mishima* (three narrative styles, as in *More American Graffiti*); Lucasfilm/Zoetrope co-production, little seen, highly regarded.

**SDI / “Star Wars” trademark, pp. 350–351 (n.66–69).** Reagan’s missile defense dubbed “Star Wars” (Ted Kennedy first). Activist ads before the Reagan–Gorbachev summit; Lucas sues, doesn’t want the film associated with “a noxious subject, particularly nuclear holocaust.” Nov. 25 hearing; Judge Gerhard A. Gesell (Watergate judge) rules against him, “non-trade use.” 1990: sues Luther Campbell / 2 Live Crew over “Luke Skyywalker”; Campbell settles $300,000 and drops the name. Twenty-five years later Campbell still angry at *The Force Awakens* ads: “I want that motherfucker George Lucas to give me my money back.”

Late 1985 on more SW: “I don’t know.… I probably won’t do any more personally.” Rough plots in his head “not written down like stories.” Wants to produce others and make experimental films “whether they’re watched, whether they’re successful.”

1986: analysts think he needs money; Marvel winding down the SW comic. ILM on *Goonies, Explorers, Back to the Future, Out of Africa,* Oscar for Howard’s *Cocoon.* “The company is designed so I don’t have to make commercially profitable movies.” *Labyrinth* with Henson (script help: concentric-circle diagram, “not… very helpful,” Mirkin). Vader delivers a good-luck card on day one at Elstree. Bombs, $12 million off $25 million. “You win some, you lose some.”

*Howard the Duck:* he loved Gerber’s comic, pressed it on the Huycks after *Graffiti*; Universal requires Lucas as EP. ILM treats Howard as a duck costume (Ed Gale, 3'4"). Huycks marginalized; they leave for Hawaii opening week. Lucas’s name above the title; he thinks that made critics unfair. “If I had to do it over, I’d do it again.”

Disney: offered head of production in 1984, refuses; advises the Bass family, endorses Eisner. Feb. 1985: Star Tours announced (military flight sims, ILM’s longest VFX to date, 4.5 minutes, TIE dogfight and Death Star assault). Opens Jan. 1987, capacity sixty hours. “I’ve always felt that there’s only one first-class amusement park operation, and this is it.” *Captain EO* with Michael Jackson, 3-D; Lucas EP, Coppola directs (title from Eos). Ellenshaw: Lucas distracted by the ranch, “like having Michelangelo come by every other day for half an hour.” $20 million for seventeen minutes, “nearly twice what Lucas had spent on *Star Wars.*” Plays a decade, returns after Jackson’s death. Charles Solomon (*Times*): “empty flash” — the chapter title.

Tenth anniversary 1987: $1.4 billion box office, $2.6 billion merchandising, but the blush is off; merch “virtually petered out”; 1984 network premiere lost to *Lace.* First SW convention, Stouffer Concourse, May 1987, 9,000 fans. Lucas appears last night: “Oh, I thought there were only seven of you here.” Deflects: *Times* “kicking it around”; *LA Times* “it will take a lot of ruminating before I can come up with the energy to do three more”; *WSJ* “there will be more; it’s just a matter of when.”

Ronstadt: quietly engaged, “ring on the finger and all”; *Canciones de Mi Padre* recorded at Skywalker Sound; honeymoon-cottage idea bogs down in zoning. Relationship ends amicably 1988, so quietly it misses the gossip magazines.

## *Tucker,* *Willow,* Berne/colorization irony, Indy 3, education, *Young Indy,* Thalberg, *Jurassic,* EU, *Radioland* (pp. 360–381) — KEY

*Tucker: The Man and His Dream:* Coppola shy about asking; Lucas: “the best project Francis had ever been involved with.” Maverick vs the system “sounds like the business both Francis and I are in,” but Tucker’s downfall parallels Coppola. $24 million, Paramount via *Godfather III* leverage. Lucas takes over as producer; Schulman told “This film is not about cars. It’s about Francis.” Wants it “upbeat and entertaining, not intellectual and messagey.” Aug. 1988, positive reviews, tepid box office, a flop. Coppola to the *Times:* “not the movie I would have made at the height of my power.” Lucas’s swipe: that height “was five or six years ago.”

*Willow:* mulled since earliest SW drafts; handshake with Ron Howard 1985; Bob Dolman, seven drafts. Moses / LOTR / Oz; little people as heroes (flirted with, then abandoned, in SW); theme: “Mr. Average Man rises to the occasion.” Ladd at MGM half of $35 million. Shoot April 1987 NZ/Wales/Elstree. ILM goat-to-woman morph: “the first use of computer morphing (or ‘morfing,’ as Lucas would always spell it)”; Oscar nom, loses to itself for *Roger Rabbit.* Opens May 20, 1988, #1 then fades. *Time:* “A ‘Star Wars’ without star quality”; Ansen: “The Great Regurgitator.” He names a villain General Kael.

**March 3, 1988, Senate Judiciary, Berne Convention, with Spielberg (pp. 365, n.113–114) — typography locked, irony Jones flags.** Testimony on moral rights / colorization (Ted Turner). “Current and future technology will alter, mutilate and destroy” works. “Tomorrow more advanced technologies will be able to replace actors, or alter dialogue and change the movement of the actors’ lips to match.” Jones: “Ironically, in less than a decade Lucas would be doing much the same to his own work” in the Special Editions. Lucas: only the creator may alter the work. “People who alter or destroy works of art and our cultural heritage for profit, or as an exercise of power, are barbarians.”

**Spring 1988 to Champlin, p. 366:** “he had ideas and notes enough not for just three trilogies but for *four.* ‘I’m not sure how I could get to all of them in my lifetime.’” Age forty-four. Ronstadt ends; he adopts Katie, and in 1993 a son, Jett. “My main focus was really raising my kids.… I knew I couldn’t direct and raise these kids at the same time.” Coppola: “He raised those kids as a mother, really.” Milius: “his own private little duchy—which was producing what? A bunch of pap.” Also helps Reggio’s *Powaqqatsi* (tone poem) and Bluth/Spielberg *The Land Before Time* (wanted no dialogue like *Fantasia*’s *Rite of Spring,* lost that argument; they tone down scares).

*Last Crusade:* treatment Sept. 1984, *Indiana Jones and the Monkey King* (Fountain of Youth); Spielberg vetoed the Grail at first, then found the hook: “The search for the father is the search for the Holy Grail.” Chris Columbus script shelved; Menno Meyjes; Jeffrey Boam; Tom Stoppard punch-up as “Barry Watson.” Lucas wanted a “Laurence Olivier type, an Obi-Wan Kenobi type” father; Spielberg wants Connery (Bond as Indy’s father). Lucas tries to buy crumbling Elstree, stiff-armed; half saved, *Star Wars* stage carted to Shepperton, rest a Tesco. River Phoenix as young Indy; Lucas loses the “morally ambiguous soldier of fortune” argument for good — opening shows a historical activist. Opens May 24, 1989, $100 million in 19 days, $450 million worldwide. On more Indy: “Three, I think, is a pretty nice number.” On SW: “It’s still sitting on the shelf.… It’ll be a few years.”

Dorothy dies March 1989, age seventy-five, two months before *Last Crusade*; George Sr. has Alzheimer’s, dies December 1991. Jones: the film “seemed to almost subconsciously offer his father both farewells and forgiveness” (Indy, like Lucas, named after his father). Kate: “When my brother made it so big, that was a real thrill for him.”

Educational turn: *GTV* with Apple and National Geographic; Showmaker “junior video editing system.” 1991 George Lucas Educational Foundation (“a morass”). Later Edutopia and Lucas Education Research (2013). ***The Young Indiana Jones Chronicles:*** intended as interactive videodisc education. Rick McCallum; Lucas writes basic stories; directors (Roeg, Terry Jones) shoot constantly; he assembles on **EditDroid for the first time**; guerrilla, “the happiest, and most engaged, he’d been with a project since college.” Also a digital-filmmaking experiment: ten actors into a crowd of two hundred, digital backgrounds. ABC funds, ~$1.6 million/episode, promotes it as “a big action thing.” Pilot March 1992, “clunky”; first season cut after six; 24 episodes over two seasons; Ford as 50-year-old Indy in a day at his ranch — Lucas intrigued by 1950s Indy as a movie premise. Failed as TV, “unqualified success” for digital tricks that “might… even make it possible to do another *Star Wars.*”

March 30, 1992: Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award (still not an Academy member). Spielberg presents. Prepared remarks thank “thousands of talented men and women, robots and aliens.” Only competitive-adjacent Oscar he ever won; 2015: “I’m too popular for that. They don’t give Academy Awards for popular films.” Spielberg: he “changed the look and the sound of not only his movies, but everybody else’s movies.” Orchestra plays *Raiders,* not SW — “an indication of the trilogy’s faded reputation.” LucasArts bundles THX, ILM, Games. *The Abyss* pseudopod; *T2* morphing; 1992 all three VFX noms are ILM.

***Jurassic Park* test, p. 378 (n.142–144).** Spielberg skeptical of CGI dinosaurs; Muren: “Prove it.” “We did a test for Steven, and when we put [the dinosaurs] up on the screen, everyone had tears in their eyes,” recalled Lucas. “It was like one of those moments in history, like the invention of the lightbulb or the first telephone call. A major gap had been crossed and things were never going to be the same.” Spielberg: “watching our future unfold.” Muren: “I don’t know where the end of this stuff is.… how real is real?”

**Expanded Universe, pp. 378–379 (n.145) — typography locked.** 1988 Lou Aronica (Bantam) writes a fan/business letter: a shame if no one learns what happened after *Jedi.* Lucas takes a year, unenthusiastic: “No one is going to buy this.” Gives the license with conditions: “the books had to be post- *Jedi*—there could be no talk of prequels, since Lucas planned to carve that space out for himself—and there would be no killing off of the existing characters, nor could they bring back any who were already dead.” Timothy Zahn, *Heir to the Empire,* #1 *Times* 1991; two more 1992–93. Dark Horse *Dark Empire.* LucasArts SW games 1993, first time he lets programmers into that universe. Fan club magazine becomes *Star Wars Insider.* To the *WSJ:* prequels “the way I’d done the other *Star Wars* films… would be astronomically expensive, over $100 million.… So we have to sort of reinvent the wheel.”

*Radioland Murders:* conceived with *Graffiti,* dangled at Universal then; Huyck/Katz script, he was to direct, Kurtz produce; sat a decade. Dusted off as a cheap CGI-set test (~$10 million hoped). Mel Smith directs; partial sets. Oct. 1994 bomb, $1.3 million of $15 million. “It came out almost exactly or even better than I hoped.… *Radioland Murders* was inexpensive and we learned quite a bit.” **Close, pp. 380–381 (n.151–152):** “I think we may have reached a level here where we have actually created reality, which, of course, is what we were trying to do all along.” “So would there be more *Star Wars* then? This time, Lucas had a definitive answer: ‘I plan to start work on the screenplays soon.’”
