# Jones, *George Lucas: A Life* (2016): notes — ch. 7 “I Have a Bad Feeling About This,” 1976–1977 (pp. 215–245)

Typography locked from the publisher XHTML. Citations: Jones p. N; Jones Notes, ch. 7 n. Y.

## Killing Kenobi; Huycks polish; Tunisia and Elstree (pp. 215–221)

**KEY, pp. 215–217 (n.1–8).** Barely two weeks into filming, Lucas is ready to kill Guinness’s Obi-Wan. In the latest draft Kenobi survives the duel by retreating through a blast door, leaving Vader “with egg on his face” and reducing the Death Star assault to “galactic breaking and entering.” “This was dumb.… It totally diminished any impact the Death Star had.” **Marcia** points out that after the escape Kenobi “didn’t have much to do”; Lucas agrees “the character stood around with his thumb in his ear.” She suggests he die in the duel and then advise Luke as a spirit guide. Murch: “Marcia was very opinionated and had very good opinions… heated creative arguments… for the good.”

In Tunisia, early April 1976, Lucas waffles when telling Guinness; the actor is privately “irritated… A bit late for such decisions.” (n.4) At Elstree Lucas makes it final; Guinness: “I’m not doing this,” threatens to walk. Lunch: Lucas argues the spirit guide “was really a Castaneda *Tales of Power* thing.” (n.6) Guinness stays — “I simply trust the director” — paid well (“lovely bread”) plus two profit points, which made the “rubbish dialogue” easier. To the *Sunday Times*: “I must confess I’m pretty much lost as to what is required of me.” Lucas publicly says Guinness “took it all very well.” Lucas to Marcia from the set: “I forgot how impossible making movies really is.… I get so depressed.” (n.10)

Jan. 1976 to Elstree; ILM still building cameras instead of shooting the 360 effects. Fourth draft opening: “A long, long time ago, in a galaxy far, far away an incredible adventure took place…” Rest he finds “painful, atrocious.” Huycks to London: sand jargon, tighten Han/Leia banter. **They add an in-joke: Leia’s Death Star cell is 2187, “a nod to the Arthur Lipsett film Lucas loved.”** Two percentage points in lieu of payment. Freeborn (apes in *2001*) on Chewie and aliens; John Mollo costumes; John Barry sets, including a full-sized *Falcon.* Lucas hates London (food, “upside-down” switches, burglary). Ladd warns budget under $10 million; Lucas “insolently” pegs it at $9,999,999.

Tunisia: Djerba then Nefta salt flats; used-universe homestead and Jawa sandcrawler (Libyan inspectors check it isn’t a military vehicle). **Filming begins 6:30 a.m. Monday, March 22, 1976** — first rainfall in nearly a decade; robots fail; Daniels falling over; the twin-sunset shot takes three days. Guinness rolls in the dirt for used-universe wardrobe. End of two-week location: Lucas “desperately unhappy.”

Elstree from April 7: British unions, 5:30 quit. Crew baffled, call Chewie “the dog.” Ford: they thought it “ludicrous and stupid.” Lucas: “this really dopey movie.” Directing “people” is “hard”; “I just didn’t enjoy it.” **Name changes mid-shoot:** “Starkiller” becomes “Skywalker” because, with the Manson murders not yet distant, Lucas “worried that the name made Luke sound too much like a serial killer.” Logo still *The Star Wars*; Lucas has “all but settled on dropping the definite article, preferring simply *Star Wars.*” Full *Falcon* won’t fit Stage 3; only half built, rest a matte. Docking Bay 94 / Jabba confrontation “a sequence Lucas would tinker with, shelve, then revive nearly twenty years later.”

## Actors, cantina, loss of control, Jympson, illness (pp. 222–230)

Lucas’s two notes: “Faster, more intense,” or “Same thing, only better.” Ford: getting a performance “is a bit of an inconvenience.” Hamill: “if there were a way to make movies without actors, George would do it.” Yet he likes them. **Hamill, p. 223 (n.26–27):** “I’m really playing *him* in the movie.” Watches Lucas act out the runaway-Artoo scene and copies the smallness; Lucas loves it. Nickname “The Kid.” Fisher like his younger sister; gaffer’s tape on her breasts — “No breasts bounce in space, no jiggling in the Empire” (Kurtz had to tell her). Insecure, fears replacement by Jodie Foster. **Ford, p. 223 (n.29):** “You can type this shit, but you can’t say it.” Tweaks and ad-libs; “Stop me if I’m really bad.” Hamill’s Chewie cell-block 1138 ad-lib kept. Lucas: “that’s a matter of letting them have their way.”

Prowse stays in the Vader suit between takes; cast dubs him **“Darth Farmer”** for his West Country accent, “to be dubbed later by James Earl Jones.” Cushing/Tarkin “smelled like linen and lavender,” which makes Fisher’s “foul stench” line hard. April 13 cantina (envisioned since the Whills treatment): Freeborn hospitalized, most “key monsters” unfinished; later pickup almost a year later. Hamill: “it looks like *The Nutcracker Suite.*”

Crew of 900; chain of command. “I tried to be in complete control, to do everything myself, but it almost killed me.” (n.41) Editor John Jympson (Fox imposed a London editor over first choice Richard Chew) cuts too long, too wide. Lucas wants a **documentary / “newsreel sort of feeling”** (Barry): multiple cameras, actors wander into frame, no establishing wides. “The film has to make us believe it really existed, that we’ve really gone to another galaxy to shoot. The success of the imaginary, it’s to make something totally fabricated seem real… credible and totally fantastic at the same time.” (n.44) Ladd visits early May after “very negative feedback”; Lucas: “this is *not* what it’s going to look like.” Ladd’s real reaction: “*utter and complete panic.*”

Lucas buckles: cough, foot infection. Fisher: “combat shooting… no time… no budget.” Trash-compactor monster reduced to “a big wide brown turd.” Behind schedule; ILM front-projection unusable (Marcia: “cardboard cutouts, the matte lines were showing”). Mid-June: Jympson fired. June 23 Dykstra meeting (Lucas has lost his voice): switch to blue screen; 360 shots, “one shot a day.” Fox board worries include “whether Chewbacca should wear pants.” Ladd: finish in a week. Spielberg offers a second unit; Lucas refuses. Spielberg: “he did not want my fingerprints anywhere around *Star Wars.*” **Principal photography ends Friday, July 16, 1976,** twenty days late, ~$600,000 over (helped by a falling pound). Spielberg in Alabama is “amazed” by stills; Lucas “so depressed” about the lighting.

## Hypertension, ILM whip, Marcia’s Death Star cut, voices, screenings, release (pp. 230–245) — KEY voices / Force / edit

ILM has “spent a year and a million dollars and had one shot” (escape pod). After landing in San Francisco, chest pains; hospital: hypertension and exhaustion. **Lucas, p. 231 (n.65):** “That’s when I really confirmed to myself I was going to change, that I wasn’t going to make any more films. I wasn’t going to direct anymore.” But: “If I left anything for a day, it would fall apart.… Whenever there is a leak in the dam, I have to stick *my* finger in it.” (n.66)

Takes Mondays/Tuesdays at ILM; George Mather the rest of the week. Dykstra’s “quasi-hippy” shop; water tank, airplane-chute slide; Lucas silently steps around a forklift-dropped refrigerator. Fox pressures him to cut the Death Star assault; he wants “a casual almost *I’ve-seen-this-before* look… World War II—but wait a minute—that isn’t any kind of jet I’ve ever seen before.” (n.71) The compiled dogfight reel guides ILM.

**Marcia edits the Death Star battle (pp. 233–234, n.73).** Neither wanted her on the picture; she thought it “beneath George’s talents” and wanted more serious work (Coppola, Scorsese, Ritchie). Pregnancy still doesn’t happen; he needs an editor who knows his rhythms. She temps in WWII dogfight footage, then swaps ILM shots; uses every last inch. Eight weeks, 40,000 feet of pilot dialogue. Quick cutting vs *2001*’s Strauss-waltz ships; “*faster and more intense*” applied to the edit. She **reduces the number of on-screen X-wing pilots to keep the focus on Luke, and pares the trench run so Luke makes only one attempt, not the two specified in the script.** Chew recuts Jympson’s first hour, then the escape dogfight; De Palma suggests Paul Hirsch as third editor. “I’ve never worked on anything this complicated.” Lucas: “Nobody has.” (n.74)

First approved ILM shot Oct. 11, 1976, initialed *GL*: “a cannon going *boom, boom, boom.*” November rough cut: no finished effects, incomplete sound, **Vader still in Prowse’s working-class English accent,** no music. Over Marcia’s objections, Lucas cuts an early Tatooine scene of Luke talking rebellion with friends (too *Graffiti*; he doesn’t want Luke introduced that early). Jabba/Mulholland scene: no time/money to matte a stop-motion Jabba; **solution is additional cantina footage with Greedo** plus new monsters (Rick Baker, six weeks, La Brea, Jan.; includes the swing band). Lucas still unhappy for nearly twenty years until digital inserts. Fox pries out only $20,000.

Christmas: Marcia leaves for Scorsese’s *New York, New York* after the previous editor dies. Hirsch: “Marcia respected Marty above all directors, and didn’t believe in *Star Wars* very much. It was not her thing.”

**Voices, pp. 237–238 (n.82–87) — typography locked.** Every sound created, none earthly. Temporary score: Holst *The Planets,* Dvořák *New World.* Artoo: “intelligent but, emotionally, a five-year-old kid. Frightened but brave.” Lucas and Burtt coo/beep/whistle into a recorder, then synthesize. Threepio: Lucas wanted “a slick used-car salesman, with a whiff of the Bronx”; Daniels had played “a fussy English butler.” After auditions including Stan Freberg, Daniels’s voice “had the most character” and stays. **Vader:** “Vader’s signature breathing had been provided by Burtt recording himself huffing into a scuba mouthpiece.” Lucas wanted a commanding voice, “originally considered Orson Welles before deciding he might be too recognizable.” Approaches **James Earl Jones.** Jones: “He picked a voice that was born in Mississippi, raised in Michigan, and was a stutterer.” Pitch perfect; Jones asks that his name be kept out of the credits, “insisting that he was ‘just special effects.’”

Jan. 1977 Williams sees the cut, writes Jan.–Feb. Fox executives: Gareth Wigan weeps. **Late February Parkhouse friends screening (pp. 238–239, n.91–98):** Spielberg, De Palma, Huycks, Milius, Barwood, Robbins. “The reaction was not a good one.” Marcia bursts into tears, certain it’s a disaster. Huyck: opening crawl “jiggly, and it went on forever.” **De Palma: “What’s all the Force shit? Where’s the blood when they shoot people?”** Rails “like a crazed dog.” Lucas: “You should talk. None of your films have made a dime.” De Palma then helps rewrite/re-shoot the crawl. Spielberg loved it; privately tells Ladd a $50–60 million hit. “Wow, were we wrong!”

March 5, Anvil Studios, Denham: Williams conducts the LSO; Lucas “visibly moved.” “A lot of the emotional content is carried through the music as much as through the scenes themselves.” (n.100) Lucas even acts out the *Falcon* chess holograms. **Lucas, p. 240 (n.102):** “I had to cut corners like crazy.… The film is about 25 percent of what I wanted it to be.” Jones flags this as part of why he would tinker for the next four decades.

Sales team ecstatic, but Fox moves release from summer to **May 25** (Lucas later claims he wanted kids to spread the word at school). Fewer than forty theaters. Lucas tells the *Times* he wants to retire to experimental films; same empty vow to *Ecran.*

**Charles Lippincott,** VP of advertising/publicity/promotion/merchandising: novelization by Alan Dean Foster (Ballantine, Judy-Lynn del Rey; Nov. 1976, first 125,000 sold out by Feb.). Marvel six-issue adaptation via Ed Summer to Roy Thomas and Howard Chaykin; Stan Lee won’t pay until 100,000 copies, but two issues must precede May 25. Comic-Con July 1976 (early movie session); Worldcon Kansas City: full-sized Vader, Threepio, Artoo, McQuarrie art, Kurtz and Hamill slideshow. Mainstream articles get almost everything wrong: “Stormtroopers were referred to as robots. Vader was a Black Knight. Chewbacca was an apeman.”

**Northpoint Theater premiere, Sunday, May 1, 1977** — same house as the *Graffiti* debut. Marcia’s test: if the audience doesn’t cheer when the *Falcon* saves Luke “when he’s being chased by Vader,” it doesn’t work. Star Destroyer rumble; McQuarrie remembers “hollering and cheering”; *Falcon* rescue explodes the house. George Sr. shakes hands: “Thank you very much for helping out George!” Lucas to Hirsch: “Well, I guess we won’t have to change anything after all.” A later Metro exec screening is cooler; Ladd: “You should have seen the Northpoint!”

Lucas fights for Dolby stereo; mixes stereo in April, mono all night at Goldwyn (Marcia editing *New York, New York* there by day). **Wednesday, May 25:** bleary from an all-nighter, late lunch with Marcia at the Hamburger Hamlet on Hollywood Boulevard, across from Grauman’s. A mob, police, lines around the block. He thinks someone must be premiering a movie. The marquee: STAR WARS.
