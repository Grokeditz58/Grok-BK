## Photo-insert captions (18 plates; no printed page anchors)

Eighteen plates (`Art_Insert1.jpg`–`Art_Insert18.jpg`), plus the author photo. Captions, typography locked:

1. High-school auto accident on the front page of the local paper; thrown free just before impact; surviving it changed his outlook and sent him toward anthropology at Modesto Junior College and then film at USC. (Courtesy of the *Modesto Bee*)
2. Lucas with Robert Duvall on the *THX 1138* set. Jones: artfully filmed, interesting ideas; fascinated critics, bewildered audiences; failure ended Coppola’s Zoetrope dream and nearly ended Lucas’s career.
3. *American Graffiti*: “rock-and-roll ode” to cruising Tenth Street. Lucas raged when Universal cut four minutes. “The studios, he growled, were filled with ‘sleazy, unscrupulous people.’”
4. Gary Kurtz (left), producer on *Graffiti, Star Wars,* and *Empire.* “Lucas grew increasingly disenchanted with Kurtz over cost overruns on *Empire,* and their relationship fractured for good over a disagreement on the story for *Return of the Jedi.*”
5. **Priority.** On the *Star Wars* set with Peter Cushing, Carrie Fisher, and Dave Prowse, “sans his Darth Vader helmet.” Lucas: “Making a movie is a terribly painful experience.” Fox refused finishing money; “Lucas never forgot it, and vowed he wouldn’t go begging for money for one of his own movies again.”
6. Lucas tapped John Dykstra to create the effects and the workshop that became ILM; accused Dykstra of being more interested in the new technology than in producing the actual effects. Dykstra’s effects won one of the film’s seven Oscars.
7. **Priority.** Marcia Lucas, “a talented and highly respected film editor, who many considered to be Lucas’s secret weapon,” edited many battle sequences. “If the audience doesn’t cheer when Han Solo comes in at the last second in the *Millennium Falcon* to help Luke when he’s being chased by Vader,” she told Lucas, “the picture doesn’t work.” Marcia won an Academy Award for her editing on *Star Wars*. “Lucas, to this day, has yet to win an Oscar.”
8. May 25, 1977: opened “in fewer than forty theaters” and sold out nearly every show (caption agrees with ch. 7, not with ch. 8’s “thirty-two”). Lucas: “I had no idea of what was going to happen. I mean, I had *no idea*.”
9. Coppola with a beardless Lucas at the 1986 *Captain EO* premiere. Spielberg: “I think Francis always looked at George as sort of his upstart assistant who had an opinion. An assistant with an opinion, nothing more dangerous than that, right?”
10. *Willow* (1988) with Ron Howard and Warwick Davis. ILM “morphing” (the chapter text preserves Lucas’s spelling “morfing”). One detractor: “The Great Regurgitator.”
11. Lucas and Spielberg on the *Last Crusade* set. After the “deliberate darkness” of *Temple of Doom*—“a chaotic period in both their lives,” said Kasdan—*Last Crusade* returned to *Raiders*-style fun.
12. They met at a student film festival in 1968. Spielberg: “valiant colleague, and great and loyal friend.” Lucas: “my partner, my pal, my inspiration, my challenger.”
13. Rick McCallum, left, ushering in digital filmmaking on *Young Indiana Jones,* then the Special Editions and the prequels, “quietly permitting Lucas to indulge his love of digital technology, often to the detriment of storytelling.”
14. Sixty-one-year-old Lucas met thirty-seven-year-old Mellody Hobson in 2005. Lucas: “If you’re more beautiful than I am and smarter than I am and you’ll put up with me, that’s all it takes.” Married 2013 at Skywalker Ranch.
15. Jett, Katie, and Amanda. “There was a point there where I lived only for movies,” Lucas said in 2005, “[but] children are the key to life.”
16. **Priority.** Lucas with Bob Iger. “Lucas believed the deal required Disney to use his treatments for Episodes VII through IX, and was disappointed when Episode VII was made largely without his input. ‘I will go my way,’ Lucas finally conceded, ‘and I’ll let them go their way.’” Caption figure is “$4 billion”; the body text gives $4.05 billion.
17. Kathleen Kennedy, handpicked successor; *The Force Awakens* “would earn more than $2 billion… and successfully relaunch the franchise.”
18. **Priority.** Lucas “at peace—mostly—at Skywalker Ranch.” “I hope I’ll be remembered as one of the pioneers of digital cinema,” he said. “[Though] they might remember me as the maker of some of those esoteric twentieth-century science fiction movies.”

## Acknowledgments (pp. 475–477)

Dated *Damascus, Maryland, July 2016.* Jones is explicit that this is an **unauthorized** biography: “The unauthorized nature of this project made it very difficult to get family, friends, coworkers, and collaborators to sit for an interview.” On-the-record interviews: **Randal Kleiser, John Korty, Gary Kurtz, Paul Golding, and Larry Mirkin.** Justin Bozung and Mani Perezcarro helped coordinate; Ken Plume “was very helpful.” Lucas himself did not sit for this book; Lucas quotations in the text are almost entirely from published interviews, commentaries, documentaries, and earlier books, as the endnotes document.

Thanks to LOC, Montgomery County and Stanislaus County libraries, Margaret Herrick, Warner and Universal, USC; Barbara Alexander (Modesto) and Claude Zachary (USC film department); Leo Adam Biga (*American Graffiti* research). Little, Brown: editor John Parsley (debates about AT-ATs), Reagan Arthur, Malin von Euler-Hogan, Karen Landry, copyeditor Amanda Heller (“terms such as *Kashyyyk* and *sarlacc*”). Agent Jonathan Lyons. Family, including his brother Cris, “who always let me be Han when we played with our Kenner *Star Wars* figures.” Wife Barb—“the *real* Dr. Jones”—and daughter Madi; “she did it all without a lightsaber.”

**Priority-line sources** (speaker + Jones Notes cite). Typography of the body quotations was locked from the XHTML in the chapter files; these are the notes Jones attaches, or the absence of a note.

- **Childhood God / reality questions, and Jones’s Force gloss** (pp. 19–20). Lucas: you reach a point and suddenly you say, “Wait a second—What is the world? What are we? What am I? How do I function in this, and what’s going on here?” Jones, not Lucas, then writes that they were questions Lucas would, “with the creation of the Force in *Star Wars,* attempt to answer in his films.” Lucas: “I have strong feelings about God and the nature of life, but I’m not devoted to one particular faith.” Religion later “a melding of Methodist and Buddhist.” (“It’s Marin County,” he said in 2002. “We’re *all* Buddhists up here.”) Notes: ch. 1 n.49 and n.51 = Pollock, *Skywalking,* 20; n.50 and n.52 = Arnold, *Once Upon a Galaxy,* 189–90; n.53 (the 2002 Buddhist line) = Jess Cagle, “Director: So, What’s the Deal with Leia’s Hair?” *Time,* April 29, 2002.
