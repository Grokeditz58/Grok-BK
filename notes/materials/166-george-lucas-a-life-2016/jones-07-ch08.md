# Jones, *George Lucas: A Life* (2016): notes — ch. 8 “Striking Back,” 1977–1979 (pp. 246–277)

Typography locked from the publisher XHTML. Citations: Jones p. N; Jones Notes, ch. 8 n. Y.

## Opening avalanche, reviews, race and meaning, optimism (pp. 246–251)

Lucas at Goldwyn still finishing the mono mix when Ladd calls: selling out in all thirty-two theaters (text earlier said thirty-seven — flag). Lucas: “some kind of aberration”; sci-fi “tends to perform strongly in [its] opening days before dropping off.” “I expect it all to fall apart next week.” (n.1–2) It doesn’t. Avco, Uptown (pot smoke, beer cans), SF bathrooms locked, Grauman’s barely swept between shows; Hefner and Playboy Bunnies sit through several. Kurtz hears opening-day radio callers who have already seen it several times. Repeat viewing becomes the experience; one theater estimates 80 percent repeats by fall; worn prints replaced at $700.

Reviews: *Variety* “magnificent”; Champlin “there is no corporate substitute for the creative passion of the individual filmmaker”; *Time* cover “The Year’s Best Movie”; Cocks “a subliminal history of the movies.” Arnold (*Post*) on Flash/Buck synthesis. Canby: “fun and funny” but no real depth. Siskel grouses that Vader looks like “a black vinyl-coated frog,” then admits it’s “a fun picture.” Dissenters: UPI “desperate boredom”; Boyum (*WSJ*) “puerile materials” (Jones notes this echoes Marcia’s plea for artier films); Hamill in the *Post*: “a Big Dumb Flick,” at least “a good one.”

**Race critiques, pp. 249–250 (n.21–25).** Walter Bremond, *New Journal and Guide,* “Star Wars and Blacks”: “one of the most racist movies ever produced. The force of evil… is dressed in all black and has the voice of a black man.… That character reinforces the old stereotype that black is evil.” Another journalist: the droids “acted, and were treated, like slaves,” sold to a young white man they called “Master.” Raymond St. Jacques: “black people… shall not exist in the galactic space empires of the future.” Lucas “flummoxed—and slightly hurt… especially since he had very nearly cast a black actor as Han Solo.” Lippincott: “We have barely dug into this galaxy.” Fans write the *Times* about species living in harmony.

Allegory: one columnist reads Obi-Wan as a savior whose disciples grow more powerful after his death. Kurtz cautions against any single theology: “almost anyone can see certain elements that fit into their lives.” Ellen Goodman closer to Lucas’s machine theme (since *1:42.08*): “We want a computer age with room for feelings.” “Still others picked up on Lucas’s Vietnam allegory, though Lucas, wary of politics, publicly disavowed any and all sociopolitical theories.”

**Lucas on what the film is for, p. 251 (n.29):** “It’s fun—that’s the word for this movie. It’s for young people.… Young people don’t have a fantasy life any more.… All they’ve got is Kojak and Dirty Harry.” No moral ambiguity; good guys and bad guys clear. *Time:* the happy ending is “a rarity these days.” Siskel: “Give us old-fashioned, escapist movies with upbeat endings.”

## Hawaii, *Raiders,* self-financing *Empire,* merchandising, ILM north (pp. 251–262)

Mono mix done early Thursday, May 26; midweek opening record. Lucas leaves for Hawaii: “I was done.” Cronkite and Carson joke about the lines; Ladd keeps calling numbers. Weekend: Spielberg and Amy Irving join them; Lucas “in a state of euphoria.” On the beach building a sandcastle: “What do you want to do next?” Spielberg wants Bond; UA won’t give it. Lucas: “a film that was even better than a James Bond” — Indiana Smith, tried with Philip Kaufman in 1974 after *The White Dawn;* Kaufman (via his Chicago dentist) supplied the Ark of the Covenant; Kaufman left for *Josey Wales.* Title *Raiders of the Lost Ark.* Fedora, bullwhip, *Treasure of the Sierra Madre,* *Don Winslow.* Spielberg: “I want to direct it.” Lucas: “It’s yours.” Directing SW was “exhausting and not very much fun”; producing “really *would* give him a great deal of control.”

Publicly he talks of a year off and starting a family (Marcia to *People*). Privately the contract clock: if the sequel isn’t under way within two years, rights revert to Fox. He briefly considers selling it all and taking his percentage. Seeing *Jaws 2* proceed without Spielberg, he blanches. “The truth of it is, I got captivated by the thing.… It’s in me now.” (n.42) Family waits again. “I expected more out of *Star Wars* than was humanly possible.… I had this dream, and it’s only a shadow of [that] dream.” (n.43)

Galled that Fox pockets 60 percent “for doing… absolutely nothing.” To *Rolling Stone:* studio execs are “rather sleazy, unscrupulous people.” Forty years later he still bristles: “What are you doing for your fifty percent?” Ladd: “I provide the money.” Lucas: “You go to a bank with a letter of credit.” Huyck: “Why don’t we borrow the money ourselves?” **Lucas will finance *Empire* himself,** *Star Wars* profits as collateral; Fox distributes, no meddling, final cut, merchandising and TV rights. Studio share decreases to 22.5 percent, Lucasfilm 77.5. Spielberg: “you are in the George Lucas business, and George is going to call every shot.”

Points shared with Huycks, Williams, Burtt, the law firm, Roos, the actors (Hamill a quarter point). Spielberg’s 2.5% *Star Wars* for 2.5% *Close Encounters* bet — more than $40 million over four decades. Chapter II Company set up. ILM rebooted in northern California (Zoetrope idea: own facilities). **Dykstra “wasn’t invited.”** No points or gifts; Lucas saw him as work for hire. Dykstra forms his own company, rents ILM equipment for *Battlestar Galactica*; Lucas already accuses Glen Larson of ripping off SW; Dykstra: “Maybe I feel guilty about that.” Dennis Muren and Brian Johnson (*Space: 1999*) lead; warehouse on Kerner Boulevard, San Rafael, Kerner Optical signs left up (models had been stolen in LA; fans pawing trash). Mantra for the sequel: *cheaper and quicker.*

Merchandising finances the sequel “one action figure at a time.” *Ecran* 1977: “I have a particular affection for games and toys.… All of this was part of the film, the intention of launching toys in supermarkets, creating books and stuff.” (n.55) Mattel and Ideal pass; Bernard Loomis at Kenner sees a “toyetic” quality, closes May at the Century Plaza, “galaxywide,” on Lucas’s condition Kenner not also do *Close Encounters.* Spielberg: “Well, it’s not *Star Wars.*” Christmas 1977: only puzzles/games ready; Early Bird Certificate Package ($14 empty-ish box) for the first four figures (Luke, Leia, Artoo, Chewie). Competitors mock it; Kenner sells 40 million figures in 1978. Image Factory $100,000 for posters/buttons; **Vader lightsaber poster outsells Farrah Fawcett,** ~$750,000 back. Lucas: maybe “a type of *Davy Crockett* phenomenon.” Lunch boxes, Coke cups, Burger Chef posters, 300,000 souvenir programs, Williams LP 650,000 by mid-July, Meco disco #1, Ken Films eight-minute Super 8 while still in theaters, Marvel 107 issues, “Lucas would finally have his R2-D2 cookie jar.”

Black Falcon (from *Blackhawk* serials) oversees merchandising: “I didn’t want the market flooded with junk” (turns down junk jewelry and toilet-seat covers). Fox auto-gets half until July 1, 1978, then Black Falcon 80 / Fox 20. Aug.: Threepio, Artoo, and Vader footprints at Grauman’s. LA Phil *Star Wars* concert. Milius: “It’s easy to be cynical. It’s hard to be corny.” Scorsese after *New York, New York* tanks: “*Star Wars* was in.… We were finished.” Friedkin (*Sorcerer* booted from Grauman’s): “*Star Wars* swept all the chips off the table.… like when McDonald’s got a foothold.” Lucas, later: “Popcorn pictures have always ruled.… That’s not my fault. I just understand what people liked to see.” (n.67)

International records; Brussels briefly prohibits it for children “largely because of the lopped-off arm,” then reclassifies “for all.” Shown at the U.S. embassy in Moscow; some hiss at Alderaan’s destruction: “What a wretched trick!” Dec. 1977: officially highest-grossing of all time, $120 million revenues vs *Jaws* $115 million. Spielberg’s *Variety* ad: Artoo snagging a shark.

## *Empire* treatment — KEY Vader/Yoda/twin; Brackett; “I am your father”; company; Ranch (pp. 262–277)

Again: “about 25% of what I wanted it to be.… I think the sequels will be much, much better.” (n.72) June 1977 they mention Foster’s *Splinter of the Mind’s Eye* as a possible “sequel novel.” Ladd tells the *WSJ* Lucas has plenty of material; Jones: he didn’t.

**Nine-page treatment titled *The Empire Strikes Back* (pp. 263–264, n.75–77) — typography locked.** More sophisticated because Lucas “had recently read Joseph Campbell’s *The Hero with a Thousand Faces*” and wanted to trace Luke’s journey more deliberately. Note: “Intimate that a rewarding, good life is within one’s reach despite adversity, but only if one does not shy away from the hazardous struggles without which one can never achieve true identity.” Structure already there: a gambler from Han’s past invites Han, Leia, and Chewie to dinner, “and they come into the room and there is Vader.” Luke studies the Force under an old Jedi master, long fight with Vader, hanging off a city in the sky. Luke-Leia-Han triangle; Threepio destroyed. **“He had also decided Luke had a twin sister, though at this early stage, it wasn’t clear whether that twin was Leia. Perhaps… she was on the other side of the galaxy and Luke could go look for her.”**

Han’s fate open (Ford not committed to three films); treatment ends with Han leaving to find a financier, “probably his stepfather,” to bankroll the rebellion. **Vader still a question mark (KEY):** “Lucas was still trying to decide who or what Vader really was. The real conflict, as Lucas currently saw it, wasn’t whether Vader would be redeemed by returning to the good side of the Force, but whether Vader would persuade Luke to give in to the Dark Side.” Most ambitious idea: “a tiny wizened Jedi master named Minch Yoda, who Lucas thought might be ‘the one who trained Ben [Kenobi].’”

Leigh Brackett hired late Nov. 1977 ($50,000 flat): pulps + *The Big Sleep,* *Hatari!,* *Rio Lobo.* Several days of brainstorming; she takes the notes Dec. 2. McQuarrie already painting, “including one of a castle for Vader on a volcano planet.” Charlie Weber hired as first CEO via a newspaper ad. Egg Company on Lankershim (former egg company, across from Universal). Personal share ~$12 million after taxes; still jeans and a Camaro, a Ferrari in the garage unused. Tong: merchandising must sustain the company until *Empire* is released. *Raiders* deal memo approved there; $6 million, Kasdan via Spielberg (*Continental Divide*). Lucas as EP: “Okay, Steven, it’s your movie.” Briefly considers friends directing SW as a competitive series, himself doing the last one “twice as good as everybody else.” (n.82)

Kurtz finds Irvin Kershner (ex-USC instructor, *Eyes of Laura Mars,* loved *6-18-67*). Zen Buddhist, good with character. Lucas promises not to hover; *People:* “I’ll approve the rough cut and I’ll say, ‘you’re doing great.’” “it was a vow Lucas wasn’t equipped to keep.”

Brackett draft Feb. 21: Lucas disappointed; it “just *felt* wrong.” Vader calls someone an “incompetent idiot”; characters quarrel; Han tells Luke off. Lucas: SW is “in me now.” Scrawls *NO* over a solemn vow “to the cause of freedom and justice.” Brackett hospitalized; dies of cancer March 18, age sixty-two. Mexico vacation: Lucas shuts himself in the hotel to write. First draft in three weeks (“almost enjoyable”). To Ladd, April: “Here’s a rough idea of the film—May the Force be with us!”

**Lucas’s handwritten draft, p. 268 — KEY typography locked:** Remodels Brackett in his own image; scraps Vader’s volcano-castle scenes; “getting a better handle on Vader.” “And here of his handwritten script, as Luke battled Vader, Lucas had inserted a key line of dialogue for the villain that he was certain would define the character of Vader even as it shocked the audience to its core: *‘I am your father.’*” He removes that page from every copy “for fear it would be leaked.” (Jones typography: “And here of his handwritten script” may be a missing word — check XHTML at seal; possibly “And here [in?] his handwritten script.”)

Boba Fett introduced, modeled on Eastwood’s Man with No Name; needed quickly for a CBS holiday special and for Kenner to market an *Empire* figure early. McQuarrie and Joe Johnston design; “an ideal action figure.” “Lucas had no idea that he had almost too casually just created an icon; he would give him only four short lines of dialogue.”

Kasdan delivers *Raiders* first draft; Lucas offers him *Empire* before reading it: “Well, if I hate *Raiders,* I’ll renege [on] this offer tomorrow.” “I was desperate. I didn’t have anybody else.” Ford still won’t commit to a third film. Budget a little over $15 million, more than Lucas’s SW profits; “I’m very overextended right now.” Merchandising has to pay.

50th Oscars, April 3, 1978: ten nominations. Sweeps tech (art, costumes, Williams, ILM/Dykstra VFX, editing for Marcia/Hirsch/Chew). Lucas shut out for writing, directing; Best Picture to *Annie Hall.* Coppola the next morning: “Good. Now George will be back with another picture.… He likes to win too much.” SFWA special award, but Ben Bova: “Those of us who work in the science fiction field professionally look for something more than Saturday afternoon shoot-em-ups. I had expected more of Lucas.” Lucas: “science fiction still has a tendency to make itself so pious and serious, which is what I tried to knock out.”

Milius to the *Times* on taking over: “George and I will take over everything in the valley.” Cracks: Coppola gets no SW points — “Why should he? He had no connection to the movie.” Milius asked to return his SW point after *Big Wednesday* tanks. An ILMer: “You must agree with George… if you don’t… George doesn’t like you.” Jeff Berg dismissed: “I just didn’t need him anymore.” “I had to build an empire simply to make the movies the way I wanted to make them.” Brief reunion offer to Coppola (buy Mann or even Fox) collapses; Lucas won’t shoulder Coppola-style debt. “I prefer playing with camera film over becoming the entrepreneur.… I refuse the power to command other people.” Wants a ranch house “in the middle of nowhere” with “the most advanced technology, where we could sit, see the trees and think about things.” Finds Bulltail Ranch, ~1,700 acres on Lucas Valley Road (named for settler John Lucas, 1882). “Okay… I’m buying.” *Skywalker Ranch.*

Kasdan/Lucas/Kersh pages: “he never tells you what he likes, just what he doesn’t. Silence was its own reward.” Yoda: “Let’s make him small.… Maybe he’s slightly froglike.” Monkey-in-a-costume test fails. Henson was across the street at Elstree during SW; “perhaps Yoda would work as a Muppet.… It would have the personality of a Muppet, only it would be realistic.” Black Falcon loans to Chapter II, ILM, and Burtt’s new Sprocket Systems. ILM ~$400,000/month; Chapter II nearly $2 million/month.

**Holiday Special (pp. 275–276).** Lucas engages early with Bruce Vilanch: Kashyyyk, Life Day, Wookiee language without subtitles. Vilanch: “The only sound they make is like fat people having an orgasm.” Lucas glares. Then he cedes it to Hemion and Smith, watches video. Korman, Jefferson Starship, miserable leads; Fisher sings Life Day to the Williams theme. **Only spark: an eleven-minute Nelvana animated segment, “which would give audiences their first look at Boba Fett.”** He takes his name off. Airs Nov. 17, 1978. Hemion: “the worst piece of crap I’ve ever done”; Lucas: a “travesty.” “He had given up control, and *Star Wars* had ended up looking stupid. It was a mistake he was determined not to make again.”

By December *Empire* budget estimated at $21 million; Lucas loans the company $20 million as bank collateral. $3.5 million Elstree sets; $2 million for a permanent soundstage big enough for a full-sized *Falcon.* “This was the perfect opportunity to become independent of the Hollywood system.” **Close, p. 277 (n.115):** showing Kershner the ranch drawings: “This… is why we’re making the second [film]. If it works, I’ll build this. If it doesn’t work, it’s over.”
