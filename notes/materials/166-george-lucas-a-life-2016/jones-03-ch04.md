# Jones, *George Lucas: A Life* (2016): notes — ch. 4 “Radicals and Hippies,” 1967–1971 (pp. 86–126)

Typography locked from the publisher XHTML. Citations: Jones p. N; endnotes as Jones Notes, ch. 4 n. Y.

## Coppola, *Finian’s Rainbow,* and the *THX* feature (pp. 86–91)

Immediate kinship despite Lucas’s brusque start. Coppola, 28, sees Lucas as the younger brother he never had (n.10). Complementary strengths: Coppola from theater/writing, Lucas from design and editing (n.14). Lucas: “Francis is very flamboyant, very Italian, very ‘go-out-there-and-do-things.’ I’m very ‘let’s-think-about-this-first.’… Aesthetically, we had very compatible sensibilities… but we were very much the opposite in the way we operated.” (pp. 89–90, n.16)

Coppola insists Lucas learn to write: “You’re going to have to learn to write if you’re ever going to learn to direct.” “Nobody will take you seriously unless you can write.” (p. 90, n.18–19) Lucas: “I’m not a writer. I *hate* writing.” “I like *cinéma vérité,* documentaries… non-story, non-character tone poems.” (n.20–21) Coppola wants *THX 1138 4EB* expanded to a feature; attaches $3,000 for the script to the Warner deal for *The Rain People* (road picture, $750,000, no script approval). Lucas: “Francis could sell ice to the Eskimos. He has charisma beyond logic. I can see now what kind of men the great Caesars of history were, their magnetism.” (p. 91, n.24) Coppola called him “the eighty-five-year-old man.” (n.23)

## Student-festival peak; first meeting with Spielberg (pp. 91–94)

*THX 1138 4EB* still circulating 1967–68. January 1968, third National Student Film Festival, UCLA Royce Hall: *THX* best dramatic film (rumor of a prevented sweep in documentary/experimental). *Time* mention; *Los Angeles Times:* “the most impressive young talent to emerge from a university cinema department in the past five years.” (n.26) Charles Champlin: “for ingenuity, power, and professionalism, Lucas’s *THX 1138 4EB* is a knock-out and must be seen.” (n.27)

**First encounter with Spielberg, Friday, January 19, 1968.** Spielberg, 21, Cal State Long Beach junior, in the audience. “When *THX* came on, there was so much virtuosity in the craft and the vision and the emotion of that story that… I couldn’t believe it was a student film.” “My first impression was ‘I hate you!’… ‘I hate that guy, man! He’s so much better than I am!’” (p. 92, n.28–29) Spielberg remembers a warm handshake and friendship from that moment (n.30). Lucas is cooler: “I think I may have met him. There were a lot of people afterward. But if we met, it was definitely [just] a handshake, ‘Hi, how are you?’” (p. 93, n.31) Jones: Lucas would later call him “my partner, my pal, my inspiration, my challenger.” (n.32) Spielberg: after *THX,* “No longer were John Ford, Walt Disney, David Lean… my role models… it was someone nearer my own age.” (n.35)

## Engagement, *The Rain People,* *filmmaker,* Korty, Zoetrope (pp. 94–111)

Lucas proposes to Marcia in Garden City while scouting *Rain People* (Feb. 1968). “It wasn’t that I saw her in the editing room and said, ‘I’m going to get that girl.’… I certainly never expected I would marry Marcia.” “We were both feisty and neither one of us would take any shit from the other.” (p. 94, n.36) Plan: northern California, avant-garde films together. Mutual love of editing as the foundation — “probably not the best foundation on which to build a marriage,” Jones adds (p. 95).

On the road: writes *THX* 4–6 a.m., then AD/gofer; also shoots the one-man documentary later titled *filmmaker.* Coppola: “We began to feel like Robin Hood and his band.… We really had a filmmaking machine in our hands, and it didn’t need to be in Hollywood. It could be anywhere.” (p. 97, n.48) Fight when Coppola goes to New York (possible mistress): “He felt he had a right to do that, and I told him it wasn’t fair.” (n.47) Ogallala, Nebraska, cut; Marcia comes as assistant editor, passing on Haskell Wexler’s *Medium Cool* at first (later does both). Jones, via Milius: Coppola “attempted to hit on Marcia”; “That was Francis.… Francis was for Francis—but Francis was great; a truly great man.” (p. 98, n.49–50)

July 1968: Lucas sits in for Coppola on a San Francisco teachers’ panel; meets John Korty (Stinson Beach barn studio, no Hollywood). July 4 visit. Korty: “From what I saw of Hollywood, they can keep it right now.” (p. 99, n.51) Coppola: “If you can do it, we can.” (n.56)

First *THX* draft Nov. 1, 1968: “This is terrible,” Lucas tells Coppola (p. 101, n.58). Oliver Hailey pass rejected. Lucas better at giving notes than writing. Milius’s Vietnam script, first *The Psychedelic Soldier,* then *Apocalypse Now* (swipe at NIRVANA NOW buttons). Lucas wants to direct it as 16 mm newsreel-style: “Put all the neat stuff in there, all the helicopters.” (Milius, n.60)

Zoetrope etymology: Coppola’s Denmark visit to Lanterna Film / Mogens Skot-Hansen; gift of a zoetrope, “life movement.” $80,000 of equipment in Cologne with nowhere to put it. Dibble Estate in Ross falls through. Warehouse at 827 Folsom Street. Lucas wanted Transamerican Sprocket Works; Coppola overrode it as sounding like a rock group; names American Zoetrope. Coppola president, Lucas executive VP. “George was like a younger brother to me. I loved him. Where I went, he went.” (p. 104, n.70) Lucas: “When we got up here, they [the Hollywood studios] said, ‘You can’t possibly make movies up here.’ And we said, ‘Well, we don’t care.’” (n.71) Recruits Murch, Korty, Huyck, Milius, Robbins, Ballard, Wexler interest. Handshake deals; contracts “sort of immoral” (Coppola, n.73). Spielberg shown *Amblin’* but kept outside: “I was the establishment… working inside the system.” (p. 105, n.74)

*Easy Rider* (July 14, 1969) proves the independent model. Lucas: “I think the student films are the only real hope.” (p. 107, n.79) Coppola on “personal filmmaking” (n.80). **Jones’s foreshadowing, p. 107:** “It wouldn’t take long for the movement to sputter out—and Lucas, to his dismay, would be largely responsible for the gradual shift away from grittier, more personal films, toward more easily accessible pop blockbusters.”

Coppola’s “multipicpac” of seven scripts to John Calley / new Warner regime; *THX* presented as already in production; *The Conversation*; *Apocalypse Now* bartered without asking Lucas — “I was shocked.… Francis hadn’t even asked me or talked to me about *Apocalypse.*” (p. 109, n.84) $300,000 as a *loan,* callable if Warner loses faith. “That was the moment that American Zoetrope was really born.” (n.86)

**Idealism vs. money, pp. 110–112.** Spielberg: “I think Francis always looked at George as sort of his upstart assistant who had an opinion. An assistant with an opinion, nothing more dangerous than that, right?” (n.95) **Lucas, p. 110 (n.96), typography locked:** “What we’re striving for is total freedom, where we can finance our pictures, make them our way, release them where we want them released, and be completely free to express ourselves. That’s very hard to do in the world of business. In this country, the only thing that speaks is money and you have to have the money in order to have the power to be free. So the danger is—in being as oppressive as the next guy to the people below you. We’re going to do everything possible to avoid that pitfall.” Zoetrope as “a loose confederation of radicals and hippies.” (p. 111, n.100) Coppola: movies “sold like soup” on $3 cartridges (n.102). Lucas predicts videocassettes, 3-D, even holograms, and a Mattel “complete filmmaking kit” for ten-year-olds: “I look forward to the dispersal—when you don’t need a place like this.” (pp. 111–112, n.103)

## Feature *THX 1138* (pp. 112–116)

Filming starts Monday, September 22, 1969, unfinished BART; also Oakland Arena, Marin Civic Center, Berkeley. Dorothy Lucas: concrete-freeway “What a world” as a seed of sterile living (n.104). USC computer that garbled schedules (Dorothy, n.106). LUH decreases THX’s drugs; love as a crime; white-void detention; SEN; budgeted pursuit; sunset ending imagined since 1966.

**Lucas, p. 113 (n.107, 1971):** walking out of confinement is “the importance of self and being able to step out of whatever you’re in and move forward rather than being stuck in your little rut. People would give anything to quit their jobs. All they have to do is do it.… They’re people in cages with open doors.”

**KEY thematic line, pp. 113–114 (n.108):** At its core, *THX 1138* was about refusing to accept the status quo. “[It’s] about a hero who lives in an anthill and dares to go outside.” “As Lucas noted, ‘this issue of leaving a safe environment and going into the unknown’ would be an underlying premise of his first three films, running in a thematic straight line from *THX* through *American Graffiti* and on into *Star Wars.* ‘I was very consistent in my cinematic obsessions.’”

*Cinéma vérité* of the future / documentary crew in a time yet to come (*American Cinematographer,* n.109). DPs Albert Kihn and Dave Meyers; available light. Dialogue “intentionally abstract,” “often unrelated to the on-screen visuals, much like Lipsett’s use of dialogue in *21-87.*” (p. 115, n.114) Cast: Duvall as THX (chosen before the script was finished), Maggie McOmie as LUH, Donald Pleasence as SEN. Lucas: “I’m not very good with people, never have been.” (n.118) Duvall: “He leaves you alone.… you felt you were in safe hands.” (n.119) Shoot ends night of November 21, 1969, Caldecott Tunnel; 250,000 feet edited in the Mill Valley attic, not at Zoetrope.

## Black Thursday, the cut, Lucasfilm (pp. 116–126)

Zoetrope opening party Dec. 12, 1969. Six months of day/night editing with Marcia and Murch (sound). Marcia: “I never cared for *THX* because it left me cold.” Lucas tells her she doesn’t understand. “All he wanted to do was abstract filmmaking, tone poems, collections of images.” (p. 117, n.126)

May 1970 first studio screening. Coppola to Lucas beforehand: “Well, this is either going to be a masterpiece or masturbation.” (p. 118, n.127) Executives baffled (“not a commercial movie”); Coppola sinks and mutters, “I don’t know what the fuck this is.” (n.129) Murch, Deschanel, and Robbins spirit the film cans out of the booth into Robbins’s VW. Ashley wants it more “accessible.” Fred Weintraub: “Put the freaks up front.” Lucas, still contemptuous of the phrase: “Forget it. I’m not doing any of this stuff.” (pp. 119–120, n.134)

*The Rain People* flops commercially, Aug. 1970. Paramount offers Coppola *The Godfather.* Lucas: “I don’t think you have any choice. We’re in debt. You’ve gotta get a job.” (p. 120, n.136) Signed Sept. 28, 1970.

**Black Thursday, Thursday, November 19, 1970.** Final *THX* plus leather-bound scripts in a silver Zoetrope box Korty later compared to a coffin. “[They] went ballistic.… That’s when the shit hit the fan. Suddenly it was the crash of ’29.” (p. 121, n.140) Calley: “Everybody thought it was a loser.” (n.141) Lucas: “It was insane. I wish I had filmed it. It was like bringing an audience to the Mona Lisa and asking, ‘Do you know why she’s smiling?’ ‘Sorry, Leonardo, you’ll have to go back and make some changes.’” (n.142) Warner will still distribute *THX* but the deal is off; they want the $300,000 back; they take the film; they toss *The Conversation* and *Apocalypse Now.*

Lucas: “They don’t respect talent. They don’t know what an idea is, and they just walk all over it.” (p. 122, n.146) Later refuses to speak to Ashley; when shopping *Raiders,* demands an apology, then gives the picture to Paramount anyway. Jones also notes the industry pivot to *Airport* and *Love Story.* Lucas, early 1971: Warner “decided not to finance any more youth-oriented, adventurous, crazy movies.… they sold us completely down the river.” (p. 122, n.150)

Rudi Fehr cuts “a little more than four minutes.” “This was George’s first experience with ‘studio interference.’” (Murch, n.151) Lucas: “There was no point for them to do it, other than [to] exercise some power.… We fought it, and they did it, and I was angry about it.” “They were cutting the fingers off my baby.” (p. 123, n.152–153) “The terrible thing about this country is that the dollar is valued above the individual.” (n.155) “Lucas wouldn’t trust studios again. Ever.”

Zoetrope in free fall. Coppola hurt that Lucas wouldn’t stay as “heir apparent” (n.159). Lucas: “I’ve had a very volatile relationship with Francis.… like we were married and we got divorced. It’s as close a relationship as I’ve had with anybody.” (p. 124, n.160) “I’m very cautious. I don’t borrow money. I’m very protective of the things that I build.” (n.161) Phone-bill breach via Mona Skager. Lucas will not apologize for *THX:* “[It was] my one chance to make an avant-garde movie. It almost brought American Zoetrope down… [and] almost destroyed my career. But it was definitely worth it at the time.” (n.164)

**1971: Tom Pollock drafts incorporation papers; Lucasfilm Ltd. opens, run from the Mill Valley house; lone employees George and Marcia.** (pp. 124–125)

*THX 1138* released March 1971. Mixed-to-baffled reviews (Turan, Ebert three stars, *New York Times* “Wow” / “iconography of contemporary graphics,” *Variety* sniping). Parents not told; they see it in Modesto in June. Lucas: “Critics are the vandals of our time, like spray painters who mess up walls.” “I basically said, ‘To hell with reviews.’” (pp. 125–126, n.171)

**Close, pp. 126 (n.174–175), typography locked.** After the film “went down the toilet,” Marcia reminds him it hadn’t involved the audience emotionally. Lucas: “Emotionally involving the audience is easy. Anybody can do it blindfolded. Get a little kitten and have some guy wring its neck.” Then: “I’m gonna show you how easy it is. I’ll make a film that emotionally involves the audience.”
