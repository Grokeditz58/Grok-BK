## Foreword: Howard Roffman, President, Lucas Licensing
- In 1986 Lucas Licensing "decided it was time to give Star Wars a rest". In 1989 Lucy Autrey Wilson was given the go-ahead to find a publisher.
- **George Lucas's original EU ground rules (canon-policy history):**
> "The stories would have to take place after Return of the Jedi; events prior to A New Hope were off limits because if George were ever to make more Star Wars films, that was where he would go; and no major characters could be killed. Other than that, George made it clear that he would be hands-off." — Foreword
  - Lucas's reaction: "No one is going to buy this".
- Heir debuted at #1 on the NYT hardcover list in 1991.

## Introduction: Timothy Zahn
- The offer came Monday, November 6, 1989, through his agent Russell Galen. Lou Aronica (Bantam Spectra) had pitched a "three-book saga that would pick up the story after Return of the Jedi".
- He had a 40-page trilogy outline by Thanksgiving 1989. The contract and approval took 6 months, and he mailed the manuscript on November 2, 1990.
- The first printing of 70,000 sold out within two weeks. The 4th printing used tan cover stock. Heir beat Grisham's The Firm on the NYT list, and Heir was the subject of a Jeopardy! clue.
- On the book today: "the story still holds up well."

## Afterword: Betsy Mitchell (editor), November 2010
- "In Heir to the Empire, Tim 'gave birth' to the unforgettable Mara Jade, Grand Admiral Thrawn, and Joruus C'baoth". She and Zahn met George Lucas at Skywalker Ranch, "who thanked Tim for his contribution to the Star Wars universe."
