# Heir 20th Anniversary: Author Annotations (Zahn "TZ" and editor Betsy Mitchell "BM"), all chapters 1–32

Official author commentary, keyed to superscript markers in the text. These are Legends-era, author-of-record statements. Everything relevant to scaling or lore is listed; trivia is condensed.

## Thrawn: design, abilities, and the "Endor" claim (key)
> "So here at last we have all the pieces that went into the creation of Grand Admiral Thrawn. He's competent and capable … He cares about his troops … And he's driven by logic and reason, not anger or ego or wounded pride. Throw in the semi-mystical art thing (through which he can anticipate his enemies' moves), and make him an alien (because the Emperor disliked aliens, and would never give such a rank to one unless he was really, really good)" — Annotations, ch. 31 n7
> "Oh, and what would have happened if Thrawn had been in command at Endor? The Rebels, in my humble opinion, would almost certainly have lost." — Annotations, ch. 31 n7
- ch. 1 n19: "he defeats an entire New Republic task force without, apparently, ever even bothering to leave his meditation room."
- **The seven "qualities of a good commander"** (ch. 1 n19, ch. 8 n2, ch. 9 n1, ch. 9 n4, ch. 30 n3, ch. 31 n5, ch. 31 n6): strategic/tactical skill; adopting good ideas from inferiors; valuing competence over pomp; planning ahead; keeping priorities straight; not wasting troops; willingness to retreat.
- ch. 1 n3: Grand Admirals are "an extra layer put in at the top of the military command, its members appointed by and answerable only to the Emperor." n7: the title comes from Shirer's *Rise and Fall of the Third Reich*. Zahn wanted a military villain, "as opposed to a governor, Moff, or Sith".
- ch. 1 n12: "Thrawn's—and Palpatine's—real agendas for the Unknown Regions campaigns were fairly vague here." n20: Thrawn "never did accept the legitimacy of the New Republic."
- ch. 1 n6: "I don't see Thrawn as the type to use unwilling conscripts."
- ch. 1 n18: "art-as-tactical-insight" had no source and just "popped into my mind". ch. 15 n1: art rather than myths, because myths would need translation. ch. 15 n2–3: the Sherlock Holmes kinship; Pellaeon is "the Dr. Watson to Thrawn's Sherlock Holmes"; no alien POV characters are used.
- ch. 15 n4: Thrawn "can be ruthless if and when necessary".
- ch. 4 n4: body armor (he wears it only when expecting danger). ch. 4 n8: Thrawn's rare emotion toward C'baoth comes from "distant memories of his encounter years earlier with the original, nonclone C'baoth" (Outbound Flight).
- ch. 27 n1: the Chimaera is "Disparate elements (human plus Chiss) … unconquerable."
- ch. 4 n14: Outbound Flight was "essentially a throwaway line … to underline Thrawn's military capabilities."
- ch. 1 n17: the Marg Sabl maneuver was reused in TCW's "Storm Over Ryloth", and the DVD featurette "specifically credits the maneuver to Heir."

## Ysalamiri and Force mechanics (key ruling)
> "What's actually happening—and we'll see it in action later—is that ysalamiri simply suppress the level of the Force to something below the threshold that Jedi can access. … Thrawn can therefore be excused for perhaps oversimplifying his explanation." — Annotations, ch. 4 n10
- This means Thrawn's "push back the Force" explanation is in-universe imprecise, per the author. "Thrawn doesn't know everything."
- ch. 3 n6: ysalamiri are rare and unused because they are "relatively unknown" and "hard to get off their trees without killing them".
- ch. 19 n5: the ysalamiri Jedi-cage was Zahn's first idea for the trilogy; it was finally used in Vision of the Future.

## Palpatine's battle meditation at Endor
> "I borrowed this from Sauron's driving of his forces in The Lord of the Rings. … I felt there had to be something going on beneath the surface to explain their victory at Endor. … Endor was to be his victory over the Rebellion, and he would have made sure he could claim it as such. Logic aside, of course, I also needed this ability for C'baoth to use later in the books." — Annotations, ch. 4 n12
- ch. 4 n13: Thrawn assumed all Dark Jedi want Palpatine-style power because "Thrawn had spent years observing Palpatine".

## C'baoth
- ch. 4 n7: C'baoth was originally "an insane clone of Obi-Wan Kenobi, created before the Clone Wars by the Emperor"; Lucasfilm vetoed it. Pronounced "SA-bay-oth".
- ch. 4 n5: Zahn is firm that C'baoth killed the real guardian ("Such speculation is wrong, of course. I think …").
- ch. 4 n9 (BM): "Thrawn's military command and tactics-oriented mind versus C'baoth's Force abilities".
- ch. 4 n2: "Dark Jedi" was used because Sith wasn't defined yet.

## Noghri / Vader
- ch. 1 n8: Vader's mask was designed to resemble a Noghri face; Zahn retracts this given RotS ("Palpatine had provided the mask … nothing whatsoever to do with the Noghri").
- ch. 1 n9: the Noghri were originally "Sith" (vetoed). n10: the skin color was changed to gray.
- ch. 25 n3: the Noghri are an answer to the Ewoks: "true warriors, and extremely deadly."
- ch. 1 n4: Vader grabbed the best stormtroopers for the 501st.

## Mara Jade (origin)
- ch. 26 n2: the Emperor's Hand was "someone who could meet Luke's Jedi power head-on … out of the normal chain of command, lest Vader get wind". She was sent to kill Luke at Jabba's palace.
- ch. 22 n10: Mara's "not picked up a lightsaber" line is sarcasm or a typo; "she's actually quite skilled with a lightsaber".

## Luke / Force
- ch. 2 n3: "Luke would be entirely on his own as a Jedi". n5: Force guidance can come as subtle nudges. n1: Ben's dream contact is limited by Ben's "weakness or distance".
- ch. 11 n1: "Yoda couldn't have affected his X-wing's systems at that distance … But Yoda could have affected Luke's perception". (This is a Force-range limit statement by the author.)
- ch. 2 n11: Endor's summit is "heavy with residual effects" of the Emperor (DFR).

## Tech, ships, and setting
- ch. 1 n14: "Assault Frigates were modified Dreadnoughts, a little more than a third the size of a Star Destroyer … Clone Wars era—but still packed a hefty punch." Source: WEG.
- ch. 1 n16: S-foils and the "etheric rudder" interact with vacuum/zero-point energy (dropped term).
- ch. 4 n1: Zahn's original hyperdrive scale ran 0 to 1 (infinite) on time-dilation math; it was superseded by WEG/LFL.
- ch. 4 n11: the cloaking shield was "at least marginally functional" by ESB. ch. 27 n2: the cloak is "a two-way shield … no sensor data getting in", per WEG.
- ch. 16 n1: the Interdictor is a WEG invention. ch. 31 n4: spacetroopers are WEG. ch. 9 n5: Lucasfilm told Zahn to coordinate with WEG sourcebooks.
- ch. 9 n12: Heir's dates are 16 years off; the Clone Wars were originally placed 35 years before ANH.
- ch. 1 n5 and ch. 25 n4: the war with the Empire continues "ten more years … until Vision of the Future".
- ch. 30 n2: the Sluis Van gathering point is "about 4.3 times the distance from our sun to Pluto".
- ch. 2 n6: Coruscant has wilderness and mountain areas.

## Production history
- The original title was "Wild Card" or "Warlord's Gambit" (ch. 1 n13).
- In the original outline Han and Leia went to Myrkr, and the book ended at Luke's escape. Mitchell asked for Sluis Van (ch. 24 n1, ch. 29 n1, ch. 30 n1, ch. 31 n3).
- Named after real people (Tuckerizations): Vandersluis, Jomark (Mark Jones), Peterson and Colclazure (charity), Klon Newell, Stonehill, LoBue, Wade Warren, Hillyard, Callen.
- Pellaeon is named after Pelleas. "Sturm und Drang". Rwookrrorro was intended as the Wookiee name for Kashyyyk. "borg" predates Star Trek in Marvel SW (1978).
- The original 501st, Allegiance, and Choices of One are cross-referenced. The beckon call payoff is in Hand of Thrawn. Shadows of Mindor came from ch. 18 n4.
