### The story “is actually recounted by R2-D2 to the Keeper of the Whills, one hundred years after *Return of the Jedi*”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, to Rob Coleman, Shoot Day 1, June 30, 2003, as reported by Rinzler.
**Source:** “George remarks to Rob Coleman that the story of *Star Wars* is actually recounted by R2-D2 to the Keeper of the Whills, one hundred years after *Return of the Jedi*. *Journal of the Whills* is what Lucas titled his first outline of *Star Wars* back in 1972.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler ROTS p. 72.

### July 7 weekend rewrite of the turn; “What am I doing?”; numb and flat at “I hate you”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, to Hayden Christensen, Shoot Day 6, July 7, 2003.
**Source:** “Yeah, but a monotone isn’t right. You’re emotional here. You’ve made a decision—but you don’t know if it’s the right decision . . . There’s always this good in you, and this little part is always asking, *What am I doing?* Even at the very end. That’s what makes you turn and kill the Emperor.” “At this point, you’re not completely cold. The point where you become numb and flat is when you say, ‘I hate you’ to your best friend.” “I spent the whole weekend rewriting the scene with you and Palpatine, where you turn.” Distinct from the June 26 fourth draft. — typography-faithful, Rinzler ROTS p. 82.

### Watching I–VI vs. theatrical order: “Now Darth Vader is a tragic character who’s lost everything”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, Shoot Day 8, July 9, 2003.
**Source:** “If I’d taken the prequel trilogy to a marketing company, Episode I would have started here [with Episode III]—and Episodes II and III would’ve been about Darth Vader killing people. But in the end, I wouldn’t be able to write movies like that.” “If you see them in order, it completely twists things about. A lot of the tricks of IV, V, and VI no longer exist. The real struggle of the twins to save their father becomes very apparent, whereas it didn’t exist at all the first time [audiences saw Episodes IV, V, and VI]. Now Darth Vader is a tragic character who’s lost everything. He’s basically a bitter old man in a suit. ‘I am your father’ was a real shock. Now it’s a real reward. Finally, the son knows what we already know.” “If you watch it the way it was released, IV, V, VI, I, II, III—you get one kind of movie. If you watch I through VI, you get a completely different movie.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler ROTS p. 84–85.
**Notes:** `notes/materials/172-making-of-revenge-of-the-sith-2005/rinzler-04-part-ii-and-chapter-four.md`

### Shoot Day 14: “In the next scene, Anakin becomes Darth Vader”

**Speaker:** Rinzler, reporting the Thursday, July 17, 2003, shoot; Hayden Christensen.
**Source:** “In the next scene, Anakin becomes Darth Vader. Christensen kneels before McDiarmid, rehearsing his dialogue to himself.” Christensen: “Anakin’s relationship with Palpatine eclipses his relationship with Obi-Wan. He isn’t really exactly set in his devotion to the Jedi or to Palpatine. He is looking to see how he can get more power, but his ideas of good and evil are not black and white.” “After a few takes… Anakin’s transition to the dark side is complete.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler ROTS p. 93.

### “How do you turn over democracy to a tyrant with applause?”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, rehearsal Saturday, July 19, 2003; Chris Neil on approach.
**Source:** “Lucas posits the political question *Revenge of the Sith* answers: ‘How do you turn over democracy to a tyrant with applause? Not with a coup, but with applause? That is the story of Caesar, Napoleon, and Hitler.’ He adds, ‘Idiocy is the controlling factor of life—particularly when you get into politics.’” Neil: Lucas’s approach is “to play Anakin as not consumed by the dark side from the very get-go, but over the course of the movie.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler ROTS p. 95.

### Editorial reorg: “take out every scene that doesn’t have anything to do with Anakin”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, Tuesday, March 9, 2004, rough assembly.
**Source:** “The first script I wrote had stories for everybody, and I cut it down and we had a script. But when we cut it together, there were still problems. Finally, I said, ‘Okay, let’s be even more hard-nosed here and take out every scene that doesn’t have anything to do with Anakin.’ But that causes you to juxtapose certain scenes that you were never contemplating juxtaposing before.” A Padmé–Bail scene between two Anakin scenes was cut, and “suddenly all sorts of weird things started to happen that weren’t intended in the script—but in some cases it actually worked much better.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler ROTS p. 176.

### “Anakin is simply greedy. There is no revenge. The revenge of the Sith is Palpatine… he’s a pawn”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, Saturday, June 19, 2004, at home in his kitchen, after showing the rough cut to Spielberg and Jeff Nathanson.
**Source:** “Some people have been having a hard time with the reason that Anakin goes bad. Somebody asked whether somebody could kill Anakin’s best friend, so that he really gets angry. They wanted a real betrayal, such as, ‘You tried to kill me so now I’m going to try and kill you.’ They didn’t understand the fact that Anakin is simply greedy. There is no revenge. The revenge of the Sith is Palpatine. It doesn’t have much to do with Darth Vader; he’s a pawn in the whole scheme.” “I decided that I’m not going to alter the film to make it more commercial or marketable. I have to be true to my vision, which is thirty years old, but I have to be true to it.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler ROTS p. 188.

### “Two hard right turns”; love that would “give up everything” said “in *two* minutes”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, Additional Photography Day 6, August 28, 2004.
**Source:** “It’s really hard in the end to express the idea, *I’m so in love with you that I would do anything to save you; I’d give up everything—friends, my whole life—for you,* and make that real—make that stick—and say it in *two* minutes, which is all the time this film has for it.” “And when I created it, I knew I wanted two hard right turns—it’s designed to be that way—and I knew I was taking a real chance that it wasn’t going to work.” Close-ups: “You realize that by telling the Jedi about Palpatine being a Sith that Padmé is going to die. Basically, you just killed her.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler ROTS p. 207.

### “must be done, Lord Vader”; a Sith is greed, “the desire to cheat death”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, Additional Photography Days 10–11, September 2–3, 2004.
**Source:** To Ian McDiarmid, for the end of the dialogue: “must be done, Lord Vader.” On the new Yoda–Anakin scene: “As Yoda says in this one, ‘You must learn to let go of everything you’re afraid to let go of.’ Because holding on is in the same category and the precursor to greed. And that’s what a Sith is. A Sith is somebody that is absolutely obsessed with gaining more and more power—but for what? Nothing, except that it becomes an obsession to get more.” “The Jedi are *trained* to let go… They can love people—in fact, they should love everybody. They should love their enemies; they should love the Sith. But they can’t form attachments. So what all these movies are about is: greed… And the ultimate state of greed is the desire to cheat death.” Lucas calls it “My last day of shooting after thirty-five years.” — typography-faithful, Rinzler ROTS p. 211, 213.
**Notes:** `notes/materials/172-making-of-revenge-of-the-sith-2005/rinzler-09-chapter-nine-and-back-matter.md`

### “The prophecy was right. Anakin was the chosen one… destroys the Emperor out of compassion for his son”

**Speaker:** George Lucas, Epilogue: The Compassion of the Jedi.
**Source:** “It really has to do with learning. Children teach you compassion. They teach you to love unconditionally. Anakin can’t be redeemed for all the pain and suffering he’s caused. He doesn’t right the wrongs, but he stops the horror. The end of the saga is simply Anakin saying, *I care about this person, regardless of what it means to me. I will throw away everything that I have, everything that I’ve grown to love—primarily the Emperor—and throw away my life, to save this person. And I’m doing it because he has faith in me; he loves me despite all the horrible things I’ve done. I broke his mother’s heart, but he still cares about me, and I can’t let that die.* Anakin is very different in the end. The thing of it is: The prophecy was right. Anakin was the chosen one, and he does bring balance to the Force. He takes the ounce of good still left in him and destroys the Emperor out of compassion for his son.” “It has the epic quality of following one person from the time he’s nine years old to the time he dies. It’s Anakin’s story…” — typography-faithful, Rinzler ROTS p. 221.
