Afternoon, Stage 4: raised platform and a table. Christensen “isn’t yet in costume as he discusses the action with Lucas.” Hayden: “Is it really going to be that hard to get out of the restraints?” Lucas: “No, but I need you to build up the anger in yourself.” Lights dimmed “creating a somber mood.” Because Christensen in the Vader costume “can’t walk very far, a makeshift dressing room has been constructed near the set.” Caption, Dave Elsey, typography locked: “My favorite prosthetic makeup is ‘Burnt Anakin,’ because, in many ways I feel like I’ve been researching this makeup since I was a kid and first saw *Return of the Jedi*. To me this was a chance to do something fresh, in every sense of the word, for *Star Wars*. Although we’d seen Vader without his helmet at the tail end of the first trilogy, I always wanted to know how he’d got so badly scarred and the reason he had to wear the Vader suit. I had read that he had fallen into volcanic lava, and had always, in my mind, been creating a prosthetic makeup that demonstrated these devastating injuries. In other words, I feel like I’ve been designing this since I was fifteen. And Hayden was fantastic in the makeup. He embraced it, brought it to life. He added a sense of dark evil.” Caption also notes the visual script moment where “‘burnt’ Anakin is rescued by the Emperor—whose gesture echoes that of Obi-Wan touching Luke’s forehead in Episode IV.” Print p. 137 is a full-bleed still of the burnt-Anakin makeup being applied (no body text).
