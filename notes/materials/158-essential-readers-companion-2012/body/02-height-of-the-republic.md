Visual source: CBR FD0WCBbA, p040–p100. No printed folios. Chapter head on S040 uses arabic **2**. Chapter 3 opens on S101. Caption-only or painting-split leaves: S069, S100 (and captions riding the tops of S049, S056, S064, S072, S078, S082, S088, S093, S098). “Click to buy …” links are ebook furniture.

Juvenile *Jedi Apprentice* / *Jedi Quest* banners lock a typography habit already seen in the Contents leaf: book 1 is **#I** (Roman I); from book 10 onward the leading 1 prints as I (**#IO**, **#II**, **#I2**, **#I3**, …). Do not regularize. *Jedi Apprentice* #1 is Dave Wolverton; #2 onward and both Special Editions are Jude Watson / Cliff Nielsen / Scholastic. *Jedi Quest* books are Jude Watson; covers Alicia Buelow and David Mattingly, Scholastic.

## S040 — chapter opener

Circle emblem, **CHAPTER 2**, **HEIGHT OF THE REPUBLIC**. Drop cap **S** then **TAR WARS:** Episode II *Attack of the Clones* (2002). Two lines of film dialogue with “major implications for galactic history”: Chancellor Palpatine, “I will not let this Republic that has stood for a thousand years be split in two”; Sio Bibble, “There hasn’t been a full-scale war since the foundation of the Republic.” The opener reconciles those lines with the thought bomb on Ruusan: the New Sith Wars end, every known Sith Lord is wiped out, Jedi ranks are decimated, the Republic resets its calendars, and a millennium of peace begins. Expanded Universe material prior to approximately 1000 BBY is therefore an older epoch; characters in the Clone Wars animated series refer to it as the Old Republic, differentiating it from the modern Galactic Republic. After this time the Republic does not experience any major conflicts, so the books of this period tell of minor struggles and of the growth of Obi-Wan Kenobi and Anakin Skywalker. “The era is not devoid of menace; evil remains in the shadows.” After Bane’s purge the new Sith Lords continue in hiding. The prologue to the very first *Star Wars* novel (1976) is quoted: “So it was with the Republic at its height. Like the greatest of trees . . . the Republic rotted from within though the danger was not visible from the outside.”

## Short fiction and *Darth Maul* / *Cloak of Deception* (S064–S072)

- **“The Monster”** — Daniel Wallace; *Star Wars Gamer* #2, November 2000; 36 BBY; Naboo. Lieutenant Panaka, Captain Magneta, Sate Pestage, Veermok. **BTS:** Pestage “has grown considerably in the Expanded Universe from his humble origins as a character cut from *The Empire Strikes Back* (1980).” An earlier draft had Vader’s holographic contact with the Emperor stalled by Imperial Grand Vizier Sate Pestage; the role was cut, but the name appeared in the 1980 TESB Collector’s Edition souvenir magazine, Kenner’s Darth Vader Star Destroyer playset, and the *Dark Empire Sourcebook* (1993).
- **DARTH MAUL: SABOTEUR** — James Luceno; David Stevenson; short story / eBook novella, Del Rey, February 2001; included with *Darth Maul: Shadow Hunter* paperback December 2001 and reissue December 2011; 33 BBY. Typography: **EBook**; **Included withDarth Maul: Shadow Hunterpaperback** (spaces dropped). Dorvalla, Eriadu, Neimoidia, Riome; lommite; Hath Monchar; Lieutenant Governor Tarkin. **BTS:** “the first Star Wars eBook.” Early Tarkin; ends with Sidious advising Maul to study Black Sun (a reference to the 2000 Dark Horse *Darth Maul* mini-series).
- **“The Starfighter Trap”** — Steve Miller; *Gamer* #1, September 2000; 33 BBY; Naboo. Essara Till, Dren Melne. **BTS:** a tie-in to the LucasArts videogame *Star Wars: Starfighter* (2000).
- **CLOAK OF DECEPTION** — James Luceno; Steven D. Anderson; HC June 2001 / PB July 2002; 32 BBY. Asmeru, Coruscant, Dorvalla, Eriadu, Karfeddion, Neimoidia. Valorum, Arwen Cohl, Havac / Eru “Havac” Matalis, Nute Gunray, Sidious. **BTS (S068):** originally titled *Vergence*; “the earliest adult novel in the time line upon its publication.” Because it preceded Episode I, Luceno likened its relationship to the rest of the saga to *The Hobbit*’s relationship with *The Lord of the Rings*. Conscious effort to introduce concepts usually taken for granted. Cohl was originally Margrave M’buele (nod to Marvel’s Katya M’buele). Cameos include Anoon Bondara, Darsha Assant, Hath Monchar, commerce entities that later fund the Separatists, an early Luminara Unduli, and Jorus C’baoth (one scene; later *Heir to the Empire* / *Outbound Flight*). Senator Grebleips from Brodo Asogi is Spielberg spelled backward; Brodo Asogi is E.T.’s home planet in *E.T.: The Book of the Green Planet*. S068 painting of Valorum with Yoda, Mace, and a standing blue figure; S069 is a caption-only leaf: **Supreme Chancellor Valorum confers with Jedi Masters Yoda and Mace Windu on the deteriorating state of the Galactic Republic.**
- **DARTH MAUL: SHADOW HUNTER** — Michael Reaves; David Stevenson; HC February 2001 / PB November 2001 / reissue December 2011; 32 BBY; Coruscant. Lorn Pavan, Darsha Assant, I-5YQ “I-Five” (glyph on the leaf should be locked from a tight crop before any certified quote), Hath Monchar, Sidious. **BTS (S070, continuity):** “The movies Episodes I and II include deliberate hints connecting Palpatine and Darth Sidious, but any explicit statement that they were the same person was carefully avoided by all Star Wars media until the 2005 release of Episode III. This novel has one of the more overt connections, with Lorn Pavan delivering the coveted invasion plans to Senator Palpatine, yet the Sith conspiracy continues unabated.” Lorn is survived by I-5YQ (later the MedStar novels) and by his infant son Jax Pavan, later of the *Coruscant Nights* novels. S071 painting of Maul vs. Darsha; caption at top of S072: **Padawan Darsha Assant defends herself against the fierce deadliness of Darth Maul in the industrial depths of Coruscant.**
- Short story **“Restraint”** (S072) — James Luceno; added to the 2012 *Shadow Hunter* paperback reissue alongside the 3-D *The Phantom Menace* and Maul’s return in *The Clone Wars*. Teenage Maul at the Orsis academy; Meltch Krakko; Mother Talzin and the Nightsisters; Osika Kirske; Sidious orders the destruction of the students on Orsis.

## Episode I novelizations, journals, *Darth Plagueis* (S072–S078)

- **STAR WARS: EPISODE I THE PHANTOM MENACE** — Terry Brooks (adult novel); Patricia C. Wrede (juvenile); “based on the story and screenplay by George Lucas.” Covers: Lucasfilm Ltd. (Del Rey novelizations and omnibus); Paul Colin (Scholastic Junior). Adult: HC April 1999, PB March 2000, reissue January 2012; compiled as part of **The Prequel Trilogyomnibus** (spaces dropped as printed), May 2007. Young reader: Scholastic, April 1999. 32 BBY; Coruscant, Naboo, Tatooine. Summary is the film plot; closer: “The identity of the second Sith, Darth Sidious, remains a mystery.” **BTS (S073, continuity / Lucas):** theatrical release brought adaptations across media. “Like all novelizations, these adaptations have a number of superficial differences from the finished film, the result of being based on the screenplay and not the final edit.” Brooks “built upon the screenplay by George Lucas for his novel, benefiting from direct input from Lucas to confidently expand the story. Episode I was the first Star Wars movie novelization to add newly created scenes unique to the book.” It begins on Tatooine and introduces Anakin first; other unique chapters include Anakin saving an injured Tusken. “One continuity curiosity is the mention of the Jedi Master under whom Qui-Gon trained. This unnamed mentor described Qui-Gon as the best he had taught in more than four hundred years of the Jedi Order. Though it is possible this is a needlessly veiled reference to Yoda, it certainly does not fit Dooku, who is revealed to be Qui-Gon’s mentor in Episode II.”
- Short story **“End Game”** (S073–S074) — James Luceno; in the 2012 3-D *Phantom Menace* novelization reissue. Maul on Naboo, Captain Magneta, King Veruna; Maul infers that Hego Damask was Sidious’s Sith Master; ends with Obi-Wan cutting Maul in two.
- **EPISODE I JOURNALS** — typography on S074 runs the three titles together with odd commas/spaces: **EPISODE I JOURNAL: ANAKIN SKYWALKER  ,EPISODE I JOURNAL: QUEEN AMIDALA  ,EPISODE I JOURNAL: DARTH MAUL**. Todd Strasser (Skywalker); Jude Watson (Amidala and Maul); Lucasfilm Ltd. covers; **PUBLICATION HISTORYJuvenile**; June 1999 (Skywalker and Amidala), March 2000 (Maul). **BTS:** Anakin’s journal is written after the Battle of Naboo; Amidala’s is kept during the events on a hidden datapad; Maul’s is the most embellished (Togorian pirates in space; Tusken Raiders on Tatooine), because of his minimal screen time.
- **DARTH PLAGUEIS** — James Luceno; Torstein Nordstrand; HC January 2012 / PB October 2012. Time line: 67–65 BBY (Part 1); 54–52 BBY (Part 2); 34–32 BBY (Part 3). Worlds typography includes **Kursid [[Q-5]** (double opening bracket, as printed). Plagueis / Hego Damask, Sidious / Palpatine, Cosinga Palpatine, Darth Tenebrous, Vidar Kim, Sate Pestage, Ars Veruna, 11-4D. Long summary (S075–S076) covers Tenebrous’s death, the Canted Circle, Palpatine’s apprenticeship, Maul as an enforcer, the Sojourn attack, Veruna’s death, and Palpatine’s betrayal and murder of Plagueis, ending on the eve of the Battle of Naboo (Sifo-Dyas, Dooku, Anakin). **BTS (S076–S077, continuity):** typography **THE READER’S COMPANIONASSUMES** (missing space). “The Reader’s Companion assumes that newcomers to Star Wars fiction have already been exposed to the story of the six films of the saga. For this reason, *Darth Plagueis* is placed here in the time line, earlier than Palpatine’s exposure as a Sith Lord during the events of Episode III (2005). Episode III was the first source to identify Darth Plagueis as Sidious’s Master.” Luceno first pitched a first-person holocron narrative (Anne Rice *Vampire Chronicles* as inspiration); the book evolved to tell Palpatine’s beginnings as much as Plagueis’s origin. “LucasBooks nearly canceled the book in its earliest development due to sensitivities regarding shining too direct a spotlight on these shadowy Sith characters. The novel survived, though, as the story underwent careful refinement with direct input from Howard Roffman, president of Lucas Licensing.” Sources include *Wild Space* (2008), “The Monster,” *Cloak of Deception*, *Saboteur*, the Yinchorri Uprising in *Acts of War* (2000), Vidar Kim in *Republic* #64 (2004), and Maul’s Black Sun mission from the 2000 comics. Romeo Treblanc and the planet Bal’demnic are “elements created by fans and officially sanctioned as Star Wars lore by the Star Wars Fan Club in 2008.” The book also weaves Luceno’s “Restraint” and “End Game,” Ryder Windham’s *The Wrath of Darth Maul* (2012), and “the developing story of the fourth and fifth seasons of *Star Wars: The Clone Wars* animated series (2011–2013).” S077 painting of the Canted Circle ambush; caption at top of S078: **Darth Plagueis fights for his life as the secret gathering of the Order of the Canted Circle is ambushed by assassins.**
- Short story **“The Tenebrous Way”** — Matthew Stover; *Insider* #130 (2011, Titan). Tenebrous’s midi-chlorian consciousness-transfer traps him in a loop in the depths of Bal’demnic.
- **“Deep Spoilers”** — Ryder Windham; *Gamer* #4, June 2001; 33 BBY; Naboo. Gungan bongo-race comedy (Spleed Nukkels, Neb Neb Goodrow, Squidfella Quiglee, Boss Nass). **BTS (S079):** many characters were first introduced in Windham’s Scholastic *Episode I Adventures* game books.

## *Jedi Apprentice* Special Editions, *Rogue Planet*, *Jedi Quest*, *Outbound Flight*, *The Approaching Storm* (S079–S100)

- **JEDI APPRENTICE SPECIAL EDITION: DECEPTIONS** — Watson / Nielsen; July 2001; 44 BBY (Part 1) / 29 BBY (Part 2). Hilo [O-13], Tentrix [N-15]. Past: the Chun family after Bruck’s death. Present: the BioCruiser, Kad Chun, Vox Chun.
- **ROGUE PLANET** — Greg Bear; David Stevenson; HC May 2000 / PB May 2001; 29 BBY. Coruscant, Seline [M-3], Zonama Sekot [M-3]. Anakin, Obi-Wan, Raith Sienar, Wilhuff Tarkin, Ke Daiv (Blood Carver), Jabitha, Charza Kwinn; seed-partners, the Far Outsiders, Sekot flees into hyperspace. **BTS (S081, continuity):** in 2000 Del Rey had already begun *The New Jedi Order*. “*Rogue Planet* can be seen as essentially a chapter in that story line,” which describes the galaxy threatened by brutal invaders who wield organic technology. “Those aliens, the Yuuzhan Vong, are not detailed by name in *Rogue Planet*, but their presence is implied in the sketchy descriptions of the Far Outsiders.” Zonama Sekot returns at the end of the NJO. Tarkin appears here as ORSF commander, not the lieutenant governor of *Cloak of Deception*. “Despite the lack of a centralized galactic military—as established in Episode II (2002)—there is a Republic Outland Regions Security Force”; *The Essential Guide to Warfare* (2012) has Ranulph Tarkin building the ORSF from local defense forces and confiscated Trade Federation warships. The Senate’s order that the Federation demilitarize after Naboo “was evidently poorly enforced,” given the droid armies of Episode II. Raith Sienar extends *The Star Wars Sourcebook* (1987); this is the first chronological appearance of the germ of the Death Star. The book introduces Jedi Thracia Cho Leem, who “remarks with disdain that Mace Windu has never married, whereas she has been married and has children. This is in direct opposition to the rules of the Jedi Code established later in Episode II.” S081 painting of young Anakin over a slain Blood Carver; caption at top of S082: **An anguished twelve-year-old Anakin Skywalker stands over Ke Daiv, a Blood Carver who he has killed through an uncontrolled burst of Force power in order to save the life of young Jabitha.**
- **JEDI APPRENTICE SPECIAL EDITION: THE FOLLOWERS** — April 2002; 39 BBY (Part 1) / 29 BBY (Part 2). Kodai [R-6], Ploo II [N-7]. Dr. Murk Lundi, a Sith Holocron, Norval. **BTS:** Norval’s benefactors are never identified beyond a “sleek gray ship”; later source material tracks this Holocron from the Jedi Archives to Lumiya. Early appearance of Kit Fisto, later revealed as Bant Eerin’s Master.
- **JEDI QUEST: PATH TO TRUTH** — Watson; Buelow and Mattingly; hardcover September 2001; 28 BBY. Ilum, Nar Shaddaa, Tatooine flashback. Krayn; Siri Tachi undercover as Zora. **BTS (S083–S084):** launched the teen-years series. In 2000 Lucas Licensing planned a *Jedi Quest* banner across books, comics, toys, and games. An internal Lucasfilm memo dated March 13, 2000, lists six possible Hasbro figures and other tie-ins; “the Jedi Quest multimedia project ultimately did not come to full fruition,” though the Scholastic series continued. Dark Horse adapted only this first book (Ryder Windham / Pop Mhan, 2001). Hasbro’s 2001 figures did not mention *Jedi Quest*; the name was used for a Kids Club. RPG, interactive, and audio tie-ins never materialized. The series was meant to offer glimpses of Episode II, but only *Path to Truth* arrived ahead of the 2002 Episode II titles. “The insectoid Colicoid aliens featured in the book were at one point planned to be the first appearance of Geonosian aliens prior to *Attack of the Clones*.”
- **JEDI QUEST #I: THE WAY OF THE APPRENTICE** (banner **#I**; May 2002; 27 BBY; Radnor [Q-16]). Introduces Ferus Olin, Tru Veld, Darra Thel-Tanis.
- **OUTBOUND FLIGHT** — Timothy Zahn; Dave Seeley; HC January 2006 / PB January 2007; 27 BBY. Barlok, Crustai, Geroon, Lonnaw, Roxuli, Yaga Minor. Mitth’raw’nuruodo (Thrawn), Jorj Car’das, Jorus C’baoth, Lorana Jinzler, Kinman Doriana, Sidious’s wish to destroy the project because of the Far Outsiders. Long summary through the radiation-bomb slaughter, Thrass, the crash, and Doriana offering Car’das a Republic information-network post. **BTS (S086):** “the first novel in the time line by author Timothy Zahn, the writer most often credited with starting the modern Star Wars Expanded Universe with *Heir to the Empire* (1991).” HTTE introduced Thrawn and C’baoth and mentioned Outbound Flight; Car’das first appeared in *Specter of the Past* (1997), though this is his first time-line appearance. C’baoth’s Jedi meld is later commonplace in the NJO, “suggesting either an increase in power and control in ‘modern’ Jedi Knights, or a less potent incarnation of the meld itself.” Fate of the wreck and of Lorana: *Survivor’s Quest* (2004). Thrawn’s next appearance: the short story “Mist Encounter” (1995). S087 painting of C’baoth’s hologram throttling Thrawn; caption at top of S088: **An infuriated Jorus C’baoth reaches out through the Force to throttle Thrawn, the alien tactical genius who has thwarted his plans for Outbound Flight.**
- **JQ #2 The Trail of the Jedi** (May 2002; 27 BBY; Ragoon VI). Granta Omega; Floria and Dane. **BTS:** Obi-Wan warns Anakin about using absolute statements—“a lesson he will memorably repeat during their fateful duel in” Episode III (2005). Didi Oddo has sold his café to Dexter Jettster.
- **JQ #3 The Dangerous Games** (August 2002; 27 BBY; Euceron [Q-7]). Galactic Games; Hekula, son of Sebulba. **BTS (error call-out):** “The book states that Anakin has not raced since he was eight years old, when in fact he was nine years old during the Boonta Eve Podrace that saw his liberation.”
