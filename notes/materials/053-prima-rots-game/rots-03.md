# 053 · Prima ROTS game guide, Part 3: Challenge Missions, Interviews, Appendix, Gallery and Credits (pp. 98–126) plus the cover

## Challenge Missions (co-op, pp. 98–105)
- **CM1, "Cruiser Cargo Hold"** (Anakin + Obi-Wan): droid waves; "There are no bacta tanks on this level."
- **CM2, "Cruiser Corridors":** escort R2; "The powers and attacks of most Jedi in the challenge missions are set. You get the same special attacks for these characters that they get in the multiplayer duels." It ends with Grievous's bodyguards: "they don’t stand a chance against two determined Jedi."
- **CM3, "Control Sphere Survival"** (Utapau): waves of droids, then "five turrets at once".
- **CM4, "The Jedi Strike Back":** Cin Drallig and Serra Keto. "Jedi Master Cin Drallig and his apprentice Serra Keto engage the invading clone forces in the Jedi Temple".
  - Eight areas end with clone walkers. "As a Jedi Master, Cin Drallig can stun more than one enemy at a time."
  - This is the only mode where Drallig and Keto are playable heroes.

## Interviews (pp. 106–115): the strongest material in the guide
### Anthony Doe, Lead Combat Designer/Scripter, The Collective (pp. 106–107)
- Gillard's training: "From the beginning, Nick showed us the basics of Jedi combat: the stances, attacks, defenses, and philosophies. It was a kind of modern-day Jedi training."
- "It’s the reason why Nick brought in his good friend, Hayden Christensen, to work with us."
- The team's process: "as combat designers at The Collective, we act out fights ... A lot of us are martial artists".
- Anecdote: Gillard says "“And now,” he said, “for something really dangerous.”" and then "broke out into a back spin. It was Jedi breakdancing!" Afterwards, "our director is doing the moonwalk".
- On Christensen: "He impressed us all by showing some serious talent with the practice saber (much more so than in the previous films)."
- The game has "over a hundred unique player character abilities".
- The two styles: "Anakin is the raw talent, unrefined, passionate and strong ... flashy yet efficient, direct and powerful, arrogant, unrestrained." By contrast, "Obi-Wan is calm, focused, and controlled". "He “glides,” as Nick described."
- Doe calls the Force "your special sauce in combat—your personal can of whoop-ass."

### Nick Gillard, Stunt Coordinator, interviewed at ILM on January 14, 2004 (pp. 108–111)
- The moves are a written notation: "a written system that only a few of us know. Only the actors and the stunt guys do it".
- On games: "I’ve only played three games, and it was, you know, knocking around with Hayden, who plays a lot."
- Game versus film: "When we do the movie, it has to be believable. But I think with a game it should be more unbelievable." Also: "there are lots of moves that don’t work on the movie. They might be too complicated, or they might be too fierce".
- **The Cin Drallig cameo, in his words:** "My character in the game is larger than he is in the film—it’s only a hologram in the film." And: "I only did it providing my name could be my own name. So I have my own name backward. Which is Cin Drallig. I play an instructor, and I get killed by Anakin (laughs). Obviously in real life, I’d cream him."
- He brought the real props: "Obi’s is always much thicker than everyone else’s. Anakin’s is longer." He taught "why Obi is so bouncy and jolly."
- On the game team: "they’re all like 17 years old (laughs)! You can’t impress them as much."
- On sword fights: "I pretty much treat it totally as a dance ... It’s more like choreography than fight arranging."
- Character faults since TPM: "When we started on Phantom Menace, I set out certain styles and, particularly, faults for characters."
- On the Mustafar duel:
  - "I think it’ll be the longest fight in cinema history."
  - "I had Hayden for maybe seven weeks of rehearsal. I tried to keep Hayden and Ewan apart for a long time."
  - "I took it on as like a fight between a husband and wife. It sounds silly, but Obi doesn’t want to kill him."
  - "hoping that Anakin was going to eventually, you know, get over it and calm down. That doesn’t happen."
- On the characters' levels: "Obi-Wan has gone up one level from Episode I to Episode III ... Anakin has gone up probably four levels from Episode II to Episode III. So he’s gone beyond Obi-Wan, but he hasn’t gone beyond him mentally."
- On Anakin now: "He now no longer cares. He knows he’s unbeatable."
- The faults: Anakin's "would be being on a slope". Obi-Wan: "Obi’s is his aggression. If he has a downfall, that’s it."
- A dream fight: "Boba Fett versus Anakin."
- Background: "I was in the circus when I was a child, and I went from that into medieval jousting." On being mugged: "once in Brighton, which was two of them making a dreadful mistake."
- On scripts: "It just says, you know, “vicious fight,” and so I just ask George how long he wants it."
- He gets lightsaber-sound tapes from Ben Burtt.

### Justin Lambros, Associate Producer, LucasArts (pp. 112–115)
- "we’ve actually included levels based on scenes that were written and shot for the film, but won’t make the final cut." This refers to the deleted Temple and Utapau material.
- "The game comes out two weeks before the movie." He says "we were very careful not to spoil key moments of the film."
- On Darth Vader: the game will "really make you understand why Darth Vader is this icon, this villain, this man who has helped control the galaxy."
- On the title of the guide's introduction: "Darth Vader’s, you know, famous line is, “The circle is now complete.” Well, we’re now going to be able to see, and actually live, the completing of that circle."
- Inspiration: "Jedi Knight II: Jedi Outcast and Jedi Academy had some really terrific saber fighting". The team also played KOTOR, Jedi Power Battles, Clone Wars and Bounty Hunter.
- Access:
  - "we got to meet with George Lucas to get his synopsis of the story before he even finished writing the first draft of the script".
  - "Rick McCallum promised us “whatever we needed,”".
  - Playtesting early builds "with Visual Effects Supervisor John Knoll".

## Experience & Skill Appendix (pp. 116–117)
- XP per kill at Fair (1.0x):
  - Battledroid 200; Crab Droid 3,000; Padawan 400; Jedi Brute 1,000; Clone Assassin 1,000.
  - Duel bosses: Count Dooku 20,000; General Grievous 20,000; Mace Windu 22,500; Serra 10,000; Cin Drallig 15,000; Anakin 25,000.
  - Vehicles: Clone Gunship 7,500; Neimoidian Shuttle 7,500.
  - The table spells it "Grievious Bodyguard" (1,250).
- Multipliers: Good 1.5x, Impressive 2.0x, Masterful 3.0x. Duel bosses show N/A beyond Good, Impressive and Masterful; the extracted table is ambiguous.
- Mission XP targets run from 20,000 (M6) to 70,000 (M11, M14, M15). "For each character, you must gain 400,000 experience points in order to completely upgrade." M17 "doesn’t earn you experience points".
- **Skill meter:**
  - It caps at 1,000.
  - Rating bands: Fair 0–34%, Good 35–69%, Impressive 70–99%, Masterful 100%.
  - Masterful lasts "for 20 seconds, your lightsaber attacks do 150% of normal damage and enemies can’t block".
  - Idle drain: 10, 20, 30 and 40 points per second after 0–10, 10–20, 20–30 and 30+ seconds.
- This matches p. 9, which says "a Good rating with a 150 percent bonus" (the "150 percent" is the 1.5x multiplier), and likewise 200 and 300 percent.

## Overall read
- This is a 127-page guide, read cover to cover through its text layer. The Gallery and cover were checked as images.
- Its lore value lies mainly in:
  - the canon-adjacent bios of game-original Jedi (Serra Keto; the Temple Brutes, Snipers, Knights and Padawans);
  - the clone assassins' Tera Kasi training and ARC lineage;
  - the game-only events: Vader shoots down Gunray's shuttle, Anakin sets the Temple beacon, Anakin and Mace have a full duel, and the M17 "rewrite history" ending;
  - the Gillard and Lambros interviews.
