## Ch. THREE (l.1017): Master Djinn Altis
- Epigraph (Altisian view): "Master Altis doesn't subscribe to those views about family. Does that place him on the dark side?… are there other Force-users on the light side?" (l.1019)
- Altis senses a world's unrest: "the collective distress and anger of a world tipping over into conflict. This was how the Force spoke to him, its unique voice, its tone different for every Force-user who listened." (l.1031)
- Altis: "I'm not a pacifist. I'll meet force with force when I have to." (l.1041)
- Geith on Altis's teaching: "If the means are morally unacceptable, then the ends must be unjustifiable." (l.1063)
- Altis's Force foresight sends his ship to the Fath system: "Altis knew with Force certainty that Wookiee Gunner would need to be there, and soon." (l.1107)
- Rex: the clones are trained to believe "Jedi were invincible and omniscient" (l.1227).
- **Full line-by-line read additions (2026-11-03):**
  - The epigraph's speaker is Padawan Bagar Nei-Leit in a Jedi Temple philosophy class (l.1019–1021): "What about the Jedi who founded our Order? They didn't ban attachment."
  - l.1029 Altis's Force-sense imagery: lights and ribbons, "microscopic pellets of hail... cold water... spinal canal".
  - l.1035 Callista Masana communes with the nav computer and is "as attuned with inanimate objects as... the tsaelkes on her parents' farm". l.1037 the mission is humanitarian relief to Yarille.
  - l.1049 Altis: the Jedi "is not a pacifist because he chooses to stop the fire by violence, on the basis that fewer will suffer that way... do ends ever justify means?" l.1061 "Compassion—compassion is an act, not an idea."
  - l.1055 married couples are "nothing remarkable in this Jedi sect, was a quiet embarrassment that the rest of the Jedi Order tried hard to ignore".
  - l.1077 Geith: "it's wrong to breed human beings and make them fight. We have no moral authority."
  - l.1079–1085 Callista was an adult on her parents' farm when she became Altis's second Padawan. Altis: "I prefer my Padawans to enter the Order with open eyes"; "this is why the orthodox Jedi way is to begin with infants. They know no better."
